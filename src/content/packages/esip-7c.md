## How the footprint was made

The hole positions are the "Mounting Hole Pattern" printed under the eSIP-7C (E package) drawing in the TOP264-271 datasheet (Rev. F, PDF page 35, drawing PI-4917). There are two rows 3.00 mm apart (1.50 mm each side of a centre line). Pins 1, 3 and 5 are in one row and pins 2, 4 and 7 in the other, 2.54 mm apart within a row, with the rows offset 1.27 mm. Pin 7 is 2.54 mm past pin 5, which the drawing confirms with a 3.93 mm diagonal from pin 5 to pin 7. There is no pin 6.

The side view says which row is which: the straight row is 1.19 mm in front of the back face (the exposed-pad side), and the other row is formed 3.00 mm further forward. The datasheet's layout example for this package (Figure 31) puts V, C and S (pins 1, 3, 5) next to the heat sink and X, F and D (pins 2, 4, 7) on the far side, so pins 1, 3 and 5 are the straight row. The body outline on the fab layer is the body seen from above: 10.16 mm wide (the middle of the range) and 2.01 mm thick, from the back face forward, centred on the pin 4 position as in the front view.

The positions were read from the rendered drawing and its printed dimensions, not from the PDF's vector paths. The pattern is marked "not to scale".

**The datasheet gives no hole or pad size, so those are ours.** The lead is 0.71–0.84 mm wide and 0.28–0.41 mm thick, so its largest diagonal is about 0.93 mm. We used a 1.10 mm drill, which leaves about 0.17 mm round that diagonal, and a 1.80 mm round pad, which leaves a 0.35 mm ring and 0.74 mm between pads in the same row. Pin 1 is a rounded square (the usual KiCad marker). The 0.25 mm courtyard, the silkscreen along the back face and both ends (the end on pin 1's side runs on past the second row as the pin 1 marker) and the pin 1 chamfer on the fab outline are also ours.

## Orientation

Seen from above with the back face (exposed pad) at the top, pin 1 is at the left of the back row and pin 7 at the right of the front row. This is the datasheet's hole pattern as drawn, and it agrees with Figure 31 turned a quarter turn, without mirroring. The part stands upright, so the footprint covers only where the leads go through the board and the body's thin base; a heat sink clipped to the back face needs its own room behind the footprint.

## Things to watch

- **Hole and pad sizes are ours.** Check them against your board maker's rules and the real lead size before ordering. A larger drill makes insertion easier but shrinks the copper ring and the gaps between pads.
- **Use the E symbol with this footprint.** This package numbers SOURCE as pin 5 and DRAIN as pin 7, unlike the eDIP-12B and eSOP-12B, so the TOP264-271 symbol file has a separate `TOP264-271_eSIP-7C` symbol.
- **DRAIN spacing.** With these pads, DRAIN (pin 7) is 2.01 mm, pad edge to pad edge, from pin 4 and 2.13 mm from pin 5. Larger pads reduce that.
- **The heat sink is SOURCE potential.** The datasheet says the exposed pad is tied to SOURCE inside the package and that a heat sink on it should not be tied to any primary ground or SOURCE node on the board, to avoid circulating currents.
- **Only the TOP264-271 drawing was compared.** Other Power Integrations parts in an eSIP-7C would need their own drawing checked.
- **Not yet opened in KiCad.** The file was generated by script and checked against the printed dimensions by an independent parse, but it has not been loaded in KiCad.
