## How the footprint was made

This one follows a land pattern in the datasheet. The BQ25190's mechanical appendix gives the package outline (YBG0030, PDF page 99), an example board layout (page 100) and an example stencil (page 101). We took the copper size, the mask opening and the paste apertures from those pages.

Each ball gets a round copper pad of 0.23 mm, the figure the example board layout gives for the metal, with a solder mask opening of the same nominal size. The datasheet marks the non-solder-mask-defined arrangement as preferred, so the pad is copper only and the mask opening is the copper plus a 0.05 mm margin on each side, which matches the drawing's "0.05 mm min" from metal to mask. The 0.05 mm figure is read from the detail view and is the least certain number here; check it against your fabricator's mask rules.

The stencil drawing shows a 0.25 mm square aperture with rounded corners (0.05 mm radius) on a 0.1 mm stencil. KiCad would otherwise make the paste shape follow the copper, so each ball has a second, unnamed pad on the paste layer only, sized and rounded as the drawing shows. Because that pad has no number, the netlist is not affected.

Everything else is ours: the courtyard, the silkscreen corner marks and pin 1 dot, and the fab-layer body outline at the middle of the size range with a 0.5 mm chamfer at the A1 corner. The courtyard is the larger of the body edge and the pad plus its mask opening, plus 0.25 mm, rounded up to the next 0.05 mm.

## Orientation

Ball A1 is at the top left, the same corner as in the datasheet's top-view pin diagram (Figure 5-1) and the A1 corner marked on its package drawing. Rows A to F run from top to bottom and columns 1 to 5 from left to right, so the long side of the die is vertical (six rows by five columns). The footprint uses the same ball map as Figure 5-1 without flipping it; the datasheet's land-pattern drawing is the check on that, and it has not been laid over the footprint at scale.

Ball names are letter then number (A1, B3, F5), the same as the datasheet's pin table. The package has no exposed pad.

## Things to watch

- **The mask and paste figures are small.** A 0.4 mm pitch with 0.23 mm pads leaves 0.17 mm between pads. Whether your fabricator can hold a 0.05 mm mask margin on every ball is a question for them.
- **No routing escape is drawn.** Five columns by six rows is small enough that the inner balls (rows B to E, columns 2 to 4) need vias or fine traces. The datasheet's layout example shows the idea but not trace widths.
- **Only one datasheet compared.** The pattern comes from the BQ25190 appendix and is checked against that part only. A different part in a 30-ball die-size package with the same pitch may have a different die size or ball diameter.
- **Wafer-chip-scale parts are fragile.** There is no mold compound; the silicon is exposed on five sides. Handling, underfill and assembly guidance is in TI's WCSP application notes, which this page does not summarise.
- **Not yet opened in KiCad.** The footprint was generated by script and checked against the datasheet numbers with an independent parser, but nobody has loaded it in KiCad or printed it at scale.
