## How it works

The ADE9039 is a seven-channel energy metering IC for meters that sense current with di/dt sensors. Figure 1 shows seven analog input pairs, IA, VA, IB, VB, IC, VC and IN, and each pair has its own programmable-gain amplifier (PGA) and ADC. A 1.25 V reference and a temperature sensor with its own converter sit beside them. The ADC outputs go to a digital block: a sinc filter with decimation, a DSP engine, the user registers, a resampling engine, event interrupts, a digital-to-frequency converter for the CF pins, a waveform buffer and an SPI port. All measurements are made on chip, so the host reads results over SPI instead of doing the arithmetic.

The current inputs are meant for **PCB-based di/dt current sensors**, which ADI's product-page subtitle ties to Rogowski sensors. The sensor output goes through an **ADE1139** amplifier before it reaches the IAP and IAN pins. The datasheet calls the ADE9039 and ADE1139 together a chipset. The voltage inputs take a resistor-divider fraction of each phase. A fourth sensor on the neutral conductor feeds the INP and INN pair.

The front page lists these measurements, and Figure 1 adds a few names to the DSP block:

- Active, reactive and apparent energy on each phase and for the whole system.
- Total and fundamental active and apparent power, and fundamental reactive power.
- Total and fundamental IRMS and VRMS, with VRMS½ and IRMS½ refreshed every half cycle. Figure 1 also names fast RMS and 10-cycle and 12-cycle RMS.
- Power factor, phase angle, total harmonic distortion (VTHD and ITHD in the figure) and line frequency, one value per phase.
- Zero crossing with a timeout, and dip and swell monitors. The figure also lists VPEAK, IPEAK, overcurrent and a phase sequence error.
- A flexible waveform buffer that can resample continuously to 64 points per line cycle, so an outside processor can run harmonic analysis, and that can be triggered to store a waveform by events such as a dip or a swell.

**What this page can and can't tell you.** The only datasheet ADI publishes for the ADE9039 at the moment is a two-page front sheet (Rev. SpB): features, applications and Figure 1. It has no pin table, no pin numbers, no electrical characteristics, no absolute maximum ratings, no register map, no SPI timing and no package drawing. Everything on this page is from those two pages, plus the package and ordering facts on ADI's product page, and each product-page fact is labelled. Anything you need to design in (supply range, input range, clock, reset and SPI behaviour) has to come from ADI's full datasheet, which the contact line on page 1 points to (www.analog.com/sales).

### The ADE1139

The ADE9039 is named as half of a chipset with the ADE1139, and every current channel in Figure 1 has one. The datasheet's front page says nothing else about it. ADI's separate ADEMO139B product page describes a "low-noise amplifier for use with PCB-based di/dt sensors and the ADE9039": a low-drift, fully differential programmable-gain amplifier on a single 2.97 to 3.63 V supply, with gains of 27.46, 42.93 and 59.77 (Av settings 30, 45 and 60), a 0.33 V output common-mode voltage that connects directly to the ADE9039, and an integrated temperature sensor. Neither the ADE9039 page nor the ADEMO139B page uses the other's amplifier name, so **we can't say whether the ADE1139 and the ADEMO139B are the same part**. Ask ADI before ordering either. The sensor name printed in each block of Figure 1, ADEMDIDT-DG-DFL0418, isn't on either product page.

### Pins in Figure 1

Figure 1 shows 28 of the package's 40 leads and gives no pin numbers. It draws no supply pin; the figure doesn't say what the other 12 leads are, and presumably they include the 3.3 V supply (our reading). Names with a bar over them in the figure are active low.

### Split-phase and other layouts

The front page says the chipset suits a "polyphase direct connect meter or split-phase meter" and lists smart breakers, EV chargers and power distribution units. Only the three-phase case is drawn. The datasheet doesn't say how to connect fewer channels, or what to do with unused inputs.

### No worked example

There is no worked example on this page, because there is nothing to calculate from. The front page gives no input range, PGA gain, ADC resolution or divider value. To size a voltage divider or a sensor you need the full datasheet's electrical characteristics and the sensor's output in volts per ampere per hertz.

## Layout

The front page has no layout text and no package drawing. ADI's product page lists the package as a 40-lead LFCSP, 6 mm × 6 mm with an exposed pad, and gives no dimensions or land pattern. Figure 1 draws a single GND pin, and the datasheet doesn't say how to connect the exposed pad. Keep to ADI's full datasheet and its evaluation material for pad connection, decoupling and the routing of the sensor lines, which carry very small signals.

## What the datasheet shows

The features list, the applications list and Figure 1 (a block diagram with the typical meter connections). That is all of the Rev. SpB document; page 2 is a notes page. The package and ordering rows on this page come from ADI's product page, not from the datasheet.

## Things to watch

- **The public datasheet is a front sheet.** There is no supply range or current, no ADC resolution or sample rate, no input range or PGA gains, no absolute maximum ratings or operating temperature, no clock frequency, no SPI protocol or timing and no register map. Get the full datasheet from ADI before designing in.
- **Figure 1 is not a buildable circuit.** It draws no supply or decoupling on the ADE9039, no clock source, no reset network and no host connection, and shows 28 of 40 leads.
- **The accuracy figures are for the system, not the chip alone.** The active energy figure (under 0.1% over 2000 to 1) and the RMS figures (under 0.1% over 1000 to 1) are at 25 °C, and the standards claim is "based on the ADE9039 and ADE1139 chipset" with a di/dt sensor. Expect different numbers with other sensors or amplifiers.
- **The 32 A to 1 kA range is a sensor-system claim.** It is stated as "ideal for AC applications from 32 A to 1 kA", and the sheet doesn't say how the range is set.
- **Check the amplifier name.** The front page says ADE1139; ADI's page for the matching di/dt amplifier is called ADEMO139B. See above.
- **Five pins are active low.** RESET, IRQ0, IRQ1, SS and EVENT carry bars in the figure.
- **The waveform buffer label is ambiguous.** Figure 1 prints "16kSPS, 4kSPS ADC samples or resampled data" and doesn't say which rate applies to which, or what the ADC rate itself is.
- **The orderable parts are ADE9039ACPZ and ADE9039ACPZ-RL.** The product page doesn't explain the -RL suffix; it is probably the reel version (our reading). The page lists two product-change notices: 25_0054 (February 25, 2025), a gold-to-copper bond wire conversion, and 24_0009 (January 7, 2026), qualification of an alternative wafer fab on TSMC's 0.18 µm mixed-signal CMOS process. Both list the part as in production.

## Datasheet notes

- **The revision is "SpB" and the document has no revision history.** The product page names Rev. SpB as the most current revision, dated 09/12/2025. A search-engine copy of the same file name showed an earlier Rev. SpA, so an older copy may be in circulation.
- **The product page's lifecycle label is not clear.** It shows "Recommended for new designs", and its change-notice table lists the part as "Production". One automated read of the English page also reported the word "obsolete" in the page's meta description. We couldn't confirm that, so check the status with ADI before relying on it.
- **Page 1 prints the contact line "For more information about the ADE9039, contact your local Analog Devices, Inc., sales office"** in place of a full document.
- **A related part with a full datasheet.** The [ADE9078](site:parts/analog-devices/ade9078/) is a different Analog Devices metering AFE, in the same 40-lead 6 × 6 mm package, with a 106-page public datasheet that has a pin table, electrical characteristics and a register map. Do not carry its numbers over to the ADE9039, because the two datasheets do not say they are alike.
