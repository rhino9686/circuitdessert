## How it works

The ADE9078 is a polyphase energy metering AFE with a DSP core, in one 40-lead package. It has **seven simultaneous 24-bit second-order Σ-Δ ADCs**: current channels IA, IB, IC and IN, and voltage channels VA, VB and VC. Each has fully differential inputs and a programmable-gain amplifier with a gain of 1, 2 or 4. A 1.25 V band-gap reference is on the chip. The full-scale input is ±1 V peak (0.707 V rms) at a gain of 1, and the ADC pins must stay within ±0.6 V of ground, so the inputs are meant for **current transformers with a burden resistor, Rogowski coils (di/dt sensors), and resistor dividers** on the voltage side. A digital integrator for Rogowski coils is built in (enable it with the INTEN and ININTEN bits in CONFIG0), and CT users get multi-range phase and gain compensation.

The clock is a 12.288 MHz crystal on CLKIN and CLKOUT, or an external clock on CLKIN. The ADC modulator runs at 1.024 MHz (CLKIN / 12). A sinc4 filter gives 16 kSPS waveform data, and an IIR low-pass stage gives 4 kSPS. The DSP then calculates, per phase:

- Total active power, reactive power (VAR) and apparent power (VA), with watt-hour, VAR-hour and VA-hour energy registers, plus fundamental VAR and VAR-hour.
- Current and voltage rms (xIRMS, xVRMS), updated at 4 kSPS, and a neutral-current rms.
- Power factor, line period (one measurement per phase), zero crossing with a timeout, and phase angle between the channels.
- Phase sequence error detection and no-load detection.

Results are read over a 10 MHz SPI port. Four pulse outputs, CF1 to CF4, carry power information for calibration, and two active-low interrupt pins, IRQ0 and IRQ1, report events. A **2048 × 32-bit waveform buffer** (addresses 0x800 to 0xFFF) stores ADC samples or DSP-processed samples, can be triggered by events, and can resample to 64 points per line cycle, so a host can run harmonic analysis (IEC 61000-4-7) without windowing.

### Power modes

The PM1 and PM0 pins choose one of four modes, and the pins are checked continuously. For normal operation both are low.

| Mode | PM1 | PM0 | Supply current (Table 8) | What works |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| PSM0, normal | 0 | 0 | 10 mA | Everything |
| PSM1, tamper measurement | 0 | 1 | 9 mA | Fast active and reactive power, IRMS and VRMS, zero crossing, period and angle. The neutral channel, waveform buffer, and energy, power and CF accumulation are off. SPI works |
| PSM2, current peak detect | 1 | 0 | 115 µA | A low-power comparator checks the current channels against a threshold and reports on IRQ0 and IRQ1. SPI does not work |
| PSM3, idle | 1 | 1 | 50 nA | Nothing. SPI does not work |

PSM2_CFG is the one register kept in PSM2 and PSM3, and it returns to its default when PSM0 or PSM1 is entered. The intended tamper scheme has the host cycle the chip through PSM3 and PSM2 about once a minute and move to PSM1 if a tamper is seen, because the meter may then be running from a battery.

### Power-on

When VDD is applied the chip reads PM0 and PM1. If the mode is PSM0 or PSM1 and RESET is high, the internal AVDD (1.9 V) and DVDD (1.7 V) LDOs switch on once VDD reaches 2.4 V to 2.6 V. A 20 ms timer then runs, the crystal starts, and about 26 ms later the **RSTDONE interrupt pulls IRQ1 low**. Only then can the host configure the chip. Each LDO is charged through a clamp of about 17 mA, so the supply has to deliver about 40 mA at start-up. A low pulse on RESET of at least 1 µs resets the chip, and a software reset (SWRST in CONFIG1) takes about 60 µs.

### What the filters are

Each current input has a 1 kΩ resistor and a 22 nF capacitor to ground, and the voltage inputs have 1 kΩ in parallel with 22 nF. The datasheet says the antialiasing corner is chosen around 7 kHz, and 1 / (2π × 1 kΩ × 22 nF) is 7.2 kHz (calculated). The datasheet uses the same corner on the voltage channels to avoid a phase error between current and voltage. In the Quick Start the voltage divider is 1 MΩ over 1 kΩ, and the 1 kΩ in Figure 27 matches that lower leg (our reading), which puts the filter corner at about 7.2 kHz with the divider's 1 MΩ in parallel (calculated).

The three sensor circuits in the ADC section show where these values come from:

- **Figure 32, current transformer.** A 2500:1 CT (100 A rms maximum, 10 A rms nominal) with two 8.2 Ω burden resistors in series and a centre tap to AGND, then 1 kΩ in each leg and 22 nF from each leg to AGND. The figure labels the maximum signal 0.656 V rms. Figure 27's current network is this one.
- **Figure 33, mains voltage.** A 1 MΩ resistor from the phase to VxP, with 1 kΩ and 22 nF from VxP to AGND, and 1 kΩ and 22 nF from VxN to AGND, giving 0.240 V rms at the pin for 240 V rms. Figure 27's voltage network is this one, without the 1 MΩ.
- **Figure 34, Rogowski coil.** A second-order filter in each leg: 100 Ω then a 22 nF capacitor to AGND, then 1 kΩ and a second 22 nF to AGND, for a 0.3535 V rms source. The datasheet says a Rogowski input needs at least a second-order antialiasing filter, so Figure 27's single-stage network is not enough for a di/dt sensor.

### Worked example: checking the voltage and power readings

The Quick Start works one example, and the numbers below redo it. A 240 V rms phase goes through a 1 MΩ and 1 kΩ divider, and the current is a 10 A load on a 1000:1 CT with a 10 Ω burden. The full-scale input is 0.707 V rms at a gain of 1. The datasheet gives the full-scale VRMS register reading as 52,866,837 and the full-scale xWATT reading as 20,823,646.

1. Voltage at the pin: 240 V × 1 kΩ / (1 kΩ + 1 MΩ) = 0.2398 V rms (calculated).
2. Fraction of full scale: 0.2398 / 0.7071 = 33.9% (calculated). The datasheet recommends about half of full scale for the nominal input, to leave room for overvoltage, so this is on the low side.
3. Expected xVRMS reading: 0.33908 × 52,866,837 ≈ 17,925,674 (calculated). The datasheet prints 17,921,858, because it rounds the fraction to 33.9% first.
4. Current: 10 A / 1000 = 10 mA through 10 Ω gives 0.1 V rms, which is 14.1% of full scale (calculated).
5. Expected xWATT reading for 2.4 kW: 0.33908 × 0.14142 × 20,823,646 ≈ 998,536 (calculated). The datasheet prints 999,535, again from rounded percentages.
6. Scaling: if the registers read 18,000,000 at 240 V, the datasheet takes 75,000 codes per volt (Volts = xVRMS / 75,000). If xWATT reads 1,000,000 at 2.4 kW, that is 416,667 codes per kW (Watts = xWATT / 416,667).

The datasheet says the sampling capacitors vary from device to device, so gain calibration (xIGAIN, xVGAIN and xPGAIN) is required, and phase calibration as well with a CT.

### Start-up sequence from the Quick Start

1. Wait for RSTDONE, which pulls IRQ1 low.
2. Write xIGAIN, xVGAIN and xPGAIN, then any other calibration registers.
3. If a CF output is used, write CFxDEN and the xTHR registers.
4. Set the line frequency in SELFREQ and write VLEVEL = 0x117514.
5. With a Rogowski coil, set INTEN (and ININTEN for the neutral) in CONFIG0 and write DICOEF = 0xFFFFE000. With CTs, INTEN and ININTEN must be 0.
6. For services other than 4-wire wye, set ICONSEL and VCONSEL in ACCMODE.
7. Write 1 to RUN (0x480), then write 1 to EP_CFG (0x4B0).

## Layout

The pin table asks for a matching pad on the PCB under the exposed pad, soldered to it, with **all four grounds (GND, AGND, DGND and REFGND) joined at that pad**. The package is CP-40-7, a 6 mm × 6 mm lead frame chip scale package, 0.75 mm high, with a thermal resistance of 27.14 °C/W junction to ambient and 3.13 °C/W junction to case. The sheet notes that PCB thermal design needs attention. Because the digital return currents are small, DGND can go to the analog ground plane. The outline drawing (Figure 114) is the only package drawing we found, and we saw no land pattern. The KiCad footprint on the package page is built from that outline, with pad sizes of our own.

Place the decoupling capacitors as the figure shows: 10 µF and 0.1 µF on VDD, and 4.7 µF with 0.1 µF on each of AVDDOUT, DVDDOUT and REF. The datasheet says not to attach external active circuitry to the LDO output pins.

For the crystal, keep the load capacitors close to CLKIN and CLKOUT, and measure the parasitic capacitance of the traces, because the datasheet's load calculation uses it (see Things to watch). The datasheet advises against an external feedback resistor across the crystal.

### SPI

The port runs at up to 10 MHz in mode 0 (CPOL = 0, CPHA = 0) or mode 3 (CPOL = 1, CPHA = 1), MSB first. Each transfer starts with a 16-bit command header: bits 15 to 4 are the register address, bit 3 is 1 for a read and 0 for a write, and bits 2 to 0 are timing bits. Data is 16 or 32 bits depending on the register. A read ends with a CRC unless the address is in the burst-read region, where the next register follows. SS must stay low for the whole transfer, and the datasheet recommends not tying SS to ground, because the falling edge starts the transaction. MISO has a weak internal 100 kΩ pull-up.

## Things to watch

- **VDD is 2.7 V to 3.63 V, with 3.96 V as the absolute maximum.** Analog input pins may go to −1.9 V and +2 V absolute maximum, but must stay within ±0.6 V in operation.
- **Gain calibration is required.** The datasheet says the sampling capacitors vary from device to device. With a CT, phase calibration is needed too.
- **Do not use read-with-reset for energy (errata).** There is a bug in read with reset when using energy accumulation. Keep RD_RST_EN at its default of 0 and use line-cycle or sample-based accumulation.
- **PSM2_CFG is not retained into PSM0 or PSM1.** Rewrite it before re-entering PSM2.
- **Registers are cleared at different times.** Addresses 0x000 to 0x0FF and 0x400 to 0x5FF return to defaults at power-on, 0x200 to 0x3FF are cleared within 500 µs after RUN changes from 0 to 1, and the waveform buffer (0x800 to 0xFFF) is not cleared by a reset.
- **The KiCad footprint and symbol are not from Analog Devices.** The footprint's pad sizes are ours, because the datasheet has no land pattern, and neither file has been opened in KiCad yet. The symbol's layout and pin types are our reading of the pin table. Pin 41 stands for the exposed pad, which the datasheet does not number.
- **Tie PULL_HIGH to VDD and NC1, NC2 to ground.** Figure 27 doesn't draw them.
- **Figure 27 draws the 10 µF and 4.7 µF capacitors with polarity marks.** The pin table calls them ceramic.
- **The crystal load capacitors depend on the crystal.** The datasheet's example uses a 12 pF crystal and 2 pF of parasitic capacitance per pin, which gives 18 pF capacitors. The load is CL = (CL1 + CP + 4 pF) / 2, so 18 pF capacitors give 12 pF with 2 pF parasitics (calculated). The evaluation board crystal (Abracon ABLS-12.288MHZ-L4Q-T) is described as having an 18 pF load capacitance, and the same formula would then call for about 30 pF capacitors with 2 pF parasitics (calculated). The datasheet doesn't say which crystal Figure 27 assumes, so work the load calculation for the crystal you pick and check it over temperature.
- **The CF pins can drive an LED directly.** Figure 27 does it through 1 kΩ from 3.3 V, so the LED current is below 3.3 mA (calculated), under the 8 mA sink rating that Table 1 gives for CF1 to CF4. The source rating is 8 mA at VDD of 2.97 V and up, and 3 mA at VDD = 2.7 V.
- **IRQ1 is also the reset-done flag.** It goes low about 26 ms after the supplies are good, so do not treat that first low as an event.

## Datasheet notes

- **The SPI section calls the ADE9078 an SPI "main".** Its text says "The ADE9078 is always a SPI main—it never initiates SPI communication," which contradicts the rest of the sheet (the pin table says "Subordinate Select", and Figure 96 is titled "Connecting the ADE9078 Subordinate SPI Port to a Main SPI Device"). It looks like a slip from the Rev. A change of "Master" and "Slave" to "Main" and "Subordinate" (our reading). The chip is the subordinate.
- **PSM3 current is stated two ways.** Table 1 and Table 8 give 50 nA typical (200 nA maximum), and the Reset section says about 2 µA, "see Table 1 for the exact PSM3 current consumption".
- **DVDDOUT is 1.8 V in the pin table and 1.7 V elsewhere.** Table 1 and the power-on text say DVDD is 1.7 V; the pin description says 1.8 V.
- **The reference current unit is printed as µA/V.** Table 1 gives the average reference current as 120 µA/V, which is probably µA (our reading).
- **The 4-wire delta (non-Blondel) row of Table 24 lists two current sensors, while Table 23 and the text say three currents are measured.**
- **The waveform buffer values are approximate.** The sinc4 code at 1 V peak is given as 4,190,000, which is 67,110,000 in the buffer, and the decimated code as 4,660,000, which is 74,520,000. Shifting left by four bits (× 16) gives 67,040,000 and 74,560,000 (calculated), so treat the buffer figures as approximate.
- **Revision history.** Rev. A is dated August 2025 and replaces Rev. 0 (August 2016). It changes Table 3 (absolute maximum ratings), Figure 27, several measurement sections and Table 31 and Table 32, renames master and slave to main and subordinate, and adds the errata section.
- **The datasheet states no lifecycle status.** The status on this page is "active" from ADI's product page as it appeared in a search result ("Production"), which we did not open in full.
- **Ordering.** ADE9078ACPZ and ADE9078ACPZ-RL (13-inch tape and reel), both −40 °C to +85 °C in CP-40-7, and the evaluation board is EVAL-ADE9078EBZ. Z means RoHS compliant.
- **Related part.** The [ADE9039](site:parts/analog-devices/ade9039/) is a different part with only a two-page public front sheet, for di/dt sensors with an external amplifier. This page's datasheet covers the ADE9078 only.
