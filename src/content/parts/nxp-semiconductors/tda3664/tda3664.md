## How it works

The TDA3664 is a fixed 5 V regulator. The block diagram is a band-gap reference, a regulator and a thermal protection block between VP and REG, and nothing else: no enable, no adjust, no reset. The datasheet describes it as made for portable, mains and telephone applications, with a built-in clamp circuit that keeps the quiescent current very low in dropout and at full load, to save battery life.

**Quiescent current.** 15 µA typical (30 µA maximum) at 14.4 V with no load, and 10 µA typical at 4.5 V. It rises with load to 0.2 mA typical at 10 mA and 1.4 mA typical at 50 mA. Figure 6 shows the quiescent current increasing at a high supply voltage.

**Supply range and dropout.** The regulator is specified from 3 to 45 V, and works down to a very low supply, below which it switches off. When VP is below VREG plus the dropout voltage, the output follows VP. The dropout is 0.18 V typical and 0.3 V maximum at 4.5 V in and 50 mA, for ambient temperatures up to 85 °C. The output tolerance of ±5% (4.75 to 5.25 V) holds from 6 V in at 0.5 to 100 mA.

**Protection.** This is where the part differs from a plain LDO. The supply can go to −25 V without a large reverse current (the leakage at VP = −15 V is 1 µA typical and 500 µA maximum). A negative transient of 50 V through a 10 Ω source for under 100 ms is withstood, and the output can take up to 18 V with the supply short-circuited. The output is short-circuit safe to ground and to VP, with a current limit of 0.17 A minimum and 0.25 A typical. Temperature protection switches the output off above 150 °C. Figure 13 shows a foldback protection mode with a pulsed load.

**Backup.** The datasheet notes that when the supply falls to 0 V or even −1 V, only a few µA flow back into REG from the output capacitor, so a large output capacitor gives a short backup supply for a microcontroller (Figure 4).

### Output capacitor

This is the part of the design that needs care. The datasheet says the output capacitor's ESR must stay below 22 Ω worst case and above a minimum that depends on the load current and capacitance (the table above). With a different type of capacitor, the minimum ESR can be created by an extra resistor in series with it (Figure 4). The figure's note recommends a total ESR of at least 3 Ω for reliable operation independent of load, temperature and capacitance. In the table, a 10 µF capacitor at 10 or 100 mA needs more than 4 Ω, a 1 µF one more than 0.5 Ω, and 100 nF and 100 µF have no minimum above 0 Ω except at 5 mA for 100 nF. Our reading: an electrolytic or tantalum capacitor, or a ceramic with a series resistor of several ohms, is what the datasheet expects, and a bare low-ESR ceramic is the thing to avoid. The datasheet also recommends not using the part below 1 mA of load, because of reduced phase margin.

**Noise.** The output noise (10 Hz to 100 kHz) depends on the output capacitor: 550 µV at 0.5 mA with 10 µF, falling to 300 µV with 100 µF, and 650 µV at 50 mA with 10 µF, falling to 400 µV with 47 µF or 100 µF.

### Heat and the largest load

The datasheet's equation is IREG(max) = (150 − Tamb) ÷ (Rth(j-a) × (VP − VREG)), which with 100 K/W and VREG = 5 V is (150 − Tamb) ÷ (100 × (VP − 5)) in amperes. It says the maximum output current is 140 mA at 21 °C ambient and 14 V in (our check gives 143 mA, which the datasheet rounds). The total thermal resistance of the SO4 can be lowered by soldering pin 4 and the body of the package to the board. The calculated table above applies the equation at 85 °C: at 14.4 V in the SO4 can carry about 69 mA, the SO8 about 45 mA, and the TSSOP8 is held to 30 mA by the datasheet's own limit.

## Layout

The datasheet has no layout section. Its notes on the board are that soldering pin 4 and the body of the SO4 to the printed-circuit board lowers the thermal resistance, and that the SO4 figures are for a package soldered in free air. Our notes: keep C2 close to REG and ground, connect both ground pins of the SO4 to a copper area, and leave the unused pins of the SO8 and TSSOP8 unconnected as the datasheet says they are not connected.

Pin 1 is VP on the SO4 (the top-left lead in the datasheet's pinning figure, with pins 1 to 3 down the left side and pin 4, the tab, on the right) and REG on the SO8 and TSSOP8 (pin 1 at top left, VP on pin 8). ## Things to watch

- **A bare ceramic output capacitor can make it unstable.** The datasheet requires a minimum ESR, up to more than 4 Ω for 10 µF at 10 mA and above.
- **The package sets the load at a high supply.** On the SO8 the 100 mA rating needs a low input; at 14.4 V in and 85 °C about 45 mA is allowed (calculated), and the TSSOP8 is limited to 15 mA at 125 °C and 30 mA at 85 °C by the datasheet itself.
- **Do not run it below about 1 mA.** The datasheet says the phase margin falls.
- **The three versions are not interchangeable pin for pin.** Output is pin 3 on the SO4 and pin 1 on the SO8 and TSSOP8, and the supply is pin 1 against pin 8.
- **No enable pin.** The output is on whenever the supply is above the minimum.
- **The datasheet is from 2007.** No lifecycle source was found, so the status is unknown; check availability before designing it in.
- **Plot data is for the SO8.** The additional-information curves are measured on the TDA3664AT.
- **The KiCad symbol is ours and not yet opened in KiCad.** It was generated by script and checked against the pin table, but nobody has loaded it in KiCad. The pin types and the symbol layout are our choices. The file holds one symbol for each of the three versions, because they number the pins differently.

## Datasheet notes

- **Table 8 is not monotonic.** The minimum ESR for 100 nF is more than 1 Ω at 5 mA and more than 0 Ω at 1, 10 and 100 mA, and for 10 µF it is 2.5, 1, 4 and 4 Ω for the four loads. The table is printed as given; the reason for the irregular rows is not stated.
- **The test circuit and Table 8.** The test circuit (Figure 15) uses C2 = 10 µF with no series resistor, while Table 8 asks for more than 4 Ω of ESR with 10 µF at 10 to 100 mA. Our reading: the test relied on the ESR of the capacitor used, which the datasheet doesn't state.
- **Quick reference data.** It repeats the electrical table, and note [2] in both tables limits the TSSOP8's load current.
- **The supply voltage row.** Its minimum is 3 V and its typical 14.4 V; note [1] says the output follows VP below VREG + VREG(drop).
- **Application current.** The text says the maximum output current is 140 mA at Tamb = 21 °C and VP = 14 V; the equation with 100 K/W gives 143 mA. The part is rated for 100 mA, so the equation's number is the thermal limit and not a rating.
- **What was compared.** The characteristics table (page 6) and the minimum-ESR table and backup circuit (page 7) were compared with rendered pages. The limiting values and thermal tables were read from the text layer only, so the page stays unverified.
