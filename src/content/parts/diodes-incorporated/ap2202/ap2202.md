## How it works

The AP2202 is a positive LDO with a pass transistor (Note 10 mentions its base current, so it is bipolar), a bandgap reference, a current limit and thermal shutdown. It is aimed at handsets, cordless phones, cameras and other equipment where the supply is a battery and the load is noise-sensitive. Its dropout is 165 mV typical at 150 mA and its standby current is a few hundredths of a microamp.

**Fixed and adjustable.** Pin 4 of the SOT-23-5 is BYP on the fixed versions and ADJ on the adjustable one. The fixed 2.5, 2.6, 2.8, 3.0 and 3.3 V versions come only in the SOT-23-5, except the 3.3 V, which is also in the SOT-89. The adjustable version sets its output from a 1.25 V reference and two resistors; the datasheet recommends 2.3 V as the lowest output.

**Noise and start-up.** A 100 pF capacitor from BYP to ground makes the internal reference quiet and lowers the output noise a lot, but it narrows the region in which the output capacitor is stable. Start-up speed is inversely proportional to the bypass capacitor, so where noise does not matter and a fast turn-on does, leave BYP open. The datasheet gives a noise figure of 260 nV/√Hz for the bypass capacitor of 100 pF and no figure without it; the noise-against-capacitor plot is on page 18.

**Enable.** EN is CMOS and TTL compatible: high enables the regulator, low shuts it down. The datasheet does not say what an open EN pin does, and both of its application figures tie EN to VIN. The SOT-89 has no EN pin, and the datasheet does not say how enable is handled inside it.

**Protection.** Current limit, thermal shutdown and reverse-current protection are listed in the features. Only the current limit has a number (320 mA typical with the output shorted); the thermal shutdown temperature and the reverse-current behavior are not given.

**Capacitors.** A 1 µF input capacitor is recommended. The output capacitor is required to prevent oscillation: 1.0 µF minimum with BYP unused, 2.2 µF minimum with the 100 pF bypass capacitor. A larger output capacitor improves transient response.

### Worked example: 2.8 V from 3.8 V at 150 mA, SOT-23-5

1. **Headroom.** 3.8 V less 2.8 V is 1.0 V, against a dropout of 165 mV typical, 275 mV maximum (350 mV over temperature) at 150 mA. 3.8 V is VOUT + 1 V, the condition the electrical tables use, and 0.5 V above the 3.3 V that Note 4 recommends as the lowest input for this version.
2. **Dissipation.** (3.8 − 2.8) × 0.15 A is 0.150 W. The ground-pin current at 150 mA adds 3.8 V × 1.3 mA = 4.9 mW typical (9.5 mW at the 2.5 mA maximum), so about 0.155 W (calculated).
3. **Junction temperature.** 0.155 W × 200 °C/W is a rise of 31 °C, so at 85 °C ambient the junction is at about 116 °C, under the 125 °C operating limit (calculated).
4. **How high the input can go.** The datasheet works the same case at 50 °C ambient and 150 mA and gets 6.133 V. That uses a 150 °C junction limit; with the 125 °C operating limit the same arithmetic gives 5.30 V. The design table above gives other ambients.

## Layout

The datasheet has no layout section. It gives package outlines and suggested pad layouts for the SOT-23-5 and the SOT-89, and the power-dissipation plots (SOT-23-5 and SOT-89, no heatsink). On the SOT-23-5, pin 1 is VIN. On the SOT-89, pin 1 is VOUT, pin 2 (the tab) is GND and pin 3 is VIN. The KiCad symbol file holds one symbol for each package, `AP2202_SOT-23-5` and `AP2202_SOT-89`, because the two number the pins differently and the SOT-89 has no EN or BYP/ADJ pin. The footprints are on the SOT-23-5 and SOT-89 package pages. Both are built from this datasheet's suggested pad layouts (pages 24 and 25), and the SOT-89's tab and centre lead are one pad, numbered 2. The pin types and the symbol layout are ours.

## What the datasheet shows

Pages 17 to 19 hold the typical performance plots, mostly for the 2.8 V version: output voltage, dropout, ground-pin current, enable current and enable threshold against junction temperature, ground-pin current against output current, noise against bypass capacitor, noise against frequency, load and line transients, enable start-up, PSRR against frequency, power dissipation against ambient temperature for both packages, and the stable region of output-capacitor ESR against load current for COUT of 1.0, 2.2 and 4.7 µF with no bypass capacitor. ## Things to watch

- **R1 and R2 are labelled the wrong way round, or the equation is.** The adjustable figure puts R1 from VOUT to ADJ and R2 from ADJ to ground, but prints VOUT = 1.25 × (1 + R2 / R1), and Note 4's example (R1 = 10 kΩ, R2 = 14 kΩ for 3.0 V) agrees with the equation, not the drawing. Read as drawn, those resistors give about 2.14 V (see the design table). Work the divider from the 1.25 V reference yourself and check the output.
- **The datasheet's power example uses a 150 °C junction.** The recommended operating range ends at 125 °C. The plots of power dissipation against ambient also run to 150 °C (about 0.62 W at 25 °C for the SOT-23-5, our reading of the plot, which matches 125 °C ÷ 200 °C/W). With 125 °C the 2.8 V case at 150 mA and 50 °C ambient allows 5.3 V in, not 6.1 V.
- **The recommended input range does not fit every output.** The operating table starts at 2.5 V, but Note 4 recommends VOUT + 0.5 V, which is 3.0 V for the 2.5 V version. At VOUT + 0.165 V the part regulates but the PSRR is poor.
- **Output capacitor ESR has a floor.** The stable-area plots (no bypass capacitor) start at roughly 0.03 Ω and run up to about 100 Ω at light load and 25 Ω at 150 mA (read by eye). A very low-ESR ceramic may sit below the stable area; the datasheet's own capacitor guidance gives no type. The bypass capacitor narrows the area further.
- **Shutdown needs EN below 0.18 V over temperature.** The 0.4 V low level applies at 25 °C; the over-temperature row is 0.18 V, and the 5 µA standby maximum is for that condition.
- **The SOT-89 is a reduced part.** No EN and no BYP, only a fixed 3.3 V version, and the pin order is reversed (pin 1 is VOUT, pin 3 is VIN) compared with the SOT-23-5. The datasheet shows two SOT-89 pinout drawings (Option 1 and Option 2); they carry the same pin names.
- **Not recommended for new design.** The ordering table marks every RoHS-compliant (E1) number and the green 2.6 V and 2.8 V numbers.
- **Reverse-current protection has no figures.** It is a feature, but there is no limit, no condition and no test in the electrical tables.
- **ESD.** Only a 200 V machine-model rating is given.
- **Standby current is 1 µA only at 25 °C.** The 1 µA in the description is the 25 °C maximum; the over-temperature maximum is 5 µA.
- **The KiCad files are ours and not yet opened in KiCad.** The symbol and the two footprints were generated by script and checked against the pin table and the datasheet's drawings, but nobody has loaded them in KiCad. The BYP/ADJ pin is typed passive because it is a bypass pin on the fixed versions and the feedback input on the adjustable one.

## Datasheet notes

- **Revision.** DS38214 Rev. 6-2, August 2022, 26 pages. Earlier revisions are in circulation (Rev. 3-2, January 2018, and Rev. 4-2, September 2020, 27 pages each).
- **Description wording.** The description says the AP2202 "has adjustable 2.5V, 2.6V, 2.8V, 3.0V, and 3.3V versions". The ordering table and the features make these fixed versions, with a separate adjustable version.
- **Block diagram.** The fixed-regulator diagram draws the BYP pin on the tap of the feedback divider, while the text calls it a bypass for the internal reference. We have not tried to resolve this.
- **Current limit.** The typical 320 mA is printed in the bold over-temperature typeface, and the maximum is not bold.
- **Noise unit.** The noise is given in nV/√Hz with no frequency; the noise-against-frequency plot is the only place a frequency appears.
- **Packaging marks.** Package outlines and pad layouts (pages 22 to 25) were not compared with rendered pages.
