## How it works

The NCP57152 and NCV57152 are one device: NCV is the prefix for automotive and other applications that need unique site and control-change requirements (AEC-Q100 qualified, PPAP capable), and NCP is the standard part. The datasheet covers both, in a D2PAK-5 and a DFN8, and describes it as a functional replacement for the MIC29150, MIC39150 and MIC37150 with a better minimum input voltage.

**Setting the output.** The regulator holds ADJ at 1.24 V. VOUT = 1.24 V × (1 + R1 ÷ R2) + IADJ × R1, with R1 from VOUT to ADJ and R2 from ADJ to ground. For R2 up to 15 kΩ the ADJ bias current can be neglected and R1 = R2 × (VOUT ÷ 1.24 − 1).

**Dropout and ground current.** Dropout is 330 mV typical at 1.5 A and 520 mV maximum, with 1.8 V enough input for the full current. The ground current is 40 mA typical at 1.5 A, which counts in the dissipation: PD = (VIN − VOUT) × IOUT + VIN × IGND.

**Enable.** EN is TTL/CMOS compatible and can be tied to VIN. In shutdown the part draws only leakage current.

**Error flag.** The DFN package has an open-collector flag that goes low when the output is 5% below nominal and can sink up to 5 mA. It is protected against a short of its pull-up resistor, though the datasheet does not recommend running that way. The D2PAK-5 has no flag.

**Protection.** Current limiting is constant, not folded back, which the datasheet says suits FPGAs that draw more than nominal while they start. Thermal shutdown disables the part. When the part is disabled and VOUT is less than 6.5 V above VIN, a backup battery on the output causes no reverse current, apart from the current through the feedback divider.

**Capacitors.** 47 µF or more of ceramic on the output, which also helps transient response and noise. 1 µF or more on the input when the part is more than 4 inches from the bulk capacitance or on a battery, within 1 inch of the part. A 5 mA minimum load is needed for proper operation.

### Worked example: 1.8 V from 2.5 V at 1.5 A

1. **Divider.** With R2 = 10 kΩ, R1 = 4.53 kΩ gives 1.24 × (1 + 0.453) = 1.802 V (calculated). The divider carries 124 µA, so the 5 mA minimum load must come from the load.
2. **Headroom.** 2.5 V less 1.8 V is 0.7 V, against 520 mV maximum dropout at 1.5 A.
3. **Heat.** (0.7 V × 1.5 A) + (2.5 V × 40 mA) is about 1.15 W (calculated, using the typical ground current). The D2PAK's 52 °C/W makes that a 60 °C rise and the DFN's 75 °C/W an 86 °C rise, so the DFN at 85 °C ambient would reach the 125 °C junction limit.

## Things to watch

- **Heat.** The ground current is 40 mA at full load, so the 1.5 A part dissipates VIN × 40 mA on top of the dropout loss.
- **Minimum load.** 5 mA is needed; the feedback divider alone does not supply it.
- **The flag exists only on the DFN.** The D2PAK-5 pin 1 is EN, and the DFN's FLG needs its own pull-up.
- **Output capacitor.** 47 µF of ceramic, so size for DC-bias derating.
- **Dropout at low input.** At 1.8 V in, 520 mV of maximum dropout leaves about 1.28 V or less for the output at 1.5 A.
- **Backup supply on the output.** No reverse current flows only up to 6.5 V above VIN, and the divider current still drains a backup battery.
- **Prefix.** NCV is the automotive-qualified version; both prefixes appear in the ordering table, and one DFN part is discontinued.
- **The KiCad symbol is ours and not yet opened in KiCad.** It was generated by script and checked against the pin table, but nobody has loaded it in KiCad. The pin types and the symbol layout are our choices. The DFN exposed pad's number is ours, because the pin table does not number it. The file holds one symbol for each package and has no pin for the D2PAK tab, which is the same net as pin 3.
