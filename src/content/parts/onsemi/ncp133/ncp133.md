## How it works

The NCP133 puts an NMOS pass transistor between IN and OUT. An NMOS pass device can have a very small drop, but its gate needs a voltage above the output, and the part takes that from a second pin, BIAS. So IN can be as low as 0.8 V while BIAS supplies the 2.4 to 5.5 V the control circuit needs. The two supplies are separate: PSRR from IN is 70 dB and from BIAS 80 dB at 1 kHz.

**Dropout.** From IN to OUT it is 37 mV typical at 150 mA and 140 mV typical at 500 mA (75 and 250 mV maximum). From BIAS to OUT it is 1.1 V typical at 500 mA with IN tied to BIAS, so BIAS must be at least VOUT + 1.5 V in the worst case. Below 0.9 V out the bias dropout doesn't apply, because 2.4 V is the minimum anyway.

**Enable and discharge.** EN is high at 0.9 V and low at 0.4 V, with a 0.3 µA pull-down. If the function is unused, tie EN to IN or BIAS. When disabled, the input and bias currents drop to 0.5 µA typical each. The NCP133A discharges the output through 150 Ω; the NCP133B has no discharge.

**Starting and protection.** Turn-on to 98% takes 150 µs typical. The current limit is 550 to 1000 mA, and thermal shutdown trips at 160 °C with 20 °C of hysteresis. A bias undervoltage lock-out acts at 1.6 V rising with 0.2 V of hysteresis.

**Capacitors.** The output wants 2.2 to 10 µF of effective capacitance, so a nominal value must allow for DC-bias derating. The datasheet recommends 1 µF at IN and 0.1 µF at BIAS.

### Worked example: 1.0 V at 500 mA from 1.5 V with a 3.3 V bias

1. **Output.** With 19.6 kΩ and 78.7 kΩ, VOUT = 0.8 × (1 + 19.6 ÷ 78.7) = 0.999 V (calculated). The divider carries about 10 µA, and R1 + R2 = 98.3 kΩ is under the datasheet's 100 kΩ limit.
2. **Bias.** VOUT + 1.6 V is 2.6 V and the minimum is 2.4 V; a 3.3 V bias leaves 0.7 V of margin.
3. **Input.** 1.5 V less 1.0 V is 0.5 V against a 250 mV maximum dropout at 500 mA.
4. **Heat.** (1.5 − 1.0) V × 0.5 A is 0.25 W, plus a negligible 0.2 mW from the bias. With RθJA of 110 °C/W that is a 27.5 °C rise (calculated, datasheet board).

## Layout

The datasheet has no layout section. The XDFN6 is 1.2 × 1.2 mm with an exposed pad that goes to ground; put the 1 µF and 0.1 µF capacitors next to IN and BIAS and the output capacitor next to OUT.

## Things to watch

- **Two supplies.** BIAS must be present, at VOUT + 1.6 V or more, for the output to be in spec.
- **The output capacitor is effective, not nominal.** 2.2 to 10 µF after derating.
- **A and B differ.** Only the A version discharges the output.
- **Orderables are mixed.** Several options are listed as discontinued; check the table before choosing a voltage.
- **Fixed range.** The text says up to 2.1 V fixed, while the ordering table lists fixed parts up to 1.8 V.
- **Status is not established.** The page's status is unknown because of the discontinued entries.

## Datasheet notes

- **Bias range.** The electrical table's minimum for the bias voltage reads VOUT + 1.40 V (and at least 2.4 V), while the conditions say VOUT + 1.6 V.
- **Text-layer glitches.** The text layer shows µA as "mA", µF as "mF", Ω as "W" and µs as "ms"; the rendered table has the right units.
- **What was compared.** Page 4 (the electrical table) and Table 1 (page 8) were compared with rendered pages; the rest came from the text layer, so the page stays unverified.
