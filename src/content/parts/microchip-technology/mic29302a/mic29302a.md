## How it works

The MIC29302A is an adjustable linear regulator with a PNP pass transistor, a 1.24 V class reference and an error amplifier that compares the ADJ pin with it. Because the pass element is a PNP, the base current grows with the load, and the ground current is the price of the low dropout: 5 mA at 750 mA and 60 mA at 3 A, typical. The benefit is a dropout of 450 mV typical at 3 A.

**Setting the output.** Two resistors set it: VOUT = 1.242 × (R1 ÷ R2 + 1), with R1 from OUT to ADJ and R2 from ADJ to ground (the front page uses 1.242; see Datasheet notes). The ADJ bias current is small, 40 nA typical, so the divider can be fairly high in value, but see the minimum load below.

**Minimum load.** The part needs 10 mA of load for regulation. The divider current counts toward it, so a divider of about 100 Ω per volt of output satisfies it by itself, and a lighter divider needs a real load as well. The datasheet's own 2.5 V example carries 6.6 mA in its divider (calculated), which is under the 10 mA.

**Enable.** EN is active high, with 0.8 V maximum for off and 2.4 V minimum for on. About 20 µA flows in at 4.2 V. EN can be tied to IN when shutdown isn't needed, as in the datasheet's example.

**Capacitors.** The datasheet calls for 10 µF on the output for stability at full load, and says an extremely low-ESR capacitor can be unstable, so it recommends tantalum. Noise falls from 400 µVRMS to 260 µVRMS with 33 µF.

### Worked example: 2.5 V out at up to 3 A from 3.3 V

1. **Output.** The example divider calculates to 2.51 V, which is within the ±2% of the 2.5 V target.
2. **Headroom.** 3.3 V in less 2.51 V is 0.79 V. The dropout maximum at 3 A is 0.80 V, so at full load and the worst case the part is right at the edge and the output can sag a little.
3. **Divider current.** 2.51 V across 378 Ω is 6.6 mA, below the 10 mA minimum load, so the load must supply at least about 3.4 mA more (calculated).
4. **Dissipation.** See the design table: 2.57 W at 3 A with the typical ground current, giving a rise of 72 °C on the TO-263 and 90 °C on the TO-252. At 85 °C ambient the TO-252 would be past the junction limit.

## Layout

The datasheet's layout notes are short. The thermal pad is the ground tab, so it is the main heat path, and the datasheet's θJA figures assume a board of its own. Keep the input and output capacitors close to the pins, and tie the divider's ground to the same node as the output capacitor.

## Things to watch

- **It is the MIC29302A.** The datasheet is for the A part, and the orderables are MIC29302AWU and MIC29302AWD.
- **Ground current is large at full load.** 60 mA typical (150 mA maximum) at 3 A is real dissipation at a high input and a drain on a small supply.
- **The divider must carry the minimum load.** The datasheet's example does not, by our arithmetic.
- **Headroom at 3 A is thin at 3.3 V in.** Dropout is 800 mV maximum.
- **The current limit is close to the rating.** 3 A minimum, 4 A typical, so a part at the low end limits at the rated load.
- **Very low ESR can oscillate.** Use a tantalum output capacitor as the datasheet recommends.
- **Status is not established.** The page is built from a 2014 Micrel-format datasheet and nothing here confirms the part's current status.
- **The KiCad symbol is ours and not yet opened in KiCad.** It was generated by script and checked against the pin table, but nobody has loaded it in KiCad. The pin types and the symbol layout are our choices. One symbol serves both packages, which number the pins the same way.

## Datasheet notes

- **Reference formula.** The front page uses 1.242 in the formula; the text uses 1.240.
- **Output range.** The front page says 1.24 V to 15 V, and the text says 1.25 V to 15 V.
- **Error flag.** The feature list mentions an error flag, but the pin table has no such pin.
- **Thermal-regulation note.** Note 9 of the electrical table has no row that refers to it.
- **Thermal shutdown.** The text puts it at the "+125 °C maximum safe operating temperature", which reads as a limit rather than a trip point.
- **Bold values.** In the electrical table, bold values apply from −40 to +125 °C.
- **What was compared.** The electrical tables and the front-page circuit were compared with rendered pages; other text came from the text layer, so the page stays unverified.
