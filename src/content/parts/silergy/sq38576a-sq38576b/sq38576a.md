## How it works

The SQ38576A is an off-line flyback switcher with its 1000 V power switch inside the DIP7 package. The output voltage is regulated on the secondary side. A shunt regulator on the output drives the LED of an optocoupler, and the optocoupler's phototransistor pulls the COMP pin down. The controller turns that COMP voltage into a peak-current command, and the ISEN pin ends each on-time when the voltage across the current-sense resistor reaches the command. The command is limited to 0.49 V typical, which sets the maximum peak primary current.

Silergy calls the control pseudo fixed frequency. At steady state the frequency is a fixed 60 kHz, like a conventional fixed-frequency controller, but the method avoids the sub-harmonic oscillation that appears in continuous conduction above 50% duty, so no slope compensation is needed. The frequency is also modulated by ±7% over a 4 ms period to spread the EMI.

The COMP voltage sets the operating mode (the datasheet's Figure 3):

- **Fold-back.** From about 1.2 V on COMP down to 0.9 V, the frequency falls from 60 kHz to a floor of 25 kHz, which the datasheet chose to keep switching out of the audible range. This lifts medium and light-load efficiency.
- **Burst mode.** If COMP falls below 385 mV the device enters sleep mode: switching stops and most of the internal circuits shut down. The output droops, COMP rises, and when it passes 435 mV the device wakes and switches again. The output ripple is a little larger in burst mode.
- **Peak power mode.** Above 1.6 V on COMP the frequency rises, reaching 178 kHz at 2 V. The datasheet's reason is a transformer disturbed by a strong magnetic field: the field lowers the inductance, so the part switches faster to keep the output voltage up. The datasheet doesn't say how much extra power this mode delivers.

The over-load timer starts when COMP goes above 2 V, which is where peak power mode tops out.

Start-up is from the DRAIN pin, which feeds a current source (1.2 mA typical) into VCC. Once VCC reaches 14.6 V, the source turns off and the part starts switching with an 8 ms internal soft start in which the COMP voltage, the peak current and the frequency all ramp. The auxiliary winding then supplies VCC. The recommended VCC range is 10 to 26 V, and the part stops switching if VCC falls below 8.8 V.

Four protections all end in an automatic restart:

- **Over-load.** When the output can't hold its level, COMP is pulled up. If it stays above 2 V for 150 ms, the part stops and then restarts with a soft start.
- **Output short circuit.** The auxiliary winding collapses with the output, so VCC falls. Below 8.8 V the part stops switching, and VCC then decays slowly towards 7.5 V through a 115 µA discharge current, which slows the restart rate and keeps the short-circuit power low.
- **VCC over-voltage.** If VCC rises above 28 V, for example after an optocoupler failure, switching stops and a timer starts. Before the trip, a 4.8 mA sink tries to clamp VCC.
- **ISEN pin short.** In the first switching cycle at start-up, after 3 µs of blanking the part checks that ISEN is above 50 mV. If it is lower, the pin is shorted to ground and the part stops.

Over-temperature protection stops switching at 150 °C and resumes after the die cools by 20 °C.

The CONT pin sets the brown-in voltage. After VCC reaches its turn-on level, a divider from the bus to CONT is compared with 0.55 V, and the supply starts when the pin reaches that level. The datasheet recommends a 1 to 22 nF ceramic capacitor from CONT to ground to reduce switching noise.

### Bus capacitor and minimum bus voltage

The datasheet sizes the bus capacitor for a chosen ripple on the bus at the lowest line voltage and full load: CBUS = PO ÷ (η × π × fAC × ΔVBUS) × [arcsin(1 − ΔVBUS ÷ (√2 × VIN,MIN)) + π/2] ÷ (2√2 × VIN,MIN − ΔVBUS). For 10.8 W, 82% efficiency, 50 Hz, 55 V of ripple and 90 Vac, that is 16.6 µF, and two 33 µF capacitors in series give 16.5 µF. The lowest bus voltage is √2 × 90 V − 55 V = 72.3 V. The datasheet also gives a quicker rule of 1 to 2 µF per watt of input power, which for the 13.2 W of input power here is 13 to 26 µF.

### Turns ratio and duty cycle

The turns ratio is limited by the stress on the power MOSFET: NPS ≤ (VMOS,BR × KDR − √2 × VIN,MAX − ΔVSN) ÷ VO,MAX. With 1000 V derated to 85%, a 380 Vac peak and a 150 V spike, the limit is 13.55, and the datasheet picks 10. The maximum duty cycle at the minimum bus voltage is DMAX = NPS × VO ÷ (VBUS,MIN + NPS × VO) = 62.4%.

### Primary inductance, turns and current

The datasheet chooses a ripple factor KRP of 0.3 to 0.9 at the lowest line and full load, and the example uses 0.65, which is continuous conduction. The inductance is LM = VBUS,MIN² × DMAX² × η ÷ (2 × PO × fSW × KRP) = 1.98 mH, rounded to 2 mH. The primary peak current at rated output is 0.48 A. The primary turns follow from the peak flux density: NP = LM × IPK ÷ (BMAX × AE), with the datasheet's range of 0.2 to 0.3 T and the example's 0.29 T on the EE16/6.5 core (AE = 25 mm²), which gives 132.4 turns; the example uses 130. The secondary has NP ÷ NPS = 13 turns, and the auxiliary winding NA = VCC,AUX × NS ÷ VO = 13 turns for a 12 V VCC. The datasheet says to fine-tune the auxiliary turns against the VCC measured at minimum output and no load, which is the worst case.

### Current sense resistor

At the OCP point, the datasheet takes the lowest input, the highest output current (130% of rated) and the peak of the line. The duty cycle there is 48.5%, and the peak primary current IPK,MAX works out to 0.535 A. The sense resistor is RISEN = VISEN,MAX ÷ IPK,MAX = 0.49 V ÷ 0.535 A = 0.916 Ω. The circuit uses two 1.8 Ω resistors in parallel, 0.9 Ω. The datasheet says to adjust the value on the real converter: larger if the OCP point comes out higher than the target, smaller if lower.

### Secondary rectifier

The diode's reverse voltage is √2 × VIN,MAX ÷ NPS + VO + VSPIKE = 75.7 V with a 10 V spike, and the datasheet recommends a 100 V part. Peak forward current is NPS × IPK,MAX = 5.35 A. The BOM names the diode P5L200 and gives no ratings.

### Brown-in divider

The brown-in voltage is the bus voltage at which the CONT pin reaches 0.55 V. With 3 MΩ on top and a target of 80 Vac, the bottom resistor is RUP ÷ (√2 × VIN,BI ÷ VBI,TH − 1) = 14.65 kΩ. The circuit's 15 kΩ gives 78.2 Vac (see the table above).

### Output voltage

The datasheet doesn't give the output divider's arithmetic. With the TL431's usual 2.5 V reference (not from this datasheet), RS4 + RS5 = 38.2 kΩ on top and RS6 = 10 kΩ below gives VO = 2.5 V × (1 + 38.2 ÷ 10) = 12.05 V.

## Layout

The datasheet gives these layout rules. The layout figures show the placement; check them in the datasheet.

- Connect the **bridge rectifier output to the bus capacitor first**, then to the switching circuit. This improves EMI and reduces line-frequency ripple.
- Keep the **switching loops small**: the primary power loop, the secondary loop and the auxiliary loop.
- Connect the **bias supply trace to the bias capacitor first**, then to the GND pin, and put the capacitor as close to the IC as possible.
- Keep the loop through the **switch, the current sense resistor and the GND pin** as small as possible.
- Follow the datasheet's **primary ground scheme**, which names eight ground points: the bus capacitor (①), the current sense resistor (②), the auxiliary winding (③), the primary Y capacitor (④), the SQ38576A's GND pin (⑤), the VCC bypass capacitor C3 (⑥), the optocoupler receiver (⑦) and the CONT pin resistor (⑧).

At these voltages the creepage and clearance distances between primary and secondary matter too, but the datasheet doesn't discuss them.

The typical-performance page shows the ISEN and drain waveforms at 90 V and 264 V in with a 12 V, 0.9 A load (10 µs per division) and with a 12 V, 50 mA load (400 µs per division). ## Things to watch

- **The current limit leaves almost no margin at the bottom of its tolerance.** With the 0.9 Ω circuit and an ISEN limit at its 0.44 V minimum, the limit is 0.489 A, only 2% above the 0.48 A rated peak at the lowest bus voltage. The typical limit gives 0.544 A, which meets the datasheet's own 0.535 A target, and the maximum gives 0.60 A. Sense-resistor tolerance and turn-off delay move this too.
- **The core flux goes above the design preset at the current limit.** The design example presets 0.29 T at rated output, but with NP = 130 the flux is about 0.33 T at the typical limit and about 0.37 T at the maximum. The datasheet doesn't name a core material, so check the saturation flux at hot temperature.
- **The design's maximum duty may reach the maximum on-time limit.** At the bus valley, 62.4% duty at 60 kHz needs 10.4 µs, and 11.1 µs at the bottom of the frequency modulation. The maximum on-time has a typical value of 14 µs but a guaranteed minimum of only 9.5 µs, so a part at that end may clip the on-time at full load and low line.
- **The heat budget is tight.** The conduction loss alone, at the highest duty and current of the line cycle, is 0.48 to 0.64 W. That is above the 0.36 W the die can dissipate at an 85 °C ambient (110 °C/W, 125 °C junction), and the recommended ambient maximum is 105 °C, where the budget is 0.18 W. The loss averaged over a line cycle is lower than the valley figure, so this is a worst case, but it shows the thermal margin needs checking on the real board.
- **The input range exceeds two of the BOM's ratings.** The design example takes the input up to 380 Vac, but the BOM's fuse is rated 250 V and the X capacitor 275 V. Check the safety ratings of the input parts against the real line.
- **The brown-in point has a wide spread.** The datasheet gives the 0.55 V threshold only as a typical value, and a 3 MΩ string of 1 MΩ resistors adds its own tolerance. With the assumed tolerances, the circuit's divider gives anywhere from about 73 to 83 Vac. At the 380 Vac peak the undamped divider would put about 2.7 V on CONT, above the 2.3 V typical clamp, and the datasheet doesn't say how much current the clamp can take.
- **The clamp resistor is a standing loss.** R9 (100 kΩ) draws at least 0.14 W at any load, because the clamp capacitor can't discharge below the reflected 120 V while the clamp is working, and up to 0.73 W at full load if the capacitor sits at 270 V. The [SQ38576B](site:parts/silergy/sq38576b/)'s 220 kΩ clamp draws less than half of that. This is calculated here; the real voltage depends on the leakage inductance, which the datasheet doesn't give.
- **VCC from the auxiliary winding has little headroom.** NA = NS gives 12 V before the diode and R10 drops, against a 10 V recommended minimum and a 12 V target in the datasheet's own text. The datasheet says to tune NA against the VCC measured at minimum output and no load.
- **Start-up takes a while.** Charging 22 µF through 1.2 mA takes about 0.27 s, and 0.49 s at the slowest limits. After an over-load or a short, each restart cycle combines the VCC decay and the recharge in the timing table, about 0.25 s and 0.13 s typical, plus the 150 ms over-load debounce for an over-load.
- **The efficiency plot doesn't state its conditions.** It is labelled 115 VDC and 230 VDC, while the rest of the datasheet uses Vac, and it doesn't give the output voltage.

- **The designators in the other figures don't match Figure 15.** Figure 1 and the layout figures label the current-sense resistor R2, the CONT resistor and capacitor R5 and C5, and the auxiliary diode D2, while Figure 15 and its BOM use R5 and R6 for the current-sense resistors and R11 and C5 for the CONT pair. - **The BOM's description of C5 disagrees with its part number.** The BOM says "1nF/25V" for C5, but its part number 0805B103K500NT carries the capacitance code 103 (10 nF), as does Figure 15 and the 1 to 22 nF the text recommends. This page uses 10 nF.
- **The electrical table's header says −40 to 125 °C, while Note 4 says limits are production tested at 25 °C.** The limits over temperature are by design or statistical correlation.
- **The brown-in formula leaves out the 1 µA pull-up current.** The electrical table lists a brown-in pull-up current, and the text doesn't say when it flows. Through a 15 kΩ resistor, 1 µA is 15 mV, about 3% of the 0.55 V threshold, if it flows into the divider's bottom.
- **The datasheet gives no output power rating.** It lists the 11 W design example and the applications, but no maximum output power for the part. Silergy's product page for this part leaves the field blank.
- **The datasheet is Rev 1.0, initial release on January 12, 2026.** Silergy's product page lists the part with an evaluation board, EVB_SQ38576AFIB. The part's production status wasn't checked.
- **About the SQ38576B.** The [SQ38576B](site:parts/silergy/sq38576b/) has its own datasheet (Rev 1.0, December 29, 2025).
