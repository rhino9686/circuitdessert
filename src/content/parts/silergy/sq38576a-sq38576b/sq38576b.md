## How it works

The SQ38576B is an off-line flyback switcher with its 1000 V power switch inside the DIP7 package. It is the same part as the [SQ38576A](site:parts/silergy/sq38576a/) in its control scheme, with a few features removed. This page describes the SQ38576B on its own and says where it differs.

The output voltage is regulated on the secondary side. A shunt regulator on the output drives the LED of an optocoupler, and the optocoupler's phototransistor pulls the COMP pin down. The controller turns that COMP voltage into a peak-current command, and the ISEN pin ends each on-time when the voltage across the current-sense resistor reaches the command. The command is limited to 0.49 V typical, which sets the maximum peak primary current.

Silergy calls the control pseudo fixed frequency. At steady state the frequency is a fixed 60 kHz, like a conventional fixed-frequency controller, but the method avoids the sub-harmonic oscillation that appears in continuous conduction above 50% duty, so no slope compensation is needed. The frequency is also modulated by ±7% over a 4 ms period to spread the EMI.

The COMP voltage sets the operating mode (the datasheet's Figure 3):

- **Fold-back.** From about 1.2 V on COMP down to 0.9 V, the frequency falls from 60 kHz to a floor of 25 kHz, which the datasheet chose to keep switching out of the audible range. This lifts medium and light-load efficiency.
- **Burst mode.** If COMP falls below 410 mV the device enters sleep mode: switching stops and most of the internal circuits shut down. The output droops, COMP rises, and when it passes 460 mV the device wakes and switches again. The output ripple is a little larger in burst mode.

Unlike the SQ38576A, there is no peak power mode. At any COMP voltage above the fold-back range the frequency stays at 60 kHz, so the datasheet's remark about transformers disturbed by a strong magnetic field doesn't apply here: nothing raises the frequency to make up for a lost inductance.

Start-up is from the DRAIN pin, which feeds a current source (1.2 mA typical) into VCC. Once VCC reaches 14.6 V, the source turns off and the part starts switching with an 8 ms internal soft start in which the COMP voltage, the peak current and the frequency all ramp. The auxiliary winding then supplies VCC. The recommended VCC range is 10 to 26 V, and the part stops switching if VCC falls below 8.8 V.

Four protections all end in an automatic restart:

- **Over-load.** When the output can't hold its level, COMP is pulled up. If it stays above 2 V for 70 ms, the part stops and then restarts with a soft start. The SQ38576A waits 150 ms.
- **Output short circuit.** The auxiliary winding collapses with the output, so VCC falls. Below 8.8 V the part stops switching, and VCC then decays slowly towards 7.5 V through a 115 µA discharge current, which slows the restart rate and keeps the short-circuit power low.
- **VCC over-voltage.** If VCC rises above 28 V, for example after an optocoupler failure, switching stops and a timer starts. Before the trip, a 5.7 mA sink tries to clamp VCC.
- **ISEN pin short.** In the first switching cycle at start-up, after 3 µs of blanking the part checks that ISEN is above 50 mV. If it is lower, the pin is shorted to ground and the part stops.

Over-temperature protection stops switching at 150 °C and resumes after the die cools by 20 °C.

Pin 5 is not connected, and the block diagram has no brown-in comparator. The SQ38576B starts as soon as VCC reaches its turn-on level, whatever the input voltage, so it has no input under-voltage setting.

### The design example

The datasheet's steps are the same as for the SQ38576A, with the same inputs (90 to 380 Vac, 12 V at 0.9 A, 82% efficiency, 55 V of bus ripple, a 1000 V MOSFET derated to 85% and a 150 V turn-off spike), so the results are the same:

- **Bus capacitor.** 16.6 µF calculated, and two 33 µF capacitors in series give 16.5 µF. The lowest bus voltage is √2 × 90 V − 55 V = 72.3 V.
- **Turns ratio.** The limit from the MOSFET stress is 13.55 and the datasheet picks NPS = 10. The maximum duty cycle at the minimum bus voltage is 62.4%.
- **Inductance and turns.** KRP = 0.65 gives LM = 1.98 mH, rounded to 2 mH, with a 0.48 A peak at rated output. On the EE16/6.5 core at 0.29 T, NP = 130, NS = 13 and NA = 13.
- **Current sense resistor.** At the OCP point the peak is 0.535 A, so RISEN = 0.49 V ÷ 0.535 A = 0.916 Ω. The circuit uses 0.9 Ω. The datasheet says to adjust it against the measured OCP point.
- **Secondary diode.** The reverse voltage is 75.7 V and the peak forward current is 5.35 A. The datasheet recommends a 100 V part, and the BOM's P5L200 has no ratings in it.

The datasheet doesn't give the output divider's arithmetic. With the TL431's usual 2.5 V reference (not from this datasheet), RS4 + RS5 = 38.2 kΩ on top and RS6 = 10 kΩ below gives VO = 2.5 V × (1 + 38.2 ÷ 10) = 12.05 V.

The loss over a whole line cycle is lower than at the valley, so these are the worst case, and the datasheet's thermal resistance is for a minimal board.

## Layout

The datasheet gives these layout rules. The layout figure shows the placement; check it in the datasheet.

- Connect the **bridge rectifier output to the bus capacitor first**, then to the switching circuit. This improves EMI and reduces line-frequency ripple.
- Keep the **switching loops small**: the primary power loop, the secondary loop and the auxiliary loop.
- Connect the **bias supply trace to the bias capacitor first**, then to the GND pin, and put the capacitor as close to the IC as possible.
- Keep the loop through the **switch, the current sense resistor and the GND pin** as small as possible.
- Follow the datasheet's **primary ground scheme**, which names seven ground points: the bus capacitor (①), the current sense resistor (②), the auxiliary winding (③), the primary Y capacitor (④), the SQ38576B's GND pin (⑤), the VCC bypass capacitor C3 (⑥) and the optocoupler receiver (⑦). The SQ38576A has an eighth for its CONT resistor.

At these voltages the creepage and clearance distances between primary and secondary matter too, but the datasheet doesn't discuss them.

## What the datasheet shows

The typical-performance page shows the ISEN and drain waveforms at 90 V and 264 V in with a 12 V, 0.9 A load and with a light load, and an efficiency plot against output current at 115 VDC and 230 VDC.

Reading the plot by eye, efficiency at 0.9 A is about 84.5% at 115 VDC and about 85.5% at 230 VDC. At low load the 230 VDC curve is lower: about 67% at 50 mA against about 71.5% at 115 VDC. The two curves cross at about 0.5 A. The numbers are in the *read by eye* table above. The curves look the same as the SQ38576A's, which isn't surprising for the same design example but means the plot says nothing about the differences between the two parts.

## Things to watch

- **There is no input under-voltage setting.** With pin 5 not connected, the part starts when VCC reaches 14.6 V, whatever the line voltage. The datasheet doesn't give a minimum input voltage for starting, so a supply that needs a defined brown-in point should use the SQ38576A, which has one.
- **The current limit leaves almost no margin at the bottom of its tolerance.** With the 0.9 Ω circuit and an ISEN limit at its 0.44 V minimum, the limit is 0.489 A, only 2% above the 0.48 A rated peak at the lowest bus voltage. The typical limit gives 0.544 A, which meets the datasheet's own 0.535 A target, and the maximum gives 0.60 A. Sense-resistor tolerance and turn-off delay move this too.
- **The core flux goes above the design preset at the current limit.** The design example presets 0.29 T at rated output, but with NP = 130 the flux is about 0.33 T at the typical limit and about 0.37 T at the maximum. The datasheet doesn't name a core material, so check the saturation flux at hot temperature.
- **The design's maximum duty may reach the maximum on-time limit.** At the bus valley, 62.4% duty at 60 kHz needs 10.4 µs, and 11.1 µs at the bottom of the frequency modulation. The maximum on-time has a typical value of 14 µs but a guaranteed minimum of only 9.5 µs, so a part at that end may clip the on-time at full load and low line.
- **The heat budget is tight.** The conduction loss alone, at the highest duty and current of the line cycle, is 0.48 to 0.64 W. That is above the 0.36 W the die can dissipate at an 85 °C ambient (110 °C/W, 125 °C junction), and the recommended ambient maximum is 105 °C, where the budget is 0.18 W. The loss averaged over a line cycle is lower than the valley figure, so this is a worst case, but it shows the thermal margin needs checking on the real board.
- **The over-load timer is short.** 70 ms (50 to 90 ms) is less than half the SQ38576A's 150 ms. A load that asks for more than the converter can supply for longer than that, such as a large capacitor charging at the output, makes the supply stop and restart. The datasheet doesn't say whether the timer runs during the 8 ms soft start.
- **The clamp resistor is a standing loss.** R9 draws at least 0.065 W (220 kΩ at 120 V) at any load, and the SQ38576A's 100 kΩ draws 0.144 W (see the clamp table). That is about 3% of the 2 W total loss at full load but a visible share of a no-load budget, so it needs tuning against the real leakage inductance, which the datasheet doesn't give.
- **The input range exceeds two of the BOM's ratings.** The design example takes the input up to 380 Vac, but the BOM's fuse is rated 250 V and the X capacitor 275 V. Check the safety ratings of the input parts against the real line.
- **VCC from the auxiliary winding has little headroom.** NA = NS gives 12 V before the diode drop, against a 10 V recommended minimum and a 12 V target in the datasheet's own text, and this circuit has no series resistor. The datasheet says to tune NA against the VCC measured at no load.
- **Start-up takes a while.** Charging 22 µF through 1.2 mA takes about 0.27 s, and 0.49 s at the slowest limits. After an over-load or a short, each restart cycle combines the VCC decay and the recharge in the timing table, about 0.25 s and 0.13 s typical, plus the 70 ms over-load debounce for an over-load.
- **The efficiency plot doesn't state its conditions.** It is labelled 115 VDC and 230 VDC, while the rest of the datasheet uses Vac, and it doesn't give the output voltage.
