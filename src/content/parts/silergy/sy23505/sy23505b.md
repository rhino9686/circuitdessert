## How it works

**Two converters, one supply and one fault system.** The PFC boost stage keeps the input current in phase with the line and holds the bulk capacitor at a fixed voltage. The LLC half bridge runs from that bus and regulates the isolated output. Both stages share VCC, ground reference and fault handling: the faults in the table below stop one stage or both, and many restart after a timer or latch.

**PFC.** The control loop compares the divided bus voltage on FBB with 1.2 V and produces a compensation voltage, VCOMPV. A current-shaping block turns it into a sinusoidal current reference that follows the line voltage seen on LNS, with the line peak fed forward so the reference holds as the line changes. The MOSFET turns off when the voltage across the sense resistor reaches that reference, and turns on again at the next valley of the drain voltage, found through the HV pin, 300 ns after the inductor current reaches zero. At heavy load that is critical conduction: inductor current flows for the whole period, so DSW, the share of the period with current in the inductor, is 100%. Below about 25% of power the stage moves to discontinuous conduction, and below 5% it bursts. The datasheet says that with RCS at the typical value the duty cycle falls from 100% to 10% as output power falls from 25% to 5%.

**LLC.** The part uses current-mode control. ISEN sees the resonant current as a voltage; FBL carries the output error as a current from the optocoupler, which sets an internal compensation voltage, VCCOMP. The high-side MOSFET turns on when the sensed signal falls below VCM − VCCOMP, and the low-side one when it rises above VCM + VCCOMP, so the switching frequency is a result and not a setting. Dead time adapts: after one MOSFET turns off, the other waits for the HB node to finish its slope (at least 185 ns, at most 1 µs, typical) so every cycle is a zero-voltage turn-on. The same logic stops the part from running in capacitive mode. At light load the optocoupler current passes a threshold and the stage goes to burst mode; RVSEND sets the entry level. Three pins carry set-up resistors: GATEB (DC input or not), ISEN (the LLC frequency range) and VSEN (the burst entry level).

**Start-up.** The HV pin takes current from the PFC drain node: 0.5 mA while VCC is under about 0.8 V (a guard against a shorted VCC pin), then 5.6 mA up to the 24 V turn-on level. Once VCC is up, the stages start in turn. The LLC begins to switch when FBB is above 1.08 V, which is 90% of the bus set point. The auxiliary winding takes over VCC as the output rises. If it doesn't, HV recharges VCC whenever it falls to 9.5 V, for at most 63 ms. When the line goes away, the HV pin discharges the input capacitors: if the LNS pin sees no rising edge for 63 ms (typical), the X-capacitor discharge starts and both stages stop. DC input turns the function off.

A 240 W class off-line power supply front end built from the circuit in Silergy's datasheet: one controller for a boost power factor corrector that holds a fixed bus voltage (390 V in the datasheet's example), and for an LLC resonant half bridge behind it, with the high-side gate driver on the chip. ### PFC output voltage (R6, R7, CFBB)

The bus voltage is VPFC = (RFBBU + RFBBD) ÷ RFBBD × VFBB_REF, with VFBB_REF = 1.2 V. The datasheet suggests RFBBU of 6–12 MΩ and a 200 µs filter on FBB (10–22 nF). FBB also does two other jobs: the LLC watches it for brown-out and brown-in, and a level above 2.3 V disables both stages.

The LLC starts only once the bus has reached 90% of its set value, so the PFC divider decides when the output stage wakes up. Split RFBBU across several resistors so no one of them sees the full bus voltage, and keep the divider and CFBB close to the pin.

### Line sense, brown-in and brown-out (R1, R2, C1)

The LNS pin reads the line through R1 and R2. The datasheet gives the brown-out line voltage as VAC_MIN = VLNS_BO ÷ √2 × (RLNSU + RLNSD) ÷ RLNSD, with VLNS_BO = 395 mV (374–425 mV) and brown-in at 475 mV (450–500 mV). It recommends 6–12 MΩ for RLNSU and a 1 nF capacitor from LNS to ground. Its example is a 70 VAC brown-out with 6 MΩ, which it works out as 24.3 kΩ.

| Choice | Brown-out (typ, min–max) | Brown-in (typ, min–max) | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| RLNSD = 24.3 kΩ (datasheet example) | 69.2 VAC | 83.3 VAC, up to 87.7 | Brown-in can be as high as 87.7 VAC, only 2.3 V under a 90 VAC line |
| RLNSD = 24.9 kΩ (ours) | 67.6 VAC (64.0–72.7) | 81.3 VAC (77.0–85.5) | Highest brown-in is more than 5% under 90 VAC |

We chose RLNSD so that the highest brown-in threshold is at least 5% under the lowest rated line, which gives 24.85 kΩ and 24.9 kΩ as the nearest E96 value. The peak voltage on LNS then runs from 0.53 V at 90 VAC to 1.54 V at 264 VAC, inside the 3.6 V absolute maximum. The datasheet's own example used 24.3 kΩ for a 70 VAC brown-out, and that number corresponds to a 0.4 V threshold, not the 395 mV in its table (which gives 24.0 kΩ).

The datasheet asks for a 100 µs filter on LNS and also for 1 nF. With these resistors 1 nF gives 25 µs, and 100 µs takes about 4.0 nF. 4.7 nF gives 117 µs. Start at 1 nF as the datasheet shows, and raise it if the LNS pin picks up noise.

### PFC current sense and protection mode (R3, R4, CCS)

The datasheet sets the sense resistor for critical conduction at the lowest line and full load: RCS ≈ VCS_LIMIT × VAC_MIN ÷ (2√2 × PIN), with VCS_LIMIT = 0.7 V typical (0.65–0.74 V). A second rule keeps the PFC overpower trip out of normal operation: RCS < VCOMPV_OPP × kPFCOPP ÷ (POUT_MAX ÷ ηLLC × RLNSD ÷ (RLNSU + RLNSD)), with VCOMPV_OPP = 1.33 V and kPFCOPP = 0.073.

R3 does two jobs. The datasheet says a value of 200–240 Ω makes the overpower, open-loop, output overvoltage, undervoltage and ISEN-limit protections stop switching and restart after the timer, and 510–800 Ω makes them latch after 16 restarts. We show 220 Ω (restart). Add 100–470 pF at the CS pin; 220 pF with 220 Ω is a 48 ns time constant (ours). The datasheet says that with no NTC in the AC input loop, the start-up surge typically exceeds 100 A and can put a large voltage across RCS; R3 is what protects the CS pin from it.

### PFC inductor (L1)

The datasheet gives no inductor equation and Figure 1 shows no value. This is our arithmetic for critical conduction at unity power factor, using PIN = 131.6 W and a bus of 386 V. The on-time is 4 × L × PIN ÷ VPK² and stays constant through the line cycle. The switching frequency is VPK² × (VBUS − VPK × sin θ) ÷ (4 × L × PIN × VBUS), lowest at the line peak and highest near the zero crossing.

| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 90 VAC | 127 | 8.8 | 76 | 4.3 | 114 |

The limits that set the value: the on-time must stay under the datasheet's minimum maximum on-time of 20 µs (tON,MAX_B, 20–45 µs), which allows up to 616 µH at 90 VAC; and the off-time at the 264 VAC line peak, where the bus is only 13 V above the line, should stay under tOFF,MAX_B (30 µs typical, 20–40 µs), which allows about 274 µH for 30 µs and 183 µH for 20 µs. We picked 270 µH, the E12 value under the first. The frequency near the zero crossing isn't reached as listed: the part limits the period to 2.9 µs (about 345 kHz) and moves to discontinuous operation. With RCS = 0.15 Ω the stage trips on inductor short or saturation at about 5.7 A (0.85 V; up to 6.0 A at 0.9 V), so choose a saturation current of at least 6 A.

### Bulk capacitor (C3)

The datasheet says a fixed boost voltage allows a smaller bulk capacitor or a longer hold-up, and gives no equation. Our arithmetic: C = 2 × P × t ÷ (VBUS² − VMIN²) for the 126 W the LLC draws, 10 ms of hold-up and a bus allowed to fall to 300 V (both are our assumptions; the minimum depends on your LLC's gain range). That gives 42.7 µF, so 47 µF at 450 V. The LLC itself shuts off on FBB below 238 V (0.74 V).

### GATEB resistor and HV resistor (R5, R12)

R5 from the PFC gate to ground selects the input type: 27–33 kΩ for AC input only, 9–11 kΩ if DC input must also work. We show 30 kΩ. (When the input is DC, the X-capacitor discharge doesn't act.) R12 sits between the HV pin and the Q3 drain; the datasheet asks for 1–5 kΩ to protect the pin from input surges, and valley detection reads the drain through it. We show 2.2 kΩ; the start-up current of 5.6 mA drops about 12 V across it.

### LLC current sense and frequency range (R8, R11, C4, C5)

The LLC senses the resonant current as a voltage on ISEN. The datasheet's capacitive method (its Figure 20) taps the resonant capacitor CR through a small series capacitor, Cseries, into a sense resistor R′ISEN, which cuts the loss in the sense resistor. The equations, in the datasheet's names:

- ΔVCR = (PIN − CJ × VBUS² × fSW) ÷ (VBUS × CR × fSW), the voltage swing on CR while the high-side MOSFET is on.
- RISEN = 2 × VCCOMP_OPP × k × VBUS × fSW ÷ (PIN − CJ × VBUS² × fSW), with VCCOMP_OPP = 380 mV and k from the table below.
- R′ISEN = CR ÷ Cseries × RISEN, the resistor actually fitted when Cseries is used (R11 here).

| RISENSET + RISEN | Working frequency | Coefficient k |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 70–100 Ω | 300 kHz | 4.1×10⁻⁷ |
| 150–180 Ω | 200 kHz | 6.15×10⁻⁷ |
| 269–300 Ω | 150 kHz | 8.21×10⁻⁷ |
| 450–520 Ω | 100 kHz | 1.23×10⁻⁶ |

The datasheet's example is 100 kHz, VBUS = 400 V, 120 W into the LLC, CR = 33 nF, Cseries = 200 pF, CJ = 200 pF. Our arithmetic gives ΔVCR = 88.5 V, RISEN = 0.320 Ω and R′ISEN = 52.8 Ω. The datasheet prints 0.31 Ω and 51 Ω; with its k of 1.23×10⁻⁶ we can't reproduce 0.31 Ω (it would need k of about 1.19×10⁻⁶), so the two differ by 3%. This page's example takes 120 W out and 126.3 W into the LLC at 95% efficiency, which gives RISEN = 0.304 Ω and R′ISEN = 50.1 Ω. We fit 51 Ω (E24), which is 0.309 Ω of effective RISEN.

R8 is RISENSET. The sum RISENSET + RISEN has to sit inside the window for the frequency range you use; the datasheet's coefficients are specified at sums of 82, 160, 285 and 475 Ω. With 51 Ω as RISEN, R8 can run from 399 to 469 Ω for 100 kHz. We picked 422 Ω (E96), a sum of 473 Ω (468–478 Ω at 1% tolerance). The datasheet doesn't say whether RISEN in its table means the sense resistor or the original RISEN before the capacitive division; we took it as the resistor on the node (R11), which is how its Figure 24 is drawn. Check that reading against your own measurement of the working range.

With 0.309 Ω of effective RISEN, the ±760 mV ISEN limit (±700 to ±820 mV) corresponds to a resonant current of 2.46 A peak (2.26–2.65 A). That is our arithmetic and assumes the capacitive divider scales the current exactly; compare it against the peak current of your own tank at the overpower point.

RISEN is inversely proportional to power. For comparison, the same equations at two overpower points:

| Overpower point (output) | PFC RCS, critical conduction (Ω) | PFC RCS ceiling (Ω) | LLC RISEN (Ω) | R′ISEN with 33 nF / 200 pF (Ω) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 120 W | 0.169 | 0.186 | 0.304 | 50.1 |
| 240 W | 0.085 | 0.093 | 0.150 | 24.7 |

Both rows assume the same 90 VAC low line, 95% LLC and 96% PFC efficiency, 100 kHz, 400 V and the datasheet's capacitor values. At 240 W the tank would use a larger CR, so treat the last column as an illustration only. As R′ISEN gets smaller, R8 has to grow to keep the sum inside the window.

C5 sees the resonant capacitor's peak voltage, which in this example is about 247 V (half the bus plus half of ΔVCR, ours), so use a high-voltage C0G part. The datasheet's typical-application page shows a capacitor at the ISEN pin (CISEN) with no value.

### Output sense and burst entry (R9, R10, CVSEN)

VSEN reads the auxiliary winding through a divider, and it does three jobs: output overvoltage (VSEN over 1.47 V for eight cycles), undervoltage (under 0.39 V for 32 ms) and the burst entry level, which RVSEND selects. The datasheet's equation for an overvoltage trip at 120% of VO is RVSENU = RVSEND × (120% × NAUX × VO ÷ (VVSEN_OVP × Nsec) − 1). It asks for 100–200 pF at the pin, and it requires NAUX ÷ Nsec × VO < the VCC shunt voltage.

### VCC supply, bootstrap and gate drive (C6, D5, C7, D6)

VCC needs two capacitors: an electrolytic for the supply and a ceramic for noise (the datasheet's typical values are 47 µF and 1 µF). The electrolytic has to carry the part from the moment the LLC starts until the auxiliary winding takes over: CVCC > IOPER × Δt ÷ ΔU, which the datasheet works for 25 mA, 15 ms and 15 V as 25 µF.

### Output regulator (RS1 to RS4, CS2, QS1, US1)

The secondary side is a standard shunt-regulator loop: a divider (RS1, RS2) to the reference of a TL431-type QS1, which sinks the current of the optocoupler LED in US1 through RS4, with CS2 and RS3 in the compensation. For 24 V and RS2 = 10 kΩ, a 2.495 V reference gives RS1 = 86.2 kΩ, so 86.6 kΩ (E96) and 24.10 V. The 2.495 V is the usual TL431 value, not a number from this datasheet. The datasheet gives no values for RS3, RS4, CS2 or the optocoupler. FBL runs at low current to save energy at no load, so it is sensitive to noise; see Layout.

### Worked example

The operating point for this page's example is a 120 W overpower point at the output. At 95% LLC efficiency that is 126.3 W out of the PFC stage, and at 96% PFC efficiency 131.6 W from the line (both efficiencies are our assumptions, held constant across the line range for simplicity). The bus is 386 V, the LLC runs at 100 kHz and the output is 24 V. Everything in this table is our arithmetic from the values chosen above.

| Line | Input current (A RMS) | Inductor peak (A) | Voltage on RCS at the peak (V) | Peak on LNS (V) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 90 VAC | 1.46 | 4.14 | 0.62 | 0.53 |
| 115 VAC | 1.14 | 3.24 | 0.49 | 0.67 |
| 230 VAC | 0.57 | 1.62 | 0.24 | 1.34 |
| 264 VAC | 0.50 | 1.41 | 0.21 | 1.54 |

The peak voltage on RCS at 90 VAC is 0.62 V, under the 0.65 V minimum current limit, so the full 126 W is available there. The LLC draws about 0.33 A from the bus at the overpower point.

**IC dissipation.** The gate drivers draw their charge from VCC. Taking 60 nC for each of three MOSFETs, 100 kHz on the LLC and, as an upper bound, 100 kHz on the PFC, the gate current is 18 mA on top of the 2 mA quiescent current. At the 18.5 V that the auxiliary winding gives, that is 0.37 W if all of it is dissipated in the chip, or 45 °C of rise at 122 °C/W. The recommended junction limit is 85 °C, which leaves room only up to about 40 °C ambient. This is an upper bound, because part of the gate energy is lost in the gate resistors, and the datasheet's 122 °C/W is the maximum, measured on a four-layer Silergy evaluation board in natural convection. Its absolute-maximum power dissipation is 1.02 W at 25 °C ambient. Gate charge is the lever: low-Qg MOSFETs, gate resistors and no more VCC than needed all reduce the heat (our reading; the datasheet gives no dissipation budget).

## Layout

The datasheet's layout rules, in its own order:

- **Shield the FBL track.** FBL runs at low current to save energy at no load, which makes it sensitive. Capacitive coupling from the HB node or the PFC drain can upset regulation. Keep FBL as short as possible and away from the power section, with grounded tracks (or a ground plane on a two-sided board) alongside it.
- **Keep the energy loops small.** Minimise the length and area of each converter's power loop (tracks A and B in its Figure 37), and connect the IC to the shared bulk capacitor through a separate ground track.
- **Separate PFC and LLC grounds.** Current pulses in shared ground tracks put wrong values on pins that reference ground. The main sources are the power switching and the gate-drive currents. Join them as in its Figure 38, with the red ground tracks short and wide.
- **Put the sense resistors close to the chip.** For FBB, LNS, CS, ISEN and VSEN, place the sensing resistors next to the pin, to keep capacitive coupling out. The same goes for CFBB, CCS and CVSEN.

Its Figure 38 numbers ten ground nodes, which a layout should keep apart until they meet at the bulk capacitor:

| Node | Ground of |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | The PFC bulk capacitor |
| 2 | The CS resistor and the PFC MOSFET source |
| 3 | The LLC resonant capacitor and the low-side MOSFET source |
| 4 | The transformer auxiliary winding |
| 5 | The IC GND pin |
| 6 | The VCC capacitor |
| 7 | The optocoupler |
| 8 | The lower FBB resistor |
| 9 | The lower VSEN resistor |
| 10 | The lower LNS resistor |

Two points are ours, not the datasheet's: pin 14 (HV, rated 650 V) sits at the end of the package next to GATEL, so check the clearance for your voltage and pollution degree; and the HV, LNS and FBB resistors carry the full line or bus voltage, so check creepage along them as well.

### Captures at 115 and 230 VAC

The captures are of the datasheet's own 24 V, 10 A evaluation board, whose component values it doesn't give, so none of the numbers on this page can be checked against them. What they show, by eye:

- **PFC steady state** (10 µs per division; drain at 100 V/div, CS at 0.2 V/div). The drain swings between ground and the bus, and CS is a triangle of about 0.65 V peak to peak that returns to its minimum as the MOSFET turns on again, which is critical conduction. At 115 VAC the period is about 20 µs.
- **LLC steady state** (10 µs per division; HB node at 100 V/div, ISEN at 0.2 V/div). The HB node is a square wave and ISEN a near-sinusoidal waveform of about ±0.35 V, a little under the 380 mV overpower reference. At 115 VAC the period is about 11 µs, which is near 90 kHz.
- **Start-up** (10 ms per division, 115 and 230 VAC). In the PFC-drain captures the PFC switches first, there is a short pause, then VOUT rises to 24 V in about 15–20 ms with the LLC running. The HB captures show VOUT following the LLC start-up with no visible overshoot at 5 V/div.
- **Shut-down** (10 ms per division). VOUT stays at 24 V until the stages stop and then falls to zero within about 10–20 ms. In the HB captures the switching envelope shrinks over roughly 60–70 ms as the bus falls, to about two-thirds of its starting amplitude, before the LLC stops. That is the right region for the LLC brown-out on FBB (238 V on this page's 386 V bus), but it is our reading.
- **Short circuit** (1 s per division, VOUT at 10 V/div, IOUT at 5 A/div). The output collapses to zero while the short is applied, with a current pulse about once a second, which matches the 1 s restart timer. When the short is removed the output returns to 24 V and 10 A. The capture lasts about five restarts, too few to show the latch after 16, so it doesn't say which R3 the board used.

## Things to watch

- **The values here are examples, and the figure has none.** Figure 1 draws the circuit without component values, and the datasheet's text gives ranges and a few examples. Our worked example uses the datasheet's 120 W overpower point; the datasheet's own performance data is for 24 V at 10 A. The schematic caption says which values are the datasheet's and which are ours. The LLC tank is not designed here, and nothing on this page is a tested design.
- **Rev 1.0 is an initial release.** The revision history has one line, "Initial Release", dated December 31, 2024, and every page is marked "Confidential-Prepared for Customer Use Only". We could not check the product page, so the part's lifecycle status is unverified. Expect revisions.
- **The pin table has two slips.** It says GATEB connects to "the LLC external MOSFET gate" and that FBB sets "the LLC voltage". The rest of the datasheet is clear that GATEB drives the PFC MOSFET and FBB sets the PFC output.
- **The start-up current threshold is given twice.** The electrical table tests the 0.5 mA level at VCC under 0.7 V, while the text and the table's VCC short-circuit protection level put the change from 0.5 mA to 5.6 mA at 0.8 V (0.6–1 V). This page uses 0.8 V.
- **The overpower example has a typo.** It selects RLNSD = 25 kΩ and RLNSU = 25 kΩ, and says that gives RCS < 185 mΩ. With those numbers the equation gives 1.5 mΩ. The printed 185 mΩ comes out if RLNSU is 6 MΩ and RLNSD stays 25 kΩ (185 mΩ), and 6 MΩ is the value the rest of the datasheet uses.
- **The brown-out example uses a different threshold than the table.** The 24.3 kΩ for a 70 VAC brown-out matches a 0.4 V threshold; the table's typical 395 mV gives 24.0 kΩ. With 24.3 kΩ the highest brown-in is 87.7 VAC, close to a 90 VAC minimum line.
- **The LNS filter figures disagree.** The text asks for a 100 µs filter and for 1 nF. With a 6 MΩ and 24.3 kΩ divider, 1 nF is about 24 µs.
- **The RISEN example doesn't reproduce.** With the datasheet's inputs and k = 1.23×10⁻⁶ we get 0.320 Ω, not 0.31 Ω, and R′ISEN of 52.8 Ω rather than 51 Ω. The two worked examples also use 120 W differently: in the RCS example it is POUT_MAX, the output, with 95% efficiency applied; in the RISEN example it is PIN_OPP, the LLC input.
- **Table 2 uses strict inequalities.** It prints 70 Ω < RISENSET + RISEN < 100 Ω and so on, and the windows don't touch. This page shows them as plain ranges.
- **What "RISEN" means in Table 2 is not stated.** It is the sense resistor on the ISEN node in Figure 24; with the capacitive method that is R′ISEN. We took it that way. Confirm the working range on the bench.
- **One plot doesn't match its title.** The "VFBB_REF vs. temperature" plot has a y-axis labelled VBG and reads 1.25–1.26 V, which is outside the table's 1.18–1.22 V. Design from the table. The VGATEB plot also rises to about 11.85 V when hot, above the 11.6 V table maximum.
- **Figure 1 leaves parts out.** CFBB, CCS, CVSEN, RBST, the ceramic VCC capacitor and the ISEN capacitor are in the text or the typical-application page and not in Figure 1. The BOM lists them; the drawing doesn't.
- **R3 sets two behaviours at once.** 200–240 Ω restarts after the timer, 510–800 Ω latches after 16 restarts, for the overpower, open-loop, output-voltage and ISEN-limit trips alike. Decide on purpose.
- **FBL thresholds are only partly listed.** The text uses a 130% overpower level and a burst-entry level for the FBL current, and the electrical table gives only the 250% (open-loop) level of 24 µA (15–33 µA) and a 1 kHz regulated burst frequency. The secondary compensation can't be designed from this datasheet alone.
- **The datasheet gives no LLC tank design.** Resonant capacitor and inductor, magnetizing inductance, turns ratio and the optocoupler's transfer ratio all come from your own design. The auxiliary winding ratio also has to meet NAUX ÷ Nsec × VO < the shunt voltage.
- **DC input changes the start-up and discharge behaviour.** R5 chooses whether DC input is supported; the datasheet says the X-capacitor discharge is off for a DC input. Check that the choice matches the product.
- **The electrical table is measured at a VCC of 15 V**, reached after first raising VCC above its turn-on threshold, so the figures in it assume the part has already started.
