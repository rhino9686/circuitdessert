## How it works

The SY81154U is a synchronous buck regulator with both power switches inside a SOT563 package. It regulates with constant on-time, ripple-based control. The controller builds an internal copy of the inductor current, adds it to the feedback voltage, and starts a new on-time whenever that sum falls below the 0.6 V reference. The on-time is worked out inside the chip from the input voltage, the output voltage and the target frequency (tON = (VOUT ÷ VIN) ÷ fSW), so the switching frequency stays close to 800 kHz as VIN changes. The compensation is built in, so the circuit needs no external compensation network.

At light load the SY81154U uses ultrasonic mode (USM) instead of letting its frequency fall into the audible range. If both switches have been off for a set time, the controller turns the bottom switch on early to take a little charge off the output capacitor, which keeps the switching frequency above audible frequencies even with no load. A separate loop matches that forced on-time to the error-amplifier output so the output voltage doesn't creep up. The trade-off shows in the datasheet's efficiency plots: at 1 mA the 12 V curve sits around 38%, well below the 50–60% of the PFM parts in this family.

Enabling the part (EN rising through about 1.2 V) starts a 300 µs turn-on delay, then an internal soft-start of roughly 1.5 ms that limits inrush current. The part can also start into an output that is already pre-biased. Current limiting is cycle by cycle: the top switch turns off at its peak limit (at least 4.5 A) and the bottom switch stays on until the current falls below its valley limit (at least 3.5 A). If the output drops below 33% of its regulated level, the part enters hiccup mode, 2.5 ms on and 7.5 ms off, and recovers by itself when the short is removed. Over-temperature protection shuts the part down at 150 °C and restarts it, after a full soft-start, once the junction has cooled by about 15 °C. Input under-voltage lockout keeps switching off until VIN is above its rising threshold.

The datasheet states a maximum duty cycle of up to 60% across temperature, so the output can't sit above roughly 60% of the input. For this 3.3 V circuit that works out to a VIN of about 5.5 V or more, above the part's own 4.5 V minimum, so the bottom of the input range only suits lower output voltages.

## Example application

A 12 V to 3.3 V buck converter built from the circuit in Silergy's datasheet. At light load the part uses an ultrasonic mode that keeps the switching frequency above the audible range. This section keeps the datasheet's component values, adds the reasoning behind each one, and works the numbers for 12 V in.

The datasheet gives one reference circuit, for a 3.3 V output, and a table of component values for 1.2 V, 1.8 V and 5 V. It does not name a nominal input voltage, so the worked example below uses 12 V, with 18 V as the worst case for ripple and peak current.

### Output voltage

The output is set by two resistors: VOUT = 0.6 V × (1 + RH ÷ RL). The datasheet recommends 1 kΩ to 1 MΩ for both, to keep light-load current low. With RH = 100 kΩ and a 3.3 V target, RL = 0.6 ÷ (3.3 − 0.6) × 100 kΩ = 22.22 kΩ. The nearest 1% value, 22.1 kΩ, gives 3.315 V nominal. With the reference's ±1% tolerance the output lands between 3.282 V and 3.348 V, before the resistors' own 1% tolerance is added.

### Inductor

Choose the inductance for a ripple current of about 40% of the maximum load at the highest input voltage: L = VOUT × (1 − VOUT ÷ VIN,MAX) ÷ (fSW × IOUT,MAX × 0.4). For 3.3 V, 18 V in, 4 A and 800 kHz that gives 2.11 µH, and the datasheet uses 2.2 µH. The part tolerates a wide range of ripple, so the final value can differ from the calculation with little effect.

Three more checks apply. The saturation current must exceed the peak current, ISAT > IOUT,MAX + VOUT × (1 − VOUT ÷ VIN,MAX) ÷ (2 × fSW × L). The winding resistance (DCR) should be below 30 mΩ for good efficiency, along with low core loss at 800 kHz. And at light load the inductance has to be high enough that the reverse-current limit is not reached with zero load.

### Output capacitor

Use an X5R or better ceramic rated for 16 V, with at least 32 µF. The reference circuit uses two 22 µF capacitors in parallel. Output ripple has two parts: the inductor ripple current across the capacitor's ESR (ΔIL × ESR), and the charge on the capacitance (ΔIL ÷ (8 × COUT × fSW)). Add them for a total. Ceramic capacitance falls as the DC voltage across it rises, so measured ripple is usually higher than the calculation; read the effective capacitance off the capacitor's DC-bias curve and recalculate.

### Input capacitor

Use an X5R or X7R ceramic rated 25 V (at least 20% above the highest input voltage), 10 µF or more, as close as possible to the IN and GND pins with the smallest possible loop. The RMS current it carries is IOUT × √(D × (1 − D)), at most IOUT ÷ 2 at 50% duty, so choose one with an RMS rating above half the load current, and add capacitors in parallel if the calculation needs it. The datasheet says capacitance matters less than RMS rating, and that a single 10 µF ceramic is enough for most applications. If VIN arrives over long wires, add bulk capacitance (electrolytic, tantalum or polymer) to damp the overshoot and ringing from the lead inductance. The reference circuit's 47 µF electrolytic, CIN1, is that kind of bulk capacitor; the datasheet doesn't say why it was included.

### Bootstrap, feedforward and enable

The bootstrap capacitor CBS is a 0.1 µF low-ESR ceramic between BS and LX; it supplies the gate drive for the top switch. The feedforward capacitor CFF sits across RH and can speed up the load-transient response; the datasheet recommends at least 100 pF for most applications and a larger value when the output LC is large. This circuit uses 220 pF.

Don't connect EN straight to VIN or another voltage source; the datasheet asks for a resistor between 1 kΩ and 1 MΩ, and EN must not float. The reference circuit uses RENH = 10 kΩ and RENL = 1 MΩ, which puts EN at 98% of VIN, so the part starts when VIN passes its own under-voltage threshold and the divider only limits current. To start at a higher voltage, size the divider from the EN thresholds (1.2 V rising, 1.1 V falling, typical): VIN,start = 1.2 V × (1 + RENH ÷ RENL). The EN pin has an internal resistor of about 1 MΩ to ground, which counts in parallel with RENL. As an example, RENH = 510 kΩ with RENL = 100 kΩ (about 91 kΩ with the internal resistor) starts the part at about 7.9 V and stops it at about 7.3 V.

> **Check the peak current at the top of the range.** At 18 V in and a full 4 A load, the 2.2 µH inductor's ripple puts the peak at 4.77 A, above the 4.5 A minimum top-switch limit. The limits in the datasheet are minimums, so a typical part will have more room, but if you need the full 4 A at 18 V in, a larger inductor lowers the peak: about 3.4 µH or more brings it to the limit, and 4.7 µH brings it to about 4.36 A. The datasheet's own table recommends 2.2 µH at 3.3 V.

## Layout

Silergy gives these layout rules, along with a suggested placement drawing (Figure 9 in the datasheet). With a package this small and fast switching edges, layout decides how well the converter works.

- Put the **bootstrap capacitor** on the same layer as the IC and keep the BS, LX and CBS path as short as possible.
- Put the **input capacitors** very close to the IN and GND pins, with the smallest loop, joined by a wide copper area.
- Connect the **output capacitors'** negative terminals to the GND pin with wide copper, not vias.
- Place **RH, RL and CFF** as close to the FB pin as possible and route the feedback line away from LX and other fast signals. Take the feedback from the output capacitor with a Kelvin connection, not from the inductor's output terminal.
- Keep the **LX copper** small to limit EMI, but wide enough to keep its resistance and inductance low.
- Use a **ground plane** if you can. Put several vias on the ground pad, on a copper area larger than the pad, for heat. The datasheet's thermal resistance (θJA) is 70 °C/W on a 6 × 6 cm, two-layer, 2 oz board.

## What the datasheet shows

Silergy's typical-performance pages cover this circuit at 25 °C, 12 V in and 3.3 V out, with COUT = 44 µF (two 22 µF). They include efficiency against load current from 1 mA to 4 A at 5 V, 12 V and 18 V in for each of the four output voltages, scope captures of output ripple at no load and full load, load-transient steps, start-up and shut-down from both EN and VIN, and a short-circuit capture that shows the hiccup behaviour.

Reading the 12 V in, 3.3 V out efficiency curve by eye, it is about 38% at 1 mA, about 88% at 0.1 A, about 94% at 1–2 A, and about 87% at 4 A. Treat these as rough, about two points either way, since they come from a graph. That plot uses 2.2 µH for 3.3 V.

## Things to watch

- **Check the peak current at 18 V in.** See the worked example: with the reference 2.2 µH inductor the peak at a full 4 A is above the minimum top-switch limit. If you need 4 A at high input voltage, use a larger inductor or limit the load.
- **Light-load efficiency is the price of ultrasonic mode.** The part keeps switching above the audible range, so at 1 mA expect efficiency far lower than the PFM parts. If you need to keep switching noise out of the audio band at idle, that is the reason to use this part. If standby current matters more, the SY81153C is the PFM option.
- **The datasheet asks for more of the passives than its siblings:** at least 32 µF of output capacitance (against 22 µF) and a CFF of at least 100 pF (against 10 or 22 pF). The reference circuit already follows both.
- **At a full 4 A the SOT563 package has to shed real heat.** The datasheet quotes 70 °C/W on its own test board; use as much ground copper as you can and check junction temperature at your worst case.
- **The duty limit is 60%**, so 3.3 V needs about 5.5 V in or more.

## Datasheet notes

The layout section talks about connecting "the exposed GND pad" to a large copper area, but the SOT563 pinout shows no exposed pad. This looks carried over from a related datasheet with a different package, so just give the GND pin plenty of copper.
