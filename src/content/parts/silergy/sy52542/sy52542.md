## How it works

The SY52542 stands in for the output diode of a flyback converter. The MOSFET is on the die, and the controller watches its own drain voltage to decide when to switch it. It works in continuous (CCM), discontinuous (DCM) and quasi-resonant (QR) converters, and the datasheet's Figure 1 shows it in both positions: low side, with the SOURCE pins on the output return and the VIN pin on the output rail, and high side, with VIN tied to the SOURCE pin.

**Turn-on.** When the primary switch turns off, the secondary drain voltage falls very fast, which is what the part looks for. It samples the drain voltage through a divider (2% of the drain voltage) and compares two levels. The turn-on is allowed only if the sampled voltage stays above a high threshold that adjusts dynamically (the datasheet gives it as 0.85 × VDRAIN) for the 310 ns blanking time and then falls to the low threshold in less than a reference time, 170 ns typical. A resonant ring in DCM or QR falls too slowly, so it doesn't pass, and a short noise pulse such as an ESD event doesn't last through the blanking time. The MOSFET then turns on after a short delay.

**In CCM,** the primary switch turns on before the secondary current reaches zero. The drain voltage rises through the 0 mV turn-off threshold, and the gate is pulled down hard after a 10 ns delay, short enough to keep the overlap with the primary switch small.

**In DCM and QR,** the secondary current falls to zero before the next primary turn-on. As the current decays, a closed loop lowers the gate drive to hold the drain-source voltage at about −40 mV, so the MOSFET conducts for as long as there is current and then turns off cleanly at 0 mV, again with a 10 ns delay.

**Blanking.** After turning on, the part ignores the turn-off comparator for a minimum on-time (700 ns typical), because the drain voltage is pulled to about −700 mV by parasitic resonance right after the primary turns off. After turning off, it ignores the drain for a minimum off-time (565 ns typical) while the drain rings. The datasheet's design notes say the SR freewheeling time should be longer than the minimum on-time at no load, or the MOSFET turns off late.

**Supply.** The part makes its own supply voltage VDD, which also drives the gate. Before VDD reaches 5.26 V it charges from the DRAIN pin. Once VIN is above 4.6 V it switches to VIN, and VDD follows VIN less about 0.5 V up to 9 V, where it is regulated. If VIN falls below 4.55 V, it goes back to the DRAIN pin and regulates VDD to 9 V. This is what lets one part serve 3.3 V to 21 V outputs. While VDD is below its turn-on level, a pull-down circuit powered from the DRAIN voltage holds the gate low (200 mA of pull-down at VGS = 1 V).

**Power saving.** After the SR MOSFET turns off, a timer starts. If it reaches 20 µs before the next turn-on, the part drops to its standby current (230 µA typical) until the next turn-on. In the datasheet's no-load waveforms the SR current is one short pulse separated by long quiet gaps.

**Over-temperature.** At 160 °C the part stops driving the gate and holds it at 0 V. It resumes below 140 °C.

### Snubber

The RC snubber (RS1 and CS1) damps the ringing of the stray inductance in the secondary loop with the MOSFET's equivalent capacitance. The datasheet measures LSTRAY with an LCR meter and reads the ringing period from the drain waveform, then takes CEQ = Tr² ÷ ((2π)² × LSTRAY), which is 1.27 nF for 50 ns and 50 nH. A resistor equal to √(LSTRAY ÷ CEQ) gives a Q of 1, 6.27 Ω, and the datasheet selects 5.1 Ω. CS1 affects the drain spike and the thermal performance, and the datasheet recommends 1 nF / 250 V.

### Supply components

In the low-side position, VIN is one of the two supplies, so the datasheet asks for a reservoir capacitor CS2 close to the pin, 0.1 µF / 25 V, and a 10 Ω resistor RS2. CS3 on VDD is 1 µF / 16 V in most applications, and the pin description asks for at least 0.47 µF.

For ESD, the datasheet suggests a series resistor of 10 to 51 Ω between the VIN pin and the output terminal, with at least 100 nF in parallel between VIN and SOURCE, which is what RS2 and CS2 are.

## Layout

The datasheet gives three layout rules and a suggested layout (its Figure 11, bottom view). Check the figure in the datasheet.

- Keep the **switching loops small**: the secondary power loop, the secondary RC snubber loop and the IC power-supply loop.
- Put a **decoupling capacitor between the output connector and the SR MOSFET output** to improve EMI and efficiency.
- To **reduce ringing**, reduce the parasitic inductance by optimizing the layout, or increase the RC snubber.

The typical-performance pages show the secondary current and the SR drain voltage of the 65 W charger at 115 Vac and 230 Vac, at full load and at no load, for the 5 V, 3 A and the 20 V, 3.25 A outputs (10 µs per division). At full load the SR current is a sawtooth that ramps down to zero each period, with the drain voltage low while it conducts. At no load the current is a single short pulse with a long quiet gap, and the drain voltage rings afterwards. ## Things to watch

- **The 105 A and 420 A drain-current ratings can't be used in this package.** They are by-design silicon figures. The 1.3 W the datasheet allows at 25 °C is used up at about 17 A RMS with 4.3 mΩ (15 A with 5.8 mΩ), and the heat-budget table above is the real limit.
- **RDS(on) is specified at VGS = 10 V, but the gate is driven from a lower VDD.** VDD is regulated to 9 V typical (8.5–9.4 V), and follows VIN less about 0.5 V below that, which gives about 4.5 V on a 5 V output. The datasheet doesn't give RDS(on) at those gate voltages, so on 5 V outputs the real conduction loss is higher than the 4.3 mΩ figure suggests. The test condition of 60 A is also far above the 3 A outputs in the examples.
- **The recommended VIN maximum is 21 V.** A 20 V output leaves 5%, so a PD supply with a positive tolerance and overshoot can reach it. The absolute maximum is 28 V. Voltages above 21 V, such as 28 V PD outputs, aren't covered.
- **The drain-voltage rating limits the transformer's turns ratio.** With 120 V on the drain, a 20 V output and a 264 Vac line, the turns ratio must be at least 3.7 even with no ringing margin (see the worked example). A lower ratio would put more than 120 V on the drain at the line peak.
- **The standby current is a no-load power.** About 4.6 mW at 20 V, if it all comes from VIN. That matters for no-load limits in the single-digit milliwatts.
- **The SR current must outlast the minimum on-time.** The design notes say the freewheeling time at no load must be longer than the 700 ns minimum on-time, or the SR turns off late.
- **The snubber capacitor is smaller than the equivalent capacitance in the example.** CS1 = 1 nF against the 1.27 nF that the example calculates. The datasheet says CS1 affects the drain spike and thermal performance and gives 1 nF as the recommended value. The snubber loss isn't given.

## Datasheet notes

- **Two notes still carry template text.** Note 2 (thermal resistance) ends with "[Use the actual condition provided by package engineering.]" and Note 5 with "[Note 5 may be omitted if all EC parameters are tested in production.]". The case temperature for θJC is measured at pin 4.
- **Signal names come from another part.** The electrical table's "Blanking Time for Sample PVS Pin Voltage" and the text's VPVS refer to a pin that the SY52542 doesn't have, and the block diagram calls the sensing unit DSEN. These appear to be the drain-sensing circuit under older names.
- **The sensing thresholds are described inconsistently.** The text says the sampled voltage is 0.02 × VDRAIN and the high threshold is 0.85 × VDRAIN, which mixes the drain voltage and its sampled value without saying which the 0.85 applies to.
- **The low-level threshold is 0 mV in the text and 20 mV in the table.** The falling-slope description says the low-level threshold VDRAIN_LTH is 0 mV, while the electrical table gives 20 mV typical (10–30). The 170 ns reference time, the 0.85 × VDRAIN high threshold, the 10 ns turn-off delay and the 200 mA pull-down appear in the text only, not in the electrical table.
- **The minimum on-time paragraph reads as a slip.** It says the GATE output is "latched off" during the blanking time, while the purpose of the blanking, to keep the MOSFET from turning off on the −700 mV ring, suggests it is held on.
- **Figure 1 and the typical application differ.** Figure 1 shows the converter with its own designators (C3, R2, D1 and so on); the typical application renames the secondary parts and adds QS1, RS3, US2 and US3 around a protocol IC. The BOM lists only the seven parts of the SR section. This page uses the typical application's designators.
- **The datasheet is Rev 1.0, initial release on March 12, 2026.** The part's production status wasn't checked. Silergy's product page lists an evaluation board, EVB_SY52542TVD.
