## How it works

The SY6045B is a digital-input Class-D audio amplifier. It takes a stereo I²S (or left- or right-justified) stream on one data pin, runs it through a 32-bit fixed-point DSP, and drives four half-bridge outputs from a 4.5–28 V supply (PVDD). Each half-bridge has a 125 mΩ high-side and a 125 mΩ low-side FET (typical, at 25 °C). The four outputs are normally used as two bridge-tied-load (BTL) channels, each driving one speaker; they can also be paralleled (PBTL) for a single lower-impedance speaker. There is no MCLK pin and no analog audio input: the part is a slave that takes all its clocks from the host.

**Clocking.** The part accepts a 1 × fs LRCLK (32, 44.1, 48 or 96 kHz) and an SCLK of 32, 48 or 64 × fs, between 2.048 and 6.144 MHz. A PLL makes an internal clock at 512 times the PWM switching frequency, which is 384 kHz (352.8 kHz when the data rate is 44.1 kHz). The sample rate can be detected automatically or set over I²C (register 0x00; the default is manual, 44.1/48 kHz). If SCLK stops or goes out of range, the part mutes, runs the PLL from its internal oscillator and returns to normal when the clock is stable again, with either a hard unmute (the default) or a soft ramp.

**Signal path.** Per channel the DSP has a mixer, two DC-blocking filters, up to 18 biquad filters (12 parametric EQ plus 6 optional "super" parametric EQ that react to signal level), a three-band dynamic range control (DRC) with a post-DRC, loudness control, a hard clipper and a power meter. Coefficients are 32-bit numbers in 6.26 fixed-point format, written 20 bytes per biquad over I²C. Volume has a master control (0 to −126 dB in 0.5 dB steps), a per-channel control (+48 to −79 dB) and a fine control of up to +7.875 dB in 0.125 dB steps.

**I²C control.** The slave address is 0x54 with ADDR_SEL low and 0x56 with it high. The bus runs at 100 or 400 kHz, with no wait states and no multi-master. Nothing works until the host has released the part from shutdown: after reset the PWM control register (0x22) holds both the shutdown and the standby bit, the outputs are high-impedance, and the master volume (0x07) starts at its mute code. This part cannot be used without a controller on I²C.

**Protection and fault reporting.** The part watches for PVDD undervoltage, overtemperature (150 °C, 30 °C hysteresis), overcurrent (5 A, typical) and short circuits, and for output DC, clock errors and coefficient-memory checksum errors. An overcurrent or short in any one half-bridge turns all four off. FAULT_B_OD outputs internal power-stage errors; pulling FAULT_B low turns the PWM path off; the MONITOR pins can be set to output fault flags, PWM signals or an I²S data stream.

### Host connections and the reset circuit

The figure's host side is worth reading closely, because the part will not start without it.

- **Reset.** RST_B has a 4.7 kΩ resistor and a 1 nF capacitor to ground. The pin table lists a weak internal pull-up with no value; the 4.7 kΩ to ground is likely to override it, so the host should drive /RESET high itself (push-pull, or with its own pull-up). The datasheet wants RST_B low for at least 100 µs (0.1 ms), and at power-up for at least 100 µs after VDD_IO has reached 3 V.
- **I²C.** SDA and SCL each have 100 Ω in series with 33 pF to ground at the pin, which is a small RC filter. No pull-up resistors to 3.3 V are drawn. The pin table lists weak internal pull-ups without values, so external pull-ups are normally needed.
- **Address.** ADDR_SEL is grounded, so the address is 0x54. Tied to 3.3 V it would be 0x56.
- **Fault and monitor pins.** FAULT_B_OD (the error output, with a weak internal pull-up) and FAULT_B go to the host, as do MONITOR_0 to MONITOR_2. The figure leaves the MONITOR pins as outputs, but they stay disabled until register 0x17 enables them.
- **TEST** is grounded and **GND_IO** is the ground of the 3.3 V logic supply.

### Starting and stopping the part

The datasheet's "Recommended Command Sequences" (page 26, Figure 26) give the order, and the start-up table above turns it into bytes (ours, with an example volume of −20 dB, sent to the write address 0x54). Registers 0x00 (44.1/48 kHz, manual) and 0x15 (I²S, 24 bits) already default to a 48 kHz I²S stream, so nothing else is needed for that format. The datasheet puts "configure the DAP" and "configure remaining registers" between the reset and the exit from shutdown, and the volume is one of those registers; writing it before the release (step 4) rather than after is our choice. Shutdown is the reverse: set both bits of register 0x22, wait at least 20 ms, then remove the supplies.

### What the output filter does (calculated)

Each speaker sees two 10 µH inductors (one per side) and 470 nF across the speaker. The two 100 nF capacitors to ground are in series across the speaker, so they add 50 nF to the differential capacitance, giving 520 nF with 20 µH. The filter table above gives the corner (49.4 kHz) and Q for 4, 6 and 8 Ω, ignoring the snubbers, the 4.7 kΩ resistors, the inductor resistance and the speaker's own inductance. A real speaker is not a resistor, so the figures show the trend only: the filter is about flat into 4–6 Ω and lifts the top of the audio band by up to 1 dB into 8 Ω. For common-mode noise, each 10 µH with its 100 nF to ground has a corner at about 159 kHz.

### Heat in practice (calculated)

The datasheet's efficiency plots (page 11) are plotted against "Output Power (W)", without saying whether that is per channel or for both. The 12 V curves end at about 8 W, close to that supply's single-channel 1% THD+N power (7.5 W into 8 Ω), so the heat table above reads the axis as power per channel with both channels loaded equally. Efficiencies are read by eye from the 8 Ω plot, so they are good to a point or two. With θJA = 23 °C/W (measured on a high-conductivity four-layer board with thermal vias) the loss turns into a junction rise, and subtracting it from the 125 °C recommended junction limit gives the highest ambient temperature.

Two readings follow from the table. First, both channels at the headline 20 W each, continuously, cannot be held at 18 V on this package: it would need an ambient near 0 °C even on the datasheet's good board. Music has a low average-to-peak ratio, so this is a limit on sine-wave tests and on thermal design, not on listening. Second, a higher PVDD costs efficiency at the same output power, so the supply should be no higher than the loudest output needs.

## Layout

The datasheet has no layout text. All it gives is the package drawing and a "Recommended PCB Layout (Reference only)" land pattern on page 52. What can be said from the datasheet's own figures and tables:

- The package has an exposed thermal pad (pinout, page 2), and θJC (bottom) of 2 °C/W is far lower than θJC (top) of 13.5 °C/W, so heat leaves through the pad. The 23 °C/W θJA assumes a four-layer board with thermal vias under it.
- Figure 1 puts one 10 µF 35 V capacitor on each pair of PVDD pins and one 22 nF capacitor from each VBS pin to its VOUT pin, and returns every snubber to ground.
- Absolute maximum on VOUT_x and PVDD is 30 V against a recommended maximum of 28 V. Note 2 says the DC rating may be derated for the switching spike if the snubber is not adequate, so the snubber and the PVDD capacitor placement are what protect that 2 V margin.

## Things to watch

- **The part starts muted and in shutdown.** Registers 0x22 (shutdown and standby, both set at reset) and 0x07 (master volume, 0x00) must be written before anything is heard, so the host needs to be able to reach the part over I²C. Writing 0x22 = 0x00 with the volume still at 0x00 gives a silent but active amplifier.
- **Reset is held by the figure's own network.** The 4.7 kΩ to ground on RST_B beats the weak internal pull-up, so a host pin that floats or is open-drain will leave the part in reset.
- **No I²C pull-ups are drawn.** The pins have only weak internal pull-ups.
- **The wait after reset differs between places.** The text says RST_B high, then wait at least 0.5 ms before I²C. The reset-timing table lists an "Enable I²C duration" of 0.1 ms in the maximum column, and Figure 26 shows both. The write list above uses the longer wait.
- **The sample-rate settings are 32, 44.1/48 and 96 kHz.** LRCLK must be 1 × fs and SCLK 32, 48 or 64 × fs, between 2.048 and 6.144 MHz. At 44.1 kHz the PWM runs at 352.8 kHz instead of 384 kHz.
- **I²C is 400 kHz at most, with no clock stretching.** The part does not insert wait states.
- **The PBTL output must be tied before the LC filter**, not after it. An overcurrent or short in either half-bridge turns all four off, so one fault mutes both channels.
- **The recommended load is 8 Ω (BTL) and 4 Ω (PBTL).** The figure and the power table use 6 Ω speakers, and the plots also include 4 Ω in BTL mode. The 4 Ω efficiency curves are the lowest (about 80 to 84%), so that is where the heat is worst.
- **Continuous full power is a thermal limit, not an electrical one.** See the heat budget: 20 W on both channels at 18 V needs more than the datasheet's board can remove.
- **Both ends of the PVDD range are tight.** The recommended minimum is 4.5 V and the undervoltage threshold on the way up is 4.0 V typical, 4.45 V maximum, so the minimum leaves only 0.05 V above the worst-case threshold. At the top, the recommended maximum is 28 V and the absolute maximum 30 V, and the datasheet says that rating can be reduced by switching spikes if the snubber is not right. Figure 1's PVDD capacitors are rated 35 V.
- **The ADDR_SEL pin can change function.** Bit 5 of register 0x19 (adr_faultb_output_en) turns it into a FAULT_B output, and bit 0 of register 0x05 lets the address follow the pin at any time. With the defaults the address is fixed at reset.
- **Several protections are off or limited by default.** PWM DC detection (register 0x1F) and both checksum checks are off after reset; protection enables (register 0x76) are all on. The overtemperature, overcurrent and PWM-modulation figures in the electrical tables are typical values tested on a demonstration board and "guaranteed by design" (note 4).
- **EQ coefficients are 32-bit 6.26 numbers and the default for every biquad is a pass-through** (b0 = 0x0400_0000, i.e. 1.0), so an unwritten filter does nothing.
- **The datasheet is marked confidential in every footer.**
- **The KiCad symbol is ours and not yet opened in KiCad.** It was generated by script and checked against the pin table, but nobody has loaded it in KiCad. The pin types and the symbol layout are our choices. It is built from the pinout figure and pin table of the SY24145B datasheet, the same part under its new name. The pin types of SCLK, SDIN, LRCLK, SDA, SCL, RST_B, FAULT_B, FAULT_B_OD, MONITOR_0 to MONITOR_2, TEST and VDD_PLL come from their names and from this page, because only half of the pin table was read. The thermal pad's number (41) is ours. There is no KiCad footprint on this site, so the symbol's Footprint field is empty.

## Datasheet notes

- **The snubber in the test conditions is not the snubber in Figure 1.** The AC characteristics and the plots are measured with a 3.3 Ω + 390 pF snubber, and Figure 1 draws 10 Ω + 390 pF. The two give different damping of the switching spike.
- **The load in the figure is not the recommended load.** The recommended operating conditions give 8 Ω (BTL) and 4 Ω (PBTL); Figure 1 draws 6 Ω speakers.
- **The loudness gain register has two addresses.** The text and Table 3 put the loudness gains at 0x4D, while the register table puts them at 0x57. Table 8 also assigns 0x4D to a DRC biquad (BQ6), with 20 bytes against 12.
- **The checksum key registers are named inconsistently.** The text says 0x9A, 0x9B and 0x9C are the EQ key for channel 1, the DRC key and the EQ key for channel 2. In the register table 0x9C is titled "amute threshold" but its description is the DRC checksum key (the same text as 0x9B), and 0x6F is the auto mute threshold.
- **The power-meter registers are described differently.** The text says to read the 10-bit level in dB from 0x58 and the linear value from 0x99; the register table puts the dB level in 0x98 and has no 0x58.
- **The modulation-limit range in the text is narrower than the register.** The text says 96.1% to 98.2%. Register 0x10 gives a maximum of FULL−2 to FULL−20 of 512, which is 99.6% to 96.1%, and the default (FULL−9) is 98.2%.
- **Register 0x96 has the same title as 0x95.** Both are headed "PWM Outflip register"; 0x96 holds noise-shaper, dither and 768 kHz controls.
- **The SPEQ4 and SPEQ5 attack and release fields carry the SPEQ3 name** in the register table (0x5D and 0x5E).
- **The loudness gain default is outside its own range.** Each gain defaults to 0x40000000, but the same row gives 0x08000000 as 0 dB and 0x0FFFFFFF as the maximum.
- **The SCLK minimum excludes some of the formats described.** The serial audio table gives a minimum SCLK of 2.048 MHz, but the 32 × fs formats (Figures 8, 11 and 14) give 1.024 to 1.536 MHz at 32 to 48 kHz, and 48 × fs at 32 kHz gives 1.536 MHz.
- **Register 0x82 and register 0x02 disagree on bit 7.** The 0x82 table says an SCLK-stop error shows in bit 7 of 0x02, which the 0x02 table gives to the EQ checksum error.
- **The address-change procedure refers to registers 0xF8 and 0xF9**, outside the register table, and gives the data as "0xF9 A5 A5A5".
- **Some units lost their symbol.** The input current limits print as "75 A" where µA is meant, and the ψJT symbol is missing from the thermal list (0.9 °C/W).
