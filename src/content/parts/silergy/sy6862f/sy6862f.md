## How it works

The SY6862F joins a USB Type-C connector's VBUS to two supplies. One is a high-voltage path from VCHG (4.5 to 23 V), the other a 5 V path from V5V. Each path is a pair of back-to-back N-channel switches, so it blocks current in both directions when it is off, and the part blocks reverse current when it is on: if the output side rises more than 50 mV above the input side, the switch turns off in about 1 µs and restarts by itself once the output is below the input again. The high-voltage path works in both directions. Set as a sink, it brings power from the port to VCHG; set as a source, it sends power from VCHG to the port. The 5 V path is a source path to VBUS.

A USB PD controller drives everything over I2C. Control register 1 chooses the active path (CH_SEL), the direction of the high-voltage path (HV_DR) and the two current limits; register 2 sets the over-current delay and the VBUS discharge; register 3 sets the over-voltage trip for the high-voltage path; and register 4 handles the CC lines. When the controller changes path, the part ramps VBUS at 2 V/ms, and the datasheet's transition drawings limit the old voltage's droop to 150 mV, with the load expected to draw under 150 mW during the transition. While a transition or a discharge is running, the BUSY bit is set and writes to registers 1 to 3 are ignored.

The CC lines are switched too. With no power on the part, CONN_CC1 and CONN_CC2 each present a 5.1 kΩ resistor to ground, so a charger sees a sink and puts 5 V on VBUS. Once VBUS is above 4.5 V, the high-voltage path turns on by itself, which powers the system (the dead-battery wake-up). After the system has started, the PD controller can join its own CC pins to the connector's with CC1_BPS or CC2_BPS (an on-resistance of 25 Ω typical), or feed VCONN from VBAT to an e-marked cable with VCONN1 or VCONN2. The VCONN switch is current limited at 660 mA.

Fast role swap lets a sink become a source within 150 µs when the charger is unplugged. With the high-voltage path sinking and CC_FRS = 0, the part watches for the CC line to be pulled below 0.52 V for 30 µs; with CC_FRS = 1 it watches for VBUS to fall to 40 mV below V5V instead. It then turns off the high-voltage path, turns on the 5 V path, and pulls VBUS above 4.75 V within 100 µs. It sets the FRS status bit and pulls ALERT_L low.

Protection flags set bits in the status register and pull ALERT_L low until the register is read: over-current on either path, reverse blocking, over-voltage, fast role swap, thermal shutdown, and over-voltage or over-current on VBAT and VCONN. On the high-voltage path, an over-current that lasts longer than the delay in register 2 turns the switch off. The 5 V path and VCONN limit current instead of tripping, and if the die reaches 150 °C the part shuts down.

### Supply capacitors and surge protection

The datasheet asks for a 10 µF or larger ceramic on VCHG, close to the pin, and a 1 µF or larger ceramic on V5V and on VBUS. A short on VBUS makes the input ring, and without these capacitors the transient can exceed the absolute maximum and damage the part. The reference circuit uses 10 µF on all three. The USB PD 3.0 limit for the sink bulk capacitance on VBUS is 100 µF, which the datasheet mentions so that the charge for voltage transitions is accounted for.

VBUS, CONN_CC1 and CONN_CC2 reach the outside world, so the layout guide says a TVS on each is necessary. The VBUS diode, SYS12V20SLC, is a 20 V part. The datasheet also recommends a Schottky diode on VBUS to clamp the negative voltage that cable inductance and the output capacitor can produce in a high-voltage transient; the reference circuit doesn't show one.

### CC lines

USB PD allows 200 to 600 pF of receiver capacitance on each CC line, so the datasheet recommends a 330 pF ceramic on each. With CCx_BPS = 0 and VCONNx = 0, CONN_CCx shows an Rd resistor. With CCx_BPS = 1 it connects to HOST_CCx. With VCONNx = 1 it connects to VBAT, whatever CCx_BPS says. Before joining a CC line to the host, remove the host's own exposed Rd, which the PD controller should present again after the swap.

### I2C and interrupt wiring

The pull-ups are 10 kΩ on SDA and SCL and 100 kΩ on ALERT_L, which is an open-drain output. The datasheet suggests an optional 100 Ω resistor in series with each of SDA and SCL, close to the part, for signal integrity. The address comes from ADDR (see the I2C address table above); with 0 Ω to ground the address is 1000000. A STOP must go before each START, and the register address increments automatically during multi-byte writes and reads.

### Choosing the over-voltage setting

OVP_SET defaults to 000, a 6 V trip for a 5 V profile. Before the charger is moved to a higher voltage, the PD controller has to write the matching OVP_SET value; the default 6 V trip would stop the path. The table above gives the trip range for each setting. The typical trip sits 18% to 26% above the profile voltage, and the minimum trip is between 12% and 19% above it. The 20 V profile's trip, 22.5 V minimum and 23.7 V typical, sits just above the 23 V top of the recommended VCHG range. The text says the high-voltage path turns off when either VCHG or VBUS exceeds the OVP_SET value.

### Current limit and path loss

Use the typical limits in the table as a guide, not a minimum. For the high-voltage path, the 5.5 A setting has a 5 A minimum, so a 5 A load has no margin at the low end of the tolerance. The 5 V path limit also changes with temperature: at 25 °C it is higher than the typical value at 90 °C by about 0.15 to 0.2 A.

The pass-switch loss at each limit is calculated here from the datasheet's on-resistance. The on-resistance is measured at 25 °C and rises with temperature.

| Path and current | Voltage drop | Loss | Temperature rise at 45 °C/W |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| High-voltage, 3 A (typical / max RDS(on)) | 81 mV / 99 mV | 0.24 W / 0.30 W | 11 °C / 13 °C |
| High-voltage, 5 A | 135 mV / 165 mV | 0.68 W / 0.83 W | 30 °C / 37 °C |

The 45 °C/W is the datasheet's θJA on a JEDEC four-layer board, so a real board will differ. At 5 A and the 5.5 A limit, the loss is already above half a watt, so the layout's copper area matters.

### Discharge

With SDSG = 1 the part discharges VBUS automatically in source mode when the path shuts down for any reason (under-voltage, channel shutdown, over-current, over-voltage or thermal). With SDSG = 0 the discharge switch follows the FDSG bit. The resistor is 200, 400, 800 or 1600 Ω and the timer is 50, 100, 200 or 400 ms. The default is 200 Ω and 200 ms. The table above shows how long a plain RC discharge to 0.8 V takes. With the 100 µF PD limit and 200 Ω the discharge needs about 64 ms from 20 V, which is longer than the 50 ms setting, and the largest resistor with 100 µF doesn't finish in 400 ms from 20 V. The power in the discharge resistor at 20 V and 200 Ω is 2 W at the start, dropping as the voltage falls.

## Layout

The datasheet gives these rules, plus a suggested placement drawing (Figure 9).

- Keep all **VBUS traces** as short and wide as possible, on at least 2-ounce copper.
- Put the **output capacitor** as close to the connector as possible, to lower the impedance between the port and the capacitor.
- Put the **input and output capacitors** close to the device and connect them to the ground plane.
- Add a **TVS to each of VBUS, CONN_CC1 and CONN_CC2**, because those pins are exposed.

## Things to watch

- **The I2C speed is stated two ways.** The text says the interface runs up to 400 kHz ("Fast-Mode"), while the electrical table gives 1 MHz as the maximum operating frequency, marked guaranteed by design.
- **Two OVP values differ between the register description and the electrical table.** For OVP_SET = 010 the register text says 11.1 V typical, the table says 11.3 V (10.7 to 11.8 V); for 011, 12.1 V against 12.3 V (11.7 to 12.9 V). The other settings agree.
- **The address table refers to a VCC pin that doesn't exist.** "ADDR tie to VCC" gives address 1000011, but the pin list has no VCC pin. The reference circuit uses 0 Ω to ground, so use that one unless Silergy says which supply was meant.
- **The FRS test conditions mention "HV_ER".** The register bit that sets the direction is HV_DR, so this is probably a typo.
- **The parts list leaves out D2, D3 and the K1 and K2 jumpers.** The TVS part SYS02H24DWD appears only in the schematic. R6 has no part number.
- **A Schottky diode is recommended in the text but not drawn.** It's for negative VBUS spikes after a high-voltage transient.
- **The 20 V profile's over-voltage trip is above the recommended VCHG range.** The trip is 22.5 to 24.9 V, and VCHG's recommended maximum is 23 V, so the setting gives little headroom on VCHG. See the over-voltage section above.
- **VBAT is specified at 2.5 V and at 4 V.** The electrical table lists VBAT from 2.5 V, the recommended operating conditions say 4 V.

## Datasheet notes

- The VBAT over-voltage threshold is labelled VOVP_CC in the electrical table, though the row names VBAT.
- The datasheet only gives maximum values for the under-voltage thresholds (4.4 V, 3.9 V, 3.9 V and 2.45 V), not typical ones.
- The 5 V path current limits in the register description (1.25, 1.75, 2.25, 3.3 A) are the typical values at 90 °C in the electrical table; at 25 °C they are higher (1.4, 1.9, 2.4, 3.5 A).
- The over-temperature text says the part restarts below 130 °C, while the thermal-shutdown hysteresis in the table is 15 °C from 150 °C, which would be 135 °C. The text and the table are two different figures for the same restart point.
