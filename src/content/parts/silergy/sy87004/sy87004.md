## How it works

The SY87004 is a synchronous boost converter built for one lithium cell. A cell runs from 4.2 V full down to about 3 V, and a load that needs, say, 3.4 V or more can't use the bottom of that range directly. Where a plain boost converter would switch all the time, this one sits in a bypass mode for as long as the cell is high enough, and boosts only when it isn't.

**Two modes, one setting.** The VOUT register sets the output voltage from 2.85 to 5.5 V in 50 mV steps, 3.4 V by default, and it is also the level at which the part changes mode. With the input above that setting, a 14 mΩ (typical) bypass FET connects the input to the output, which then follows the input minus a small drop and is not regulated. With the input below it, the converter boosts and holds the output at the setting. The change is automatic (AUTO_BYPASS bits 10 or 11 in CONFIG, the default), and CONFIG can also force bypass, with or without a lower quiescent current. The datasheet gives no hysteresis or switching point other than "above" and "below" the target (our reading).

**The boost stage.** It uses adaptive constant on-time control at a typical 2.1 MHz, with a 17 mΩ low-side FET and a 17 mΩ synchronous FET. At light load it drops into PFM, where the switching frequency falls with the load; setting MODE_CTRL forces PWM instead. Spread spectrum moves the frequency ±15% on a triangular wave with a 100 µs period (about 10 kHz), and a ripple-cancellation bit removes the 10 kHz ripple that this causes on the output.

**Current limits.** In boost mode the inductor's valley current is limited, cycle by cycle, to a value set in ILIMSET: 4 to 9 A in 0.5 A steps, 7 A typical (5.5 to 8.5 A over temperature) at the default. The bypass FET has a separate limit, 9 A typical at 3.6 V in, with no setting for it. A flag in STATUS records each limit event.

**Start-up.** After EN goes high with the input above its 1.65 V UVLO, the part charges the output linearly from the input, first at 1 A for 800 µs (LIN1) and then at 2 A for 1600 µs (LIN2), until the output passes 0.9 × VIN. If it hasn't by then, the part goes into fault mode. Otherwise it soft-starts to the VOUT setting, and if that takes more than 2 ms it also goes to fault. The SOFT-START bit in ILIMSET (set by default) holds the boost soft-start to a 2 A valley current. The datasheet doesn't say how the part leaves fault mode.

**Enable.** EN must be high, and it must not be left floating. With EN high, the DEV_EN bit (CONFIG bit 6, set by default) also has to be 1 for the output to be on. With EN low the output is off, and the part draws 0.1 µA typical (3 µA maximum) and disconnects the load.

**Discharge.** An internal pull-down (75 Ω typical, 110 Ω maximum) discharges the output when the part is off, if the VOUT_DISCHG bit is 1 (the default), and also when the output goes over 120% of its target. The datasheet's table and its register text disagree about what happens with the bit at 0; see the notes at the end.

**Protection.** Input over-voltage at 5.8 V typical stops switching and sets the fault flag. An output below 88% of the input shuts the part down. Thermal shutdown is at 150 °C with 20 °C hysteresis, and the part restarts with a full soft-start. The FAULT flag covers thermal, over-current, start-up failure, under-voltage and input over-voltage events.

**The variant with an A.** Silergy also sells the [SY87004A](site:parts/silergy/sy87004a/), with its own datasheet. It has the same pins, package and registers, but different defaults: a 5.05 V output and a 5.5 A valley limit, against 3.4 V and 7 A here, a 2.2 MHz clock, and device ID 0x11 against 0x10 here. See the About the source note below.

### Output voltage and mode change

The VOUT register holds the output setting in bits 5:0: 2.85 V plus 50 mV per count, so the default 3.4 V is code 001011 and 5.0 V is 101011. The table *Output voltage register words* above lists the whole register byte, with VC_GM left at its default 01 (so the byte is 0x40 plus the code; the default 0x4B is 3.4 V). Codes 110101 to 111111 all give 5.5 V.

Because the same setting is the point where the part changes from bypass to boost, it decides two things at once: the lowest output the load will see, and how much of the cell's range is spent in bypass. The 3.4 V default suits a load that needs more than about 3.3 V. Set it at the lowest voltage the load needs: a higher setting makes the converter work, and waste power, over more of the cell's range.

**In bypass the output is not regulated.** It follows the cell, so a load that can't take the full cell voltage must not sit on this output without something else limiting it. The output is only held at the setting while the input is below it (our reading).

### Inductor (L1)

The datasheet's rule is a ripple of about 40% of the maximum average input current: L = (VIN ÷ VOUT)² × (VOUT − VIN) ÷ (fSW × IOUT_MAX × 40%). For the 3.4 V default the worst case is at a 2.27 V input (two thirds of VOUT), and the result is 0.60 µH at 1 A of output, 0.30 µH at 2 A and 0.20 µH at 3 A (calculated, ideal efficiency). The 0.47 µH in Figure 1 therefore suits about 1.3 A on that rule, and gives 23% of the input current as ripple at 2 A and 15% at 3 A from 2.0 to 2.5 V in (calculated, assuming 92% efficiency). The datasheet says it tolerates different ripple. The inductor's saturation current must exceed the peak, and the datasheet asks for a DCR below 30 mΩ.

The valley limit, not the average, decides how much the converter can deliver, and the tables *Load the valley limit allows* above show it for the 0.47 µH inductor (calculated, with an assumed efficiency): at a 3.4 V output the 5.5 A minimum limit allows about 3.1 A at 2.0 V in and 4.9 A at 3.3 V in, and at the typical 7 A about 3.9 A and 6.2 A. The datasheet gives no output-current rating in boost mode, only the 6 A for bypass.

### Capacitors

**Input.** The text recommends a 22 µF X5R-or-better ceramic as close as possible to VIN and PGND, while the pin table asks for at least 4.7 µF. Figure 1 adds a 100 µF capacitor for bulk, drawn polarised. The bulk capacitor matters at the low end of a cell, when the input current is high: at 2.5 V in and a 3.4 V, 3 A output the average input current is about 4.7 A (calculated, below).

**Output.** Four 10 µF X5R-or-better ceramics in parallel, rated 6.3 V; the text asks for more than 10 µF in total, and the pin table for at least 4 × 10 µF. On a 5.5 V output a 6.3 V part keeps little margin, and its capacitance falls with DC bias: check the bias curve of the part you choose (our note, not the datasheet's). The output capacitance also sets how much the start-up current limits can charge. The linear start-up charges the output with 1 A for 800 µs and 2 A for 1600 µs, which is 4 mC; that's about 1 mF at 4.2 V in, with no load (calculated). Four 10 µF is far inside that, and charges from 0 to 0.9 × 4.2 V in about 150 µs at 1 A.

### Enable, discharge and the 1 MΩ resistor

EN is high-active and must not be driven open. If the chip driving EN floats in shutdown while VIN comes straight from a battery, add 1 MΩ from EN to PGND (in the datasheet's layout guide, not in Figure 1) so noise can't turn the part on.

The VOUT_DISCHG bit (CONFIG bit 3, set by default) lets the internal pull-down discharge the output when the part is off, whether EN is low or DEV_EN is 0. At 75 Ω typical it draws 45 mA from a 3.4 V output at first, and with 40 µF on the output the time constant is about 3 ms (calculated, ignoring capacitor derating). The datasheet gives no discharge time.

### Worked example: a 3.4 V rail from a lithium cell, at the defaults

A load that needs 3.4 V or more at up to 2 A, powered from a cell that runs from 4.2 V down to 2.5 V. At its defaults the part needs no I2C traffic at all: it is set to 3.4 V, with automatic bypass and a 7 A limit. The efficiencies are read from the datasheet's Figure 2 at 3.4 V out, by eye (94.3%, 91.9% and 90.5% at 2 A for 3.0, 2.7 and 2.5 V in), and the bypass FET is taken at its 14 to 22 mΩ. Everything below is calculated.

| Cell | Mode | Output | Input current | Loss |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 4.2 V | Bypass | 4.16 to 4.17 V | 2.0 A | 56 to 88 mW |
| 3.7 V | Bypass | 3.66 to 3.67 V | 2.0 A | 56 to 88 mW |
| 3.5 V | Bypass | 3.46 to 3.47 V | 2.0 A | 56 to 88 mW |
| 3.0 V | Boost, 3.4 V out | 3.4 V | 2.40 A (ripple 0.36 A, valley 2.22 A, peak 2.58 A) | 0.41 W |
| 2.7 V | Boost, 3.4 V out | 3.4 V | 2.74 A (ripple 0.56 A, valley 2.46 A, peak 3.02 A) | 0.60 W |
| 2.5 V | Boost, 3.4 V out | 3.4 V | 3.01 A (ripple 0.67 A, valley 2.67 A, peak 3.34 A) | 0.71 W |

**The load must tolerate the whole cell range.** At full charge the output is 4.2 V, not 3.4 V. That is the design intent of a bypass boost, and the reason to choose the setting by the lowest voltage the load accepts, not its nominal voltage.

**The current limit is not the constraint at 2 A.** The valley current stays below 2.7 A, well under the 5.5 A minimum valley limit. At 3 A and 2.5 V in, the right-hand edge of the datasheet's plot, the efficiency reads 86%, so the input current is about 4.7 A with a valley of 4.4 A, which still clears the 5.5 A minimum by about 1 A. Further down the cell the margin goes: the table *Load the valley limit allows at a 3.4 V output* above gives about 3.1 A at 2.0 V in on the minimum limit.

**Heat is the constraint.** At 2 A the boost loses 0.4 to 0.7 W in total. At 3 A and 2.5 V in it loses about 1.7 W, close to the 2 W the datasheet gives for power dissipation at 25 °C. At its 50 °C/W that would raise the die 83 °C above the board if all of it were lost in the part (the inductor and the board take some), which on a 40 °C board is 123 °C, just under the 125 °C recommended junction limit. A low cell at full load on a warm board is the case to check (calculated).

## Layout

From the datasheet's layout guide:

- **CIN, COUT and L close to the IC.**
- **A large PGND copper area**, a ground plane if the board allows.
- **CIN close to VIN and PGND**, with the smallest loop.
- **The smallest copper area on LX**, to avoid noise.
- **1 MΩ from EN to PGND** if the EN driver floats in shutdown and VIN is a battery.

The datasheet's Figure 6 shows a suggested layout, which we haven't redrawn. The package is a 1.65 mm square chip-scale part with a 4 × 4 ball grid, so the inductor and the capacitors sit directly beside it on the same side or just under it.

## What the datasheet shows

At 2.7 V in and a 3.4 V output unless stated, with the 0.47 µH CSAB0420-R47M: efficiency against output current from 0 to 3 A for 2.5, 2.7 and 3.0 V in (by eye, in the table *Efficiency at a 3.4 V output* above; the curves read 95.3%, 95.9% and 97.1% at 0.5 A, so the feature list's "up to 95%" is not a ceiling); the output ripple at no load and at 3 A in forced PWM (on a 20 mV per division scale; we haven't read the amplitude); start-up and shut-down from EN at a 1 A load; and short-circuit protection (the output drops, then recovers). We haven't read values off the waveforms beyond these.

## Talk to it from an ESP32-S3

This section wires an ESP32-S3 dev board to an SY87004 over I2C. The sketch scans the bus, reads and writes the five registers, and lets you name them. It is built from the [Arduino interface template](site:contribute/arduino/), and, unlike most parts here, the datasheet gives everything it needs: the address, the register map and the transaction format. The answer to whether it works with that setup is yes, with the settings below. The part is a 1.9 to 5.5 V device, but its bus pins take 3.3 V logic directly. The [SY87004A page](site:parts/silergy/sy87004a/) has the same section for the variant; the sketch is the same apart from its name, but the register bytes you write differ (see the pitfalls).

The console logic was also run on a PC against a simulated SY87004 that follows the datasheet's transaction format, and the simulator flags a repeated-START read that is sent with a STOP, or a write with other than one data byte. ### Wiring

![ESP32-S3 dev board wired to an SY87004 over I2C. Our own design, not a datasheet circuit. The part is powered from its own battery supply; the ESP32-S3 only supplies the two pull-ups and the logic. EN is jumpered high so that the bus answers.](schematic:sy87004-esp32s3-i2c-wiring.svg)

| Signal | ESP32-S3 | SY87004 pin |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ground | GND | D2, D3, D4 (PGND) |
| SCL | GPIO9 | B1 |
| SDA | GPIO8 | C1 |
| ADDR | none | D1. To ground for 0x75, to VIN for 0x76 (our reading), or leave open for 0x77 |
| EN | none, or a free GPIO | A1. Jumpered to VIN here, with 1 MΩ to ground |
| VIN | none | A2, A3, A4. The battery or a bench supply, 1.9 to 5.5 V |
| 3.3 V | 3V3 | Only to the two pull-ups, not to the part |

- **SCL and SDA each have a pull-up to the ESP32-S3's 3.3 V**, RPU1 and RPU2, 4.7 kΩ by our choice. The part's pins are 3.3 V tolerant (rated −0.3 to 6 V in Rev 1.0A) and its logic levels are low enough that the bus connects straight across, with no level shifter.
- **The grounds must be joined**: the battery's negative, PGND and the ESP32-S3's GND.
- **GPIO8 and GPIO9** are the default I2C pins of the ESP32S3 Dev Module package. The sketch sets them itself, so they can move to other free pins.
- **EN is jumpered high** so the bus can be tried before the sketch controls anything. The part has no reset pin; EN is the only hardware switch, so for a software-controlled enable use a free GPIO into EN, still with the 1 MΩ to ground.
- **The datasheet doesn't say whether the bus answers with EN low or DEV_EN at 0.** Keep EN high while testing.
- **The power stage** (L1, CIN, COUT) is as in the reference circuit above. Use a bench supply with a current limit, and a dummy load, for the first power-up.
- **The part's SDA and SCL are not described when VIN is off.** If the pull-ups are on and the part is not powered, test that the bus still idles high before relying on it (our note).

### Level and pull-up check

These are the checks behind connecting the bus straight across, with the ESP32-S3 figures taken at VDD = 3.3 V from the DC characteristics in the [ESP32-S3 datasheet](https://www.espressif.com/sites/default/files/documentation/esp32-s3_datasheet_en.pdf). The rise-time limits come from the I2C-bus specification, not from this datasheet, which gives no timing.

| Check | Numbers | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Voltage | The part's SCL, SDA and ADDR are rated −0.3 to 6 V in Rev 1.0A (Rev 1.0 gave 3.6 V). The ESP32-S3's pins are 3.3 V only | Safe to connect, in either revision. Don't pull the bus up to 5 V, because of the ESP32-S3 |
| Bus high | Pull-ups put the bus at 3.3 V. The part needs VIH of 0.9 V minimum and the ESP32-S3 needs 0.75 × VDD, about 2.5 V | Both are met |
| Bus low, pulled by the ESP32-S3 | VOL is 0.1 × VDD maximum, 0.33 V. The part needs VIL of 0.4 V maximum | Met, with 70 mV to spare |
| Bus low, pulled by the part | The datasheet gives no SDA output-low voltage or sink current. The ESP32-S3 needs VIL of 0.25 × VDD maximum, about 0.83 V | Can't be checked from the datasheet |
| Pull-up value | At 100 kHz (Standard-mode) the I2C-bus specification allows 1000 ns of rise time; at 400 kHz, 300 ns. Rise time is about 0.85 × R × C | 4.7 kΩ allows about 250 pF of bus capacitance at 100 kHz and 75 pF at 400 kHz; 2.2 kΩ about 540 pF and 160 pF; 10 kΩ about 120 pF and 35 pF. | Pull-up current | 3.3 V across 4.7 kΩ is 0.7 mA, and across 2.2 kΩ 1.5 mA | The part's sink capability isn't given, so we keep to 4.7 kΩ |

The 70 mV of margin on the bus-low level is small. It leaves little room for ground offset, so keep the ground wire between the board and the part short and thick, and don't add series resistance in SDA or SCL.

### The sketch

Paste it into a new sketch, choose the board *ESP32S3 Dev Module* (or your own ESP32-S3 board), and open the Serial Monitor at 115200 baud with line ending *Newline*. The first block is the only one to edit.

```cpp
/*
  SY87004 register console for an ESP32-S3, over I2C.

  Status: compiles for the ESP32-S3 (arduino-esp32 3.3.12). Not yet run against
  a real device.
  Wiring: see the table and drawing above. The bus is 3.3 V, so SDA and SCL
  connect straight across, each with its own pull-up to 3.3 V.

  Tools > Board: ESP32S3 Dev Module (or your board). Serial Monitor: 115200
  baud, line ending Newline.
*/
#include <Wire.h>

// ===== 1. DEVICE CONFIG: edit this block ==================================

// 7-bit I2C address from the datasheet: 0x75 with ADDR low, 0x76 with ADDR high,
// 0x77 with ADDR floating. This sketch assumes ADDR is tied to ground. If it
// is not, use 'scan', or 'addr 0x76'.
const uint8_t  DEVICE_ADDR = 0x75;

// Register width on the bus: eight-bit registers, one byte each.
const uint8_t  WORD_BYTES  = 1;

// Highest register number: the datasheet's map ends at STATUS, 0x04. Anything
// above that is not documented, so the console refuses it.
const uint16_t REG_MAX     = 0x04;

// I2C clock. The datasheet gives 100 to 1000 kHz; start at 100 kHz.
const uint32_t BUS_HZ      = 100000;

// ESP32-S3 pins for SDA and SCL: the board package's default I2C pins. Any
// free GPIO works.
const int8_t   I2C_SDA_PIN = 8;
const int8_t   I2C_SCL_PIN = 9;

// The part has no reset pin (EN switches the converter off), so there is
// nothing for the 'reset' command to pulse. -1 means none.
const int8_t   RESET_PIN   = -1;

// Wait after power-up or reset. The datasheet gives no time for the I2C
// interface to become ready; the converter's own start-up takes at most about
// 4.4 ms.
const uint16_t BOOT_MS     = 100;

// Registers the 't' command reads. Reading STATUS clears its flags.
struct NamedReg { const char *name; uint8_t reg; };
const NamedReg REGS[] = {
  {"DEVICE_ID", 0x00}, {"CONFIG", 0x01}, {"VOUT", 0x02},
  {"ILIMSET", 0x03}, {"STATUS", 0x04},
};

bool parseNum(const char *s, uint32_t &out);
void printHex(uint32_t v, uint8_t digits);

// ===== 2. BUS LAYER: I2C (Wire library) ===================================
// A register read is a "random read": write the register address, then a
// repeated START and read WORD_BYTES bytes, most significant byte first.
// The address pointer is set before every read, so nothing depends on where
// an earlier access left it. On the ESP32 the Wire library sends the address
// write and the read as one transaction, with the repeated START between them.

// Changed at run time by the 'addr' and 'scan' commands.
uint8_t devAddr = DEVICE_ADDR;
// Wire's endTransmission() code, kept for messages.
static uint8_t lastErr = 0;

bool busBegin() {
  if (!Wire.begin(I2C_SDA_PIN, I2C_SCL_PIN, BUS_HZ)) return false;
  // Give up after 25 ms instead of the default 50 ms if the bus is stuck.
  Wire.setTimeOut(25);
  return true;
}

bool busReady() { return devAddr != 0; }

bool devRead(uint16_t reg, uint32_t &value) {
  Wire.beginTransmission(devAddr);
  Wire.write((uint8_t)reg);
  lastErr = Wire.endTransmission(false);   // false: repeated START, no STOP
  if (lastErr) return false;
  if (Wire.requestFrom(devAddr, (size_t)WORD_BYTES) != WORD_BYTES) {
    lastErr = 4;
    return false;
  }
  value = 0;
  for (uint8_t i = 0; i < WORD_BYTES; i++) {
    value = (value << 8) | (uint8_t)Wire.read();
  }
  return true;
}

bool devWrite(uint16_t reg, uint32_t value) {
  Wire.beginTransmission(devAddr);
  Wire.write((uint8_t)reg);
  for (int8_t i = WORD_BYTES - 1; i >= 0; i--) {
    Wire.write((uint8_t)(value >> (8 * i)));
  }
  lastErr = Wire.endTransmission();        // the STOP starts the write
  return lastErr == 0;
}

const __FlashStringHelper *busError() {
  switch (lastErr) {
    case 2:  return F("no ACK on the address: nothing answered there");
    case 3:  return F("no ACK on a data byte");
    case 4:  return F("no data back: no ACK on the address, or SDA or SCL stuck?");
    case 5:  return F("bus timeout: SDA or SCL stuck low?");
    default: return F("I2C error");
  }
}

// I2C-only console commands. 'scan' lists the addresses that answer and
// 'addr 0x..' sets the address.
bool busCommand(const char *cmd, const char *arg) {
  uint32_t n;
  if (!strcmp(cmd, "scan")) {
    uint8_t found = 0, first = 0;
    for (uint8_t a = 0x08; a < 0x78; a++) {
      Wire.beginTransmission(a);
      if (Wire.endTransmission() == 0) {
        Serial.print(F("  ACK at 0x")); printHex(a, 2); Serial.println();
        if (!found++) first = a;
      }
    }
    if (found == 1) {
      devAddr = first;
      Serial.println(F("One device found; using it."));
    } else if (!found) {
      Serial.println(F("Nothing answered. Check power, wiring, pull-ups."));
    }
    return true;
  }
  if (!strcmp(cmd, "addr")) {
    if (arg && parseNum(arg, n) && n > 0 && n < 0x78) devAddr = n;
    Serial.print(F("device address 0x")); printHex(devAddr, 2); Serial.println();
    return true;
  }
  return false;
}

// ===== 3. CONSOLE (the same for every bus) ================================
// Serial Monitor at 115200 baud, line ending "Newline". Commands:
//   help | scan | addr 0x2A | r <reg> | w <reg> <value> | d <first> <count>
//   | t | reset
// Numbers can be decimal or 0x hex.

bool parseNum(const char *s, uint32_t &out) {
  char *end;
  out = strtoul(s, &end, 0);
  return end != s && *end == 0;
}

void printHex(uint32_t v, uint8_t digits) {
  for (int8_t i = digits - 1; i >= 0; i--) {
    Serial.print((uint8_t)((v >> (4 * i)) & 0xF), HEX);
  }
}

void printWord(uint32_t v) {
  const uint8_t bits = WORD_BYTES * 8;
  int32_t s = (int32_t)v;
  if (bits < 32 && (v & (1UL << (bits - 1)))) {
    s = (int32_t)(v | (0xFFFFFFFFUL << bits));      // sign-extend
  }
  Serial.print(F("0x")); printHex(v, WORD_BYTES * 2);
  Serial.print(F("  unsigned ")); Serial.print(v);
  Serial.print(F("  signed ")); Serial.println(s);
}

void deviceReset() {
  if (RESET_PIN < 0) {
    Serial.println(F("RESET_PIN is not set."));
    return;
  }
  // Only ever pulled LOW. The 3.3 V pull-up on the part makes it high.
  pinMode(RESET_PIN, OUTPUT);
  digitalWrite(RESET_PIN, LOW);
  delay(2);
  pinMode(RESET_PIN, INPUT);
  delay(BOOT_MS);
  Serial.println(F("Reset done."));
}

void showRead(uint16_t reg) {
  uint32_t v;
  if (devRead(reg, v)) printWord(v);
  else Serial.println(busError());
}

void help() {
  Serial.println(F("r <reg>            read one register"));
  Serial.println(F("w <reg> <value>    write one register (this can change"));
  Serial.println(F("                   the device's setup: be careful)"));
  Serial.println(F("d <first> <count>  read a run of registers"));
  Serial.println(F("t                  read the named registers in REGS[]"));
  Serial.println(F("scan / addr <a>    I2C only: find the device / set address"));
  Serial.println(F("reset              pulse the reset pin, if RESET_PIN is set"));
}

void handle(char *ln) {
  char *cmd = strtok(ln, " \t");
  char *a1 = strtok(NULL, " \t");
  char *a2 = strtok(NULL, " \t");
  uint32_t reg, val;
  if (!cmd) return;
  if (busCommand(cmd, a1)) return;
  if (!strcmp(cmd, "help") || !strcmp(cmd, "?")) { help(); return; }
  if (!strcmp(cmd, "reset")) { deviceReset(); return; }
  if (!busReady()) {
    Serial.println(F("No device address yet: type 'scan' or 'addr 0x..'."));
    return;
  }
  if (a1 && parseNum(a1, reg) && reg > REG_MAX) {
    Serial.println(F("Register number is above REG_MAX."));
    return;
  }
  if (!strcmp(cmd, "r") && a1 && parseNum(a1, reg)) {
    showRead(reg);
  } else if (!strcmp(cmd, "w") && a1 && a2 &&
             parseNum(a1, reg) && parseNum(a2, val)) {
    if (devWrite(reg, val)) Serial.println(F("written"));
    else Serial.println(busError());
  } else if (!strcmp(cmd, "d") && a1 && a2 &&
             parseNum(a1, reg) && parseNum(a2, val)) {
    for (uint32_t i = 0; i < val && reg + i <= REG_MAX; i++) {
      Serial.print(F("[0x")); printHex(reg + i, 2); Serial.print(F("]  "));
      showRead(reg + i);
    }
  } else if (!strcmp(cmd, "t")) {
    for (uint8_t i = 0; i < sizeof(REGS) / sizeof(REGS[0]); i++) {
      Serial.print(REGS[i].name);
      Serial.print(F("  [0x")); printHex(REGS[i].reg, 2); Serial.print(F("]  "));
      showRead(REGS[i].reg);
    }
  } else {
    Serial.println(F("?  (type help)"));
  }
}

char lineBuf[40];
uint8_t lineLen = 0;

void setup() {
  Serial.begin(115200);
  // On the native USB port, text sent before the Serial Monitor connects is
  // lost, so wait up to 3 s for it. A UART port is ready at once.
  for (uint32_t t0 = millis(); !Serial && millis() - t0 < 3000; ) delay(10);
  // High-impedance until a reset is asked for.
  if (RESET_PIN >= 0) pinMode(RESET_PIN, INPUT);
  delay(BOOT_MS);                     // let the device finish starting up
  if (!busBegin()) Serial.println(F("The bus did not start. Check the pin numbers."));
  Serial.println(F("Register console ready. Type help."));
  if (!busReady()) Serial.println(F("Type scan to find the device."));
}

void loop() {
  while (Serial.available()) {
    char c = Serial.read();
    if (c == '\n' || c == '\r') {
      if (lineLen) {
        lineBuf[lineLen] = 0;
        handle(lineBuf);
        lineLen = 0;
      }
    } else if (lineLen < sizeof(lineBuf) - 1) {
      lineBuf[lineLen++] = c;
    }
  }
}
```

### Using it

1. Build the wiring with the power off. Join the grounds, jumper EN to VIN, and connect a bench supply set to about 3.0 V with a current limit of 1 A or so, and a dummy load (a resistor, or nothing at first).
2. Power up. With the VOUT setting at its default 3.4 V and a 3.0 V input, the part boosts, so expect about 3.4 V on VOUT. At a 3.7 V input it would be in bypass instead, with VOUT close to 3.7 V.
3. Upload the sketch and open the Serial Monitor.
4. Type `scan`. With ADDR tied to ground, one device answers at 0x75, and the sketch uses it. If you left ADDR open it will be 0x77, and with ADDR high 0x76. Put the address in `DEVICE_ADDR` so it is there next time.
5. Type `t`. A part that has just powered up reads DEVICE_ID 0x10, CONFIG 0x68, VOUT 0x4B and ILIMSET 0x16 (the datasheet's defaults). STATUS shows the live state, and reading it clears its flags. Its bit 4 (OPMODE) is 1 in boost and 0 in bypass.
6. To set the output, write the VOUT register with a byte from the table *Output voltage register words* above, for example `w 2 0x4F` for 3.6 V. The output changes at once. CONFIG and ILIMSET words are in the second table.
7. Be careful with `w`. `w 1 0x28` turns the output off, `w 1 0xE8` resets every register and cycles the output, and setting ILIM_OFF in ILIMSET (`w 3 0x36`) disables the valley current limit.

If `scan` finds nothing, check these in order:

- EN must be high, and VIN must be between 1.9 and 5.5 V.
- SDA and SCL need a pull-up each to 3.3 V, and the grounds must be joined.
- SCL goes to B1 and SDA to C1. They are easy to swap on a chip-scale part.
- A message of "bus timeout" means SDA or SCL is stuck low. Look for a short, or for a missing ground connection.
- If the address is right but reads fail with "no data back", see the first pitfall below.

### Pitfalls with the ESP32-S3

- **Two USB ports.** Many ESP32-S3 dev boards have two. The one marked UART goes through a USB-to-serial chip and works with the default settings. The one marked USB is the chip's own port: set *USB CDC On Boot* to *Enabled* in the Tools menu to make `Serial` use it. The sketch waits up to 3 s for the Serial Monitor on that port, so the first lines aren't lost.
- **The ESP32-S3 may reset when the Serial Monitor opens**, on boards where the USB-to-serial chip drives the reset line. The part doesn't reset, because it is powered separately, so it keeps its registers. A reset of the ESP32-S3 doesn't change what the part is doing.
- **Don't power the ESP32-S3 from the part's output while you test.** The registers can turn the output off or cycle it, which would reset the board in the middle of a command.
- **Pick free pins.** Avoid GPIO0, 3, 45 and 46 (boot strapping), GPIO19 and 20 (the USB port) and GPIO26 to 37 (flash and PSRAM on many modules). GPIO8 and 9 are free on the usual dev boards.
- **A missing device shows as a failed read.** The ESP32 `Wire` library sends the register address and the read as one transaction, so the sketch reports "no data back" rather than "no ACK" when the address is wrong. Use `scan` to find the address first.
- **The read uses a repeated Start, as in the datasheet's figure.** The datasheet's text says a Stop must be sent before each Start, which the figure contradicts. If a real part doesn't answer reads but does answer `scan` and writes, try changing `endTransmission(false)` in `devRead` to `endTransmission(true)`. We haven't tried this on hardware.
- **A stuck bus** would otherwise hold the library for its default 50 ms on every call. The sketch sets a 25 ms time-out, so it reports "bus timeout".
- **Start at 100 kHz.** That is the datasheet's stated lowest clock. `BUS_HZ` can go up once things work, and the datasheet allows 1 MHz, but the rise-time table above shows how little bus capacitance a 4.7 kΩ pull-up allows at 400 kHz.
- **Reading STATUS clears its flags.** The `t` and `d 0 5` commands both read it, so a limit or fault flag seen once is gone on the next read.
- **Every write goes straight to the power stage.** The sketch checks the register number but not the value, so a bad byte in VOUT changes the output at once.
- **Don't copy register bytes between this page and the [SY87004A page](site:parts/silergy/sy87004a/).** The voltage code in VOUT bits 5:0 means the same on both, but the VC_GM field above it (bits 7:6) defaults to 01 here and 11 on the A, so the whole byte differs (0x4B and 0xCB for 3.4 V). The datasheet says nothing about VC_GM beyond listing 6 to 24 µS, so write the byte from this page's table. The ILIMSET defaults differ as well (0x16 here, 0x13 there).

## Things to watch

- **In bypass the output is the cell, not the setting.** At full charge it is the cell voltage minus a few tens of millivolts, about 4.1 to 4.2 V from a full lithium cell. The VOUT setting is a floor the part boosts to, not a level it holds. Pick the load, and the setting, with the whole cell range in mind.
- **The defaults come back at every power-up.** The datasheet describes no non-volatile storage, so we read the settings as reset when the part loses power. That makes this part usable with no I2C at all: 3.4 V floor, 7 A valley limit, automatic bypass. A load that needs a different floor needs the host to write VOUT after every power-up, and until it does the output is whatever the defaults give.
- **No output-current rating in boost mode.** The 6 A is for bypass. In boost the valley limit decides (5.5 A minimum at the default), and the table *Load the valley limit allows at a 3.4 V output* above shows what that gives; the efficiency plot stops at 3 A.
- **The bypass limit has no setting.** ILIM_BYP flags it at 9 A typical, and the current limit in ILIMSET applies to boost mode only.
- **Thermal limits are tight at 6 A.** The bypass drop is 84 to 132 mV and the die rises about 40 °C at the maximum, on the JEDEC board; the table *Bypass-mode drop and heating* above. BYPASS_WAY may change this and the datasheet gives no number for it.
- **The part may not answer with EN low.** The datasheet describes DEV_EN as something software can use when EN is high, and says nothing about the bus with EN low.
- **The reset bit interrupts the output.** CONFIG bit 7 resets every register and cycles the output off and on, so the load sees a brown-out.
- **Output voltage steps are 50 mV, and 53 or more is 5.5 V.** There is no code above 5.5 V, and the absolute maximum on VOUT is 6 V.
- **Input over-voltage.** The input must stay at or below 5.5 V. At 5.8 V (typical) the part stops switching and sets the fault flag; the datasheet doesn't say what the bypass FET does then, so don't count on it to protect the load from the input.
- **Start-up needs the output to reach 0.9 × VIN in about 2.4 ms** (800 µs at 1 A, then 1600 µs at 2 A). A load that is already drawing current when the part starts takes its share of that charge, and the datasheet doesn't say how the part leaves fault mode.
- **The A variant has different defaults, so don't share register values.** The [SY87004A](site:parts/silergy/sy87004a/) boots at 5.05 V with a 5.5 A limit and reads 0x11 from DEVICE_ID; this part boots at 3.4 V with a 7 A limit and reads 0x10. A host can read DEVICE_ID to tell the two apart (our reading of the two datasheets' defaults).

## Datasheet notes

- The VOUT_DISCHG description says that with the bit at 0 the output isn't discharged when the regulator is disabled, but the datasheet's table shows discharge whenever EN is low, whatever the bit. The text for 1 says "when the regulator is enabled", which we read as a slip for disabled. This page follows the table.
- The text says "A STOP condition must be sent before each START condition", while the read figure shows a repeated Start between the address write and the read. The sketch follows the figure.
- The electrical table gives SCL's clock frequency as 100 kHz minimum and 1000 kHz maximum. A minimum is unusual for I2C, and we read it as the lowest tested clock.
- The datasheet says the interface address "can be set by RADDR", as though by a resistor. The pin table and the address list describe the pin pulled high, low or left floating, and this page follows those.
- The pin table asks for at least 4.7 µF on VIN, and the text recommends 22 µF. Figure 1 shows two input capacitors, 100 µF and 22 µF. This datasheet has no schematic page or bill of materials; the SY87004A one does.
- The features list says up to 95% efficiency, but the efficiency plot reads about 97% at 0.5 A for 3.0 V in (by eye).
- The memory map describes ILIMSET as setting the "input current valley limit", and the register text and the application text call it the inductor valley current limit. This page uses the latter.
- Features list "0.9V signaling", and the thresholds are VIH 0.9 V minimum and VIL 0.4 V maximum, so a 0.9 V signal sits exactly on the limit. Use a higher level where you can.
- VC_GM (VOUT bits 7:6) is listed as 6, 12, 18 or 24 µS with no other description. We left it at its default, 01, in every register byte on this page.
- The part is "SY87004" on the front page and "SY87004WUS" in the ordering table, with top mark HFC.
- The transaction figure's legend says "driven by SBxx", a leftover from another datasheet.
- Typos: "contant" for constant on-time, "blocs" for blocks, and "LiFeP04" for LiFePO4.
