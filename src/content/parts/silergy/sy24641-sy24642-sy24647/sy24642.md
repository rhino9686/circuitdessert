## How it works

The SY24642 is a fixed-gain differential amplifier built to sit across a shunt resistor. It measures VSENSE = VCSP − VCSN, multiplies it by 100, and adds the voltage on the REF pin:

OUT = 100 × VSENSE + VREF

Current in the shunt makes VSENSE, so the output is a voltage that follows the current, and REF decides where the output sits at zero current.

**A common-mode range that doesn't depend on the supply.** The inputs work from −0.3 V to 26 V whatever VCC is (3 V to 5.5 V). That is what lets a 3.3 V microcontroller's supply watch a shunt on a 12 V or 24 V rail (high side), lets the shunt sit in the ground return (low side, where the inputs are near 0 V and need the −0.3 V), and lets the part keep measuring when the rail is shorted. The amplifier does the translation from the shunt's common-mode voltage to a ground-referenced output.

**Zero-drift input stage.** The shunt voltage is small, 45 mV at most on this version, so the offset is what limits the low end. The datasheet gives ±50 µV maximum for the SY24642 (±1 µV typical), with 0.5 µV/°C maximum drift, and the datasheet describes the architecture as a low-offset, zero-drift one. The gain error is ±0.5% maximum and ±0.02% typical.

**Unidirectional or bidirectional.** The REF pin takes any voltage from GND to VCC. With REF at ground the output is 0 V at zero current and rises as the current flows (Figure 3 of the datasheet, and the example below). With REF at half the supply (2.5 V on a 5 V supply) the output is at mid-scale at zero current, goes up for current one way and down for current the other way (Figure 4). The linear output range is 0.05 V to VCC − 0.2 V, and anything outside it saturates.

**Three versions.** The SY24641, SY24642 and SY24647 differ in gain (50, 100 and 200 V/V). A larger gain gives a bigger output for the same shunt voltage, so it allows a smaller shunt voltage (and a lower dissipation) for the same output, and it is paid for with a smaller input range (90, 45 and 24 mV with REF at ground), lower offset (±100, ±50 and ±35 µV maximum) and lower bandwidth (60, 28 and 14 kHz). See the first design table.

### Choosing RSENSE

The shunt is set by two limits, and the smaller resistor wins.

- **Input limit.** The shunt voltage at full current must stay inside the amplifier's range: 45 mV for the SY24642 with REF at ground (and ±20 mV with REF at mid-supply). So Rmax1 = 45 mV ÷ ImaxP for a unidirectional design, and 20 mV ÷ the larger of ImaxN and ImaxP for a bidirectional one.
- **Output limit.** The output has to stay below VCC − 0.2 V (and above 0.05 V). Unidirectional: Rmax2 = (VCC − 0.2 V − VREF) ÷ (100 × ImaxP). Bidirectional: the smaller of VREF ÷ (100 × ImaxN) and (VCC − 0.2 V − VREF) ÷ (100 × ImaxP).

RSENSE,max is the smaller of Rmax1 and Rmax2, and the datasheet's recommended values (the second and third design tables) are those limits rounded down to standard values. We checked every row of the SY24642 tables against the formulas, and they agree. With a 5 V supply the input limit is the smaller one in both cases (per amp of full scale, 45 mΩ against 48 mΩ unidirectional, and 20 mΩ against 23 mΩ bidirectional). With a 3.3 V supply the output limit is the smaller one (31 mΩ against 45 mΩ unidirectional, and 14.5 mΩ against 20 mΩ bidirectional), which is why the 3.3 V column is lower.

A bigger shunt gives a bigger signal and a smaller error from the offset, and costs more dissipation (I²R) and more voltage lost in the supply rail. The fourth design table (calculated) shows the trade for the SY24642: a 10 mΩ shunt covers 0.1 A to 4.5 A with 0.2 W at full scale, and a 1 mΩ shunt covers 1 A to 45 A with 2 W.

### REF pin

- **Unidirectional.** Tie REF to ground. The output then clamps near ground for small currents (it is specified to reach GND + 5 mV typical and GND + 50 mV maximum), and the datasheet gives the usable range as starting at 1 mV across the shunt. If you need to see very low currents, the text says to bias REF to a convenient value above 50 mV.
- **Bidirectional.** Connect REF to a reference voltage, for example half of VCC (Figure 4 uses a 2.5 V source). The output rises above REF for positive current and falls below it for negative current.
- **Drive REF from a low impedance.** Like any differential amplifier, the part's CMRR gets worse with impedance at REF. Connected straight to a reference or supply it is not a problem. Behind a resistor divider, the REF pin has to be buffered by an op-amp (Figure 5). The datasheet gives no input resistance or current for REF, so it can't be sized from the table (our note).
- **Divider without a buffer.** If the ADC is differential, or two single-ended ADC channels are used, measure OUT − REF directly instead (Figure 6), and the divider can drive REF without a buffer, since the impedance error is common to both.

### Input filtering and the gain error

An RC filter on the inputs (Figure 7) can reduce noise on the measured rail. The datasheet recommends series resistors RF below 10 Ω, because the two input bias currents differ and the mismatch creates an error voltage across the filter resistors, which reduces the gain. For the SY24642:

Gain error = (10000 ÷ (6.88 × RF + 10000) − 1) × 100%

With RF = 10 Ω it is −0.68% (the datasheet's figure; −0.63% for the SY24641 and −0.78% for the SY24647). With 5 Ω it would be −0.34%, and with 1 Ω −0.07% (calculated here from the datasheet's formula). The same resistor in both inputs also forms the filter with CF, and with no filter the error is zero.

### Supply bypass

The datasheet says a bypass capacitor is required for stability and recommends a 0.1 µF MLCC as close to VCC and GND as possible. The bill of materials has a 100 nF, 50 V, X7R in 0603.

### Worked example: the datasheet circuit, 0 to 4.5 A

These numbers are calculated here from the datasheet's values, and nothing was measured. The circuit is the one in the application schematic: SY24642 (gain 100), RSENSE = 10 mΩ, VCC = 5 V, REF = ground.

- **Range.** Input limit 45 mV ÷ 10 mΩ = 4.5 A. Output limit (5 − 0.2) V ÷ (100 × 10 mΩ) = 4.8 A. So the range is set by the input, 4.5 A, with 4.5 V on OUT. The lowest current in the datasheet's range (1 mV) is 100 mA.
- **Transfer.** OUT = 100 × 10 mΩ × I = 1 V per amp.
- **Dissipation.** 0.2 W in the shunt at 4.5 A, well inside its 1 W rating (which would be reached at 10 A, far beyond the amplifier's range). The voltage lost in the rail at full scale is 45 mV.

The error depends on the current, because the offset is fixed and the gain error is a percentage:

| Current | VSENSE | OUT (ideal) | Offset error (50 µV) | Offset and gain error (0.5%) | With the 1% shunt tolerance |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0.1 A | 1 mV | 0.100 V | 5.0% | 5.5% | 6.5% |
| 0.25 A | 2.5 mV | 0.250 V | 2.0% | 2.5% | 3.5% |
| 0.5 A | 5 mV | 0.500 V | 1.0% | 1.5% | 2.5% |
| 1 A | 10 mV | 1.000 V | 0.50% | 1.0% | 2.0% |
| 2 A | 20 mV | 2.000 V | 0.25% | 0.75% | 1.75% |
| 4.5 A | 45 mV | 4.500 V | 0.11% | 0.61% | 1.61% |

These are worst-case sums of the datasheet's maximum values at 25 °C. They leave out the common-mode, supply and temperature terms below, the ADC's own error and the nonlinearity (±0.01% typical), and in practice errors rarely all add in the same direction. Two things stand out: below about 0.5 A the offset matters more than the gain, and the 1% shunt (the bill of materials part) is a bigger error than the amplifier's 0.5% gain error.

**Terms left out above (our arithmetic):**

- **Temperature.** The offset limit is given at 25 °C, with a drift of 0.1 µV/°C typical and 0.5 µV/°C maximum. Over the 100 °C from 25 to 125 °C the maximum drift adds up to 50 µV, so the offset could reach ±100 µV at 125 °C, which is 10 mA at 10 mΩ. The gain drift (10 ppm/°C maximum) adds up to 0.1% over the same 100 °C, and the datasheet states the same ±0.5% maximum gain error over temperature.
- **Common mode.** The offset is specified with the common-mode at 12 V. The CMRR is 105 dB minimum over temperature, or 5.6 µV/V, so a common-mode voltage 14 V higher (26 V) can move the input offset by up to 79 µV, more than the offset limit itself. At the 125 dB typical it is 7.9 µV.
- **Supply.** The PSR of ±6 µV/V maximum means a 2.5 V change in VCC moves the offset by up to 15 µV.

**The same shunt with REF at 2.5 V (bidirectional).** The range is ±20 mV, so −2 A to +2 A, with the output from 0.5 V to 4.5 V around 2.5 V.

## Layout

The datasheet's two rules, with the layout of its Figure 8 (top layer only):

- **Use a Kelvin connection from CSP and CSN to the shunt.** With a few milliohms, the resistance of the traces between the shunt pads and the amplifier pins is large next to the shunt itself, and it adds an error that can't be ignored. A Kelvin connection (the two input traces taken from the shunt's own pads, not from the high-current trace) means only the shunt's resistance is measured. Keep the loop area formed by these two traces small.
- **Put a 0.1 µF MLCC bypass capacitor as close as possible to VCC and GND.**

In the figure, the six pins run REF, GND, VCC down the left side and OUT, CSN, CSP down the right, the capacitor sits beside pins 2 and 3 on the VCC and GND copper, and the traces from pins 4 and 5 run to the shunt's two pads.

## Things to watch

- **It is one datasheet for three versions.** Check the gain in the part number (SY24641AHT, SY24642AHT, SY24647AHT) and the top mark (nxyz, qxyz, hxyz). The ranges, the offset and the bandwidth all follow the gain. The −0.1 V common-mode lower limit applies to the SY24647 only.
- **Everything is specified at VCC = 5 V.** The supply range is 3 V to 5.5 V, but the electrical table gives the supply current, swing, bandwidth and gain error at 5 V and 25 °C only. The recommended-RSENSE tables include a 3.3 V column, so the part is intended for 3.3 V, but the datasheet gives no 3.3 V figures for the accuracy.
- **With REF at ground, small currents aren't measured.** The output clamps near ground (GND + 5 mV typical, GND + 50 mV maximum), and the datasheet's range starts at 1 mV across the shunt (100 mA at 10 mΩ). Bias REF above 50 mV, as the text says, if the low end matters.
- **The offset limit sets the low end, and it grows with common mode and temperature.** See the terms in the worked example: up to 79 µV from a 14 V common-mode change and up to 50 µV more from temperature, against a 50 µV limit at 25 °C.
- **The CMRR minimum is given over temperature only.** The table has no 25 °C minimum for it, only the 125 dB typical.
- **The inputs draw a large bias current.** 38 µA typical and up to 45 µA is large next to the 80 µA supply current (our note), and the datasheet doesn't say in which direction it flows. It is why the series resistors must stay under 10 Ω, and why a high-impedance source or a large filter resistor changes the gain.
- **The REF pin must be driven from a low impedance.** There is no REF input resistance or current in the table.
- **The output has no drive spec.** The swing is tested into 10 kΩ to ground, there is no output current or short-circuit figure, and the maximum capacitive load is 1 nF (no sustained oscillation). An ADC sampling capacitor larger than that directly on OUT needs a series resistor or a buffer (our note).
- **The bandwidth is 28 kHz and the slew rate 0.4 V/µs.** A full-scale 4.5 V output takes about 11 µs to slew (calculated here), so this is not a fast overcurrent detector, and fast current edges are filtered. The SY24641 is twice as fast, at 60 kHz.
- **The absolute maximum common-mode is 29 V, the recommended one 26 V.** The datasheet gives no surge or spike information. The differential absolute maximum is ±29 V. REF and OUT can't go above VCC.
- **Shunt tolerance is the largest error.** The bill of materials shunt is 1%, which is twice the amplifier's gain error. The datasheet gives no shunt temperature coefficient (our note).
- **The thermal figures hardly matter.** At 5 V and 100 µA the part dissipates about 0.5 mW, against the 0.31 W package rating (θJA 321 °C/W on a four-layer board).
- **The revision history has two entries for Rev 1.0.** Dec. 10, 2022 (the SY24641 production release) and Aug. 2024 (the three versions merged, with no day given). The page footers say © 2024 and the notice page says © 2023.
- **Typos.** Figure 1 labels the microcontroller "Mirco-controller", and the application text says "the output can become will saturate low".
