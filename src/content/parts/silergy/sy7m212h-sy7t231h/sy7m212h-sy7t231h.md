## How it works

The SY7M212H and SY7T231H are the same chip in two packages: a 100-pin LQFP and a 68-pin QFN. It is an electricity metering system on a chip for single-phase and dual-phase meters, built on what Silergy calls Teridian measurement technology. Three analog-to-digital converters sit at the front. Two are dedicated "staring" converters for current (ADCS, one per current input pair) and one is a multiplexed converter (ADCM) that takes the voltages. A 20 MHz compute engine (CE) multiplies the samples and accumulates energy, and shares RAM with a 32-bit ARM Cortex-M0 that runs at up to 10 MHz and handles communication, the display and housekeeping. There is 256 KB of flash, 24 KB of RAM and a small block of battery-backed NV-RAM.

For current the chip takes current transformers (CTs) or Rogowski coils straight onto IAP/IAN and IBP/IBN. For a shunt it uses up to two SY7M007 remote interface ICs, which sit on the high-voltage side, digitize the shunt voltage and send it back across a capacitor barrier. The chip supplies those remotes with pulses on RMT_DRV_H and DIO72/RMT_DRV_L through a transformer. For voltage it takes a resistor divider from each phase onto VA and VB. The datasheet's own figures of the CT and shunt arrangements are on pages 2, 3 and 32.

**The datasheet covers the hardware only.** The CE runs code that Silergy supplies as precompiled binary modules, and the datasheet sends the reader to the CE Code Reference Manual and to a Hardware Reference Manual for the registers, calibration and temperature compensation. We haven't seen either, so this page has no register map, no start-up sequence and no firmware detail.

### Power modes

The chip has five modes (page 28). The Cortex-M0 commands two of them, and the supply level triggers the other.

| Mode | When | What runs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mission (MSN) | V3P3SYS and V3P3A are in range (3.0 to 3.6 V) | Everything |
| Brownout (BRN) | V3P3SYS falls below its threshold; supplies switch to VBAT | Metrology and the Cortex-M0 keep running until firmware slows or stops them. The datasheet says BRN can last until VBAT falls below 1.8 V |
| LCD-only | Commanded by the Cortex-M0 | Only the LCD, from V3P3SYS, VBAT or an external VLCD. RAM stays powered, and up to 15 LCD images can be stepped through without the CPU |
| Sleep (SLP) | Commanded by the Cortex-M0 | Only the RTC and the non-volatile memory, powered from VBAT_RTC or VBAT. The only way out is a wake event |
| Wake events | Timer, UART0 or UART1 receive, the four wake pins, the wake comparator, temperature limits, reset | Exit from SLP |

The primary supply pins are V3P3SYS (digital) and V3P3A (analog), usually tied to the same rail but bypassed separately. V3P3D is the output of an internal switch that selects V3P3SYS or VBAT, and **the datasheet says to bypass it and never use it to drive external loads**. VDD, V3P3RTC and VLCD are bypass points. VLCD can also take an external voltage for the LCD.

### Battery options

How the two battery pins are wired decides which low-power modes work (page 9).

| VBAT | VBAT_RTC | BRN mode | SLP mode | Result |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Battery 1 | Battery 2 | Supported | Supported | RTC and NV-RAM are kept by battery 2 when battery 1 is flat and the board supply is down |
| One battery on both pins | (same battery) | Supported | Supported | BRN mode on VBAT and SLP mode on VBAT_RTC. RTC and NV-RAM are lost when the battery is flat and the board supply is down |
| V3P3SYS | Battery | No | Supported | RTC and NV-RAM kept; no BRN mode when the board supply is down |
| Battery | V3P3SYS | Supported | No | RTC and NV-RAM are lost when the board supply is down |
| V3P3SYS | V3P3SYS | No | No | No BRN mode; the datasheet calls this typical for a panel meter |

The second row is one cell spanning both columns in the datasheet, which we read as a single battery tied to both pins (our reading).

### Worked example: a 230 V, 100 A dual-phase meter

All values here are ours; the datasheet gives none. The numbers are in the "Worked example" table above, and they come from three limits in the datasheet: the voltage and current inputs take ±125 mV peak, the ADCM input impedance is 14 to 27 kΩ, and the CT mode is rated for a 5,000:1 current range.

1. **Voltage divider.** A 230 V rms phase peaks at 325 V. With six 560 kΩ resistors in series on top (3.36 MΩ, so no single resistor sees more than 38 V rms) and 1 kΩ on the bottom, VA sees about 90 to 93 mV peak (calculated), depending on where the ADCM input impedance falls in its 14 to 27 kΩ range. At +20% mains that rises to 108 to 112 mV, which is 87% to 90% of the 125 mV limit.
2. **Current input.** A 2000:1 CT carrying 100 A rms delivers 70.7 mA peak, and a 1.5 Ω burden turns that into 106 mV peak, 85% of full scale. The input reaches 125 mV at 118 A rms (calculated). The preamp, which only phase A has (gain 8, ±15.62 mV input range), is not needed here.
3. **Bottom of the range.** 5,000:1 below 100 A is 20 mA rms, which is 21.2 µV peak at the burden, or about 5,136 LSB at the 2521 Hz setting (4.13 nV per LSB). The datasheet's noise figure is 400 LSB at that setting (1.65 µV, calculated), so the signal is about 13 times the noise (calculated). This ignores CT phase error and offset, and the accuracy claim (±0.1% Wh over 5,000:1 in CT mode, from a plot at 240 V and 50 Hz) is the datasheet's, not our calculation.
4. **Calibrate.** The divider ratio moves with the input impedance, so a meter needs gain calibration for each part, and phase compensation for the CT. The CE does phase compensation for CTs and delay compensation for shunts, according to the datasheet, but the settings are in the manuals we haven't seen.

This is a mains circuit, with a divider that sits across line and neutral. The datasheet says nothing about creepage, clearance or isolation, and we haven't checked any of it.

## Things to watch

- **The absolute maximum equals the operating maximum on the main supply.** V3P3SYS and V3P3A are rated 3.0 to 3.6 V in operation and −0.5 to 3.6 V absolute. There is no headroom, so a 3.6 V supply with any overshoot exceeds the rating.
- **Never load V3P3D, and keep the DIO drive light.** The switch from V3P3SYS is 5 to 8 Ω and from VBAT 10 to 15 Ω, and the sum of all DIO pull-up current drops across it, lowering both V3P3D and the output high level. At 7 mA that is 35 to 56 mV on V3P3SYS and 70 to 105 mV on VBAT (calculated). The datasheet says to pull LEDs and relay coils low with a DIO pin, not drive them high.
- **Floating inputs cost battery life.** In the battery modes, digital inputs should be below 0.1 V or above the battery voltage minus 0.1 V. Wake pins, DIO72 and RMT_DRIVE_H must be pulled high or low in SLP and LCD-ONLY, or they leak.
- **Some pins run from V3P3RTC, not V3P3D.** DIO39 to DIO43, RST and TEST are in the non-volatile power domain, so their logic levels are 0.15 and 0.7 times V3P3RTC, and their high output is only specified at 0.5 mA.
- **DIO16 to DIO20 have no pull-up or pull-down and are 5 V tolerant.** The SPI slave pins are on them. If they are not used for SPI, the datasheet's component table puts a 100 kΩ pull-down on each.
- **The ADCM input varies part to part.** At 14 to 27 kΩ it makes a 1 kΩ lower divider leg vary by 3.3% (calculated), so calibrate each meter. Keep the lower leg small relative to 14 kΩ.
- **Absolute temperature needs calibration.** The sensor reads 3,700 LSB nominal at 22 °C (3,500 to 3,900), and the datasheet says the factory reference is not accurate. It asks you to average several readings at calibration and store them as stempCAL. A ±200 LSB error is about ±16.6 °C (calculated from 0.08286 °C per LSB).
- **The battery monitor is coarse.** Resolution is 25 mV per LSB with ±100 mV error, and the de-passivation load is 60 to 140 µA.
- **Crystal.** The 20 pF values assume 4 pF of stray capacitance per pin. If that means each pin sees 20 pF plus 4 pF, the pair puts about 12 pF across the crystal (calculated, assuming the usual series formula), so choose a crystal with a matching load capacitance, and check the oscillator margin yourself. The datasheet gives transconductance of 8 to 50 µS.
- **Sleep current is not 1 µA.** The first page says 1 µA in sleep. The tables give 3.5 µA typical, 8 µA maximum with VBAT = VBAT_RTC and RTC compensation off, and 1.7 µA typical, 4 µA maximum on VBAT_RTC with VBAT at 0 V. On a 230 mAh coin cell 3.5 µA lasts about 7.5 years and 8 µA about 3.3 years (calculated, ignoring self-discharge).
- **BRN mode is a few milliamps.** 7 mA typical and 8.6 mA maximum with metrology running. A 230 mAh cell lasts about 33 hours at 7 mA (calculated).
- **Thermal limits.** The datasheet's 2.35 W (LQFP) and 4.14 W (QFN) at 25 °C are what you get from a 150 °C junction, not from the 125 °C continuous limit, which gives 1.88 W and 3.32 W (calculated). The chip itself dissipates about 33 mW at the typical supply current (calculated), which raises the die by about 1.7 °C in the LQFP (calculated).
- **Tape and reel is LQFP only.** The tape table gives 1,000 pieces per 13-inch reel for the LQFP14x14-100 and nothing for the QFN-68.
- **Flash.** 100,000 program/erase cycles, 10 years retention at 85 °C, and the data is "not tested in production".

## Datasheet notes

- **The compute engine is 64-bit in some places and 32-bit in others.** The features list and block diagram say 64-bit at 20 MHz; the "32-Bit Compute Engine" paragraph says 32-bit DSP.
- **Package names differ.** The ordering table says "100 LQFP", the pinout figure is titled "LQFP100, top view" but its chip label says "TQFP-100", and the tape table says "LQFP14x14-100".
- **LQFP pin 32 is labelled I2C_CLK twice.** In the pin table the name column reads DIO-SEG2/I2C_CLK/BLINKA, while the function column and the pinout figure read I2C_DATA. The QFN figure labels pin 25 I2C_DATA. It is the data line (our reading). Pins 20 and 21 (SPI_M1_MISO and MOSI) also lack their SPI function in the function columns of the pin table.
- **The remote drive pins have two names.** The pin table says RMT_DRIVE_H and DIO72/RMT_DRIVE_L; the pinout figures and text say RMT_DRV_H; and the remote power pulse driver row in the electrical table puts the positive pin on DIO73 and the negative on DIO72. The pin table has no DIO73 (the part has DIO0 to DIO72).
- **The unused-pins table lists VC.** The part has no phase C voltage input (VA and VB only).
- **The remote receiver supply current is for "3 remotes enabled".** The overview says up to two SY7M007 remotes (IAP/IAN and IBP/IBN).
- **The sleep current on page 1 is lower than the tables.** See Things to watch.
- **NV-RAM size is stated two ways.** The features list says 4 KB of NV-RAM; the detailed description says 64 bytes (16 × 32-bit words).
- **Two or three staring ADCs.** The overview and block diagram show two (IAP/IAN and IBP/IBN), but the supply-current conditions in the electrical table enable "3" ADCS and list adcs0, adcs1 and adcs2 bias settings (our reading: a carry-over from a three-phase part).
- **ADCS LSB at 6554 Hz.** 75.59 nV in the table is 5% above the formula and probably a misprint for 72.59 nV (our reading). The other nine rows, and all six ADCM rows, are about 0.8% above the formula.
- **Reset pulse.** The text says to hold RST high for at least 5 µs ("RTS" in the text). The electrical table says positive spikes narrower than 1.2 µs are ignored and that the pin must be held at least as long as the 11.5 µs maximum limit, so 5 µs may not be enough (our reading).
- **Units in the electrical table.** The pull-down resistance row prints µA where kΩ is meant (45, 72 and 110). The ADCM and ADCS gain-error rows print "%" in the unit column for an input in percent of supply.
- **DIO ranges.** The LCD driver heading says DIO38 to DIO43 where the absolute maximum table says DIO39 to DIO43 for the V3P3RTC-powered pins.
- **BRN mode floor.** The text says BRN can run down to 1.8 V on VBAT, while the recommended operating range for VBAT is 2.5 to 3.6 V and the VBAT falling threshold is 2.26 to 2.42 V.
- **Datasheet link and revision.** The product page lists a datasheet at https://www.silergy.com/download/downloadFile?id=6427&type=product&ftype=note, with status "Production" and no revision. We used the uploaded Rev. 1.1 (January 2024) and did not open that link, so the revision there may differ. The SY7T231H has no page of its own that we found.
- **Revision history.** Rev. 1.0 (March 2023) is the initial release. Rev. 1.1 (January 2024) clarifies the RX wake pins, updates the pinouts, corrects the pin table (VB is on pins 72 and 48), adds the unused-pins table, updates the thermal ratings and adds TC1 and TC2 coefficients for the QFN.
- **Ordering.** SY7M212HBGEF (tray) and SY7M212HGEF (tape and reel), 100-pin LQFP; SY7T231HBTIQ (tray) and SY7T231HTIQ (tape and reel), 68-pin QFN. All −40 to +85 °C with 256 KB flash. The top mark is a part name plus revision, year, work week and lot code.
- **Related part.** The [SY7T6xx](site:parts/silergy/sy7t6xx/) is Silergy's single-chip energy measurement processor with one ADC, for hosts that read results over SPI, UART or I2C. It is a different family with its own datasheet.
