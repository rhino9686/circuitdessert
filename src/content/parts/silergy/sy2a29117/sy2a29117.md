## How it works

The SY2A29117 is a synchronous boost with a third switch. The main switch pulls LX to ground and charges the inductor, and the rectifier switch passes the inductor's current from LX to BD, the boost output. Both are N-channel, so the rectifier's gate is driven from a bootstrap capacitor between BS and LX. The third switch, the **disconnect switch**, sits between BD and OUT, driven by a charge pump. When the part is off, it opens and the load is cut off from the input, which a plain boost can't do: there, the rectifier's body diode always passes the input to the output. The datasheet calls this true shutdown.

**Power.** An internal 3.3 V regulator on VCC powers the control circuits and drivers, from whichever of SVIN and BD is higher. The part needs SVIN at 2.7 V to start. Once BD is at 4 V or more, VCC runs from BD, and the input can then fall to 1.2 V without the part stopping. With SVIN tied to VIN, as in the typical application, the input has to reach 2.7 V once to start (our reading).

**Control.** Adaptive constant off-time peak current mode: the main switch turns on until the inductor current reaches the level set by COMP, then off for a time that adapts to the input and output voltages to keep the frequency near 400 kHz (320–480 kHz). The inner current loop needs no external parts. The voltage loop compares FB with the 1 V reference in a transconductance amplifier (120 µS), and needs a Type 2 or Type 2a network on COMP.

**Two current limits.**

- *Peak current in the main switch*, set by a resistor on ILIM: ILIM (A) = 1200 ÷ RILIM (kΩ), from 3 to 15 A. This is the "15 A" in the title.
- *Output current in the disconnect switch*, 3 A minimum and 3.3 A typical, with VOUT above VIN. A current-limit amplifier compares the disconnect switch's current with a 1 V reference and clamps COMP when the current is too high, so the output voltage falls instead. With VOUT below VIN (while the output capacitors charge at start-up, or during a short), the disconnect switch limits the current linearly to about 1.5 A.

**Battery monitor.** BAT is a comparator input with a 1 V falling and 1.2 V rising threshold. In a backup supply, a divider from the main battery drives it: while the main battery is healthy, BAT is above 1.2 V and the boost sits in standby at about 25 µA (instead of 300 µA regulating without switching). When the main battery falls and BAT drops below 1 V, the boost becomes active. Without the function, BAT goes to ground, which keeps the boost active. Don't leave BAT floating.

**Shutdown and feedback.** EN low turns the part off at 2.2 µA typical. The bottom of the feedback divider returns to FGND, which goes to SGND through a 10 Ω switch inside the part that opens in shutdown, so the divider draws no current then.

**Protection.** Output overvoltage at 17 V on the OUT pin (15.5–18 V), or at 1.15 V on FB. If the part runs in current limit and VOUT falls below VIN, it stops for about 20 ms and restarts with a full soft start (about 5 ms), and repeats this while the short lasts. Thermal shutdown is at 150 °C, with a restart through a full soft start after the die cools by about 15 °C.

### Output voltage (R1, R2)

VOUT = 1 V × (1 + R1 ÷ R2), so R2 = R1 ÷ (VOUT − 1 V). The datasheet suggests 10 kΩ to 1 MΩ for both resistors, as large as practical to save light-load current, and uses R1 = 200 kΩ as its example. The table *Feedback resistors with R1 = 200 kΩ* above gives R2 for outputs from 5 V to 15 V (calculated). R2 returns to FGND, not ground, so the divider draws nothing in shutdown.

The datasheet doesn't give an output voltage range. Two limits bound it: the OUT pin overvoltage threshold of 15.5 V minimum, and the 18 V rating of LX, which swings up to BD every cycle. The datasheet's own conditions are 8 V and 12 V.

### Peak current limit (RILMT)

ILIM (A) = 1200 ÷ RILIM (kΩ), so RILIM = 1200 ÷ ILIM, between 80 kΩ (15 A) and 400 kΩ (3 A). The ILIM pin sits at 1 V. The datasheet gives the tolerance only for 120 kΩ: 8–12 A, so ±20%. The table *Peak current limit against RILIM* above gives standard values (calculated).

Set the limit above the peak inductor current at full load and minimum input, including the tolerance, and choose the inductor's saturation current above the limit's maximum (our suggestion). The output current limit in the disconnect switch (3 A minimum) caps the load anyway, so a high peak limit only matters at low input, where the input current is several times the output current. The table *Load current at the peak current limit* above shows how much load each setting supports.

### Inductor (L)

The datasheet suggests a ripple of about 40% of the maximum average input current, and gives

**L = (VIN ÷ VOUT)² × (VOUT − VIN) ÷ (fSW × IOUT_MAX × 40%)**

and a minimum saturation current of

**ISAT,MIN > (VOUT ÷ VIN) × IOUT,MAX + VIN × (VOUT − VIN) ÷ (2 × fSW × L × VOUT)**.

It adds that the part is not very sensitive to the ripple, so the final value can be somewhat off the calculation, and asks for a DCR below 10 mΩ. Calculated here at 8 V out, 400 kHz:

| Case | L at 40% ripple | Ripple with 1 µH | Peak with 1 µH (datasheet equation) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 3.3 V in, 2 A out | 2.5 µH | 4.85 A p-p (93% of the 5.2 A input current) | 7.3 A |
| 3.3 V in, 3 A out | 1.7 µH | 4.85 A p-p (61% of 7.9 A) | 9.7 A |
| 1.8 V in, 2 A out | 0.98 µH | 3.49 A p-p (34% of 10.3 A) | 10.6 A |

So the 1 µH on the efficiency graph suits the lowest input, and at 3.3 V in it runs a larger ripple than the 40% rule. The saturation equation leaves out the efficiency; with it (86–93% from the graph), the peaks are about 5–15% higher, for example 12.1 A at 1.8 V in and 2 A out (our calculation).

### Capacitors (CIN, CSVIN, CBD, COUT, CVCC, CBS)

- **CIN.** The input capacitor carries the triangular inductor ripple, ICIN,RMS = VIN × (VOUT − VIN) ÷ (2√3 × L × fSW × VOUT): 1.4 A at 3.3 V in and 8 V out with 1 µH (calculated). The datasheet recommends 10 µF of low-ESR ceramic close to VIN and PGND.
- **CSVIN.** The datasheet asks for 1 µF in the pin table and 2.2 µF in the application text, close to SVIN and SGND, with a small loop.
- **CBD and COUT.** The boost output (BD) carries the pulsed rectifier current, so CBD must sit close to BD and PGND with a small loop (layout rule 3). The pin table asks for at least 10 µF on BD, and the application text for "more than 44 µF" of 25 V X5R or better for CBD and COUT, sized for the steady-state ripple and transients. A 25 V rating against a 16 V output leaves room for the DC-bias loss, which a ceramic capacitor needs.
- **CVCC.** 1 µF ceramic from VCC to SGND. The 250 µs maximum EN turn-on delay is specified with this value.
- **CBS.** 100 nF low-ESR ceramic from BS to LX, with no series resistor (Rev 1.0A added this).

### Soft start and enable (CSS, EN)

tSS (ms) = CSS (nF) × 1 V ÷ 6 µA, so 10 nF gives about 1.7 ms. The charging current is 3–9 µA, so the time can be from about two-thirds to twice that (1.1–3.3 ms for 10 nF, calculated). The soft start acts on COMP, and the datasheet calls it a COMP voltage soft start.

EN is rated to 28 V. It turns the part on above 0.9 V and off below 0.4 V (the application text says above 0.8 V). If the device driving EN goes high-impedance in shutdown and SVIN connects directly to a battery, the layout notes ask for a 1 MΩ pull-down from EN to SGND.

### Loop compensation (RZ, CZ, CP)

The datasheet's procedure, with no example values:

1. **Crossover.** fc at the lower of 1/5 of the right-half-plane zero and 1/10 of fSW, with fRHPZ = (1 − DMAX)² × VOUT ÷ (2π × L × IOUT).
2. **RZ** = VOUT ÷ (gm × Gfc × VREF), with gm = 120 µS and Gfc = (1 − DMAX) ÷ (2π × fc × COUT × Ri), Ri = 75 mΩ.
3. **CZ** = VOUT × COUT ÷ (IOUT × RZ), which puts the zero at the pole of the load and COUT.
4. **CP** = RESR × COUT ÷ RZ, to cancel the output capacitor's ESR zero (Type 2). Without CP it is Type 2a.

Calculated here for 8 V out, 2 A, 1 µH and 44 µF of output capacitance (nominal; the BD and OUT capacitors are joined through the 30 mΩ disconnect switch, so we count them together):

| Design point | DMAX | fRHPZ | fc | RZ | CZ |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Lowest input 1.8 V | 0.78 | 32 kHz | 6.4 kHz | 39.6 kΩ | 4.4 nF |
| Lowest input 3.3 V | 0.59 | 108 kHz | 21.7 kHz | 72.6 kΩ | 2.4 nF |

Design at the lowest input the circuit will see: the right-half-plane zero is lowest there. With the 1.8 V values (39 kΩ), the crossover at 3.3 V in would be about 12 kHz, still well below that input's right-half-plane zero (our calculation). These are first-order estimates from the datasheet's equations and its typical gm and Ri.

### Battery monitor (R3, R4)

The datasheet's backup-battery figure puts a divider from the main battery to BAT: R3 on top, R4 to ground. The boost goes active when BAT falls below 1 V (0.9–1.1 V), and back to standby when it rises above 1.2 V (1.1–1.3 V). It gives no equation or values. Our calculation for a 12 V main battery, with the backup taking over below about 9 V:

| Quantity | Value | How |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Divider ratio | 9.06 | 1 + R3 ÷ R4, with R3 = 806 kΩ and R4 = 100 kΩ |
| Main battery voltage where the boost goes active | 9.06 V (8.15–9.97 V) | 1 V × 9.06 (0.9–1.1 V) |
| Main battery voltage where it returns to standby | 10.9 V (9.97–11.8 V) | 1.2 V × 9.06 (1.1–1.3 V) |
| Divider current at 14 V | 15 µA | 14 V ÷ 906 kΩ |

The threshold tolerances (±10% falling, about ±8% rising) are wider than the resistors', so they set the spread. The divider current comes from the main battery, on top of the boost's 25 µA standby current from the backup battery.

In the same figure, a diode D1 feeds the system from the main battery, and two optional Schottky diodes protect the disconnect switch: D2, in series between OUT and the system, blocks current from the system supply back into the part when that supply varies a lot, and D3 gives the current a path past the disconnect switch's body diode if BD is shorted to ground. The datasheet gives no part numbers.

## Layout

Silergy's rules, with a layout drawing (Figure 3):

- Place **CSVIN, CBD, COUT, CVCC, L, R1 and R2 close to the IC**.
- Give PGND as much copper as possible, ideally a ground plane, for heat and noise.
- **Keep the SVIN loop small**: CSVIN close to SVIN and SGND.
- **Keep the BD loop small**: CBD close to BD and PGND. This loop carries the switched current.
- **Keep the LX copper small**, for noise.
- **Keep R1, R2 and the FB trace away from LX.**
- If the device driving EN is high-impedance in shutdown and SVIN connects directly to a battery such as a Li-ion cell, **add a 1 MΩ pull-down from EN to SGND.**

## What the datasheet shows

Efficiency against load (1 mA to 3 A) at 8 V out with a 1 µH PCMB104T-1R0MT inductor, for 1.8, 3.3 and 4 V in: read off the graph, about 92% at its peak for 1.8 V in (about 86% at 2 A, where the curve ends), about 93–94% at 1–3 A for 3.3 V in and slightly higher for 4 V in, and 68–72% at 1 mA. ## Things to watch

- **The 15 A is the switch current, not the load current.** The disconnect switch limits the output to 3 A minimum, 3.3 A typical, and at low input the peak limit allows less (table above). The datasheet gives no output current rating.
- **No output voltage range.** The OUT overvoltage threshold (15.5 V minimum) and the 18 V LX rating bound it from above. The electrical table is specified at 12 V out and the graphs at 8 V.
- **An input above the output.** The input range goes to 16 V. With VOUT below VIN the boost can't regulate, the output follows the input through the inductor and the rectifier, and the disconnect switch limits the current to about 1.5 A (our reading of the linear current limit). An input above about 15.5 V can reach the OUT overvoltage threshold.
- **Start-up needs 2.7 V.** The 1.2 V figure only applies once BD is at 4 V or more. A backup battery that is already below 2.7 V can't start the part.
- **The peak current limit is ±20%,** and that is stated only at 120 kΩ. Both worked examples are close to the minimum.
- **Only typical on-resistances.** 10, 20 and 30 mΩ, with no maximum and no temperature dependence, so the loss estimates are typical only.
- **The datasheet disagrees with itself on a few values.** SVIN decoupling is 1 µF in the pin table and 2.2 µF in the application text. CBD is "at least 10 µF" in the pin table and, with COUT, "more than 44 µF" in the text. The EN high threshold is 0.9 V in the table and 0.8 V in the text.
- **Wide soft-start tolerance.** The charging current is 3–9 µA, so the soft-start time can be from about two-thirds to twice the calculated value.
- **BAT must not float.** Ground it if the battery monitor isn't used, as Figure 1 does.
- **A short on BD or a jumping system supply can damage the disconnect switch** through its body diode. The datasheet recommends Schottky diodes D2 and D3 for backup systems (see Battery monitor above).
- **Units have dropped out of the PDF** in a few places ("44 F", "6( A)"), read here as µF and µA, as the pin table and electrical table give them.
