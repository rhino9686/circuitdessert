## How it works

The SY70201 is a power-management IC for SSD power systems. From one 2.6–5.5 V supply it makes six outputs: four synchronous buck converters with built-in FETs (Buck1, Buck2, Buck3 and Buck5), a synchronous boost converter (Boost6) that makes a 12 V rail, and a load switch (LSW) that passes VIN to its output pin. The bucks switch at 2.0 to 2.5 MHz and each needs three external parts: an input capacitor, an inductor and an output capacitor. The channel numbers skip 4. The datasheet draws SW4, VOUT4, PVIN4 and PGND4 in its figures and lists a CH4 in several tables, but the package has no such pins (see Datasheet notes), so this page treats the part as four bucks.

The rails are sized for a typical SSD. Buck1 (3.5 A, 1.2 V by default) and Buck2 (3.8 A, 1.2 V, or 1.1 V with BK2SEL tied low) suit core rails, Buck3 (3 A, 1.8 V) and Buck5 (4 A, 2.5 V) suit I/O and DRAM-type rails, and Boost6 gives 12 V at up to 0.4 A for rails such as the NAND high-voltage supply. The datasheet does not say what each rail is meant to feed; those uses are our reading of the voltages.

**Strap pins set the defaults.** Two pins are read as VIN comes up and must be settled before it does. VSLT picks the supply and undervoltage level: low means a 3.3 V input with a falling UVLO of 2.7 V, high means a 5 V input with a falling UVLO of 3.6 V, and a floating pin reads low through an internal 5 MΩ pull-down. BK2SEL picks Buck2's default: tied to GND gives 1.1 V, tied to VIN gives 1.2 V. The start-up delays, the RSTO delay and the other defaults are programmed by OTP at the factory.

**Sequencing and reset.** When VIN passes the UVLO threshold the part waits a system delay of about 2 ms, then turns each output on after its own delay of 0 to 7.5 ms in 0.5 ms steps (the sequence tables below). RSTO is an open-drain output released 10 ms after VIN passes UVLO. An EN pin is an output, not an input: the part drives it high after its own delay so it can enable another block. I²C starts working only after RSTO is released, and is disabled again when RSTO falls.

**I²C control.** After start-up the host can change every output voltage (with a slew-limited step of 2, 4 or 32 µs per code), enable or disable each output, choose the discharge path and resistor, choose forced PWM or automatic PWM/PFM, and read the power-good, overvoltage and overcurrent flags (the register tables below).

**Protection.** Input overvoltage (5.8 V) turns every output off. An output overvoltage on any channel also turns every output off, and it latches: only recycling the input restarts the part. Overload (output below about 80% for 150 µs) turns off the channel and latches. Overcurrent is cycle by cycle on the high-side FET of a buck and the low-side FET of the boost. Thermal shutdown is at 150 °C with 20 °C hysteresis.

### Start-up and shutdown sequence

The datasheet gives the sequence as delays after the system delay (TSYS, 2 ms typical, 1.6 to 2.4 ms). Every delay can be set by OTP from 0 to 7.5 ms in 0.5 ms steps. The last column adds TSYS to each delay, our arithmetic, to give the time from VIN passing the UVLO rising threshold.

| Output | Default delay after TSYS | From UVLO rising |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Buck3 | 0 | 2 ms |
| LSW | 0 | 2 ms |
| Buck5 | 0 | 2 ms |
| Buck2 | 1 ms | 3 ms |
| EN | 2 ms | 4 ms |
| Buck1 | 3 ms | 5 ms |
| Boost6 | 4 ms | 6 ms |
| RSTO released | 10 ms from UVLO rising (7 to 14 ms by OTP, 1 ms steps) | 10 ms |

Soft start takes 200 µs for each buck and for the load switch, and 1.5 ms for the boost (10% to 90%, ±30%). When VIN falls below the UVLO threshold, every output is disabled at once, whatever its voltage, and its discharge turns on. Power-off delays are 25 to 175 µs in 50 µs steps, 25 µs by default, and also apply to EN. The datasheet's table lists CH4 with a default of "VIN" for its voltage.

### Protection

| Protection | Threshold | What happens |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Input undervoltage (UVLO) | Falling 2.7 V (VSLT low) or 3.6 V (VSLT high), ±2%, 100 mV hysteresis on the rising edge | All outputs off at once with discharge on; RSTO falls; I²C disabled |
| Input overvoltage | 5.8 V (5.66 to 5.94 V), 200 mV falling hysteresis | All outputs off at the same time, after at least 2 µs. Restart needs the input to recover |
| Output overvoltage | Buck1 2.231 V, Buck2 1.31 V, Buck3 2.025 V, Buck5 3.63 V, Boost6 14.25 V (±2.5%) | All outputs off and latched. Only recycling the input restarts |
| Overload | Output below about 80% of target for 150 µs | That channel off and latched. Recycle the input |
| Overcurrent | Buck1 6 to 8 A, Buck2 5.7 to 7.5 A, Buck3 6 to 7.8 A, Buck5 7 to 9.1 A (high-side FET); Boost6 3.1 to 4.5 A (low-side FET, VIN = 3 V) | Cycle by cycle |
| Load switch short circuit | 2 V threshold, 150 µs deglitch; current limit 260 to 600 mA | Short-circuit protection |
| Thermal shutdown | 150 °C, 20 °C hysteresis | All outputs off, back on after cooling |

The OVP thresholds in the table above are the datasheet's Table 3. They are for each rail's default voltage; the datasheet does not say whether they follow a voltage you set over I²C (the boost can be set up to 15 V, above its 14.25 V threshold, see Datasheet notes).

### Choosing VSLT and BK2SEL

Both pins are read as VIN rises, so they must be at their final level before it does. They do not change anything once the part is running.

- **VSLT** low is for a 3.3 V input (UVLO falling at 2.7 V); high is for a 5 V input (UVLO falling at 3.6 V). A floating VSLT reads low through its 5 MΩ pull-down. In the schematic it is pulled down by a 100 kΩ resistor, which is the 3.3 V case. For a 5 V input it would go to VIN.
- **BK2SEL** tied to GND gives Buck2 a default of 1.1 V, and tied to VIN gives 1.2 V. In the schematic R8 is a 100 kΩ pull-down, which our reading takes to be the 1.1 V choice. The pin text says to tie the pin to GND or VIN and does not mention a resistor.
- The input monitor bit in register 0x12 is stated for VSLT low only.

### Worked example (calculated): Buck1 at 1.0 V and Buck5 at 2.0 V

Suppose the SoC core wants 1.0 V on Buck1 and an I/O rail wants 2.0 V on Buck5, after power-up.

- Buck1: (1.825 − 1.0) ÷ 0.00625 = 132, which is 84h in register 0x00. The move from the 1.2 V default is 32 steps, 32 × 2 µs = 64 µs.
- Buck5: (2.75 − 2.0) ÷ 0.0125 = 60, which is 3Ch in register 0x05. The move from the 2.5 V default is 40 steps, 40 × 4 µs = 160 µs.
- Buck2 at 1.1 V over I²C (what BK2SEL tied low gives by default) would be (1.2 − 1.1) ÷ 0.00625 = 16, which is 10h in register 0x01. This one is our calculation; the datasheet gives no example code for it.

Write each register with one I²C write: 0x60 (address, write), the register number, the code. We found no statement in the datasheet text about writing several registers in one transfer, so write one register per transfer unless you have tested it. I²C works only after RSTO is released, so the first write comes 10 ms or more after VIN passes UVLO. Output overvoltage and overload latch until the input is recycled, and the datasheet does not say whether the registers keep their values through a restart, so assume the host must write the voltages again after one.

### Input current at full load (calculated)

All outputs at their default voltage and maximum load, from a 3.3 V supply, give 24.2 W of buck output and 4.8 W from the boost: the input current table in the design tables above. The upper bound of 9.03 A assumes 100% efficiency; with 90% for each buck and 80% for the boost (our assumptions, the plots are in the by-eye table) it is 10.2 A. The table's note gives the figure at 2.6 V in. The schematic's input capacitors are 10 µF per power input and 2.2 µF on the boost; the datasheet gives no input-capacitor sizing rule beyond its pin notes (10 µF on PVIN3 and PVIN5), so check the supply's own ripple and droop.

### Boost input current and the inductor

At 12 V and 0.4 A the 1 µH boost inductor sees an average input current from 1.45 A (3.3 V in, 100% efficiency) to 2.31 A (2.6 V in, 80%), and a peak of 2.87 A at 2.6 V in with 80% (the boost input current table). The datasheet's minimum low-side current limit is 3.1 A, specified at VIN = 3 V; at 2.6 V in the 2.87 A peak leaves less than 0.3 A of margin, and the datasheet gives no limit for lower inputs. Check the inductor's saturation current with these numbers. The BOM gives L1 a 4.3 A rating; the 0.22 µH inductors have none printed, and the table of inductor ripple and peak current shows peaks up to 5.35 A on Buck5 at 5.5 V in, with a minimum high-side limit of 7 A. Buck2 has the smallest margin: 0.83 A to its 5.7 A minimum limit at 5.5 V in.

## Things to watch

- **EN is an output.** The part drives the EN pin high after its own delay, and bit 2 of register 0x08 sets its level. It is not an enable input for the part. The datasheet gives no output type for it: the figure draws a pull-up resistor (R5 to VIN), and the electrical table gives its high and low levels with 100 kΩ to GND and to VIN.
- **I²C is dead until RSTO is released,** 10 ms after UVLO by default, and goes dead again when RSTO falls. A host that starts at power-on must wait or retry.
- **Any output overvoltage turns every output off and latches it.** Only recycling VIN restarts the part, and the host has to write its settings again. Overload latches the channel the same way. Input overvoltage at 5.8 V is only 0.3 V above the top of the recommended range of 5.5 V.
- **The strap pins must be settled before VIN rises.** A floating VSLT reads as the 3.3 V setting, so a 5 V input needs a real connection to VIN. With VSLT low the part uses the 2.7 V UVLO setting, and the datasheet gives the 4.0 V input monitor threshold for that case only.
- **The discharge and resistor bits do not follow one rule.** Channels 2 and 6 discharge on a 0 in register 0x0A, the others on a 1. In 0x0B the 0 and 1 meanings alternate between 2 Ω and 10 Ω by channel, and Boost6 is 300 Ω or 100 Ω. See the bit table.
- **In register 0x0C, 0 means forced PWM.** The default of 1 is automatic PWM/PFM, so the light-load efficiency is the PFM curves' (71% to 86% at 1.5 mA for the bucks).
- **The boost has little margin at a low input.** At VIN = 2.6 V, 12 V and 0.4 A with 80% efficiency need a 2.87 A peak in the inductor, against a 3.1 A minimum current limit specified at 3 V. See the boost table in the design tables.
- **Buck2 has the smallest margin to its current limit** (0.83 A at 5.5 V in) and Buck5 and Buck3 reach 5.35 A and 4.10 A. The 0.22 µH inductors have no current rating printed in the BOM.
- **The boost pins are the only 20 V pins.** Every other pin has a 6 V absolute maximum, and the OVP table gives 14.25 V for the boost although its range goes to 15 V.
- **PG and Gate are hard to read.** PG is an NFET drain controlled by the Gate pin, and PG is also set low by a fault in register 0x0D. The datasheet does not describe how the two interact. Gate has an internal pull-down of 1.5 MΩ in the table and 1 MΩ in the figure.
- **The KiCad symbol is ours and not yet opened in KiCad.** It was generated by script and checked against the pin table, but nobody has loaded it in KiCad. The pin types and the symbol layout are our choices. The exposed pad's number (37) is ours, because the pin table does not number it.
- **There is no KiCad footprint on this site.**
- **Quiescent current is 400 µA at most** with every channel enabled and no load at 3.3 V.
- **Related parts.** The [SY70202K](site:parts/silergy/sy70202k/) from Silergy is another SSD power-management IC with four 2 MHz bucks (and two LDOs) and I²C control, but it is not pin-compatible: it is a 34-ball BGA with GPIO pins, while this part is a 36-pin QFN with the VSLT and BK2SEL strap pins.
