## How it works

The SY67134 holds four N-channel MOSFETs in two half-bridges, one on OUT1 and one on OUT2, with a brushed DC motor connected between the two outputs. A charge pump powers the high-side gate drivers, so the high sides can stay on indefinitely. The block diagram also shows a power-management block on VM, a temperature sensor, the overcurrent detection in the gate drivers, and a comparator that compares the voltage on ISEN with VREF.

**Driving it** takes two logic inputs (the datasheet's Table 1, repeated in the design tables above). IN1 high and IN2 low drives OUT1 high and OUT2 low, which runs the motor forward. IN1 low and IN2 high reverses it. Both high turns on both low sides, which shorts the motor terminals at ground (a brake). Both low turns everything off, so the motor coasts. Either input can carry a PWM signal of up to 100 kHz. The datasheet's waveform captures, all at 12 V and 1 A, show four ways to do it: PWM on IN1 with IN2 low (the bridge alternates between forward and coast) or on IN2 with IN1 low (reverse and coast), and PWM on IN2 with IN1 high (forward and brake) or on IN1 with IN2 high (reverse and brake). The forward-and-brake way keeps the current flowing through the low sides between pulses, and the coast way lets it decay faster (our reading of the truth table, the datasheet doesn't compare them).

**Current chopping.** A sense resistor from ISEN to ground carries the bridge current. When the voltage across it reaches VREF ÷ 10, so the current reaches **ICHOP = VREF ÷ (10 × RSENSE)**, the bridge goes into decay for a fixed 24 µs: first fast decay for about a third of that time, then slow decay for the rest (the datasheet's Figure 3). It then drives the motor again. A 2 µs blanking time after each turn-on keeps the switching spike from tripping the comparator. The datasheet's example: 50 mΩ and VREF = 1 V give 2 A. If you don't need the current limit, connect ISEN directly to ground.

**Synchronous rectification.** During decay the part turns on the MOSFETs that the current is flowing through, instead of leaving it in their body diodes, which lowers the loss. It turns them off again when the current reaches zero, so the current doesn't reverse.

**Standby.** When IN1 and IN2 have both been low for 1 ms, the part shuts down most of its circuits, including the charge pump and the regulator, and draws less than 15 µA. After the inputs come back, the datasheet says to let the charge pump reach its voltage, which takes up to 200 µs, before sending PWM commands.

**Protection.**

- *Overcurrent.* An analog limit on each MOSFET holds its current at the overcurrent level (3.65 A minimum, 4.5 A typical). If that lasts longer than the regulation time (1.5 µs typical), all four MOSFETs turn off, and the part tries again after 3 ms. It repeats this while the fault lasts and resumes normal operation when it clears.
- *Undervoltage.* If VM falls below the lockout threshold, all circuits stop and the logic resets. The part restarts when VM rises above it again.
- *Overtemperature.* Above about 165 °C the part shuts down, and it restarts when the die cools by about 30 °C.

### Supply capacitors (CBULK, CVM)

The pin table asks for a 10 µF ceramic capacitor from VM to GND "and sufficient electronic capacitor", meaning an electrolytic bulk capacitor. The datasheet gives no value, voltage or ripple rule for it. The bulk capacitor supplies the motor's current pulses during PWM and absorbs the energy the motor returns during fast decay, when the winding current flows back into VM (our reasoning), so it matters more at higher current and lower PWM frequency.

### Current limit (RSENSE, VREF, CREF)

ICHOP = VREF ÷ (10 × RSENSE). Two limits from the datasheet shape the choice:

- **RSENSE under 100 mΩ.** With 100 mΩ, the full VREF range of 0.1–3 V gives 0.1–3 A.
- **ISEN between −0.3 V and 0.3 V.** At the trip point ISEN sits at VREF ÷ 10, so this is the same as keeping VREF at or below 3 V, the top of its electrical range. The recommended operating conditions allow VREF up to 3.3 V, which would put ISEN at 0.33 V (see Things to watch).

Above about 3.6 A the chopping current would be above the rating and above the 3.65 A minimum overcurrent limit, so the overcurrent protection would trip first and the bridge would go into its 3 ms retry. The table *Chopping current against VREF and RSENSE* above gives the values for four resistors (calculated). The current gain is 9.5 to 10.5, so the trip point is ±5% before the tolerances of VREF and the resistor. The resistor dissipates about ICHOP² × RSENSE while the current is at the limit: 0.2 W for the datasheet's 2 A and 50 mΩ.

CREF is at least 0.1 µF of ceramic on VREF. If VREF comes from a divider, the divider and CREF form a low-pass filter, and if it comes from a filtered PWM signal, CREF can be part of that filter (our suggestion).

### Worked example: a 12 V brushed motor with a stall limit

Everything here is calculated, nothing was measured. The motor is our assumption: a 12 V brushed DC motor that draws 0.8 A when running and has a 4 Ω winding, so it would draw about 2.7 A at stall (12 V ÷ (4 Ω + 0.5 Ω)) without a limit.

| Quantity | Value | How |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Loss, running at 0.8 A | 0.32 W | 0.8² × 500 mΩ |
| Rise, running | 20 °C | 0.32 W × 62 °C/W |
| Chopping current with the datasheet's example (50 mΩ, 1 V) | 2.0 A (1.90–2.11 A) | 1 V ÷ (10 × 50 mΩ), gain 9.5–10.5 |
| Loss and rise, stalled at 2 A | 2.0 W, 124 °C | 2² × 500 mΩ; × 62 °C/W |
| VREF for a 1.2 A stall limit | 0.6 V | 1.2 A × 10 × 50 mΩ |
| Loss and rise, stalled at 1.2 A | 0.72 W, 45 °C | 1.2² × 500 mΩ; × 62 °C/W |
| Sense resistor loss at 1.2 A | 72 mW | 1.2² × 50 mΩ |

**Running** is easy: 0.32 W and a 20 °C rise.

**Stalled at the datasheet's 2 A**, the bridge would hold 2 A and dissipate 2 W, a 124 °C rise on the datasheet's two-layer board. At 25 °C ambient that is about 149 °C, above the 125 °C maximum operating junction temperature, and at higher ambients it reaches the thermal shutdown. So with this package, use the current limit as a stall limit set close to what the part can carry continuously, not at the 3.6 A rating.

**Stalled at 1.2 A** (VREF = 0.6 V with 50 mΩ), the loss is 0.72 W and the rise 45 °C, so the junction stays below 125 °C up to about 80 °C ambient. The motor gets 1.2 A of stall current instead of 2.7 A, which limits its starting torque to under half of what it could have. If the motor needs more torque to start, raise VREF and check the stall time against the temperature.

**PWM below about 1 kHz.** With PWM on IN1 and IN2 low (forward and coast), both inputs are low for the whole off part of each period. If that lasts 1 ms or more the part goes into standby, and it then needs up to 200 µs to restart the charge pump (our reading of the standby description). Use a PWM frequency well above 1 kHz, or the forward-and-brake method with one input held high.

### Heat

The motor current always flows through one high side and one low side, and the datasheet gives only their combined maximum on-resistance, 500 mΩ, at 25 °C. With θJA = 62 °C/W on a two-layer board, the continuous current that keeps the junction at 125 °C is about 1.8 A at 25 °C ambient and 1.1 A at 85 °C (calculated, conduction loss only, with the on-resistance held at 500 mΩ; it rises with temperature in practice, so the real limit is lower). The table *Conduction loss and temperature rise against load current* above gives more points. The 3.6 A rating is only usable for short pulses, such as start-up, unless the board removes much more heat than the datasheet's two-layer test board.

## Layout

The datasheet's layout guidance is a recommended footprint for the SO8E package with its exposed pad (reference only) and the pin table's notes:

- **Connect the exposed pad to ground** for heat. On a two-layer board, give it as much copper as possible on both layers with vias under the pad (our suggestion, given the 62 °C/W θJA on a two-layer board).
- **Decouple VM** with the 10 µF ceramic capacitor close to VM and GND, and the bulk capacitor nearby.
- **Decouple VREF** with at least 0.1 µF to GND.
- **Return RSENSE to ground** close to the GND pin and the exposed pad, so that the motor current doesn't add a voltage to the measurement (our suggestion).

Six operation waveforms, all at VM = 12 V and a 1 A load with 20 µs/div: PWM on IN1 and on IN2 with the other input low, PWM on one input with the other held high, and the two inputs driven together. ## Things to watch

- **The supply range in the features is 5–40 V, but the part runs to 32 V.** The recommended operating conditions and the electrical table both give 5–32 V. The 40 V is the absolute maximum rating of VM, OUT1 and OUT2. Silergy's product page table gives 32 V too.
- **3.6 A is a rating, not a continuous current.** The 500 mΩ maximum on-resistance and 62 °C/W give about 1.1–1.8 A continuous (heat table above), and the datasheet's own note says power dissipation and thermal limits must be observed.
- **The overcurrent limit is barely above the rating.** The minimum is 3.65 A against a 3.6 A rating, and the typical is 4.5 A (the "peak output current" on Silergy's product page). A motor that briefly pulls more than 3.65 A at start-up can trip the protection and wait 3 ms before retrying.
- **The VREF range and the ISEN range disagree.** The recommended operating conditions allow VREF up to 3.3 V, but the electrical table gives 0.1–3 V, and ISEN must stay within ±0.3 V. At the trip point ISEN = VREF ÷ 10, so keep VREF at or below 3 V.
- **No margin between the undervoltage lockout and the minimum supply.** The lockout rises at 4.7 V typical and up to 5 V, which is the minimum supply. A 5 V supply that sags under load can reset the part, and the hysteresis is only 200 mV.
- **Only a maximum on-resistance.** 500 mΩ for the high side and low side together, at 25 °C, with no typical value and no temperature curve.
- **Standby on a slow or idle PWM.** Both inputs low for 1 ms puts the part in standby, and the charge pump needs up to 200 µs to come back. See the worked example.
- **Several values are "guaranteed by design" and not tested** (Note 4): the dead time, the time to standby, the overcurrent regulation and retry times, and the thermal shutdown threshold and hysteresis.
- **The datasheet is Rev 0.9 from 2019** and has no revision history. The part has since been renamed SY21634FCC, and the current datasheet sits behind a MySilergy sign-in, so the numbers here may have changed.
- **No component values in the typical application.** The bulk capacitor's value is not given anywhere, and VREF's source is not shown.
