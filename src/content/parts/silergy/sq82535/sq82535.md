## How it works

The SQ82535 is a series reference: like a small linear regulator, it sits between the supply and the load and holds its output at a fixed voltage, set by an internal band-gap and driven by a buffer amplifier (Figure 2). The difference from a regulator is the precision. The output is trimmed to ±0.025% at 25 °C and moves at most 3 ppm per °C from −40 to 125 °C, so the 2.5 V version stays within about ±1.25 mV over the whole range, initial error included.

**Sources and sinks.** The buffer can source or sink up to 10 mA, so the output can feed an ADC's reference input, a DAC, a bridge sensor or a comparator threshold, and it holds even when the load pushes current back. The load regulation is 10 ppm/mA sourcing and 20 ppm/mA sinking at most, so a reference input that draws a few hundred microamps costs almost nothing.

**Supply.** The input runs from 3 V (for the 2.5 V version) to 16 V. Line regulation is 5.5 ppm/V at most, so even the whole 13 V input range moves the output by no more than 72 ppm, and a 3.3 V rail with ±5% ripple moves it by less than 2 ppm. It draws 720 µA typical, 1150 µA maximum. The datasheet gives a dropout voltage only for the 3.0 V version (20 mV at no load, 200 mV at 10 mA); for the 2.5 V version, it states a 3 V minimum input.

**TRIM/NR.** Pin 5 reaches the band-gap's output through an internal 40 kΩ and 2 kΩ network (Figure 2). With a 0.1 µF capacitor to ground, it filters the band-gap's noise: wideband noise from 10 Hz to 10 kHz drops from 18.5 µVrms with the pin open to 7.6 µVrms. The price is a slower start: settling to 0.1% takes 32 ms instead of 1.6 ms. Alternatively, the pin takes a trim network (Figure 3) that moves the output by up to about ±0.5% to calibrate out a system error. It can't do both at once.

**TEMP.** Pin 3 gives a voltage proportional to the die temperature: 2.61 mV/°C, 577 mV at 25 °C. It comes from a 60 kΩ source, so it needs a high-impedance reader, and the datasheet doesn't specify its accuracy: it is meant for trends and thermal management, not absolute measurement.

**Precision depends on handling.** The datasheet spends a page on soldering, because the package stress from reflow shifts the output: solder it in the last reflow step, and where long-term stability matters, the datasheet suggests a 125 °C, half-hour bake at the module or system level before the chip is soldered. The thermal hysteresis is 70 ppm after the first temperature cycle and 27 ppm after the second, and the long-term drift is 37 ppm over the first 1000 hours, both typical and guaranteed by design.

### Input (C1)

The datasheet recommends 0.1 µF in parallel with 1 µF or 10 µF on the input, for high and low frequencies, and its bill of materials fits 1 µF. It also suggests a clean trace from the regulator that feeds nothing else (layout guide). With 5.5 ppm/V line regulation, the reference rejects slow supply changes well; the capacitors deal with fast ones. Use capacitors with a low voltage coefficient in 0603 or 0805: larger ceramics can turn board flexing into noise (the piezoelectric effect).

### Output (C2, C3)

The output needs between 0.1 µF and 22 µF to be stable, 10 µF typical. The bill of materials fits 10 µF and 0.1 µF. Count everything on the output net, including capacitors at the load: an ADC that wants its own 10 µF across its reference pins brings the total to about 20 µF, still inside the range. The datasheet suggests C0G/NP0 capacitors for low dielectric absorption, so the output recovers accurately after power-up or a load step; at 10 µF that means a large part, and X7R is the common compromise (our note). An optional RC filter after the output lowers noise further, at the cost of load regulation (layout guide).

### Noise reduction (C4) or trim

0.1 µF on TRIM/NR cuts the 10 Hz to 10 kHz noise from 18.5 to 7.6 µVrms and stretches the turn-on time to 32 ms. Place it close to the pin.

![Output trim circuit, redrawn from the SQ82535 datasheet (Figure 3) with its values.](schematic:sq82535-trim.svg)

For trim instead, Figure 3 connects a 10 kΩ potentiometer (R1) and 30 kΩ (R2) from VOUT to ground, with the wiper driving TRIM/NR through 1 MΩ (R3). The datasheet's formula gives a range of ±12.7 mV with these values, about ±0.51% of 2.5 V, the wiper at its middle giving no change (our reading of the formula). The formula scales the range by R3 ÷ 1 MΩ, which would make a larger R3 trim more, while a larger series resistor into the pin should trim less; we think the term is inverted, and would check the range on the bench.

The pin is a high-impedance node. The datasheet warns that residue on the board, such as flux or ionic contamination, can make a 10 MΩ path to the nearby ground and shift the output by about 2 mV, eight times the initial tolerance. Clean the board around it after assembly.

### Worked example: the reference for an SA89853 ADC

The [SA89853](site:parts/silergy/sa89853/) 12-bit ADC needs an external 2 to 3 V reference on REFP, which presents about 31 kΩ, 81 µA at 2.5 V, with 10 µF across REFP and REFM. Here the SQ82535C supplies it from the board's 3.3 V rail. Everything below is calculated.

| Quantity | Value | How |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Headroom | 300 mV at the nominal 3.3 V, 135 mV at 3.135 V (−5%) | Against the 3 V minimum input |
| Load | 81 µA | 2.5 V ÷ 31 kΩ |
| Output capacitance | About 20 µF | C2 plus the ADC's 10 µF; inside 0.1–22 µF |
| Dissipation and die rise | 3.8 mW, 0.8 °C | 3.3 V × 1.15 mA, × 208 °C/W |
| Error at 25 °C, after reflow | ±1.02 LSB | Initial accuracy, ±625 µV |
| Added over −40 to 125 °C | ±1.01 LSB | Temperature coefficient, maximum |
| Hysteresis and long-term drift | About 0.3 and 0.15 LSB | Typical figures |
| Line and load | Under 0.02 LSB | 3.3 V ±5%, 81 µA |
| Noise, 0.1–10 Hz | 3.5 µVp-p, under 0.01 LSB | 1.4 µVp-p/V × 2.5 V |

**At 12 bits, the reference is close to invisible.** Its worst case over the whole temperature range is about ±2 LSB, of the same order as the ADC's own offset and gain limits (±5 LSB and −4 to +2.5 LSB on range 1), and a one-point calibration at room temperature removes the initial part. At 16 bits the picture changes: the initial error is ±16 LSB and the drift another ±16, so a 16-bit system would want the trim, a calibration, or both.

**The headroom is the thing to watch.** At 3.135 V the reference has 135 mV over its 3 V minimum input; the 3 V figure is a minimum input voltage, so the circuit stays in specification, but a rail that sags lower during a load step would not (our reading). A 5 V supply, if the board has one, removes the question at a cost of about 2 mW.

## Layout

From the datasheet's PCB layout guide:

- **Ceramic capacitors close to VIN and VOUT.**
- **A separate, clean supply trace** from the regulator, not shared with digital or other analog circuits.
- **A solid ground plane under the reference**, as a low-impedance return and a shield.
- **Keep the output trace away** from clocks, data lines, switching nodes and any other periodic signal.
- **The NR capacitor as close to TRIM/NR as possible.**
- **An optional RC filter on the output** for lower noise, where the load is steady.
- **Clean the board around TRIM/NR** after assembly (from the trim section).

## Things to watch

- **Only the 2.5 V version is in production.** The other seven voltages are marked "Future product", and only the 3.0 V version has an electrical table, marked preliminary.
- **Assemble it last.** Reflow stress shifts the output. Solder it in the final reflow step; the datasheet also suggests a 125 °C, half-hour bake before soldering where long-term drift or hysteresis matters.
- **Keep TRIM/NR clean.** A 10 MΩ leakage path to ground shifts the output by about 2 mV, per the datasheet.
- **Read TEMP with a high-impedance input.** Its source is about 60 kΩ: a 1 MΩ input reads 12.5 °C low, a 10 MΩ input 1.3 °C low (calculated). Its accuracy isn't specified.
- **NR or trim, not both.** The pin does one job at a time, and with the NR capacitor the output takes 32 ms to settle to 0.1%.
- **Hysteresis, long-term drift and noise are typical figures**, guaranteed by design or statistical correlation and not production tested (Note 5).
- **The trim formula looks inverted** in its R3 term (see the trim section). Check the trim range on the bench.
- **Small errors in the datasheet.** The 2.5 V table lists a 2.5 µA "shutdown mode" current, but the part has no shutdown pin. The dropout voltage appears only in the preliminary 3.0 V table, and that table's wideband-noise conditions say "2.5V". The plot titles say "Trasient". The product page gives 0.05% initial accuracy and 0.74 mA, against the datasheet's ±0.025% and 720 µA.
- **For a lower-cost option**, Silergy's [SQ82550](site:parts/silergy/sq82550/) shunt reference is ±0.1% and up to 40 ppm/°C, about ten times less accurate, in a SOT-23.
