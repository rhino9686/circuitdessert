## How it works

The SQ52201QDQ is a digital monitor for one power rail. It has two inputs that matter: the shunt, measured differentially between IN+ and IN−, and the bus voltage on the VBUS pin. A 16-bit ADC measures each in turn. The shunt result is in the shunt voltage register (2.5 µV per bit, ±81.92 mV full scale, two's complement) and the bus result in the bus voltage register (1.25 mV per bit, never negative). From those two the part calculates current and power in the background, so it takes no extra conversion time, and puts them in the current and power registers. The host reads all four over I2C or SMBus.

**The bus is independent of the supply.** The shunt and the bus can sit anywhere from 0 V to 36 V while the part itself runs from VS, 2.7 V to 5.5 V. The shunt can be on the high side (between the supply and the load) or on the low side (in the ground return), and the current can flow in either direction, because the shunt register is signed. The bus voltage register is not: its top bit is always zero.

**Current and power come from a calibration value you write.** Until the calibration register (05h) holds a value, the current and power registers stay at zero. The host writes CAL once, from the shunt value and the current step it wants (see the calibration table). After that:

- Current register = shunt register × CAL ÷ 2048, and the current is the register times Current_LSB.
- Power register = current register × bus register ÷ 20000, and the power is the register times 25 × Current_LSB.

Without any programming the part runs continuously at the reset settings (1.1 ms conversions, no averaging, shunt and bus both on), so a host that only wants shunt and bus voltage can read registers 01h and 02h and ignore the rest.

**Averaging and conversion time.** The shunt and bus conversion times are set separately, from 140 µs to 8.244 ms, and the host can average 1 to 1024 samples. The datasheet says the longest conversion with the most averaging gives the best accuracy. In continuous mode each sequence converts the shunt and then the bus, and the registers update only when the whole averaging run has finished. There are also three triggered modes, each of which takes one set of conversions every time the configuration register is written, and a power-down mode that takes 40 µs to leave.

**One alert pin.** A single alert limit register (07h) can be tied to one of five events: current over-limit, current under-limit, bus over-voltage, bus under-voltage or power over-limit. The pin is open drain, active low by default (a bit changes it), and can be latched until the Mask/Enable register is read. The conversion-ready flag can also drive the pin, in which case the host reads the flags to tell the two sources apart. The part also answers the SMBus alert-response address (0001 100), so several devices can share one alert line.

**Addressing.** The A0 and A1 pins each go to GND, VS, SDA or SCL, which gives sixteen addresses, 1000000 to 1001111.

### Choosing the shunt

The datasheet leaves the shunt value to you and points only at two things: the shunt voltage must stay inside ±81.92 mV, and the maximum current sets the Current_LSB (equation 1: Imax ÷ 2^15).

- **Range.** The full-scale current is 81.92 mV ÷ RSHUNT: 41 A at 2 mΩ, 8.2 A at 10 mΩ and 1.6 A at 50 mΩ (see the calibration table).
- **Small currents.** The 10 µV maximum offset is a fixed error. At 2 mΩ it is 5 mA, and it reaches 1% of the reading at 0.5 A (offset table). The 2.5 µV step is 1.25 mA of current at the same shunt, so the register can't resolve less than that.
- **Heat.** At full scale the shunt dissipates (81.92 mV)² ÷ RSHUNT: 3.4 W at 2 mΩ. A bigger shunt gives more resolution and costs more voltage and heat, so many designs run well below the full-scale shunt voltage. The example in the datasheet is 20 mV at 10 A, a quarter of full scale.
- **Choosing CAL.** Pick Current_LSB from the maximum current, round it up to a convenient value, then CAL = 0.00512 ÷ (Current_LSB × RSHUNT) (calculated here from equation 2). CAL = 2048 gives a Current_LSB equal to one shunt step divided by the shunt value; a CAL below that comes from a coarser LSB, above it from a finer one.

### Connecting VBUS

The two datasheet figures draw VBUS differently. Figure 1 ties it to the supply side of the shunt. Figure 4 ties VBUS to IN−, the load side, and says the bus reads 11.98 V as a result, because 20 mV is dropped across the shunt. The choice sets whether the power register shows the power in the load (load side) or the power drawn from the source (supply side). The VBUS pin's input impedance is 830 kΩ typical, so a source resistance of 1 kΩ gives about 0.12% error and 10 kΩ about 1.2% (calculated here). The pin draws about 14 µA at 12 V and 43 µA at 36 V (our arithmetic from 830 kΩ).

### Worked example: the datasheet's 12 V, 2 mΩ, 10 A case

The register values are the datasheet's (Table 1 and the text); the extra columns are calculated here.

- **Current_LSB.** With a maximum current of 15 A, equation 1 gives 457.7 µA per bit. The datasheet rounds this up to 500 µA or 1 mA and uses 1 mA.
- **CAL.** 0.00512 ÷ (0.001 × 0.002) = 2560, which is 0A00h.
- **Shunt register.** 10 A × 2 mΩ = 20 mV, which is 8000 counts (1F40h).
- **Current register.** 8000 × 2560 ÷ 2048 = 10000 (2710h), which is 10 A at 1 mA per bit.
- **Bus register.** The VBUS pin sees 11.98 V (12 V less 20 mV), which is 9584 counts (2570h).
- **Power register.** 10000 × 9584 ÷ 20000 = 4792 (12B8h), and at 25 mW per bit that is 119.82 W.

Things the example doesn't say, worked out here:

- **Range of the current register.** At 1 mA per bit the signed 16-bit register tops out at 32.767 A, which is 65.5 mV across 2 mΩ. The shunt itself goes to 40.96 A (81.92 mV). Above 32.767 A the arithmetic overflows and the OVF flag is the datasheet's warning that current and power may be invalid. A 2 mA step covers the whole shunt range (calibration table).
- **Resolution of the current register.** CAL 2560 makes each shunt step 1.25 current steps, so the current register can't show every value (the datasheet doesn't say how it rounds). The real resolution is the shunt step, 1.25 mA, not the 1 mA written on the label.
- **Accuracy at 10 A.** The shunt voltage is 20 mV, so the 10 µV maximum offset is 0.05% and the 0.2% maximum gain error adds to 0.25% (error budget table). On the bus, the 10 mV maximum offset is 0.083% of 12 V and the gain error 0.25%, 0.33% together. Adding the two worst cases gives a power error of up to about 0.58% (our sum). The product page's 0.45% power accuracy equals the two gain errors added (0.2% and 0.25%); the datasheet gives no power accuracy. A real shunt's tolerance adds to all of this.
- **Shunt dissipation.** 10 A through 2 mΩ is 0.2 W and 20 mV; at the 15 A used to find Current_LSB it is 0.45 W.

## Layout

The datasheet gives no layout guidance beyond a recommended footprint on the package page (reference only). The points below are our notes, not the datasheet's:

- Bring IN+ and IN− to the shunt with a Kelvin connection (two traces from the shunt's own pads, not from the high-current copper), and keep the loop they form small. The shunt is only a few milliohms.
- Put a bypass capacitor close to VS and GND. The datasheet's figures show one but give no value.
- If VBUS shares the IN− node, take it from the shunt's load-side pad.
- The exposed pad may be grounded or left floating, as the pin table says.
- Keep the A0 and A1 straps short. The part samples them at every bus transaction.

## Things to watch

- **A 3.3 V host can't drive the part from a 5 V supply.** The digital input high level is 0.7 × VS minimum. At VS = 3.3 V that is 2.31 V, but at 5 V it is 3.5 V and at 5.5 V it is 3.85 V, so the "3.3 V I2C compatible" claim holds only up to a supply of about 4.7 V (3.3 ÷ 0.7, our arithmetic). A host pulling the lines to VS does not have the problem.
- **No calibration, no current and no power.** Both read zero until CAL is written, and the write has to be repeated after any reset, since all registers go back to defaults.
- **Pick Current_LSB so the register doesn't overflow.** At 1 mA per bit the current register tops out at 32.767 A, below the shunt's full scale at 2 mΩ. See the worked example and the calibration table.
- **Triggered conversions need a write every time.** The datasheet says a triggered mode runs once per write of the configuration register, even if the mode doesn't change. A write also stops any conversion in progress and starts again with the new settings.
- **Reading Mask/Enable has side effects.** It clears the conversion-ready flag and, in latch mode, the alert flag and the alert pin. Read it once and use that one value for both flags.
- **Only one alert function is active at a time.** If several are set, the highest bit wins (COL, then CUL, BOL, BUL, POL). The datasheet's own example is current over-limit and current under-limit set together, and only the over-limit one acts.
- **An alert arrives only after the averaging run.** The limit is compared when the shunt, bus and power registers update, so with 16 averages at 1.1 ms the delay is about 35 ms and at 8.244 ms and 64 averages about a second (update interval table). The part is a monitor, not a fast overcurrent trip.
- **The shunt step is the real resolution.** At 2 mΩ one step is 1.25 mA, whatever Current_LSB you choose.
- **The bus offset matters on low rails.** The 10 mV maximum is 0.3% of 3.3 V and 1% of 1 V, though it is 0.08% at 12 V.
- **Bus voltage input limits.** The ADC scale goes to 40.96 V but the datasheet says not to apply more than 36 V, and the absolute maximum is 40 V on VBUS and 40 V common mode on the shunt inputs.
- **Offset drift is large on the bus.** 40 µV/°C maximum is 4 mV over 100 °C (calculated here), on top of the 10 mV offset. The shunt's 0.1 µV/°C maximum adds up to 10 µV over 100 °C, as much as the 10 µV offset itself.
- **Shutdown isn't free of leakage.** The inputs leak 0.1 µA typical and 0.5 µA maximum in power-down, and the part needs 40 µs to come out.
- **The datasheet gives no pull-up value, no bypass value and no input-filter advice.** The bias current is one figure of 7 µA typical, with no statement of which pin it flows into, so any resistor in series with the shunt inputs adds an uncertain offset.
- **The 28 ms SMBus timeout resets the interface** whenever SCL stays low for longer than that, so a stalled host can't hold the bus.
- **Thermal limits are not a concern.** At 5.5 V and 460 µA the part dissipates about 2.5 mW (our arithmetic), against 0.847 W.

## Datasheet notes

- **The Mask/Enable bit names disagree.** The text calls the bus limits BOV and BUV and the table calls them BOL and BUL. The text and Figure 3 describe the first two bits as current limits, while the table headings say "Current Over-Voltage" and "Current Under-Voltage", and "Bus Voltage Over-Voltage" for the third. All the descriptions in the table compare the shunt voltage register for COL and CUL, the bus voltage register for BOL and BUL, and the power register for POL.
- **The alert limit has no encoding.** The datasheet doesn't say in which register units the limit is written for each function. We read it as the unit of the register being compared (see the alert limits table).
- **The calibration value is written two ways.** Table 1 gives the contents as 0A00h and the text as A00h. It is the same value, 2560.
- **Configuration register name.** The description of the shunt and bus mode setting refers to the "Conversion Register (00h)" where the configuration register is meant.
- **A garbled sentence.** The text on alert latching ends with "…or the Configuration Register (00h) is written to.0h) is written to or the Mask/Enable Register is read." It reads as clearing on either of the two events.
- **The read frame and the text differ.** Figure 6 sets the register pointer with a stop condition and then reads; the read text says the transfer may end with a not-acknowledge "or a start or stop condition". We don't know from the datasheet whether a repeated start between the pointer write and the read is supported.
- **The configuration register's bit 14 is set at reset (4127h) but has no name or description.**
- **Differential range.** The electrical table gives the upper limit as +81.9175 mV, and the recommended conditions table +81.91 mV.
- **Bus register full scale.** The register description gives the full scale as 40.96 V "(hexadecimal = 7FFF)". 7FFF is 32767 steps, which is 40.9588 V; 40.96 V is 32768 steps, one more.
- **Maximum bus offset.** ±10 mV in the datasheet, 7.5 mV on the product page.
