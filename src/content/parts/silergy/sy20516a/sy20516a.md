## How it works

The SY20516A is a four-switch buck-boost converter. Its four switches form two half-bridges, one on each end of a single inductor: the LX1 pin on the input side and the LX2 pin on the output side. The datasheet calls the switches an NFET and a PFET (100 mΩ each, typical) and doesn't draw them, so the arrangement below is our reading of the usual scheme: with the input above the output the LX1 side switches as a synchronous buck and the LX2 side stays on, and with the input below the output the LX2 side switches as a synchronous boost and the LX1 side stays on. The datasheet doesn't describe what happens when the input is close to the output, only that the part covers inputs above, below and equal to the output. Its graphs include 3.3 V in with 3.3 V out.

**PWM and PFM.** The part switches at a fixed 1 MHz (0.8 to 1.2 MHz) in PWM, with a "seamless transition between operating modes". At light load it switches to PFM, a power-saving mode that the datasheet doesn't describe beyond the changeover. The changeover is set from the output current: an RC network on ISET (R5 and C7 in the typical design) senses the average output current at 5 µA per amp at a 1 A load, and the part goes into PFM when the voltage on ISET is below 0.1 V (0.08–0.12 V). ISET tied to ground gives forced PWM, and it must not float.

**Compensation and soft start.** The voltage loop is closed with an external RC network on COMP (20 kΩ in series with 2.2 nF in the typical design). The soft-start ramp is set by a capacitor on SS, charged with 5 µA to 0.7 V, and SS must not float. The datasheet's 20 nF gives 2.8 ms.

**Protection.**

- *Undervoltage lockout* at 2.45 V typical, rising, with 0.2 V hysteresis.
- *Peak input current limit* of 3.5 A minimum and 4 A typical. There is also a negative current limit of −1 A typical.
- *Short circuit.* If the output is below about 50% of the set point while the part is in current limit, it goes into a hiccup mode with a cycle of about 4 ms. It then restarts, with a full soft start, on the nearest on-time once the fault is gone.
- *Overvoltage.* If FB rises above 125% of the reference, the protection acts after a 16 µs delay and the part resumes once the overvoltage is gone. The datasheet doesn't say what the protection does to the switches.
- *Thermal shutdown* at 150 °C with 15 °C hysteresis, and a restart through a full soft start.
- *Power good.* PG is an open-drain output, pulled low when FB is below 0.48 V or above 0.72 V (20% under or over the target). It is also held low from the moment VIN passes the lockout until the output reaches 80% of the target, and it needs a pull-up to a rail within the operating range.
- *Output disconnect.* In shutdown, with EN low, the load is disconnected from the battery and the input current drops to 0.1 µA typical (1 µA maximum).

### Output voltage, power good and overvoltage (R1, R2)

VOUT = 0.6 V × (1 + R1 ÷ R2), or R2 = 0.6 V × R1 ÷ (VOUT − 0.6 V). The datasheet recommends 10 kΩ to 1 MΩ for both resistors, large to keep the light-load consumption low, and gives 100 kΩ for R1 as its example. The typical design's 100 kΩ and 22.1 kΩ give 3.32 V at the typical reference and 3.27–3.37 V over the reference tolerance, before resistor tolerance. The divider takes 27 µA from the output, which isn't in the 60 µA quiescent current (that is specified with FB at 105% of the reference). The table *Feedback resistors for other outputs* above gives R2 for five outputs from 2.6 V to 3.8 V (calculated).

The same divider sets the power-good window and the overvoltage level, since both are measured on FB. For 3.3 V that is a power-good window from 2.65 V to 3.98 V and an overvoltage trip at 4.14 V (calculated from the typical reference).

### Inductor (L1)

The datasheet's recommendation is a ripple of about 40% of the maximum average input current, with L ≥ VOUT × (1 − VOUT ÷ VIN,MAX) ÷ (fSW × IOUT,MAX × 40%). It adds that the part is "less sensitive to ripple current variations", so the final value can be a little different. It must also carry the peak current: ISAT ≥ (VOUT ÷ VIN,MIN) × IOUT,MAX + VIN,MIN × (VOUT − VIN,MIN) ÷ (2 × fSW × L × VOUT), at the minimum input voltage. The DCR should be below 15 mΩ.

For the typical design (1 MHz, 3.3 V out, 1.2 A, 5.5 V maximum in) the first equation gives 2.75 µH, and the typical 2.2 µH gives a ripple of 0.6 A p-p at 5.5 V in (50% of 1.2 A), 0.32 A at 4.2 V, 0.25 A at 2.6 V and 0.12 A at 3.0 V (the table *Inductor ripple* above, calculated). The first equation is the buck-mode form, and the datasheet's text and equation disagree about the current it refers to (see Datasheet notes).

The boost side sets the saturation current. With an efficiency of about 80% (the graph reads 82% at 3.0 V in and 1.2 A; there is no curve at 2.6 V) the input current at 2.6 V in and 1.2 A out is about 1.9 A, and the peak about 2.0 A, well under the 3.5 A minimum input current limit. The bill of materials has a 5.1 A inductor. The limit has no maximum value in the datasheet, so a saturation current above 4 A, as the BOM's, leaves room above the typical limit (our suggestion).

### Capacitors (C2, C3, C4, C5, C11)

- **Input.** At least 22 µF, X5R or better, 6.3 V, on PVIN, close to the pin and the ground pad. The text asks for low-ESR ceramics able to handle the ripple current, and two in parallel (22 µF × 2 in the typical design) when a lower input ripple is wanted.
- **SVIN.** A separate 1 µF ceramic from SVIN to ground, "strongly recommended" by the layout notes.
- **Output.** At least 22 µF on OUT, X5R or better, 6.3 V, close to the pin and the ground pad. The datasheet recommends two in parallel and a total over 22 µF, sized for both steady-state ripple and transients. The typical design uses 2 × 22 µF, and the graphs are with 44 µF. DC-bias derating of 6.3 V X5R ceramics at 3.3 V is large, so the capacitance you get is lower than the label (our note; the datasheet doesn't discuss it).

### Light-load mode (R5, C7)

The table *PFM threshold against R5* above gives the load at which the part changes between PFM and PWM, assuming the sensed current scales linearly with the load. The typical 150 kΩ gives about 0.133 A, and the efficiency graph agrees: it jumps from about 85% to about 95% at 0.12–0.2 A for the 3.0 V and 3.3 V curves, and a little later (about 0.18 A) for the 4.2 V curve. R5 with C7 also forms a filter of 150 kΩ × 10 nF = 1.5 ms, so the mode follows the average current over a few milliseconds rather than cycle by cycle (our calculation).

Tying ISET to ground forces PWM at all loads. The datasheet has no efficiency curve for forced PWM, so it doesn't show what that costs at light load. See Things to watch for how low the PFM efficiency is.

### Soft start, enable and compensation (C6, K1, R4, R3, C8)

The soft-start time is tSS = 0.7 V ÷ 5 µA × CSS, so the typical 20 nF gives 2.8 ms and 10 nF gives 1.4 ms (table above, calculated). Don't leave SS floating.

EN turns the part on above 1.5 V and off below 0.4 V, and has an internal 1 MΩ pull-down, so it is off when left open. The schematic uses a switch (K1) from VIN to EN and a further 1 MΩ (R4) to ground, which puts EN at VIN when K1 is closed, so a closed K1 draws VIN ÷ 0.5 MΩ, about 8 µA at 4.2 V (our calculation). To start the part from a logic output, make sure its high level is at least 1.5 V.

The compensation is a series 20 kΩ and 2.2 nF on COMP. The datasheet says a simple series RC network keeps the loop stable over the load and input range, and gives no procedure or equations, so for other COMP values or output capacitors the loop has to be checked on the bench.

**Heat at the bottom of the lithium range.** At 3.0 V in the loss is 0.85 W. On the datasheet's θJA of 38 °C/W, which is for a low-conductivity single-layer JEDEC board, that is about 32 °C above the ambient, or 117 °C at 85 °C ambient. The recommended junction limit is 125 °C and the thermal shutdown is at 150 °C typical. Below 3.0 V the efficiency is lower still, because there is no curve for it. A board with thermal vias under the exposed pad will do better than the test board, but at 1.2 A, low battery voltage and high ambient the heat is what limits the part, not the 3.5 A current limit.

**Input current is not a problem for the limit.** At 3.0 V in the average input current is 1.6 A, and the ripple is only 0.12 A p-p (table above), so the peak is about 1.7 A, under half of the 3.5 A minimum limit.

**Beyond 1.2 A.** The graph continues to about 1.7 A, where the 3.0 V curve ends at about 73%, the 3.3 V curve at about 83% and the 4.2 V curve at about 86%.

## Layout

Silergy gives these rules and a layout drawing.

- Place **CIN, COUT, L and the feedback resistors R1 and R2 close to the IC**.
- Give the GND pins as much copper as the board allows, with **vias under the ground pad** for soldering and heat.
- **SVIN is the supply of the control circuit.** Don't connect SVIN to PVIN directly, and decouple SVIN with its own 1 µF ceramic to ground.
- **Keep the input loop small**: CIN close to the VIN and GND pins, with the loop between the input capacitors, PVIN and the ground pad as small as possible.
- **Keep the output loop small**: COUT close to OUT and the ground pad.
- **Keep the copper on the LX pins small** to improve the noise immunity.
- **Keep R1, R2 and the FB or OUT traces away from the LX nodes**, to keep noise away from the FB and OUT pins.

Pins 1 to 7 (PVIN, SVIN, OUT, EN, FB, COMP, SGND) are on one side and 8 to 14 (NC, SS, ISET, PG, LX2, PGND, LX1) on the other. The power pins (PVIN, SVIN, OUT on one side, LX2, PGND and LX1 on the other) are at the top end of the pinout and the control pins at the bottom end (our reading of the pinout). The datasheet's layout drawing puts R3, C8, R1 and R2 along the control side and the inductor and CIN at the power end.

## What the datasheet shows

Typical graphs at 25 °C, 3.3 V out, 2.2 µH and 44 µF. Efficiency against load for 3.0 V, 3.3 V and 4.2 V in (read off by eye in the table above): about 72–76% at 1 mA, 82% at 10 mA, about 95% at 0.2 A, falling to about 82–89% at 1.2 A. There are oscilloscope captures at 3.0 V, 3.6 V and 4.2 V in of the output ripple at 0.6 A and 1.2 A, a 0.2 to 1.2 A load transient, start-up and shutdown from EN and from VIN at 1.2 A, and short-circuit protection with no load and with 1.2 A. We haven't read values off the captures.

There is no recommended-components table apart from the application schematic's bill of materials, no output-current derating curve, no efficiency curve in forced PWM, and no information on the loop other than the typical compensation values.

## Things to watch

- **PFM efficiency is low.** From 1 mA up to the changeover at about 0.13 A the efficiency is only about 75–85% (the graph reads 76% at 1 mA and 82% at 10 mA), against about 95% in PWM at 0.2 A. At 1 mA the loss is about 1.1 mW, five times what the 60 µA quiescent current alone gives at 3.3 V (0.2 mW; our calculation). At 0.1 A, just below the threshold, the loss is about 60 mW, against about 30 mW at twice that load (0.2 A) in PWM. A larger R5 moves the threshold down (table above), but the datasheet shows no PWM efficiency below 0.1 A, so the best value is a bench question.
- **Heat limits 1.2 A at low battery voltage.** At 3.0 V in and 85 °C ambient the junction reaches about 117 °C on the datasheet's single-layer test board (worked example above), close to the 125 °C recommended maximum.
- **The overvoltage protection trips above the OUT pin's absolute maximum for outputs over 3.2 V.** It acts at 125% of the target, which is 4.14 V for a 3.3 V output and up to 4.76 V for 3.8 V, while OUT is rated to 4 V. The protection is not a guarantee against exceeding the pin rating, so keep transients off the output (our reading).
- **The output is limited to 3.8 V, and the input to 5.5 V.** There is no 5 V output, and OUT, unlike the other pins (6 V), is rated only to 4 V.
- **The input peak current limit has no maximum.** The datasheet gives 3.5 A minimum and 4 A typical, so choose the inductor's saturation current with the unlisted maximum in mind.
- **Some inputs must not float.** SS and ISET must be connected, and ISET to ground gives forced PWM. EN has an internal pull-down, so a floating EN is off.
- **EN at VIN draws current.** With the internal 1 MΩ and the 1 MΩ of R4 in parallel, EN at VIN draws about 8 µA at 4.2 V (our calculation), on top of the 60 µA quiescent current.
- **R7 is a 0 Ω link between PVIN and SVIN.** The layout text says not to connect SVIN to PVIN directly. The schematic does it through R7 with a separate 1 µF capacitor, which gives a place for a small filter resistor if input noise reaches the control circuit (our reading).
- **R6 draws current while PG is low.** PG is an open-drain output pulled up to VIN through R6 in the typical design. While it is low (start-up, a fault) it draws VIN ÷ 100 kΩ, 42 µA at 4.2 V (our calculation).
- **Several values have no tolerance.** The soft-start current, the ISET current, the power-good and overvoltage thresholds, the on-resistances and the current limits other than the input peak limit are typical values only.

## Datasheet notes

- **The inductor text and equation disagree.** The text says to choose a ripple of about 40% of the maximum average input current, but the equation uses the maximum load current IOUT,MAX, and it is the buck-mode form, VOUT × (1 − VOUT ÷ VIN,MAX), which is zero or negative when the highest input is at or below the output. We follow the equation for a buck at the top of the input range and calculate the boost side separately.
- **Shutdown current.** The Enable Operation text says it drops "lower than 0.1 µA"; the electrical table gives 0.1 µA typical and 1 µA maximum.
- **EN thresholds are one-sided.** The rising threshold has only a minimum (1.5 V) and the falling threshold only a maximum (0.4 V), so the table gives no highest turn-on level or lowest turn-off level. The text says above 1.5 V turns the part on and below 0.4 V turns it off.
- **The bill of materials and the schematic differ.** C1 (100 µF, 16 V, electrolytic) is in the list but not on the schematic, and K1 is on the schematic but not in the list. C11, R1 to R7 and C6 to C8 have no part numbers or makers.
- **The first-page figure and the schematic page differ on EN.** Figure 1 ties EN straight to VIN, so the part starts as soon as the input passes the lockout. The schematic page puts a switch (K1) between VIN and EN, with a 1 MΩ pull-down (R4). The rest of the two drawings (0 Ω and 1 µF on SVIN, 2 × 22 µF on each side, the feedback, compensation, soft-start, ISET and PG parts) agrees.
- **The input peak current limit has no maximum**, and the negative current limit has only a typical value.
- **The efficiency graph runs past the rating.** The rating is 1.2 A, and the three curves in the graph end at about 1.7 A.
- **Junction temperature.** The absolute maximum table lists "Junction Temperature, Operating" as −40 to 150 °C, while the recommended operating range is −40 to 125 °C. We take 150 °C as the absolute maximum.
- **Revision.** The file is marked "DS_SY20516A Rev.1.0" with © 2021 and has no revision-history table, so we can't tell how it differs from the earlier SY9701A datasheets that some distributors list.
