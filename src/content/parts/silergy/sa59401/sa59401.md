## How it works

The SA59401 is a delta-sigma ADC: a switched-capacitor modulator samples the input much faster than the output data rate, and a digital filter averages the bit stream into one 16-bit result per conversion. Fewer conversions per second means more averaging. A programmable gain amplifier (PGA) in front of the modulator sets the full-scale range, and an internal reference, an oscillator and a temperature sensor complete the chip, so it needs nothing outside but a bypass capacitor and whatever filtering the signal wants.

**Inputs.** The ADC always measures a differential voltage, INP − INN, and rejects the common mode (100–108 dB at DC). A multiplexer chooses the pair: AIN0–AIN1 (the default), AIN0–AIN3, AIN1–AIN3 or AIN2–AIN3. That gives two independent differential channels, or three inputs measured against AIN3. **There is no setting that measures against GND**: for a single-ended signal, tie AIN3 (or AIN1) to ground and measure against it. Every input must stay between GND and VDD, whatever the full-scale range. ESD diodes to both rails protect the pins, and the datasheet suggests external Schottky diodes and series resistors if an input can go beyond them. An overdriven unused input can disturb the conversion on the others.

**Full-scale range.** The PGA gives ±4.096, ±2.048, ±1.024, ±0.512, ±0.256 (the default) or ±0.128 V, with an LSB of FSR ÷ 2¹⁶, from 125 µV down to 3.9 µV. The ±4.096 V range is a scaling, not a permission: with VDD at 3.3 V the input can't reach 4.096 V, so the top of the code range is lost.

**Noise.** The datasheet's noise tables give 1 LSB RMS at every range and data rate, and 1 LSB peak-to-peak except at the fastest rates (2 LSB at 468 and 890 SPS on some ranges). So the noise is at the level of the quantization step: slowing down doesn't buy resolution beyond 16 bits, and 890 SPS is nearly as quiet as 8 SPS (our reading of Tables 1 and 2). Lower rates still filter more of the noise coming in with the signal.

**Two modes.** In continuous mode (MODE = 0) the part converts at the programmed rate, one result after another, and the latest one can be read at any time. In single-shot mode (MODE = 1, the default at power-up) it sits powered down until a 1 is written to SS, makes one conversion, and powers down again. The part starts powered down, so nothing happens until the controller writes the Config register.

**Temperature sensor.** Setting TS_MODE makes the next conversion read the die temperature instead of the inputs. The result is 12 bits, left-justified in the 16-bit word, at 0.125 °C per LSB, in two's complement: shift right by four and multiply by 0.125. It is accurate to ±3 °C over −40 to 125 °C (0.5 °C typical), and meant for system monitoring or for the cold junction of a thermocouple.

**SPI.** Four wires, or three with CS_N tied low when the part is alone on the bus. There are no register addresses. Every transfer is 16 or 32 bits: while the controller shifts a Config word in on DIN, the part shifts out the conversion result, and in a 32-bit transfer it then shifts out a readback of the Config register. A Config word is written only if its NOP field is 01, so a word of zeros reads the result without changing anything. DOUT/DRDY_N doubles as the data-ready flag: with CS_N low it goes low when a new result is waiting, and a read that starts while it is high returns the previous result again. The result locks into the output register when the transfer starts, so a conversion finishing mid-transfer can't corrupt it. With CS_N high the pin is high-impedance with a weak (400 kΩ) pull-up. Holding SCLK low for 28 ms resets the interface.

### Input filters (R1 to R4, C1 to C6)

Each differential pair gets 100 Ω in series with each input, 1 µF across the pair, and 0.1 µF from each input to ground. Calculated here from those values:

- **Differential corner**: 1 ÷ (2π × 200 Ω × (1 µF + 0.05 µF)) ≈ **760 Hz**. The two 0.1 µF capacitors appear in series across the pair, adding 0.05 µF.
- **Common-mode corner**: 1 ÷ (2π × 100 Ω × 0.1 µF) ≈ **16 kHz**.

The differential capacitor is ten times the common-mode ones, so a mismatch between C2 and C3 (or C5 and C6) turns only a little common-mode noise into a differential error (our reasoning; the datasheet gives no rationale). The series resistors also limit the current into the ESD diodes if an input is overdriven.

They do cost a little gain: the 200 Ω of R1 + R2 forms a divider with the ADC's differential input impedance, which falls from 800 kΩ at ±4.096 V to 135 kΩ at ±0.128 V. The table *Gain error from 100 Ω input resistors* above gives 0.025% to 0.15% (calculated). It is systematic, so a gain calibration removes it, but it is as large as the datasheet's maximum gain error on the most sensitive range.

The filters are wired for the two differential pairs. With the multiplexer set to AIN0–AIN3 or AIN1–AIN3, the two inputs are filtered only to ground, not across each other.

### Supply and SPI lines (CBYPASS, RSCLK, RCS_N, RDOUT, RDIN)

0.1 µF from VDD to GND, as close to the pins as possible: the pin table asks for 100 nF, and the bill of materials names a 50 V X7R part. The 50 Ω series resistors on the SPI lines damp ringing on the edges. The layout guide puts each one near the pin that drives it: RDOUT near the ADC, and RSCLK, RCS_N and RDIN near the controller.

### Worked example: a K-type thermocouple with cold-junction compensation

A thermocouple produces a voltage set by the temperature difference between its hot end and the cold junction, where it joins the copper of the board. The ADC measures that voltage, the temperature sensor gives the cold-junction temperature (with the part placed at the connector), and the controller adds the two. Everything here is calculated, nothing was measured. The type K sensitivity of about 41 µV/°C is a typical figure for that thermocouple, not from the datasheet; use the standard thermocouple tables in practice.

| Quantity | Value | How |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Range | ±0.128 V (PGA 101) | Type K reaches about 55 mV at 1372 °C, so the smallest range covers it |
| LSB | 3.9 µV, about 0.095 °C | 0.128 V ÷ 32768; ÷ 41 µV/°C |
| Offset error | ±7.8 µV maximum, about ±0.19 °C | ±2 LSB, specified at ±2.048 V |
| Gain error at 1000 °C (41.3 mV) | 62 µV, about 1.5 °C | 0.15% maximum, specified only at ±2.048 V; gain match between ranges adds up to 0.5% more |
| Input filter error | 0.15%, about 1.5 °C at 1000 °C | Table above; calibrate it out |
| Cold-junction error | ±3 °C maximum | The temperature sensor's accuracy, added directly |
| Self-heating of the die | About 0.3 °C, converting continuously | 350 µA × 3.3 V × 255 °C/W |
| Readings | Alternate one ADC conversion (8B0Bh) and one temperature conversion (8B1Bh) | Single-shot, 8 SPS; about 0.25 s per pair |

**The cold junction dominates.** At ±3 °C maximum, the temperature sensor sets the error unless you calibrate it, and the ADC's own offset adds only about 0.2 °C. The die also runs about 0.3 °C above the board when converting continuously (single-shot reduces that), and any temperature difference between the ADC and the thermocouple connector adds directly: put the part right at the connector, on the same copper.

**Bias the thermocouple.** A thermocouple is usually floating, but the inputs must stay between GND and VDD, so it needs a path that sets its common mode. The datasheet doesn't show one. A common arrangement (our suggestion) is a high-value resistor from AIN0 to VDD and another from AIN1 to GND, for example 1 MΩ each: they bias the inputs near mid-supply through the thermocouple and drive the reading to positive full scale if the thermocouple breaks, which the controller can detect. Their current flows through the thermocouple's own resistance, so keep them large: 1.65 µA through 10 Ω is 16.5 µV, about 0.4 °C.

## Talk to it from an ESP32-S3

This section wires an ESP32-S3 dev board to the SA59401 over SPI. The sketch reads and writes the Config register, reads the last conversion, and adds commands that make one single-shot conversion on any input pair and range and print it in millivolts, or read the die temperature. It is built from the [Arduino interface template](site:contribute/arduino/), with block 2 rewritten for the part's 32-bit frame. The datasheet gives no SPI timing at all, so the sketch runs the clock at a conservative 500 kHz.

> **Status:** Compile-checked for the ESP32-S3 (arduino-esp32 3.3.12, board ESP32S3 Dev Module), with no warnings (`-Wall -Wextra`). The console logic was also run on a PC against a simulated SA59401 that follows the datasheet's frame format, NOP rule, single-shot timing and data-ready behaviour. Not yet run on a real ESP32-S3 or a real SA59401.

| Pull-ups | None needed. DOUT/DRDY_N has its own 400 kΩ pull-up while CS_N is high | |
| Supply | 520 µA at most | Negligible for a dev board's 3.3 V regulator |

### The sketch

Paste it into a new sketch, choose the board *ESP32S3 Dev Module* (or your own ESP32-S3 board), and open the Serial Monitor at 115200 baud with line ending *Newline*. The first block is the only one to edit.

```cpp
/*
  SA59401 console for an ESP32-S3, over SPI.
  Wiring (ESP32-S3): GPIO12 = SCLK, GPIO11 = DIN, GPIO13 = DOUT/DRDY_N,
  GPIO10 = CS_N. Everything is 3.3 V.
  Register 0 is the Conversion register (read only), register 1 the Config
  register. Extra commands: conv, temp, mux <0-3>, fsr <0-5>, rate <0-7>.
*/
#include <SPI.h>

// ===== 1. DEVICE CONFIG: edit this block ==================================
// From the SA59401 datasheet (Rev 1.0A): a 32-bit frame carries the
// conversion result and a readback of the Config register while the
// Config word goes in on DIN, MSB first, mode 1.

// ESP32-S3 pins: the board package's default SPI pins. Any free GPIO works.
const int8_t   SCK_PIN       = 12;
const int8_t   MISO_PIN      = 13;         // DOUT/DRDY_N
const int8_t   MOSI_PIN      = 11;         // DIN
const int8_t   CS_PIN        = 10;         // CS_N (active low)

const uint32_t BUS_HZ        = 500000;     // the datasheet gives no maximum
const uint8_t  SPI_DATA_MODE = SPI_MODE1;  // SCLK idles low; DIN is latched on
                                           // the falling edge, DOUT changes on
                                           // the rising edge
const uint8_t  WORD_BYTES    = 2;          // both registers are 16 bits
const uint16_t REG_MAX       = 1;          // 0 = Conversion, 1 = Config

// The SA59401 has no reset pin. The datasheet gives no start-up time.
const int8_t   RESET_PIN     = -1;
const uint16_t BOOT_MS       = 10;

struct NamedReg { const char *name; uint8_t reg; };
const NamedReg REGS[] = {
  {"CONVERSION", 0},
  {"CONFIG", 1},
};

// Config register fields (datasheet Table 7).
const uint16_t CFG_SS     = 0x8000;  // start a single-shot conversion
const uint16_t CFG_MODE   = 0x0100;  // 1 = power-down and single-shot
const uint16_t CFG_TS     = 0x0010;  // 1 = temperature sensor
const uint16_t CFG_PULLUP = 0x0008;  // weak pull-up on DOUT/DRDY_N
const uint16_t CFG_NOP_WR = 0x0002;  // NOP[1:0] = 01: this word is written
const uint16_t CFG_NOP    = 0x0006;  // the NOP[1:0] field

// Settings the conv command uses: change them with mux, fsr and rate.
uint8_t muxSel = 0;   // 0 = AIN0-AIN1, 1 = AIN0-AIN3, 2 = AIN1-AIN3, 3 = AIN2-AIN3
uint8_t fsrSel = 1;   // 0 to 5 = +-4.096, 2.048, 1.024, 0.512, 0.256, 0.128 V
uint8_t rateSel = 4;  // 0 to 7 = 8, 15, 30, 61, 121, 239, 468, 890 SPS
const float    FSR_V[6]  = {4.096f, 2.048f, 1.024f, 0.512f, 0.256f, 0.128f};
const uint16_t RATE_SPS[8] = {8, 15, 30, 61, 121, 239, 468, 890};
const char    *MUX_NAME[4] = {"AIN0-AIN1", "AIN0-AIN3", "AIN1-AIN3", "AIN2-AIN3"};

bool parseNum(const char *s, uint32_t &out);
void printHex(uint32_t v, uint8_t digits);

// ===== 2. BUS LAYER: SPI, SA59401 32-bit frames ===========================
// There are no register addresses. Every frame sends a Config word (and a
// second one, here always 0x0000, which the part ignores because its NOP
// field is 00) and returns the conversion result and the Config readback.
// A Config word is written only if its NOP[1:0] field is 01.

const __FlashStringHelper *lastError = F("SPI has no error codes: check wiring and mode");

bool busBegin() {
  pinMode(CS_PIN, OUTPUT);
  digitalWrite(CS_PIN, HIGH);
  SPI.begin(SCK_PIN, MISO_PIN, MOSI_PIN);   // chip select is driven by hand
  return true;
}

bool busReady() { return true; }
const __FlashStringHelper *busError() { return lastError; }

static uint16_t xfer16(uint16_t out) {
  uint16_t hi = SPI.transfer((uint8_t)(out >> 8));
  uint16_t lo = SPI.transfer((uint8_t)(out & 0xFF));
  return (uint16_t)((hi << 8) | lo);
}

// One 32-bit frame. If csLow is true, CS_N is already low (after a wait for
// data ready) and is left for this function to raise.
static void frame32(uint16_t cfgOut, uint16_t &conv, uint16_t &cfgBack, bool csLow) {
  if (!csLow) {
    SPI.beginTransaction(SPISettings(BUS_HZ, MSBFIRST, SPI_DATA_MODE));
    digitalWrite(CS_PIN, LOW);
  }
  conv = xfer16(cfgOut);
  cfgBack = xfer16(0x0000);
  digitalWrite(CS_PIN, HIGH);
  SPI.endTransaction();
}

bool devRead(uint16_t reg, uint32_t &value) {
  uint16_t conv, cfg;
  frame32(0x0000, conv, cfg, false);    // NOP = 00: nothing is written
  value = (reg == 0) ? conv : cfg;
  return true;
}

bool devWrite(uint16_t reg, uint32_t value) {
  if (reg != 1) {
    lastError = F("Register 0 (Conversion) is read only.");
    return false;
  }
  uint16_t w = (uint16_t)((value & ~CFG_NOP) | CFG_NOP_WR);
  uint16_t conv, cfg;
  frame32(w, conv, cfg, false);
  // SS always reads back 0 and bit 0 always reads back 1.
  if ((cfg & 0x7FFE) != (w & 0x7FFE)) {
    lastError = F("Config readback differs: check wiring and mode.");
    return false;
  }
  return true;
}

// Start one single-shot conversion and wait for DOUT/DRDY_N to go low.
static bool convertOnce(uint16_t cfg, int16_t &code) {
  uint16_t conv, back;
  cfg = (uint16_t)(((cfg & ~CFG_NOP) | CFG_SS | CFG_MODE | CFG_PULLUP | CFG_NOP_WR));
  frame32(cfg, conv, back, false);
  // One conversion period, plus 10% for the data-rate tolerance and a margin.
  uint32_t limitMs = 1100UL / RATE_SPS[(cfg >> 5) & 7] + 5;
  SPI.beginTransaction(SPISettings(BUS_HZ, MSBFIRST, SPI_DATA_MODE));
  digitalWrite(CS_PIN, LOW);            // with CS_N low, DRDY_N shows on DOUT
  for (uint32_t t0 = millis(); digitalRead(MISO_PIN) == HIGH; ) {
    if (millis() - t0 > limitMs) {
      digitalWrite(CS_PIN, HIGH);
      SPI.endTransaction();
      lastError = F("No data ready: check CS_N, DOUT/DRDY_N and the supply.");
      return false;
    }
    delay(1);
  }
  frame32(0x0000, conv, back, true);
  code = (int16_t)conv;
  return true;
}

static uint16_t adcConfig() {
  return (uint16_t)((muxSel & 3) << 12 | (fsrSel & 7) << 9 | (rateSel & 7) << 5);
}

static bool setField(const char *a1, uint8_t maxVal, uint8_t &field) {
  uint32_t v;
  if (!a1 || !parseNum(a1, v) || v > maxVal) {
    Serial.print(F("Give a number from 0 to ")); Serial.println(maxVal);
    return false;
  }
  field = (uint8_t)v;
  return true;
}

static void showSettings() {
  Serial.print(MUX_NAME[muxSel]);
  Serial.print(F(", +-")); Serial.print(FSR_V[fsrSel], 3);
  Serial.print(F(" V, ")); Serial.print(RATE_SPS[rateSel]); Serial.println(F(" SPS"));
}

bool busCommand(const char *cmd, const char *a1) {
  int16_t code;
  if (!strcmp(cmd, "help") || !strcmp(cmd, "?")) {
    Serial.println(F("conv               one single-shot conversion, in mV"));
    Serial.println(F("temp               one internal temperature reading"));
    Serial.println(F("mux <0-3>          0 AIN0-AIN1, 1 AIN0-AIN3, 2 AIN1-AIN3,"));
    Serial.println(F("                   3 AIN2-AIN3"));
    Serial.println(F("fsr <0-5>          +-4.096, 2.048, 1.024, 0.512, 0.256,"));
    Serial.println(F("                   0.128 V"));
    Serial.println(F("rate <0-7>         8, 15, 30, 61, 121, 239, 468, 890 SPS"));
    Serial.println(F("Registers: 0 = Conversion (read only), 1 = Config."));
    return false;                       // the console prints the rest
  }
  if (!strcmp(cmd, "mux"))  { if (setField(a1, 3, muxSel))  showSettings(); return true; }
  if (!strcmp(cmd, "fsr"))  { if (setField(a1, 5, fsrSel))  showSettings(); return true; }
  if (!strcmp(cmd, "rate")) { if (setField(a1, 7, rateSel)) showSettings(); return true; }
  if (!strcmp(cmd, "conv")) {
    if (!convertOnce(adcConfig(), code)) { Serial.println(busError()); return true; }
    float mv = code * FSR_V[fsrSel] * 1000.0f / 32768.0f;
    Serial.print(MUX_NAME[muxSel]);
    Serial.print(F("  code ")); Serial.print(code);
    Serial.print(F("  ")); Serial.print(mv, 4); Serial.println(F(" mV"));
    if (code == 32767 || code == -32768) Serial.println(F("(clipped at full scale)"));
    return true;
  }
  if (!strcmp(cmd, "temp")) {
    if (!convertOnce((uint16_t)(adcConfig() | CFG_TS), code)) { Serial.println(busError()); return true; }
    float degC = (code >> 4) * 0.125f;  // 12-bit result, left-justified
    Serial.print(F("Die temperature ")); Serial.print(degC, 3); Serial.println(F(" C"));
    return true;
  }
  return false;
}

// ===== 3. CONSOLE (the same for every bus) ================================
// Serial Monitor at 115200 baud, line ending "Newline". Commands:
//   help | scan | addr 0x2A | r <reg> | w <reg> <value> | d <first> <count>
//   | t | reset
// Numbers can be decimal or 0x hex.

bool parseNum(const char *s, uint32_t &out) {
  char *end;
  out = strtoul(s, &end, 0);
  return end != s && *end == 0;
}

void printHex(uint32_t v, uint8_t digits) {
  for (int8_t i = digits - 1; i >= 0; i--) {
    Serial.print((uint8_t)((v >> (4 * i)) & 0xF), HEX);
  }
}

void printWord(uint32_t v) {
  const uint8_t bits = WORD_BYTES * 8;
  int32_t s = (int32_t)v;
  if (bits < 32 && (v & (1UL << (bits - 1)))) {
    s = (int32_t)(v | (0xFFFFFFFFUL << bits));      // sign-extend
  }
  Serial.print(F("0x")); printHex(v, WORD_BYTES * 2);
  Serial.print(F("  unsigned ")); Serial.print(v);
  Serial.print(F("  signed ")); Serial.println(s);
}

void deviceReset() {
  if (RESET_PIN < 0) {
    Serial.println(F("RESET_PIN is not set."));
    return;
  }
  // Only ever pulled LOW. The 3.3 V pull-up on the part makes it high.
  pinMode(RESET_PIN, OUTPUT);
  digitalWrite(RESET_PIN, LOW);
  delay(2);
  pinMode(RESET_PIN, INPUT);
  delay(BOOT_MS);
  Serial.println(F("Reset done."));
}

void showRead(uint16_t reg) {
  uint32_t v;
  if (devRead(reg, v)) printWord(v);
  else Serial.println(busError());
}

void help() {
  Serial.println(F("r <reg>            read one register"));
  Serial.println(F("w <reg> <value>    write one register (this can change"));
  Serial.println(F("                   the device's setup: be careful)"));
  Serial.println(F("d <first> <count>  read a run of registers"));
  Serial.println(F("t                  read the named registers in REGS[]"));
  Serial.println(F("scan / addr <a>    I2C only: find the device / set address"));
  Serial.println(F("reset              pulse the reset pin, if RESET_PIN is set"));
}

void handle(char *ln) {
  char *cmd = strtok(ln, " \t");
  char *a1 = strtok(NULL, " \t");
  char *a2 = strtok(NULL, " \t");
  uint32_t reg, val;
  if (!cmd) return;
  if (busCommand(cmd, a1)) return;
  if (!strcmp(cmd, "help") || !strcmp(cmd, "?")) { help(); return; }
  if (!strcmp(cmd, "reset")) { deviceReset(); return; }
  if (!busReady()) {
    Serial.println(F("No device address yet: type 'scan' or 'addr 0x..'."));
    return;
  }
  if (a1 && parseNum(a1, reg) && reg > REG_MAX) {
    Serial.println(F("Register number is above REG_MAX."));
    return;
  }
  if (!strcmp(cmd, "r") && a1 && parseNum(a1, reg)) {
    showRead(reg);
  } else if (!strcmp(cmd, "w") && a1 && a2 &&
             parseNum(a1, reg) && parseNum(a2, val)) {
    if (devWrite(reg, val)) Serial.println(F("written"));
    else Serial.println(busError());
  } else if (!strcmp(cmd, "d") && a1 && a2 &&
             parseNum(a1, reg) && parseNum(a2, val)) {
    for (uint32_t i = 0; i < val && reg + i <= REG_MAX; i++) {
      Serial.print(F("[0x")); printHex(reg + i, 2); Serial.print(F("]  "));
      showRead(reg + i);
    }
  } else if (!strcmp(cmd, "t")) {
    for (uint8_t i = 0; i < sizeof(REGS) / sizeof(REGS[0]); i++) {
      Serial.print(REGS[i].name);
      Serial.print(F("  [0x")); printHex(REGS[i].reg, 2); Serial.print(F("]  "));
      showRead(REGS[i].reg);
    }
  } else {
    Serial.println(F("?  (type help)"));
  }
}

char lineBuf[40];
uint8_t lineLen = 0;

void setup() {
  Serial.begin(115200);
  // On the native USB port, text sent before the Serial Monitor connects is
  // lost, so wait up to 3 s for it. A UART port is ready at once.
  for (uint32_t t0 = millis(); !Serial && millis() - t0 < 3000; ) delay(10);
  // High-impedance until a reset is asked for.
  if (RESET_PIN >= 0) pinMode(RESET_PIN, INPUT);
  delay(BOOT_MS);                     // let the device finish starting up
  if (!busBegin()) Serial.println(F("The bus did not start. Check the pin numbers."));
  Serial.println(F("Register console ready. Type help."));
  if (!busReady()) Serial.println(F("Type scan to find the device."));
}

void loop() {
  while (Serial.available()) {
    char c = Serial.read();
    if (c == '\n' || c == '\r') {
      if (lineLen) {
        lineBuf[lineLen] = 0;
        handle(lineBuf);
        lineLen = 0;
      }
    } else if (lineLen < sizeof(lineBuf) - 1) {
      lineBuf[lineLen++] = c;
    }
  }
}
```

### Using it

1. Build the wiring with the power off, join the grounds, and connect a test voltage across AIN0 and AIN1.
2. Upload the sketch and open the Serial Monitor.
3. Type `r 1`. A connected part answers `0x098B` after power-up (its reset value). If you see `0xFFFF` or `0x0000`, check the wiring, CS_N and the SPI mode.
4. Type `conv`. The sketch makes one single-shot conversion of AIN0–AIN1 on ±2.048 V at 121 SPS and prints the code and the voltage.
5. Change the setup with `mux 0` to `mux 3` (the input pair), `fsr 0` to `fsr 5` (±4.096 V down to ±0.128 V) and `rate 0` to `rate 7` (8 to 890 SPS), then `conv` again.
6. Type `temp` for the die temperature.
7. `w 1 <word>` writes the Config register directly (the sketch sets NOP = 01 for you and checks the readback), and `r 0` reads the last result without changing anything. A write with MODE = 0 starts continuous conversion; `conv` and `temp` switch the part back to single-shot.

If `conv` says "No data ready":

- CS_N must reach the part: the data-ready flag only shows while CS_N is low.
- DOUT/DRDY_N must reach GPIO13. With CS_N high it should sit near 3.3 V on its pull-up.
- Check `r 1` first. If the Config readback is wrong, the problem is the wiring or the SPI mode, not the conversion.

### Pitfalls with the ESP32-S3

- **Two USB ports.** On boards with two, the one marked USB is the chip's own port: set *USB CDC On Boot* to *Enabled* to make `Serial` use it. The sketch waits up to 3 s for the Serial Monitor on that port.
- **The sketch reads DOUT/DRDY_N with `digitalRead()`** on the MISO pin, between SPI transfers, to wait for data ready. This works on the ESP32 because the pin's input stays readable while the SPI peripheral owns it. If you move MISO to a pin that can't be read as an input, the wait times out.
- **Pick free pins.** Avoid GPIO0, 3, 45 and 46 (boot strapping), GPIO19 and 20 (the USB port) and GPIO26 to 37 (flash and PSRAM on many modules).
- **Don't pause inside a frame.** SCLK idles low in mode 1, and SCLK held low for 28 ms resets the SA59401's interface. Between frames that is harmless (it starts the next frame cleanly), but a frame interrupted for 28 ms or more is lost. The sketch sends each frame in one go.
- **Give the SA59401 its own chip select.** With CS_N high the part ignores SCLK, so another SPI device can share SCLK, MOSI and MISO.
- **Start slow.** The datasheet gives no maximum clock, so `BUS_HZ` stays at 500 kHz, and a 32-bit frame takes 64 µs. In continuous mode, DOUT/DRDY_N goes back high 8 µs before the next result, so a read must start soon after it falls and finish before then: at 890 SPS that leaves about 1.1 ms, plenty at this clock.

## Layout

From the datasheet's PCB layout guide, with a top- and bottom-layer drawing:

- **Put a good capacitor across each differential input pair**, with short, symmetric connections.
- **Place the 0.1 µF bypass capacitor as close as possible to VDD and GND.**
- **Place each SPI series resistor near the pin that drives the line**: RDOUT near the ADC, RSCLK, RDIN and RCS_N near the controller.
- For a thermocouple, put the ADC next to the connector so the temperature sensor sees the cold junction (our suggestion).

## What the datasheet shows

No graphs. The datasheet gives the electrical table, two noise tables (noise in µV RMS and peak-to-peak, and the effective and noise-free bits, for every range and data rate at VDD = 3.3 V with the inputs shorted), the full-scale and LSB table, the temperature data format with worked conversions, the output code table, timing drawings of the 16-bit and 32-bit transfers, the register map, the application schematic with its bill of materials, and a layout drawing. There are no SPI timing figures and no plots of accuracy against temperature or supply.

## Things to watch

- **No SPI timing.** The datasheet gives no maximum SCLK frequency and no setup, hold or CS_N timing. Start slow and check the signals.
- **No input measured against GND.** The multiplexer offers only AIN0–AIN1, AIN0–AIN3, AIN1–AIN3 and AIN2–AIN3. A single-ended signal needs AIN3 (or AIN1) tied to ground. MUX codes 100 to 111 are not described.
- **The full-scale range is not the input range.** Every input must stay between GND and VDD (−0.3 V and VDD + 0.3 V absolute). The ±4.096 V range can't reach full scale with VDD below 4.096 V. The datasheet gives no common-mode limit for the PGA beyond that.
- **Gain error is specified only on ±2.048 V.** On other ranges, the gain match between ranges (0.5% maximum) can add to the 0.15% maximum. The offset and INL figures are also given only for ±2.048 V.
- **The temperature sensor is ±3 °C maximum.** For a thermocouple that is the largest error, and it measures the die, not the connector.
- **Power-down current rises a lot with temperature**: 5 µA typical and 10 µA maximum at 25 °C, but 45 µA maximum over −40 to 125 °C. Battery estimates should use the high figure.
- **The data rate can be 10% off**, so a 121 SPS conversion can take up to about 9.2 ms, and an 8 SPS one up to about 139 ms. Don't time reads on the nominal rate; wait for DOUT/DRDY_N.
- **The 100 Ω input resistors cost up to 0.15% of gain** on the ±0.128 V range (table above).
- **A familiar register layout, but not the same part.** The Config register's fields and frame resemble TI's ADS1118 (our observation), but the SA59401 has four multiplexer settings instead of eight, different data rates (up to 890 SPS), a 12-bit temperature result at 0.125 °C, and a different reset value (098Bh, ±0.256 V). Check every field before reusing an ADS1118 library.
- **Small errors in the text.** "Table 55" means Table 5, "Cofig" means Config, and the temperature examples call the result "12-bit, left-justified" (so shift the 16-bit word right by four). Units dropped out of the application schematic's values (read as µF, as the bill of materials gives them).
