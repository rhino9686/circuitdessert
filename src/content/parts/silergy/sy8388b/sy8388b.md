## How it works

The SY8388B is a constant-on-time synchronous buck with a fixed 3.338 V output. The control method holds a near-constant switching frequency (600 kHz) over line, load and output voltage, needs no compensation network, and works with low-ESR ceramic output capacitors.

**Two enables, three states.** EN2 and EN1 give three states: EN2 low turns everything off; EN2 high with EN1 low turns on only the 3.3 V LDO; both high turn on the LDO and the buck. A voltage above 1 V enables a pin and below 0.4 V disables it. Do not tie EN to IN directly; use a 1 kΩ to 1 MΩ resistor if it is pulled up by IN. EN2 can also be used with a divider to adjust the input UVLO.

**Light-load mode.** The EN1 voltage selects the mode: 1 to 1.6 V gives ultrasonic mode (USM, about 27 kHz at no load, kept above the audible range by forcing the low-side switch on) and 2.2 V up to VIN gives PFM. An internal 2.5 MΩ pulls EN1 to ground.

**LDO and bypass.** The LDO supplies 3.3 V at up to 100 mA from IN. Once EN1 is also high, the output has passed the 2.9 to 3.1 V bypass threshold and PG is high-impedance, the LDO turns off and a 1.2 Ω bypass switch connects the LDO pin to the buck output to save power. It needs a 4.7 µF low-ESR ceramic capacitor.

**Soft start and discharge.** The output ramps over about 1.2 ms. The output is discharged when the converter shuts down from VIN, EN2 or EN1, or on thermal shutdown.

**Protection.** There is a valley and peak current limit on each cycle (12 A minimum on the high-side FET and 10 A minimum on the low-side). Output under-voltage (60%) and over-voltage (120%) both latch the buck off, as does over-temperature at 150 °C with 15 °C of hysteresis. The LDO recovers automatically from its own faults and stays alive in a buck over-temperature shutdown.

**Feedforward.** An RC from the output to FF (the datasheet uses 1 kΩ and 220 pF) is part of the control loop. The datasheet gives 1 kΩ and 2.2 nF for low load current.

### Worked example: 12 V in, 3.338 V at 8 A

The datasheet's own design example uses these numbers, and we repeated its arithmetic.

1. **Ripple.** With L = 1.5 µH at 12 V, ΔIL = 2.68 A (our table gives 2.68 A).
2. **Output ripple voltage.** The datasheet gives 5.36 mV from the ESR and 8.46 mV from the capacitance, 13.82 mV in total, for 3 × 22 µF.
3. **Load step.** A 4 A step gives about 31.78 mV of undershoot and 54.47 mV of overshoot with ceramic capacitors, according to the datasheet.
4. **Current limit.** The output limit is at least 10 A + 1.34 A, so an 8 A load has margin (calculated).

## Layout

The datasheet recommends a 4-layer board with 2-ounce copper and four ground vias on the exposed pad. Keep the FF trace away from LX and BS, Kelvin-connect the OUT pin to the output capacitor, run LX wide between pins 5 and 15/16, and keep the input capacitors close to IN and GND. Do not tie EN to IN directly. A PG pull-up of about 100 kΩ is suggested. The KiCad symbol and the QFN2.5×2.5-16 footprint are ours, built from the pin table and the package drawing (see the package page). Pin 1 is BS.

## Things to watch

- **Latch-off faults.** An output under-voltage, over-voltage or over-temperature event latches the buck off, and EN1 or EN2 or VIN has to be cycled.
- **The output is fixed.** 3.338 V cannot be adjusted.
- **EN pins.** They are rated 26 V, so do not connect them straight to a 24 V rail; use a series resistor.
- **Abs max.** IN is 28 V, which is little above the 24 V maximum operating input.
- **BS and LDO** are rated only 4 V against the LX and ground.
- **Package heat.** The 3 W rating is for the package; the 8 A load needs a good board.
- **The KiCad files are ours and not yet opened in KiCad.** The symbol and the QFN2.5×2.5-16 footprint were generated by script and checked against the pin table and the datasheet's drawing, but nobody has loaded them in KiCad. The pin types and the symbol layout are our choices. The exposed pad's number, 17, is ours.
