## How it works

The SY2A27352 is a low-dropout linear regulator. An error amplifier compares the ADJ pin with a 0.6 V reference and drives a pass FET between IN and OUT, and R1 and R2 turn the 0.6 V into the output you want. The pass element is on the die, there is no external compensation, and a soft start of about 1 ms limits the inrush at power-up.

**The output sets the voltage, the input sets the heat.** An LDO passes the load current straight through, so it dissipates (VIN − VOUT) × IOUT, and at 12 V in and 3.3 V out that is 2.6 W at 300 mA, which is all the 38.3 °C/W package can take at 25 °C ambient (calculated here; see the worked example). Efficiency is VOUT ÷ VIN. It suits a small drop, a small load, or a design where simplicity matters more than efficiency.

**Dropout.** At 300 mA the datasheet gives a drop of 300 mV typical and 540 mV maximum, and at 10 mA 10 mV and 20 mV. That is a pass element of about 1 Ω typical and 1.8 Ω maximum (our arithmetic). The output holds regulation down to VOUT plus the dropout, and the 4 V minimum input applies as well, so a 3.3 V output works from 4 V and a 5 V one from 5.54 V.

**The reset output.** /RESET is an open-drain pin. When the output rises above the reset threshold (90% of its set point, 85–95% over the range), the part starts charging the capacitor on TO with 16 µA. When the voltage on TO passes 1.8 V, /RESET is released, and an external pull-up takes it high. If VOUT falls back below the threshold (5% hysteresis), the part discharges CTO quickly, and /RESET is driven low when TO is below VTF. That gives a timeout of CTO × 1.8 V ÷ 16 µA, and with the TO pin floating the internal capacitance gives 1.6 µs, so /RESET follows the output almost at once.

**Output short.** If the output falls below 16% of its set point (the table puts the ADJ threshold at 8–30% of VREF), the part shuts down for about 16 ms, and then restarts with a complete soft start. If the short is still there, the cycle repeats ("hiccup mode") until thermal shutdown. The current limit (900 mA typical) is specified with the output at 90% of its set point.

**Over-temperature.** The part turns off when the junction reaches 150 °C and comes back about 20 °C lower.

**Undervoltage.** The input UVLO rises through 2.9–4 V (3.3 V typical) with 200 mV of hysteresis, and EN has its own thresholds (1.5 V and 0.4 V).

### Feedback divider (R1, R2)

VOUT = 0.6 V × (1 + R1 ÷ R2), so with R2 chosen, R1 = R2 × (VOUT ÷ 0.6 V − 1). The datasheet recommends 10 kΩ to 2 MΩ for both resistors, with large values for low power at light load.

The divider draws about VREF ÷ R2 from the output, whatever the output voltage: 21.9 µA with the datasheet's 27.4 kΩ, and 12 µA with 49.9 kΩ. That is three times the 7 µA quiescent current, and the datasheet doesn't say whether the 7 µA includes the divider (our note). If it doesn't, the no-load current from the input is about 29 µA with the datasheet's resistors. A larger R2 saves current, but the ADJ pin has a bias current of up to ±50 nA (measured with EN low and ADJ floating, so it may not apply in operation), and that current flows through R1 and R2 in parallel. With R2 = 49.9 kΩ it moves the ADJ voltage by about 2 mV (0.4%) at most. Near the top of the recommended range, at R1 = R2 = 2 MΩ, it would be 50 mV, or 8% of the reference (our arithmetic).

The first design table above gives the outputs of the bill of materials, calculated here, and the second one gives R1 for nine outputs from 1.2 V to 24 V with R2 = 49.9 kΩ. The tolerances are the datasheet's VREF range (±2%) and, in the last column of each, 1% resistors on top, which together give up to about ±3.7% at the output in the worst case.

With the bill of materials, R1 = 124 kΩ gives 3.315 V at the typical reference, and the 123 kΩ in the drawing would give 3.293 V, which is 0.7% lower and well inside the reference tolerance.

### Reset timeout (R3, C3)

TTIMEOUT = CTO × 1.8 V ÷ 16 µA, so CTO = 16 µA × TTIMEOUT ÷ 1.8 V. At typical values, a reset of 1 ms needs 8.9 nF, 10 ms needs 89 nF, 100 ms needs 0.89 µF and 1 s needs 8.9 µF. The 100 nF in the bill of materials gives 11.25 ms at typical values, and a window of 5.8–27.5 ms from the datasheet's limits on the charge current and the threshold, before the capacitor's tolerance. The datasheet's start-up capture, with CTO = 100 nF, shows /RESET released about 10 to 12 ms after the output has risen (read off the 4 ms/div plot, roughly).

With TO left open the timeout is 1.6 µs, which, at 16 µA and 1.8 V, means about 14 pF of internal capacitance (our arithmetic). TO is rated 3.3 V recommended and 3.6 V absolute maximum, unlike every other pin, so only the capacitor goes there.

R3 is a pull-up to VOUT in the datasheet. /RESET is recommended up to 36 V, so it can be pulled to another rail, such as a microcontroller supply that is lower than VOUT. While /RESET is low, R3 draws VOUT ÷ R3, which is 0.33 mA at 3.3 V with 10 kΩ and 0.9 mA at 9 V. The datasheet gives the low level as 0.4 V maximum without a sink current, so how low R3 can go is not stated.

### Input and output capacitors (C1, C2)

The datasheet recommends a 4.7 µF X5R or better ceramic close to the IN and GND pins, with a voltage rating 20% above the maximum voltage, and a loop area between the capacitor and the pins that is as small as possible. For 36 V that is a rating of at least 43 V, so a 50 V part, as in the bill of materials. On OUT it recommends a 4.7 µF low-ESR ceramic for stability over the full temperature range, and says that more capacitance improves the noise, the load transient and the PSRR. The datasheet gives no maximum output capacitance and no ESR range.

The bill of materials uses 50 V parts on both, in 1206. A 50 V 1206 part loses much of its capacitance with a DC bias of 9 V, so the real value is lower (check the maker's DC-bias curve), but the 4.7 µF is a minimum, and the loss is not a problem on the 3.3 V output (our note).

### Enable (K1, R5)

EN is high above 1.5 V and low below 0.4 V, and the datasheet says not to leave it floating. In the example circuit, K1 connects EN to VIN, and R5 pulls it to ground when the switch is open. EN's recommended range (0–36 V) is the same as the input's, so it can be tied to VIN directly. The datasheet gives no EN pin current. With K1 closed, R5 draws VIN ÷ R5: 12 µA at 12 V and 36 µA at 36 V with 1 MΩ, and ten times that with the 100 kΩ of the bill of materials, which at 12 V is already 120 µA, well above the 7 µA quiescent current.

The datasheet's own example would not survive a hot enclosure: at 85 °C ambient the junction is at about 142 °C, 8 °C from the 150 °C shutdown, and the thermal-resistance figure comes from a single-layer test board, so a good board with copper on the exposed pad does better (our reading). **At 12 V in and 3.3 V out**, 300 mA dissipates 2.61 W, 100 °C of rise, which needs a junction at 125 °C when the ambient is 25 °C; the efficiency is 27.5%. Half the load, 150 mA, is 1.3 W and a rise of 50 °C. At 36 V in and 3.3 V out the package can carry about 80 mA at 25 °C and about 32 mA at 85 °C.

**Overload.** In the current limit, with the output held up, the pass element carries 900 mA typical (1.2 A maximum) across the headroom: at 14 V in and 9 V out that is 4.5 W, more than the 2.6 W the package is rated for, so the thermal shutdown ends up deciding the behaviour in a sustained overload, not the current limit (our reading; the datasheet doesn't describe foldback).

## Layout

Silergy gives these rules, with a two-layer example board (top and bottom layers) that has the components around the SO8E.

- Keep **all power traces as short and wide as possible**. A 2-layer or 4-layer board is recommended for heat and current flow.
- Put **at least 6 vias around each power pin** (IN and OUT), to spread the current across the layers.
- Place the **input and output capacitors close to the LDO**, for transient performance.
- **Maximize the copper area connected to the exposed pad**, which carries the heat out. The pin table says to connect the pad to the ground plane.

Pin 1 is /RESET, pin 2 OUT, pin 3 ADJ, pin 4 GND, pin 5 NC (no connection), pin 6 EN, pin 7 IN and pin 8 TO.

## Things to watch

- **Heat sets the load current, not the 300 mA.** The datasheet's 2.6 W at 25 °C is about (125 − 25) ÷ 38.3. At 85 °C ambient it is 1.04 W, so from a 12 V input to 3.3 V the part can supply only about 120 mA, and from 36 V about 32 mA (calculated here). A wide input range makes this the main design limit.
- **The input range doesn't cover automotive transients.** The recommended maximum is 36 V and the absolute maximum 40 V, and the datasheet says nothing about load dump, reverse battery or a reverse current from OUT to IN. An automotive supply needs its own clamp and reverse protection in front (our note).
- **The reset-delay formula needs the capacitor's tolerance too.** The datasheet's formula gives the typical timeout. With its limits on the charge current and the threshold, the window is 0.5× to 2.4× the typical, before the capacitor's own tolerance (see the reset-timeout table).
- **The falling TO threshold is missing.** The text says /RESET is driven low when the voltage on TO is lower than VTF, and VTF has no value in the electrical table. How fast /RESET goes low when the output collapses is therefore not specified, apart from "discharges CTO fast".
- **/RESET low is a maximum with no test current.** The table gives 0.4 V with no sink current, so the pull-up value is a choice you make (R3 = 10 kΩ in the example).
- **The input UVLO reaches the minimum input.** The rising threshold is 2.9–4 V, and 4 V is also the minimum recommended input, so a supply right at 4 V is just inside what is guaranteed. The 200 mV of hysteresis is a typical value only.
- **Ground current under load isn't specified.** The features say "low ground current", but the table gives only the 7 µA typical, 14 µA maximum at no load. The supply-current graph shows about 8 µA, above the 7 µA typical. Neither figure says whether it includes the divider, which draws about 22 µA with the datasheet's resistors.
- **R1 is 123 kΩ in the drawings and 124 kΩ in the bill of materials.** For 3.3 V they give 3.293 V and 3.315 V at the typical reference, a difference of 0.7%.
- **R5 is 1 MΩ in the drawings and 100 kΩ in the bill of materials.** The two differ by a factor of ten in the current drawn from VIN when K1 is closed (see the enable section).
- **C3's part number doesn't match its description.** The description is "100 nF / 50 V / 0603", but the part number GRM033R61E104KE14D reads, in Murata's code, as a 0201 part with a 25 V rating (our reading of the code). Check the part before you copy the bill of materials.
- **The output-short threshold is given two ways.** The table has 8%, 16% and 30% of VREF for the ADJ pin, and the text says 16% of the OUT set point. They agree for the typical value.
- **The product page lists an auto-discharge feature.** The datasheet says only that the part discharges CTO quickly when the output falls. It doesn't say that the output capacitor is discharged when EN goes low (our reading), so don't rely on it for a fast discharge.
- **Units can drop out of a copy of the PDF.** The default reset rise time is printed as 1.6 µs. If your copy shows "1.6 s", it has lost the µ sign: with the datasheet's own formula, 1.6 s would need 14 µF inside the chip.
- **Minor typos.** The figure labels read "R/RESERT" and "/REST", the general description says "ca be adjusted", and the pin table calls the pin /RESET.
- **The revision date is given two ways.** The revision history says Rev 1.0 is dated Sep. 25, 2023, the product page dates the same revision 2023-10-20, and the page footers carry © 2021 and © 2022.
