## How it works

The SY6813C is a switch between IN and OUT. The block diagram shows the pass switch, a soft-start circuit, a control block, an output-discharge resistor and the pins IN, OUT, GND and ON; the datasheet doesn't say what kind of transistor the switch is. The input range is 1.2 to 5.5 V, and every pin is rated to 6 V.

**Turn-on.** When ON goes above its threshold the output ramps up at a controlled rate instead of following the input at once. With 3.6 V in, 0.1 µF on the output and a 10 Ω load, the rise takes 550 µs; with 1.8 V in it takes 280 µs. Both work out to about 6.5 V per millisecond (calculated), so the rise time scales with the input voltage and the charging current into a capacitor is proportional to its size (the inrush table above). The feature list also mentions a controlled turn-on delay, but no delay time is given.

**Turn-off.** When ON goes low the switch opens and a 50 Ω resistor (35 to 70 Ω) pulls OUT toward ground, so the output of a lightly loaded rail falls quickly instead of floating.

**ON pin.** Active high, with 1 V as the guaranteed high level and 0.4 V as the guaranteed low level. The datasheet's plot of the thresholds shows the actual trip points between those limits, about 0.6 V at 1.2 V in rising to about 0.78 V at 5.5 V, with a falling threshold about 0.04 V lower (read by eye). Design to the table's limits, not to the plot. The pin must not be left floating, and its recommended range is −0.3 V to VIN + 0.3 V.

**What is not there.** The datasheet describes no current limit, no short-circuit or over-temperature protection, and no reverse-current blocking. Treat it as a switch, not an eFuse. Silergy's site describes other load switches, such as the SY20813 family, as having reverse-current blocking and a current limit; this part's datasheet claims neither.

### Capacitors (CIN, COUT)

**Input.** The datasheet recommends a 1 µF ceramic capacitor close to IN and GND, to limit the input current's disturbance at turn-on, and says a larger value reduces the voltage drop at the input when the switch turns on into a large capacitive load. It names no dielectric or voltage rating. For a 5 V input, a 10 V rating or more with DC-bias derating in mind is our suggestion; check the capacitor's own bias curve.

**Output.** A 1 µF ceramic capacitor, to stop parasitic board inductance from forcing VOUT below ground when the switch opens. The rise-time figures are measured with only 0.1 µF, so the ramp with 1 µF or more is not shown. The output capacitance also sets the discharge time (table above).

### Slew rate and inrush

Treat the controlled ramp as setting a charging current of about 6.5 mA per microfarad of load capacitance (calculated; see the inrush table and its assumption). A 47 µF bulk capacitor then draws about 0.3 A while it charges, a 220 µF capacitor about 1.4 A, which is a large share of the 3 A rating and is added to whatever the load itself draws as it powers up. The upstream supply must be able to deliver that, and the input capacitor, which holds only 3.6 µC at 3.6 V (1 µF), supplies almost none of it. If a load capacitor and a heavy resistive load are switched on together, the peak current is the sum.

### ON control

The ON pin tolerates VIN + 0.3 V, not the 6 V absolute maximum, in normal use. A 3.3 V logic signal on ON with a 1.8 V input is therefore outside the recommended range, and so is 1.8 V logic on a 1.2 V input (table above). The datasheet's own rise-time measurement at 1.8 V in drives ON at 3.6 V. If the logic is on a higher supply than the switch, use a level shifter or a series resistor with a clamp, and check the pin's input current, which the datasheet doesn't give.

### Output discharge (50 Ω)

The discharge resistor is switched in when ON is low. With 100 µF on the output it takes about 11 ms to reach 10% (table above). It is a feature when the load must be defined off; it is a problem when OUT is held high by something else, because the resistor then draws VOUT ÷ 50 Ω, 100 mA from a 5 V rail, as long as the switch is off. The datasheet doesn't describe OUT being driven above IN.

### Worked example: a 3.3 V peripheral rail at 1 A

A 3.3 V rail feeds a peripheral that draws up to 1 A and has 47 µF of local capacitance. The switch is turned on and off by a 3.3 V logic signal. The figures marked "by eye" are read from the datasheet's plots; the rest is calculated.

**What matters here.** The switch itself costs next to nothing at 1 A: a few tens of millivolts and a few tens of milliwatts. The part of the design that needs thought is the turn-on: 0.3 A for half a millisecond into the capacitor, added to the peripheral's own current, taken from the 3.3 V rail through a 1 µF input capacitor. The 3.3 V logic on ON is within range with a 3.3 V input, and out of range if the switch's input is lower. And there is no protection if the peripheral shorts, so size the upstream supply's own current limit for that.

- **Power traces** short and wide, in at least 2 oz copper.

## Things to watch

- **There is no protection beyond the soft start.** No current limit, short-circuit protection, thermal shutdown or reverse-current blocking is described. A shorted load is limited only by the source and the switch's own resistance.
- **Inrush is about 6.5 mA per microfarad.** That is 0.3 A into 47 µF and 1.4 A into 220 µF, on top of the load (calculated from the datasheet's rise times, assuming the ramp rate doesn't change with the load).
- **ON must stay within VIN + 0.3 V.** 3.3 V logic on a 1.8 V rail, or 1.8 V logic on a 1.2 V rail, is outside the recommended range.
- **On-resistance is given only at 5 V in.** At 1.2 to 3.3 V it is probably higher, as it is for most switches of this kind, but the datasheet doesn't say; measure it if the drop matters.
- **The output discharge loads the output whenever the switch is off.** If another supply can hold OUT up, it will draw 100 mA at 5 V through the resistor.
- **A backfed output is not described.** The datasheet says nothing about OUT above IN, or about a body diode.
- **The thermal figure is from a poor board.** θJA is 123 °C/W on a low-conductivity single-layer board; the 3 A, 30 mΩ case is 33 °C of rise on that board, about 118 °C at 85 °C ambient (calculated), so it fits the 125 °C operating limit, with little margin on a board like it.

## Datasheet notes

- **On-resistance: table and plot.** The electrical table gives 22 mΩ typical (30 mΩ maximum) at VIN = 5 V. The RDS(ON) plot, for 5 V in and ON = 3 V, reads about 25 mΩ at 0.5 A and 26.2 mΩ at 3 A, above the typical value. The plot's ON voltage (3 V) differs from the 5 V ON level, which the table doesn't state, so they may not be the same condition.
- **The 1.8 V rise-time test** drives ON at 3.6 V, over the recommended maximum of VIN + 0.3 V (2.1 V), though within the 6 V absolute maximum.
- **Controlled turn-on delay** is in the feature list; no delay value appears in the electrical table or the plots' text.
- **The 3 A rating** is in the feature list and the title page only. The electrical table has no current rating or test condition for it.
- **Quiescent current** is 4 µA typical at 5 V in the table, with no minimum; the plot gives lower values at lower input voltages.
- **Revision dates.** The revision history dates Rev 1.0 (production release) January 7, 2023 and Rev 0.9 (initial release) January 7, 2022, one year apart on the same day; the footer says © 2025.
- **The capacitor values** are 1 µF at the input and output in Figure 1 and the text; the captures use 0.1 µF at the output.
