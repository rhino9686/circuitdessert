## How it works

The SY22101A is a boost converter that regulates current instead of voltage. LEDs need a set current, and a string of them in series needs more voltage than a lithium cell gives: ten white LEDs at 20 mA take about 30 V. The converter steps the input up to whatever voltage the string needs to carry the current set by R1.

**Current regulation.** The LED string runs from the output to FB, and R1 runs from FB to ground, so the whole string current flows through R1. The converter adjusts its duty cycle to hold FB at 200 mV, which makes the LED current 0.2 V ÷ R1 whatever the LEDs' forward voltage. Only 0.2 V is lost in R1, which matters at high currents: 20 mW at 100 mA.

**The power stage.** A 400 mΩ, 40 V switch inside the part pulls LX to ground at a fixed 1 MHz, storing energy in the inductor; when it opens, the inductor current flows through an external Schottky diode into the output capacitor and the LEDs. The switch current is limited to 1 A (typical), which sets how much LED current the part can deliver: high for short strings and high inputs, lower for long strings and low inputs. The table *Highest LED current at the switch limit* above gives typical figures (calculated). The duty cycle can reach 90% (guaranteed) or 93% (typical), so the input must be at least about a tenth of the output voltage, and in practice somewhat more.

**Dimming.** A PWM signal on EN/PWM, between 10 kHz and 1 MHz, sets the FB regulation voltage to its duty cycle times 200 mV: 50% duty gives 100 mV and half the current. The LEDs see a steady, lower current rather than a chopped one, so there is no visible flicker and no audible noise from the output capacitor. Accuracy falls at low duty: at 5% the FB voltage is 4 to 16 mV, against 10 mV nominal. Alternatively, a DC voltage, or a filtered PWM signal, fed through resistors into FB lowers the current as it rises (Figures 4 and 5).

**Protection.** The OVP pin watches the output. If the string opens, FB falls to zero and the converter would drive the output up without limit; OVP stops it at 37 V or above (the datasheet gives only the minimum), below the switch's 40 V rating. Input UVLO, a soft start at power-up and thermal shutdown at 150 °C complete the protection.

**Shutdown.** EN/PWM low shuts the part down to about 1 µA. A boost converter has a path from the input to the output through the inductor and diode even when it is off, so the string must need more voltage to light than the input can give. With several white LEDs that is automatic; a string of one or two infrared LEDs from a 4.2 V cell would light, uncontrolled, without switching (our note).

### LED current (R1)

R1 = 0.2 V ÷ ILED: 10 Ω for 20 mA. The reference is ±2%, so with a 1% resistor the current is within about ±3% (the table *Sense resistor against LED current* above). Keep R1 and the FB trace away from LX (layout guide). The datasheet gives no LED current rating; the switch limit sets the ceiling, and the duty-cycle and switch-limit tables above show where it falls for a given string and input.

### Inductor and diode (L, D)

The datasheet recommends 10 µH, with low DCR and core loss at 1 MHz and a saturation rating above the peak current; its suggested part saturates at 1.13 A, above the 1 A switch limit, so it can't saturate even into the limit. In the worked example below the peak is about 0.36 A.

The diode must be a Schottky, for its low drop and fast switching, rated above the highest output voltage. That voltage is the OVP level when the string opens, 37 V or more, so a 40 V diode is the practical minimum. Its average current is the LED current and its peak the inductor peak.

### Capacitors (CIN, COUT)

Input: the text recommends an X5R or better ceramic of more than 4.7 µF close to IN and GND, though Figure 1 shows 1 µF. The ripple current is VIN × (VOUT − VIN) ÷ (2√3 × L × fSW × VOUT), about 0.09 A in the worked example (calculated).

Output: the minimum is IOUT × (VOUT − VIN) ÷ (fSW × VOUT × VRIPPLE), and the datasheet recommends more than 1 µF. For 20 mA at 30 V from 3.6 V, 1 µF gives about 18 mV of ripple (calculated). Its voltage rating must cover the open-LED case, when the output rises to the OVP level: a 50 V part, and allow for the loss of capacitance under DC bias (our suggestion).

### Dimming

**PWM on EN/PWM.** FB regulates at duty × 200 mV, so the LED current follows the duty cycle linearly. Keep the frequency between 10 kHz and 1 MHz. At low duty the accuracy suffers: 5% duty gives 0.4 to 1.6 mA at a 20 mA setting, against 1 mA nominal (calculated from the 4–16 mV limits). The datasheet doesn't say how long EN must stay low before the part shuts down rather than treating the low time as part of a PWM cycle.

**DC voltage into FB (Figure 4).** A resistor R1 (in that figure) from FB to the top of the sense resistor RFB, and R2 from FB to a dimming voltage VDC, give ILED = (VFB × (R1 + R2) − VDC × R1) ÷ (R2 × RFB). Raising VDC lowers the current. For example, with 10 kΩ and 100 kΩ and RFB = 11 Ω, the current is 20 mA at 0 V, 10.9 mA at 1 V and zero at 2.2 V (calculated). Note that the figure reuses the name R1 for this resistor; on this page R1 is the sense resistor.

**Filtered PWM (Figure 5).** The same with R2 split into two equal resistors R2 and R3 and a capacitor C1 from their midpoint to ground, so a PWM signal of amplitude VPWM acts like a DC voltage of duty × VPWM. Choose the filter's corner, set by R2 ∥ R3 and C1, well below the PWM frequency.

**EN pull-down.** If EN is driven by a chip whose output floats in shutdown while the input stays connected to a battery, add 1 MΩ from EN to ground so noise can't turn the part on (layout guide).

### Worked example: ten white LEDs from a lithium cell

Ten white LEDs at 20 mA, about 30 V across the string and R1 (the datasheet's test condition), powered from a lithium cell from 4.2 V down to 3.0 V. R1 = 10 Ω, 10 µH, and 88% efficiency, close to the datasheet's plot for ten LEDs above about 3.5 V (by eye; it reads about 87% at 3.0 V and 83% at 2.5 V). Everything below is calculated.

| Quantity | 3.0 V in | 3.6 V in | 4.2 V in |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Output power | 0.6 W | 0.6 W | 0.6 W |
| Duty cycle (ideal) | 90.1% | 88.2% | 86.2% |
| Input current | 227 mA | 189 mA | 162 mA |
| Inductor ripple | 0.27 A | 0.32 A | 0.36 A |
| Peak inductor current | 0.36 A | 0.35 A | 0.34 A (discontinuous) |
| Input capacitor ripple current | 0.08 A | 0.09 A | 0.10 A |

**The low end of the cell is the problem, not the current.** The peak current is far below the 1 A limit, but at 3.0 V the converter needs more than 90% duty, the guaranteed maximum, and losses add a few points to the ideal figure. The datasheet's own plots run ten LEDs from 2.5 V, which relies on the 93% typical maximum. A part at the low end of that spread could let the LED current sag below about 3.1 to 3.3 V of input. Options: use nine LEDs (27 V), accept the dimming near the end of the cell, or, if the LEDs have a lower forward voltage, check the string voltage at the coldest temperature, where it is highest (our reading).

**Losses and heat.** At 3.6 V the switch conducts about 16 mW, the inductor's 237 mΩ about 10 mW, the diode 8 mW and R1 4 mW, plus switching losses not modelled here. The switch's share raises the die by about 4 °C at 227 °C/W.

**Open string.** If an LED fails open, the output climbs to the OVP level, 37 V or more, and stops. The diode, the output capacitor and the LX pin all see that voltage, which is why they need 40 V or more.

## Layout

From the datasheet's layout guide:

- **CIN, COUT, L, R1 and D close to the IC.**
- **A large ground copper area**, a ground plane if space allows.
- **CIN close to IN and GND**, with the smallest loop.
- **The smallest loop through LX, D, COUT and GND**, the boost converter's fast-switching loop.
- **Small LX copper** to limit noise.
- **R1 and the FB trace away from LX.**
- **1 MΩ from EN to GND** if the EN driver floats in shutdown and the input is a battery.

## Things to watch

- **Ten white LEDs need more than 3 V in.** At 30 V the duty cycle exceeds the 90% guaranteed maximum below about 3.0 V ideally, and somewhat higher with losses. The datasheet's plots rely on the 93% typical.
- **"Up to 40 V" is the switch rating, not the string voltage.** The title says 40 V, but the recommended maximum on LX and OVP is 38 V and OVP trips at 37 V or more. Keep the string, at its coldest, a few volts below 37 V.
- **The switch current limit is typical only** (1 A at 90% duty, no minimum), so the LED-current ceilings above are typical too.
- **The OVP threshold has only a minimum** (37 V) against a 40 V absolute maximum, so the margin can't be checked from the datasheet.
- **PWM dimming is inaccurate at low duty**: at 5% the LED current can be 40% to 160% of nominal.
- **The input UVLO's maximum is 2.5 V**, the same as the minimum input, so a part may not start right at 2.5 V. Silergy's product page gives 2.7 V as the minimum input.
- **Short strings light from the input.** The string voltage must stay above the highest input voltage plus the diode drop, or current flows through L and D even with the part off.
- **Small inconsistencies in the datasheet.** The text recommends more than 4.7 µF on the input, Figure 1 shows 1 µF. The efficiency plot is labelled "SY7310A", the part's other name. Figures 4 and 5 call their dimming resistor R1, the same name as the sense resistor in Figure 1. The package is TSOT23-6 in the datasheet and SOT23-6 on the product page. "Ptotection" in the block diagram and "Starup" in a plot title.
