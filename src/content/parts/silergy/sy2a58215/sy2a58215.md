## How it works

The SY2A58215 holds four MOSFETs in two half-bridges. Q1 and Q2 are the high and low side of the half-bridge on OUTA, and Q3 and Q4 are those on OUTB. Each MOSFET has a gate driver and an overvoltage clamp. Each high side adds a current limit, each low side adds an overcurrent shutdown, and each output has an open-load detector. A logic block with fault detection controls them all, and a multiplexer chooses which high-side current mirror feeds the CS pin. Undervoltage lockout and overtemperature protection sit on the supply.

**Driving it** takes four signals. In the datasheet's Table 1 and Table 4, a high on INA with INB low turns on the high side of OUTA and, while PWM is high, the low side of OUTB, which drives the motor forward. INA low with INB high does the reverse. When PWM goes low in either direction, the low side switches off and the high side stays on. Both inputs high turns on both high sides, which shorts the motor terminals together at the supply (a brake). Both inputs low with PWM high turns on both low sides, which shorts them at ground (also a brake), and both inputs low with PWM low turns everything off, so the motor coasts. PWM drives the low-side MOSFETs only, according to the pin table. The high side responds slowly (delays of 25 µs on and 15 µs off, and a 10 µs rise time, typical), so the speed signal goes on PWM at up to 20 kHz.

**Changing direction** adds a cross-conduction delay, tcross, of 40 to 350 µs (140 µs typical). The part never turns on a high side and a low side in the same half-bridge together. The low side waits for the high side to turn off, which is slower than turning on.

**Standby** begins when all four logic inputs have been low for tDSTB (0.2–1.8 ms). The part then draws a few microamps at most, and any input rising from low to high wakes it.

**CS** has three jobs. In normal operation it sources the load current of the selected high side divided by K (Equation 1), and an external resistor turns that into a voltage (Equation 2). SEL0 high selects half-bridge A, and SEL0 low selects B, when the high side in question is on. When the selected side faults, CS is instead driven to the fixed voltage VSENSEH. And with the bridge off (INA = INB = PWM = 0), CS reports an open load: an external pull-up holds the output that SEL0 selects (OUTA for SEL0 high) above the threshold VOL of 2–4 V when the motor is missing. The check becomes active 40–350 µs after the inputs fall (tDIADLF), and CS reaches VSENSEH 5–30 µs after the output crosses VOL (tDIADLR).

**What trips a fault.** Each protection acts on a half-bridge, and the CS pin shows it only when SEL0 selects that half-bridge (Table 2):

- **High side:** a short to ground makes the high side current-limit (15–30 A), and after about 10 µs the driver disables it and CS goes to VSENSEH. Overtemperature (170 °C typical) disables it too.
- **Low side:** a hard short to VCC shuts the low side down at 18–36 A after a filter time of about 5 µs. A resistive short, with less current, ends in the low-side thermal shutdown instead.
- **Latching.** All of these latch. A short pulse on the matching INx pin (3–20 µs) clears the fault, once the cause is gone.
- **Supply.** Below the undervoltage lockout for about 5 µs, everything is disabled and the logic resets, and it restarts about 1 µs after VCC recovers. Above the clamp (38–52 V), the part forces the low side into its linear region and the high side into saturation, which dissipates a lot of power, so the datasheet says external protection is needed.
- **Ground and supply loss.** If GND disconnects, the chip's supply current (about 10 mA, IGND_loss) leaves through the ESD structures of the logic pins, the part detects it, enters standby and switches the MOSFETs off. If VCC disconnects, the motor runs from the bulk capacitor until VCC falls below the lockout.

### Supply capacitors (E1, C1 to C3)

E1 (100 µF, 50 V) is the bulk capacitor, and it supplies the motor when VCC is lost. C1 to C3 (3 × 10 µF, 50 V, 1206) filter VCC and must sit as close to the VCC and GND pins as possible. The datasheet says to put two decoupling capacitors on the same layer for noise immunity, and to join the layers with several vias if VCC and GND are not on one layer. The voltage ratings are 50 V against a 28 V supply and an overvoltage clamp that starts at 38 V (46 V typical, 52 V maximum), so they sit between the typical and the maximum clamp level. The datasheet gives no reason for the figure and no ripple-current rule. Its operation figures use 1000 µF instead of 100 µF.

### Output capacitors (C4, C5)

100 nF from each output to ground (50 V, 0603). The datasheet's only comment is that this reduces sparking from a DC motor.

### Logic input resistors (R1 to R4)

Each input has a 1 kΩ series resistor. The datasheet's reason is the loss-of-ground case: if GND disconnects, about 10 mA leaves through the ESD structures of the logic pins, and the series resistor limits what the microcontroller pin has to take. The datasheet gives the condition and the minimum value:

**VMCU − ⅓ × IGND_loss × Rinput − VESD ≤ VIL** (Equation 3), or **Rinput ≥ (VMCU − VIL − VESD) × 3 ÷ IGND_loss** (Equation 4).

The datasheet doesn't give VESD or explain the ⅓. VIL is 0.9 V at most, and IGND_loss is "about 10 mA". The worked example below uses VESD = 0.7 V, which is our assumption.

### Current-sense network (R7, R8, R9, C8)

CS is a current source with a fixed ratio, so the voltage you read is ISENSE × a resistance. In the application schematic, we read the drawing this way: CS goes through R7 to a junction, R8 goes from the junction to ground, and R9 goes from the junction to the ADC terminal, which has C8 to ground. The CS pin then drives R7 + R8 = 540 Ω, the junction voltage is ISENSE × R8 = ISENSE × 270 Ω, and R9 with C8 forms a low-pass filter into the ADC. The worked example gives the numbers. Figure 1 shows a different network, with no values: a series resistor R5 into the CS node, a resistor R6 from the node to ground, and a capacitor C2 on the terminal side of R5. Figures 10 and 11 use 4.7 kΩ in series, 1 kΩ to ground and 1 µF. The datasheet doesn't say why the three differ.

### Open-load pull-up

For open-load detection with the bridge off, a resistor Rpull-up from the supply to the monitored output must hold that output above VOL when the motor is missing. The datasheet gives the upper limit: **Rpull-up ≤ (VBAT − VOL_MAX) ÷ (−2 × IL(off2)_MIN)** (Equation 5), with VOL_MAX = 4 V and IL(off2)_MIN = −135 µA. It gives no lower limit. With the motor present, the pull-up current flows through the winding, so the resistor also has to be large enough that the output stays below the 2 V minimum threshold for a healthy motor. That depends on the winding resistance, which the datasheet doesn't cover (our reasoning).

With the high side on, the motor current passes through one high side and one low side, so the conduction loss is I² × (RDS(on) high + RDS(on) low). The datasheet gives 40 mΩ + 20 mΩ = 60 mΩ typical at 25 °C, and 85 mΩ + 60 mΩ = 145 mΩ as the maximum over −40 to 125 °C. θJA is 55 °C/W, measured on Silergy's demo board. #### Fault level against normal readings

When CS signals a fault, it is driven to VSENSEH, which is 5–7 V in the electrical table (measured with 1 kΩ). Into 540 Ω that is 9–13 mA, inside the 7–30 mA the datasheet gives for ISENSEH, and the junction sees half of it, **2.5–3.5 V**. At 15 A and K = 1540 the normal reading at the junction is 2.63 V, and over the datasheet's K range for 3.5 A (1315–1779) it is 2.28–3.08 V. So above about 14 A the normal sense voltage at the ADC terminal reaches the fault band, and the two can't be told apart by voltage alone (our arithmetic, assuming VSENSEH is 5–7 V with this load).

#### Filter

R9 and C8 give a corner of 1 ÷ (2π × 4.7 kΩ × 1 µF) = 34 Hz, or 32 Hz including the 270 Ω source resistance of the junction. That is slow: the ADC sees an average of the load current, with a time constant of about 5 ms, so short current spikes are filtered away and a fault flag takes several milliseconds to show at the ADC. A smaller C8 or R9 gives a faster reading (our observation; the datasheet doesn't discuss the filter).

#### Logic input resistors

#### Timing

At 20 kHz the PWM period is 50 µs. The low side has a typical turn-on delay of 0.4 µs and turn-off delay of 2 µs, so its on-time at the output runs about 1.6 µs longer than the PWM pulse, 3% of the period. Direction changes cost up to 350 µs of dead time, and the high-side delays of 25 µs and 15 µs mean the INA and INB inputs can't carry a 20 kHz signal.

## Layout

Silergy gives these rules, along with a reference board (top layer and silkscreen) in the datasheet.

- Plan the floor first. Keep the current loops small, avoid sharp corners and narrow paths, so the current can flow smoothly.
- Put the **VCC filter capacitors (C1 to C3) as close to the pins as possible**, to keep the parasitic capacitance and inductance of the traces low.
- Put two decoupling capacitors on **the same layer**, which improves noise immunity. If the GND and VCC copper are on different layers, connect them with several vias.
- Put a **capacitor between each output and ground** (C4, C5) to reduce sparking with a DC motor.
- Use a **multilayer board** rather than a two-layer one for heat, with 2 oz or thicker copper if possible, and join several ground planes with vias.

The thermal resistance is 55 °C/W (θJA) and 17 °C/W (θJC), measured on Silergy's demo board in still air at 25 °C. The pin table lists pins 1 and 16 as GNDA, 8 and 9 as GNDB, 4, 5 and 12 as VCC, 2 and 15 as OUTA, and 7 and 10 as OUTB. The schematics draw each as a single pin and the datasheet doesn't say how to treat the extra pins; connecting all pins of the same name is our assumption. The package outline shows top, side and front views only, with no land pattern.

## What the datasheet shows

The typical-performance pages are six oscilloscope captures at 20 µs per division. They show forward (OUTA to OUTB) and reverse (OUTB to OUTA) operation at VCC = 13 V with loads of 5 A and 10 A, and at VCC = 28 V with 15 A. Each shows OUTA, OUTB, CS and the load current. We haven't read values off the captures. There are no efficiency, thermal-derating, on-resistance-against-temperature or sense-linearity plots.

The timing pages define the switching delays, cross-conduction, the off-state diagnostic delays and the fault unlatch pulses (Figures 3 to 9). The detailed description adds waveforms for normal operation with the four drive modes (Figures 10 and 11), power-on and standby (Figures 12 and 13), the overvoltage clamp (Figures 14 and 15), undervoltage lockout (Figure 16), loss of ground and of supply (Figures 17 and 18), short-circuit behaviour (Figures 19 to 22), shoot-through protection (Figure 23) and open-load detection (Figure 24).

## Things to watch

- **15 A is a peak figure, not a continuous rating.** At 60 mΩ and 55 °C/W, the conduction loss alone puts the junction at 150 °C near 6 A from a 25 °C ambient, and near 4 A at the maximum on-resistance (see Heat). The datasheet gives no duty-cycle or pulse-length limit for 15 A, and the high-side limit starts at 15 A, so a motor stall at exactly 15 A can already hit it.
- **The electrical table is specified for 7–28 V.** The recommended range starts at 4 V, but the rising undervoltage lockout is 4 V typical and 5 V maximum, so at 4–5 V the part may not run. Test cold-crank behaviour.
- **The fault flag and a 15 A reading can look alike at the ADC.** With the datasheet's 270 Ω pair, the fault level at the ADC terminal is 2.5–3.5 V, and the normal reading near 14–15 A is 2.3–3.1 V (see the worked example). It depends on our reading of the drawing, so check on a real board.
- **K is specified to 5.5 A only.** The output current ratio has no spread above that, the sense-saturation figures (4.6 mA, 8 A minimum) aren't explained, and the reference circuit, as we read it, would put 5.3 V on the CS pin at 15 A. The electrical table's "Max analog sense output voltage" is 5 V at VCC = 7 V and 10 kΩ.
- **CS can read zero in a hard current limit.** The datasheet's note says that when a high side is in current limit and its output falls 3 V below VCC, CS stops sourcing current and is pulled down until the thermal protection trips and sets the fault flag. A reading of zero is not "no current".
- **A fault shows on CS only for the half-bridge that SEL0 selects.** In Table 2, the last two rows (INA = INB = 1) show CS as Hi-Z with a latched fault in the high side that SEL0 does not select, while Table 1 says CS would carry the other, healthy side's sense current. Either way, a firmware that wants both sides has to alternate SEL0 (our reading).
- **The reset pulse is described two ways.** The text says to drive "a short high-level pulse" on the INx pin to restart after a fault. The high-side figure (Figure 8) and the timing table's test condition (5 V to 0 V) show a low pulse on an input that was high. The low-side figure (Figure 9) and its condition (0 V to 5 V) show a high pulse on an input that was low.
- **Open load on OUTB must be checked quickly.** The fixed voltage on CS is available only before the part enters standby, tDSTB (0.2–1.8 ms) after all inputs go low, and CS is pulled down 47 µs after that. The datasheet singles out the OUTB check (SEL0 low). Our reading is that standby needs SEL0 low too (Table 5), so a check on OUTA with SEL0 high never enters standby, while the OUTB check has to finish within that window.
- **The datasheet describes the pull-up in two ways.** Equation 5, Figure 24 and Table 3 put it on the monitored output, and a flag then means an open load. The "no fault" paragraph on page 14 puts it on the other output, where the flag means the motor is present.
- **Table 4 labels a PWM-driven state "Coast".** Modes 2 and 4 have INA = INB = 0 with PWM at 20 kHz, and by Table 1 PWM high with both inputs low turns both low sides on, which brakes. The datasheet doesn't say which it means.
- **The voltage ratings overlap.** The absolute maximum on VCC and the outputs is 38 V, and the clamp starts at 38 V (46 V typical), so a transient that reaches the clamp is past the rated maximum, and the datasheet gives no energy rating for the clamp beyond the 100 mA, 1 ms test.
- **Three different CS networks are drawn.** Figure 1 (R5, R6, C2), the application schematic (R7, R8, R9, C8) and the operation figures (4.7 kΩ, 1 kΩ, 1 µF) differ, as do the designators on the logic inputs and the bulk capacitor (100 µF against 1000 µF).
- **Units have dropped out of the PDF.** The micro sign is missing in many entries, so "1 A" and "8 A" for the sleep current, "5 s" for the low-side shutdown time and "25 s" for the power-on delay are read as µA and µs, as the text and the product page agree. The standby text says "less than 3uA", and the table allows 8 µA at 125 °C.
- **The revision label is mixed.** The footer says Rev.1.0 and the revision history ends at 1.0A (formula 3 corrected, June 2, 2025). The product page dates the file 2026-01-15 and gives no revision.
