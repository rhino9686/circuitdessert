## How it works

The SY5867Z converts a lighting dimmer's control signal into a PWM signal. Whatever is connected to DIMI is turned into a PWM waveform on DIMO whose duty is reversed: the datasheet draws the result as "100% − DDIMO", so more dimming voltage at DIMI means less DIMO high time. The DIMO signal goes to the dimming input of a constant-current regulator, or to an optocoupler that carries it across an isolation barrier.

**Three input types, one pin.** The part decides which one it sees:

- **0/1–10 V analog.** The voltage on DIMI is converted into a reversed duty. A passive dimmer needs a current to drive it, and the part supplies one out of DIMI.
- **Resistor.** A variable resistor on DIMI turns the source current into a voltage (IDIMI × R), and from there it behaves as the 0/1–10 V input does.
- **PWM.** A PWM signal on DIMI comes out as a reversed PWM. The datasheet's PWM thresholds are 2.3 V maximum to read high and 0.8 V minimum to read low. Its Note 3 says that a 100% duty PWM whose amplitude is not 10 V cannot be told from a DC level, so the part may not recognise it as PWM; at a 10 V amplitude the maximum duty is 100%.

**The transfer curve.** For a 0–10 V input the dimming duty rises along a straight line from a minimum duty at 1.37 V (VMIN, typical) to a maximum duty at 9.37 V (VHIGH, typical), as the datasheet draws its curve, and stays flat above 9.37 V. In low clamp mode the part dims to off when DIMI falls below 0.97 V (typical), and turns on again once DIMI rises past 1.17 V, so there is hysteresis of about 0.2 V. The two ends are set by two clamps:

- **Low clamp (VLOW pin)** sets the minimum duty. The datasheet says it is meant for regulating a light load in special applications.
- **High clamp (HCLP pin)** sets the maximum duty, for regulating the full load in special applications.

Each clamp is a resistor divider from the 2.21 V on ISET to ground, so the pin voltage is 2.21 V × RD ÷ (RU + RD). Only some combinations are clamp modes: the datasheet says that when VHCLP is above 1.336 V the upper turning point of DIMI is always 9.37 V, and when VVLOW is below 1.023 V the lower turning point is always about 1.36 V. The formulas are in the specification tables and worked through in the design example below.

**Frequency.** A 20 µA current charges and discharges the capacitor on FSET to make a triangle wave, and the output frequency is 30×10⁻⁶ ÷ ((3 × VHCLP − VVLOW) × CFSET). So the clamp voltages change the frequency, not just the duty range.

**Supply.** An internal 60 V regulator powers the chip from VIN, so the supply can come through a resistor (RVIN) from a higher rail, with a capacitor (CVIN) on VIN. Before VIN reaches the turn-on threshold (9.1 V typical) DIMO is low; after that DIMO follows DIMI. The turn-off threshold is 7.7 V typical. Its Figure 4 (the internal LDO) shows a shunt that takes current if VIN goes above 55 V, which the datasheet says protects the part, and the recommended range is 12 V to 55 V.

### How the ISET pin does three jobs

The ISET pin is a 2.21 V source, and in the datasheet's circuit it does three things at once:

- it is the reference for the HCLP divider (maximum duty);
- it is the reference for the VLOW divider (minimum duty);
- it sets the current out of DIMI, IDIMI = 5 × 2.21 V ÷ RISET.

In the example RISET is not a separate resistor: it is the parallel combination of the two divider chains, (RHCLP_U + RHCLP_D) ∥ (RVLOW_U + RVLOW_D). That means the current out of DIMI follows from the dividers: 5 × the current both chains draw from ISET (60 µA and 42 µA for the final circuit, 507 µA in all after the factor of five, our arithmetic). So the clamp resistors and the source current are not independent. The datasheet's design steps pick the VLOW divider first, then the HCLP divider's total resistance so that the parallel result gives the wanted current. Whether a separate resistor on ISET alone is allowed is not stated: the pin table says "RISET", and no schematic shows one.

### Setting the minimum and maximum duty

The datasheet's order is to choose RVLOW_D (20 kΩ in the example), find the VLOW pin voltage that gives the minimum duty, compute RVLOW_U, then find the HCLP voltage for the maximum duty, and split the HCLP chain.

- **Minimum duty** depends on both clamp voltages: DMIN = (1.023 − VVLOW) ÷ (3 × VHCLP − VVLOW), with an extra 0.08 × (VVLOW − 0.75)² added by the pin table when VVLOW is 0.65 V or below. The first calculated table gives DMIN against VVLOW and VHCLP. Higher VVLOW lowers DMIN, and it reaches zero at 1.023 V.
- **Maximum duty** also depends on both: DMAX = (8.016 − 2 × VVLOW) ÷ (6 × VHCLP − 2 × VVLOW), less (VHCLP − 1.8) × 0.014. Higher VHCLP lowers DMAX (second calculated table).
- **Where the formulas apply.** The datasheet states the minimum-duty formula for a VVLOW below 1.023 V and the maximum-duty formula for a VHCLP above 1.336 V. Its first-page transition curve shows a different shape on the other side of 1.336 V, and it gives no formulas there.

### Setting the frequency

Choose CFSET after the clamps, because the clamp voltages are in the formula (calculated table above). With the final circuit's clamps, 8.2 nF gives about 1055 Hz, and the frequency scales as 1 ÷ CFSET. The datasheet gives no tolerance or range for fDIM.

### Powering a resistor dimmer

With a resistor on DIMI, the dimming voltage is IDIMI × R, so the resistor values for the 1.37 V and 9.37 V corners follow from the current (calculated table, final circuit). In Fig. 15 the 100 Ω RDIMI sits in series with the dimming input, which is where the datasheet's "curve translation" resistor RTRS goes in its Figure 12: a larger resistor there moves the converted curve (curve A to curve B). In the final circuit it adds about 51 mV to the DIMI voltage at 507 µA (our arithmetic). The datasheet does not say that RDIMI and RTRS are the same part, but they are drawn in the same place.

### Input protection

The figure shows three parts on the DIMI node besides RDIMI: CDIMI (100 pF, from DIMI to ground), DDIMI (drawn as a 12 V zener to ground) and DVIN (a diode from DIMI to VIN). The text gives only CDIMI (100 pF recommended) and says DDIMI is the DIMI protection diode, "usually" a 1N4148. It does not explain DVIN. The absolute maximum on DIMI and DIMO is 20 V, so a 12 V zener holds DIMI well below that while passing a 10 V signal, and DVIN's orientation (cathode on VIN) would stop DIMI from rising more than one diode drop above VIN. These are our readings of the figure, not statements in the datasheet.

### Worked example: the datasheet's design

The datasheet's six steps, with the arithmetic checked here. The first design table lists the targets, the datasheet's values and the result recalculated from the final parts.

- **#1 Targets.** IDIMI 500 µA, fDIM 1 kHz, DMIN 5%, DMAX 90%. The 1.37 V and 9.37 V turning points are fixed by the part.
- **#2 VLOW.** The datasheet takes RVLOW_D = 20 kΩ. From DMIN and DMAX it finds VVLOW = 0.847 V, computes RVLOW_U = (2.21 V − VVLOW) × RVLOW_D ÷ VVLOW, and chooses 33 kΩ.
- **#3 HCLP.** From DMAX = 90% it gets VHCLP = 1.453 V, then RISET = 2.21 V ÷ (500 µA ÷ 5) = 22 kΩ, which with the VLOW chain (53 kΩ) fixes the HCLP chain at about 37.6 kΩ. It chooses 13 kΩ over 24 kΩ.
- **#4 Frequency.** CFSET = 8.55 nF, so it uses 8.2 nF.
- **#5, #6.** The result table and Figure 15.

The result: with the chosen resistors and capacitor, our recalculation gives VVLOW 0.834 V, VHCLP 1.4335 V, DMIN 5.45%, DMAX 91.6% (92.1% with the pin table's extra term), fDIM about 1055 Hz and IDIMI 507 µA. All are close to the targets and within the part's own tolerances (for example the datasheet's DMIN limits are ±1.5 points around 6%), but the stated targets of 5%, 90% and 1 kHz are not hit exactly, and the example does not say so.

## Layout

The datasheet's package page (page 11) has only the SO8 outline and a recommended pad layout marked "reference only", and no layout rules. The points below are ours, from the circuit and not from the datasheet:

- Put CVIN right at the VIN and GND pins. The supply runs through RVIN, so the capacitor is what holds the pin up.
- Keep the capacitors on HCLP, VLOW and FSET (CHCLP, CVLOW, CFSET) close to their pins and return them to the GND pin's ground, since the clamp and frequency settings come from small voltages and a 20 µA current.
- Put the DIMI network (RDIMI, CDIMI, DDIMI) at the dimming input connector, not at the chip, so that wiring from the dimmer is protected before it reaches the board.
- Keep the dimming wires away from the switching nodes of the LED driver.

## Things to watch

- **The 2.21 V is not a fixed number.** The VISET plot shows it falling with the DIMI source current, from about 2.234 V at zero to about 2.20 V at 2 mA (by eye). At the final circuit's 507 µA that is about 2.23 V, 0.7% above the 2.21 V used in every formula here. If both dividers are fed from that pin, as in Figure 15, both clamp voltages rise by the same 0.7%, which moves DMIN from 5.45% to about 5.25% and DMAX from 91.6% to about 90.8% (our arithmetic, assuming the plot applies to this circuit).
- **The clamps, frequency and source current interact.** The dividers set the duty clamps, the same dividers set IDIMI (through RISET), and both clamp voltages enter the frequency formula. Change one resistor and recheck all three.
- **The output duty is approximate.** The typical maximum duty at the test condition is 70.2% with limits of 69% to 71.4%, and the minimum duties have ±1.5 and ±1.8 point limits. A system that needs an exact light-load current should measure it.
- **Resistor values from the datasheet land close to the target, not on it.** The example's final circuit gives 5.45% minimum, 91.6% maximum and 1055 Hz against targets of 5%, 90% and 1 kHz (design table).
- **No supply-current figure.** The datasheet gives no supply current, so RVIN cannot be sized from it. The 1 kΩ is the example's value, with a 60 V regulator behind it. The recommended VIN range is 12 V to 55 V and the turn-on threshold is 9.1 V typical (up to 9.9 V), so the supply must stay above that after the resistor drop.
- **DIMO levels are not specified.** The datasheet gives DIMO an absolute maximum of 20 V and says it drives an optocoupler or a dimmable regulator, but it lists no output voltage or current, so check the load on DIMO (including an optocoupler LED current) on the bench.
- **PWM at 100% duty can be missed.** Note 3 says a 100% duty PWM that is not 10 V amplitude cannot be told from a DC level.
- **The frequency has no accuracy figure.** Our reading is that its error includes CFSET's tolerance and the error in both clamp voltages, since all three are in the formula.
- **Dimming to off.** The off and on thresholds (0.97 V and 1.17 V) are described under low clamp mode. If you rely on a dim-to-off function with the clamp set another way, check it on the bench.

## Datasheet notes

- **The RVLOW_U arithmetic is off.** The design example gives 31.92 kΩ, but (2.21 − 0.847) × 20 ÷ 0.847 is 32.18 kΩ. The chosen 33 kΩ is unaffected.
- **The HCLP ratio does not match its VHCLP.** The example finds VHCLP = 1.453 V and then writes the divider ratio as 0.51411, which corresponds to 1.4597 V (a ratio of 0.5210 would give 1.453 V). The chosen 13 kΩ and 24 kΩ give 1.4335 V.
- **The targets are not met exactly.** The final circuit gives about 0.834 V, 1.4335 V, 5.45%, 91.6% and 1055 Hz rather than the example's 0.847 V, 1.453 V, 5%, 90% and 1 kHz.
- **The maximum-duty formula differs between pages.** The pin table has a last term, −(VHCLP − 1.8) × 0.014, that the formula on page 7 and the example leave out. The two differ by about 0.5 points in the final circuit.
- **The minimum-duty formula differs between pages as well.** The pin table adds 0.08 × (VVLOW − 0.75)² when VVLOW is 0.65 V or below. Page 7 does not mention it, and the example's VVLOW is above that.
- **The lower turning point is 1.36 V in one place and 1.37 V in another.** The text of the low clamp mode says 1.36 V, while VMIN is 1.37 V typical (1.27–1.47 V) in the table and the plot.
- **Component names are inconsistent.** The text calls DDIMI a protection diode, usually a 1N4148, Figure 14 shows DDIMI and ZDDIMI, and Figure 15 labels a 12 V zener DDIMI and a separate diode DVIN. The text never describes DVIN.
- **The electrical table header says "VVCC = 15 V",** but the pin is VIN.
- **The recommended operating table lists "VIN, DRV".** The part has no DRV pin. DRV is a pin of the LED controller in Figure 1.
- **The two minimum-duty plots look swapped.** The plot titled D0~10V_MIN1 runs at about 8.7–9.0% and the one titled D0~10V_MIN2 at about 6.0–6.1%, but the table has MIN1 at 6% and MIN2 at 8.8% (by eye).
- **Typos.** "HLCLP" in the absolute maximum table, "VHIGN" in the first plot title, and "VLOW" for "VVLOW" in the frequency formula on page 7. The text extraction of the pin table also loses the minus sign in 10⁻⁶, which the rendered page shows.
