## How it works

The SY50328 is an off-line flyback converter with its 730 V power switch inside the DIP7 package. The output voltage is regulated on the secondary side. A shunt regulator on the output drives the LED of an optocoupler, and the optocoupler's phototransistor pulls the COMP pin down. The controller turns that COMP voltage into a peak-current command, and the ISEN pin ends each on-time when the voltage across the current-sense resistor reaches the command. The command is limited to 0.9 V typical, which sets the maximum peak primary current.

Silergy calls the control pseudo fixed frequency. At steady state the frequency is a fixed 100 kHz, but the method avoids the sub-harmonic oscillation that appears in continuous conduction above 50% duty, so no slope compensation is needed. The datasheet lists frequency modulation to reduce EMI as a feature but gives no amplitude or period.

The COMP voltage sets the operating mode:

- **Fold-back.** Below 1.0 V on COMP the frequency starts to fall, and it reaches the minimum of 23.5 kHz typical at 0.6 V. The datasheet keeps the floor above 20 kHz to stay out of the audible range.
- **Burst mode.** If COMP falls below 400 mV the device enters sleep mode: switching stops and most of the internal circuits shut down. The output droops, COMP rises, and when it passes 500 mV the device wakes and switches again. The output ripple is a little larger in burst mode.

Start-up is from the DRAIN pin, which feeds a current source (2.5 mA typical) into VCC. Once VCC reaches 16 V, the source turns off, the device checks the PRT pin (below) and starts switching with a 3.2 ms internal soft start. The peak-current limit and the frequency each rise in eight steps, then the feedback loop takes over. The auxiliary winding then supplies VCC. The recommended VCC range is 10 to 26 V.

The PRT pin sets input protection with one divider. The pin is compared with 0.6 V when VCC reaches its turn-on level, and switching begins only if it is above that. In operation, PRT below 0.5 V for 64 ms is a brown-out, and PRT above 2.15 V is an input over-voltage. The over-voltage level is always 4.3 times the brown-out level. If the pin is below 100 mV the function is off, so the datasheet says to tie PRT to GND when it isn't used. The datasheet recommends a 1 to 10 nF capacitor from PRT to ground for noise.

Five protections all end in an automatic restart:

- **Over-load.** When the output can't hold its level, COMP is pulled up. If it stays above 2.15 V for 64 ms, the part stops and then restarts with a soft start. The datasheet lists a 2 s auto-recovery time.
- **Secondary diode short.** If the secondary rectifier shorts, the primary current rises very fast. When ISEN exceeds the 1.1 V primary OCP threshold for four switching cycles in a row, the part stops.
- **VCC over-voltage.** If VCC rises above 29 V, for example after an optocoupler failure, switching stops and a timer starts. Before the trip, a 5 mA sink tries to clamp VCC.
- **ISEN pin short.** During the soft start, after 3.25 µs of blanking the part checks that ISEN is above 50 mV. If it is lower, the pin is shorted to ground and the part stops.
- **Over-temperature.** Switching stops at 150 °C and resumes after the die cools by 60 °C.

### Bus capacitor and minimum bus voltage

The datasheet sizes the bus capacitor for a chosen ripple on the bus at the lowest line voltage and full load: CBUS = PO ÷ (η × π × fAC × ΔVBUS) × [arcsin(1 − ΔVBUS ÷ (√2 × VIN,MIN)) + π/2] ÷ (2√2 × VIN,MIN − ΔVBUS). For 24 W, 87% efficiency, 50 Hz, 45 V of ripple and 90 Vac, that is 42.3 µF, and the datasheet picks 44 µF (CE1 and CE2). The lowest bus voltage is √2 × 90 V − 45 V = 82.3 V. The datasheet also gives a quicker rule of 1 to 2 µF per watt of input power, which for the 27.6 W of input power here is 28 to 55 µF.

### Turns ratio and duty cycle

The turns ratio is limited by the stress on the power MOSFET: NPS ≤ (VMOS,BR × KDR − √2 × VIN,MAX − ΔVSN) ÷ VO. With 730 V derated to 85%, a 264 Vac peak and a 120 V spike, the limit is 10.6, and the datasheet picks 8. The maximum duty cycle at the minimum bus voltage is DMAX = NPS × VO ÷ (VBUS,MIN + NPS × VO) = 53.8%.

### Primary inductance, turns and current

The datasheet chooses a ripple factor KRP of 0.3 to 0.6 at the lowest line and full load, and the example uses 0.45, which is continuous conduction. The inductance is LM = VBUS,MIN² × DMAX² × η ÷ (2 × PO × fSW × KRP) = 791 µH, rounded to 800 µH. The primary peak current at rated output is 0.9 A. The primary turns follow from the peak flux density: NP = LM × IPK ÷ (BMAX × AE), with the datasheet's range of 0.2 to 0.3 T and the example's 0.26 T on the EF20 core (AE = 33.5 mm²), which gives 82.6 turns; the example uses 80. The secondary has NP ÷ NPS = 10 turns, and the auxiliary winding NA = VCC,AUX × NS ÷ VO = 12 turns. The datasheet says to fine-tune the auxiliary turns against the VCC measured at minimum output voltage and no load, which is the worst case.

### Current sense resistor

At the OCP point, the datasheet takes the lowest input, the highest output current (130% of rated) and the peak of the line. The duty cycle there is 43%, and the peak primary current IPK,MAX works out to 1.0 A. The sense resistor is RISEN = VISEN,MAX ÷ IPK,MAX = 0.9 V ÷ 1.0 A = 0.9 Ω, which the circuit builds from two 1.8 Ω resistors in parallel. The datasheet says to adjust the value on the real converter against the measured OCP point.

### Secondary rectifier

The datasheet recommends a 100 V rectifier. The peak forward current is NPS × IPK,MAX = 8.0 A, and the BOM's PT15V100SP is rated 15 A / 100 V. The reverse voltage is √2 × VIN,MAX ÷ NPS + VO + VSPIKE; see the notes for the 84.2 V the datasheet gives.

### PRT divider

The upper resistor is set by the power the divider may dissipate: RH ≥ 2 × VIN,MAX² ÷ PRD = 5.6 MΩ for 25 mW, so the example uses 6 MΩ (three 2 MΩ). The lower resistor for a 70 Vac brown-out is RL = RH × VPRT,BO ÷ (√2 × VIN,BO − VPRT,BO) = 30.5 kΩ, and the circuit uses 30.9 kΩ. The over-voltage level follows at 4.3 times the brown-out level. The thresholds this divider gives, with the datasheet's tolerances, are in the table above.

### Output voltage

The datasheet doesn't give the output divider's arithmetic. With the TL431's usual 2.5 V reference (not from this datasheet), RS4 + RS5 = 38.2 kΩ on top and RS6 = 10 kΩ below gives VO = 2.5 V × (1 + 38.2 ÷ 10) = 12.05 V.

## Layout

The datasheet gives these layout rules. The layout figures show the placement; check them in the datasheet.

- Connect the **bridge rectifier output to the bus capacitor first**, then to the switching circuit. This improves EMI and reduces line-frequency ripple.
- Keep the **switching loops small**: the primary power loop, the secondary loop and the auxiliary loop.
- Connect the **bias supply trace to the bias capacitor first**, then to the GND pin, and put the capacitor near the device.
- Keep the loop through the **ISEN pin, the current sense resistor and the GND pin** as small as possible.
- Place the **PRT pin resistor divider close to the device**.
- Follow the datasheet's **primary ground scheme**, which names eight ground points: the bus capacitor (①), the VCC capacitor (②), the auxiliary winding (③), the PRT pin divider (④), the primary Y capacitor (⑤), the current sense resistor (⑥), the device's GND pin (⑦) and the optocoupler receiver (⑧).

At these voltages the creepage and clearance distances between primary and secondary matter too, but the datasheet doesn't discuss them.

## What the datasheet shows

The typical-performance pages show start-up waveforms at 90 V and 264 V in (12 V, 2 A), steady-state ISEN and drain waveforms at both inputs at 2 A (continuous conduction at 90 V, discontinuous at 264 V), at 0.2 A (frequency fold-back), and at 10 mA (burst mode, 1 ms per division), and an efficiency plot against output current at 115 VAC and 230 VAC for a 12 V output.

Reading the plot by eye, efficiency peaks at about 88% for both inputs, near 1.2 A at 115 VAC and near 1.6 A at 230 VAC. At 2 A it is about 86.8% at 115 VAC and about 88% at 230 VAC, and the 230 VAC curve is lower at light load: about 79.8% at 0.2 A against about 83% at 115 VAC. The curves cross at about 1.4 A. The numbers are in the *read by eye* table above. The plot stops at 0.2 A and gives nothing below it.

## Things to watch

- **The ratings of the two versions look reversed.** The SY50329 has the lower RDS(on) (2.4 Ω maximum against 3.0 Ω) and the higher power rating (28 W against 24 W), yet the datasheet gives it a lower drain-current rating (2.5 A against 4 A), a higher thermal resistance (156.1 against 111.5 °C/W) and a lower power dissipation (0.8 against 1.1 W). The revision history says the drain-current ratings were added in Rev 1.0A. Check the SY50329 numbers with Silergy before relying on them. The SY50328 figures are used on this page.
- **The brown-in point is close to the bottom of the input range.** With the circuit's 6 MΩ and 30.9 kΩ, the start threshold is 83 Vac typical and 90 Vac at the top of its tolerance, which is the datasheet's minimum line. At 90 Vac the peak PRT voltage is 0.652 V, just above the 0.65 V maximum brown-in threshold. A part and divider at the unlucky end could refuse to start on a 90 Vac line. Lowering RL moves all three thresholds.
- **The over-voltage margin is small too.** The lowest over-voltage threshold is 269 Vac, only 2% above the 264 Vac top of the input range, and the divider's resistor tolerance would reduce it.
- **The datasheet doesn't say how the 64 ms brown-out debounce treats ripple.** The bus at 90 Vac and full load falls to 82.3 V between line peaks, which puts the PRT pin at 0.42 V, below the 0.45 to 0.55 V brown-out threshold, for part of every half cycle. The debounce is much longer than a half cycle, so the logic must act on the peak or restart the timer, but the datasheet doesn't describe which.
- **The current limit is about 5% above the rated peak at the bottom of its tolerance.** With the 0.9 Ω circuit and an ISEN limit at its 0.85 V minimum, the limit is 0.944 A, against the 0.9 A rated peak at the lowest bus voltage. The typical limit gives 1.0 A, which meets the datasheet's own target, and the maximum gives 1.056 A. Sense-resistor tolerance and turn-off delay move this too.
- **The core flux goes above the preset at the current limit.** The design example presets 0.26 T at rated output, and NP = 80 (rounded down from 82.6) gives 0.269 T at rated peak current. At the current limit it is about 0.30 T typical and about 0.32 T at the maximum, against the 0.2 to 0.3 T range in the datasheet. The datasheet doesn't name a core material, so check the saturation flux at hot temperature.
- **The heat budget is tight.** The conduction loss alone, at the highest duty and current of the line cycle, is 0.53 to 0.67 W. That is above the 0.36 W the die can dissipate at an 85 °C ambient (111.5 °C/W, 125 °C junction), and the recommended ambient maximum is 105 °C, where the budget is 0.18 W. The loss averaged over a line cycle is lower than the valley figure, so this is a worst case, but it shows the thermal margin needs checking on the real board. The SY50329's thermal resistance is higher still.
- **The HV source turns back on above the turn-off level.** It restarts when VCC falls to 9 V typical (8.1 to 9.9 V), above the 8 V typical turn-off. The datasheet doesn't describe what that does in burst mode at no load, where the auxiliary winding is quiet for long periods, so check VCC there on the real board.
- **Start-up is quick but recovery is slow.** Charging 10.1 µF through 2.5 mA takes about 65 ms. After a fault the datasheet's auto-recovery time is 2 s, so a persistent over-load or short cycles about every 2 s.

## Datasheet notes

- **The 84.2 V reverse voltage doesn't follow from the datasheet's formula.** With NPS = 8, √2 × 264 V ÷ 8 + 12 V + 10 V is 68.7 V, and the datasheet's 84.2 V corresponds to an NPS of 6. The 100 V recommendation is safe either way, and the 8.0 A peak current uses the right NPS.
- **The datasheet gives two different values for the minimum frequency.** The feature list and the electrical table say 23.5 kHz typical (20 to 27 kHz), while the text says 25 kHz is reached at 0.6 V and that the frequency is kept above 20 kHz.
- **The auxiliary supply voltage is stated as 12 V but calculated with 14 V.** The text says VCC from the auxiliary winding is set to 12 V, while the NA formula uses 14 V, which gives the 12 turns. With 12 turns the VCC is 14.4 V before drops, as in the worked example above.
- **Figure 1 and Figure 15 use different names from the BOM.** Figure 15 labels the shunt regulator QS1 and the output capacitors CS1 and CS2 (CS1 and CS2 are also the compensation and snubber capacitors in the BOM); the BOM calls them US1, CO1 and CO2. Figure 15 draws a DB106S bridge and the BOM lists a DB107S. This page uses the BOM's names.
- **Some part numbers don't match their descriptions.** R5 and R6 (1.8 Ω) carry the same number as R8 and R9 (200 Ω, 1206W4J0201T5E), and R7 (2 Ω, 0805) carries 1206W4J0751T5E, which reads as a 1206 part of 750 Ω. Check the values against Figure 15 before ordering. The BOM also names the device SY50328/9FIB.
- **The bus ripple has no unit in the design example's table.** It is written "45" and used as 45 V in the formulas.
- **The datasheet gives no output power rating beyond the ordering table.** Silergy's product page for the SY50328FIB lists up to 25 W, against 24 W in the ordering table, and its parameter table lists the MOSFET breakdown differently from the datasheet's 730 V.
- **The datasheet is Rev 1.0A.** Rev 1.0 was the initial release on October 24, 2024. Rev 1.0A (December 25, 2025) raised the MOSFET breakdown voltage from 700 V to 730 V and added the 4 A continuous and 8 A pulsed drain current ratings. Silergy's product page lists the part as in production, with an evaluation board, EVB_SY50328FIB.
