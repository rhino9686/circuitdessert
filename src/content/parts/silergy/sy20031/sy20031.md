## How it works

The SY20031 is a synchronous buck regulator with both power switches inside a five-pin SOT23-5 package. It uses a constant off-time, peak current mode control scheme. The top switch turns on, and turns off when its sensed current reaches an internal control level. The bottom switch then turns on for a fixed off-time, which the chip works out from the input voltage, the output voltage and the target frequency: tOFF = (1 − VOUT ÷ VIN) ÷ fSW. Because the off-time is fixed and the on-time is whatever the loop asks for, the switching frequency stays close to 1.5 MHz while the controller can react within a single cycle. The compensation is built in, so the circuit needs no external compensation network.

At light load the part switches to pulse-frequency modulation (PFM). Once the load falls below half the inductor ripple current, IOUT = VOUT × (1 − D) ÷ (2 × fSW × L), the bottom switch turns off before the current can reverse, and the controller skips cycles so the switching frequency falls with the load. In this circuit at 5 V in, with a 2.2 µH inductor, that crossover is about 0.17 A. Above it, the part runs in continuous conduction at nearly constant frequency. In PFM the part stops switching as soon as FB rises above the reference, so no separate over-voltage protection is needed.

Raising EN above 1 V enables the part. Switching starts after a turn-on delay of about 0.3 ms, and the output then rises over about 0.7 ms. EN below 0.4 V shuts the part down and cuts the input current to under 1 µA. The electrical table lists a 50 Ω discharge on the output, which pulls it down when the part is switched off.

Current limiting is cycle by cycle: the top switch turns off at its current limit (1.5 A minimum and 2.5 A maximum, from the electrical table) and the bottom switch takes over. If the output falls below 50% of its regulated level, the output under-voltage protection (UVP) acts.
The part then goes into hiccup mode, 1.6 ms on and 1.6 ms off, and recovers by itself when the short is removed. Over-temperature protection shuts the part down at 160 °C and restarts it, after a full soft-start, once the junction has cooled by about 20 °C. Input under-voltage lockout keeps switching off until VIN is above 2.5 V, with 0.15 V of hysteresis.

The part can run at up to 100% duty cycle, so the output can follow the input down in dropout. At the other end, the minimum on-time (50 ns, guaranteed by design) limits how low the duty cycle can go. The datasheet says the part supports duty cycles down to about 10% over the full temperature range; for example, 0.6 V from 5.5 V is 10.9%.

## Example application

A 5 V to 1.8 V buck converter built from the circuit in Silergy's datasheet. The five-pin SOT23-5 package has no bootstrap pin and no power-good pin, so the whole circuit is the IC, an inductor, a feedback divider and a handful of capacitors. This section keeps the datasheet's component values, adds the reasoning behind each one, and works the numbers for 5 V in.

The datasheet gives one reference circuit, for 1.8 V, and a table of component values for 0.9, 1.8, 2.5 and 3.3 V. Its performance plots were taken at 5 V in, so the worked example below uses 5 V, with 5.5 V as the worst case for ripple and peak current.

### Output voltage

The output is set by two resistors: VOUT = 0.6 V × (1 + RH ÷ RL). The datasheet recommends 1 kΩ to 1 MΩ for both, to keep light-load current low. With RH = 100 kΩ and a 1.8 V target, RL = 0.6 ÷ (1.8 − 0.6) × 100 kΩ = 50.0 kΩ, and the nearest 1% value, 49.9 kΩ, gives 1.802 V nominal. With the reference's ±1.5% tolerance the output lands between 1.775 V and 1.829 V, before the resistors' own tolerance is added.

### Inductor

Choose the inductance for a ripple current of about 40% of the maximum load at the highest input voltage: L = VOUT × (1 − VOUT ÷ VIN,MAX) ÷ (fSW × IOUT,MAX × 0.4). For 1.8 V, 5.5 V in, 1 A and 1.5 MHz that gives 2.02 µH, and the datasheet uses 2.2 µH. The part tolerates a wide range of ripple, so the final value can differ from the calculation with little effect.

Two more checks apply. The saturation current must exceed the peak current, ISAT > IOUT,MAX + VOUT × (1 − VOUT ÷ VIN,MAX) ÷ (2 × fSW × L). And the winding resistance (DCR) should be below 50 mΩ for good efficiency, along with low core loss at 1.5 MHz.

### Output capacitor

Use an X5R or better ceramic rated for 6.3 V, with at least 10 µF. The reference circuit uses one 10 µF, 10 V ceramic. Output ripple has two parts: the inductor ripple current across the capacitor's ESR (ΔIL × ESR), and the charge on the capacitance (ΔIL ÷ (8 × COUT × fSW)). Add them for a total. Ceramic capacitance falls as the DC voltage across it rises, so measured ripple is usually higher than the calculation; read the effective capacitance off the capacitor's DC-bias curve and recalculate.

### Input capacitor

Use an X5R or better ceramic rated 10 V (the rating should be at least 20% above the highest input voltage, so 6.6 V at 5.5 V in), 10 µF or more, as close as possible to the IN and GND pins with the smallest possible loop. The RMS current it carries is IOUT × √(D × (1 − D)), at most IOUT ÷ 2 at 50% duty, so choose one with an RMS rating above half the load current, and add capacitors in parallel if the calculation needs it. The datasheet says capacitance matters less than RMS rating, and that a single 10 µF ceramic is enough for most applications. If VIN arrives over long wires, add bulk capacitance (electrolytic, tantalum or polymer) to damp the overshoot and ringing from the lead inductance. The reference circuit's 47 µF electrolytic, CIN1, is that kind of bulk capacitor; the datasheet doesn't say why it was included.

### Feedforward and enable

The feedforward capacitor CFF sits across RH and can speed up the load-transient response. The datasheet gives no minimum value, and says to raise it when the output LC is large; all four rows of its table use 22 pF.

Don't connect EN straight to VIN or another voltage source; the datasheet asks for a resistor between 1 kΩ and 1 MΩ. The reference circuit uses RENH = 10 kΩ and RENL = 1 MΩ, which puts EN at 99% of VIN, so the part starts when VIN passes its own under-voltage threshold and the divider only limits current. You can raise the start voltage with a larger RENH ÷ RENL ratio, but the datasheet gives only limits for the EN thresholds (on above 1 V, off below 0.4 V), with no typical value and no hysteresis. That makes the exact turn-on voltage uncertain, so don't rely on EN as a precise voltage monitor.

### Worked example: 5 V to 1.8 V

These numbers are calculated here from the datasheet's formulas. The datasheet doesn't list them, and nothing below was measured. They assume ideal switches and nominal component values.

| Quantity | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Duty cycle at 5 V in | 36.0% (VOUT ÷ VIN) |
| Off-time at 5 V and at 5.5 V in | 427 ns and 448 ns |
| Recommended inductance (40% ripple at 5.5 V) | 2.02 µH; the circuit uses 2.2 µH |
| Inductor ripple current at 5 V and at 5.5 V | 0.35 A (35% of full load) and 0.37 A (37%) |
| Peak inductor current, 5.5 V in, 1 A load | 1.18 A, below the 1.5 A minimum top-switch limit |
| Inductor saturation rating needed | Above 1.18 A |
| Crossover to PFM at 5 V in | About 0.17 A |
| Input capacitor RMS current at 5 V in | 0.48 A (worst case 0.5 A at 50% duty) |
| Input ripple with one 10 µF ceramic at 5 V in | 15 mV (17 mV worst case), before DC-bias loss |
| Output ripple from the capacitance, 10 µF, 5 V in | 2.9 mV, plus ΔIL × ESR |

> **The peak current sits well inside the limit.** At 5.5 V in and a full 1 A load, the 2.2 µH inductor's ripple puts the peak at 1.18 A, which leaves 0.32 A below the 1.5 A minimum top-switch limit. The electrical table gives that limit as 1.5 A minimum and 2.5 A maximum, so design to the minimum.

> **A 10 µF, 10 V ceramic in 0805 loses capacitance under DC bias.** The effect is small at the 1.8 V output but larger on the input side at 5 V. If ripple matters, read the effective capacitance off the capacitor's own DC-bias curve and rerun the ripple numbers above.

## Layout

Silergy gives these layout rules, along with a suggested placement drawing (Figure 7 in the datasheet). In its drawing the pins are EN 1, GND 2, LX 3, IN 4, FB 5.

- Put the **input capacitor** very close to the IN and GND pins, with the smallest loop, joined by a wide copper area.
- Connect the **output capacitor's** negative terminal to the GND pin with wide copper, not vias.
- Place **RH, RL and CFF** as close to the FB pin as possible and route the feedback line away from LX and other fast signals. Take the feedback from the output capacitor with a Kelvin connection, not from the inductor's output terminal.
- Keep the **LX copper** small to limit EMI, but wide enough to keep its resistance and inductance low.
- Don't tie **EN** straight to VIN or another source; use a resistor between 1 kΩ and 1 MΩ.
- Put an adequate number of **ground vias** around the device, and maximize the copper connected to the GND pin. A ground plane is strongly recommended.

The datasheet's thermal resistance (θJA) is 130 °C/W on a 6 × 6 cm, two-layer, 2 oz board, which corresponds to a power dissipation of 0.77 W at 25 °C ambient.

## What the datasheet shows

Silergy's typical-performance pages cover this circuit at 25 °C, 5 V in and 1.8 V out, with L = 2.2 µH and COUT = 10 µF. They include efficiency against load current from 1 mA to 1 A for the four output voltages at 3.3 V, 4.2 V and 5 V in (4.2 V and 5 V for the 3.3 V output), scope captures of output ripple at no load and at 1 A, load-transient steps (0 to 0.5 A and 0.1 to 1 A), start-up and shut-down from both EN and VIN, short-circuit captures that show the hiccup behavior, and Bode plots at 1 A for each output voltage.

Reading the curves by eye at 5 V in: for 1.8 V out, efficiency is about 73% at 1 mA, about 84% at 10 mA, about 89% at 0.1 A, peaks at about 92% between 0.3 and 0.4 A, and falls to the low 80s at 1 A. For 3.3 V out it is about 82% at 1 mA, about 93% at 0.1 A, about 95.5% at its peak near 0.2–0.3 A, and about 90% at 1 A. Treat these as rough, about two points either way, since they come from a graph. The fall toward full load is mostly conduction loss in the switches and the inductor.

## Things to watch

- **The bill of materials says X5R, but the part number is an X7R part.** The datasheet describes CIN2 and COUT as "10 µF/10 V/X5R, 0805", but the Murata part it lists, GRM21BR71A106KA73L, is from Murata's X7R series (the "R7" in the number). Either is fine here. If you buy by description rather than part number, check the voltage rating and the DC-bias curve of what you receive.
- **The EN thresholds are loose.** The datasheet gives only a 1 V minimum for high and a 0.4 V maximum for low, with no typical value or hysteresis, so don't count on EN as a precise turn-on voltage.
- **Full-load efficiency is modest.** With a 260 mΩ top switch, 1.8 V out from 5 V in reads in the low 80s at 1 A. If that matters, the [SY80082ABT](site:parts/silergy/sy80082abt/) has about half the switch resistance at twice the current.
- **There is no power-good pin and no bootstrap capacitor.** The five-pin package keeps the circuit small, but the only status you can read is the output voltage itself.
- **Mind the ambient limit and the package.** The datasheet's recommended ambient range stops at 85 °C, and θJA is 130 °C/W on its own test board, so check the junction temperature at your worst-case input voltage and load.
- **The input has little headroom.** The recommended range tops out at 5.5 V and the absolute maximum on IN is 6.5 V, so a noisy 5 V rail with spikes deserves a look.
