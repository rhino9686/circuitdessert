## How it works

The SY2A29311 is a four-switch buck-boost converter. Its four switches form two half-bridges, one on each end of a single inductor: SW1 (high side) and SW2 (low side) on LX1, on the input side, and SW4 (high side) and SW3 (low side) on LX2, on the output side. The high-side gates are driven from bootstrap capacitors on BS1 and BS2, and a 3.3 V regulator on VCC powers the drivers and the control circuits.

**Buck or boost, depending on the input.** The datasheet says the part runs in buck mode or boost mode. In the usual four-switch scheme, when the input is above the output SW4 stays on and the LX1 side switches as a synchronous buck, and when the input is below the output SW1 stays on and the LX2 side switches as a synchronous boost (our reading; the datasheet doesn't spell this out, though its current-limit text, with the limit measured in SW1, fits it). The datasheet doesn't describe what happens when the input is close to the output, only that the part covers inputs above, below and equal to the output (its graphs include 12 V in, 12 V out).

**Average current control.** An inner loop regulates the average inductor current and is compensated inside the chip. The outer voltage loop compares FB with the 1.0 V reference in a transconductance amplifier (88 µS typical), whose output on COMP sets the current. That loop needs an RC network on COMP, and the design has to work in both modes. The datasheet says the boost mode is usually the harder one, because of its right-half-plane zero.

**Three current limits.**

- *Peak inductor current*, 6 A minimum and 7 A typical, measured in SW1 while it is on. When it trips, SW1 and SW3 turn off and SW2 and SW4 turn on for the rest of the cycle.
- *Reverse inductor current*, −2 A to −3.5 A, only in forced PWM. It is measured in SW4, and when it trips SW4 turns off and SW3 turns on.
- *Average output current*, set by a shunt in the output path between ISP and ISN. The limit is 55 mV typical across the shunt (48–62 mV). If you don't want it, short ISP to ISN and connect both to OUT.

**Light load.** With SYNC/MODE low or open, the part runs in PSM, a power-saving mode with a 500 mA zero-current detection threshold. The datasheet doesn't describe PSM further, but its graphs show it keeps the efficiency far higher than forced PWM at light load. With SYNC/MODE tied to VCC, or driven by an external clock, it runs in forced PWM at a fixed frequency, and the inductor current can go negative. A clock on SYNC/MODE also sets the switching frequency.

**Protection.** The power-good output is released when FB rises above 93% of the reference and pulled low below 91%. If FB stays below 50% of the reference for about 200 µs, the part stops for about 2.6 s and then tries again for about 2.4 ms, and repeats this while the short lasts. Two overvoltage thresholds watch the output: FB at 120% of the reference and the OUT pin at 22–26 V. When the output rises above regulation, SW1 and SW3 turn off and SW2 and SW4 stay on until the inductor current reaches zero. If FB then stays above the 120% threshold for more than 15 µs, the overvoltage protection mode starts, and the part resumes regulation when the overvoltage goes away. The datasheet doesn't describe the OUT pin threshold beyond its value. The thermal shutdown is at 160 °C, with a restart through a full soft start after the die cools by about 15 °C.

The datasheet gives no bill of materials, only the values printed in the figure, and no output current for this design. Its graphs at 12 V out run to about 1.5 A at 5 V in and 2–3 A at 12 V and 20 V in. The worked examples below are calculated here from the datasheet's numbers.

### Output voltage (RFB1, RFB2)

VOUT = 1.0 V × (1 + RFB1 ÷ RFB2), so with RFB2 chosen, RFB1 = RFB2 × (VOUT − 1 V). The typical design's 110 kΩ and 10 kΩ give 12.0 V at the typical reference, and 11.76–12.24 V over the reference range (0.98–1.02 V), before resistor tolerance. The divider draws 100 µA. The table *Feedback resistors for other outputs* above gives RFB1 for six outputs from 3.3 V to 20 V with RFB2 = 10 kΩ. Those values are calculated here.

The divider sits on the VOUT side of RSENSE, so the regulated point is after the shunt and its drop (up to 55 mV at the limit) doesn't show up as an output error (our reading of Figure 1).

The datasheet recommends 10 kΩ to 100 kΩ for both resistors, to keep the light-load consumption down. That range only reaches 1 V × (1 + 100 ÷ 10) = 11 V, and the typical design's own RFB1 is 110 kΩ, so for 12 V and above you have to go outside it on one side.

### Switching frequency and synchronisation (RFS, SYNC/MODE)

fSW (kHz) = 50000 ÷ RFS (kΩ), from 200 kHz (250 kΩ) to 600 kHz (83.3 kΩ). The typical design's 100 kΩ gives 500 kHz, which is 430–570 kHz over the datasheet's tolerance. The table *Switching frequency against RFS* above gives standard values (calculated). A clock on SYNC/MODE (above 1.5 V high, below 0.8 V low, 20–80% duty) overrides the internal oscillator and forces PWM. The datasheet doesn't say how far the clock may be from the frequency that RFS sets.

SYNC/MODE also picks the light-load mode: low or open for PSM, tied to VCC (or a clock) for forced PWM. The pin has a 1 MΩ pull-down inside, so leaving it open gives PSM.

### Inductor (L1)

The inductor has to work at both ends of the input range: as a buck at the highest input and as a boost at the lowest. The datasheet gives two critical values, with the peak-to-peak ripple ΔIL at 30–50% of the maximum output current in buck mode and of the maximum input current in boost mode, and recommends the larger of the two:

- Buck: **L ≥ VOUT × (1 − VOUT ÷ VIN,MAX) ÷ (fSW × ΔIL)**
- Boost: **L ≥ VIN,MIN × (VOUT − VIN,MIN) ÷ (VOUT × fSW × ΔIL)**, with IIN,MAX = VOUT × IOUT ÷ (VIN,MIN × η)

It must also carry the peak current without saturating. In buck mode that is IOUT + VOUT × (1 − VOUT ÷ VIN,MAX) ÷ (2 × fSW × L), and in boost mode it is VOUT × IOUT ÷ (VIN,MIN × η) + VIN,MIN × (VOUT − VIN,MIN) ÷ (2 × VOUT × fSW × L).

**The 2.2 µH in the typical design is well below what these equations give at 40% ripple** (our calculations, 500 kHz, 12 V out):

| Case | Load | L at 40% ripple | Ripple with 2.2 µH |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Buck, 36 V in | 2 A | 20 µH | 7.27 A p-p (364% of the load) |
| Buck, 20 V in | 2 A | 12 µH | 4.36 A p-p (218%) |
| Boost, 5 V in (η = 0.9) | 1.5 A, 4.0 A in | 3.6 µH | 2.65 A p-p (66% of the input current) |
| Boost, 4 V in (η = 0.9) | 1.0 A, 3.3 A in | 4.0 µH | 2.42 A p-p (73%) |

So the typical design runs a large ripple, especially as a buck at high input. A larger inductor lowers the ripple, but the datasheet notes that it also moves the right-half-plane zero down and limits the loop bandwidth. The last design table above shows what the ripple costs in load current at the peak limit.

### Output current limit (RSENSE)

RSENSE = 55 mV ÷ IOUT_LIMIT. The typical design's 20 mΩ gives 2.75 A typical, 2.40–3.10 A over the 48–62 mV window, and dissipates about 150 mW at the limit. The table *Current-sense resistor against output current limit* above gives other values (calculated).

This limit sits on top of the peak switch limit, and at low input the peak limit is the lower one: with 2.2 µH and 12 V out, the 6 A minimum peak limit supports about 1.44 A at 4 V in and 1.75 A at 5 V in (last design table above). At those inputs the shunt doesn't set the limit at all. At 20 V in the peak limit allows about 3.8 A, and the shunt is what stops the output at about 2.75 A.

ISP and ISN are rated to 28 V, like OUT. To disable the function, short ISP to ISN and connect both to OUT.

### Capacitors (CIN, COUT, CVCC, CBS1, CBS2)

- **CIN.** In buck mode the input capacitor carries the switching current, ICIN,RMS = IOUT × √(D × (1 − D)), at most half the load current at D = 0.5. The datasheet recommends a 22 µF low-ESR X5R or better ceramic close to IN and GND, with the loop as small as possible. For a 36 V input, a 50 V rating with its DC-bias derating in mind (our suggestion).
- **COUT.** More than 66 µF of X5R or better ceramic for most designs, sized for ripple and transients, and the datasheet says to take the derating with DC voltage into account. In boost mode the output capacitor supplies the load while SW3 is on, so the capacitive ripple is about IOUT × D ÷ (fSW × COUT): 27 mV at 5 V in and 1.5 A with 66 µF nominal (calculated here; less capacitance at bias means more ripple).
- **CVCC.** At least 1 µF low-ESR ceramic on VCC. The typical design uses 2.2 µF.
- **CBS1, CBS2.** 100 nF low-ESR ceramic between each BS pin and its LX pin, placed as close as possible. Rev 1.0A added the instruction not to put a resistor in series with them.

### Soft start and enable (CSS, REN1, REN2)

The soft-start time is tSS (ms) = CSS (nF) × 1 V ÷ 5 µA, so the typical design's 10 nF gives 2 ms (1.7–2.5 ms over the 4–6 µA charging current, calculated here). With SS open the default is 1.6 ms (0.7–3.2 ms).

EN is a high-voltage input (rated to 36 V) with logic-level thresholds: on above 1.3 V, off below 0.8 V. Don't leave it floating. If EN is pulled up to VIN, the datasheet asks for a 10 kΩ to 100 kΩ resistor in series. The typical design uses 10 kΩ from VIN and 1 MΩ to ground, which puts EN at 99% of VIN. So the part starts as soon as the input passes its 3.3–3.9 V undervoltage lockout, and the divider draws VIN ÷ 1.01 MΩ (12 µA at 12 V, 36 µA at 36 V). To switch the part off, pull EN below 0.8 V with an open-drain output. With the 10 kΩ REN1, that output then sinks VIN ÷ 10 kΩ, up to 3.6 mA at 36 V, so a larger REN1 (the datasheet allows up to 100 kΩ) is better if you add one (our calculation). The layout notes add that if the chip driving EN is high-impedance in shutdown and IN is wired directly to a battery, a 1 MΩ pull-down from EN to ground keeps noise from turning the regulator on.

### Loop compensation (RZ, CZ, CP)

The datasheet's procedure, for boost mode:

1. **Crossover.** Choose fc at 1/5 of the right-half-plane zero and 1/10 of the switching frequency, with fRHPZ = (1 − D)² × VOUT ÷ (2π × L × IOUT).
2. **RZ** = VOUT ÷ (Gm × Gfc × VREF), with Gm = 88 µS and the power-stage gain at crossover Gfc = (1 − D) ÷ (2π × fc × COUT × RS), where RS = 100 mΩ.
3. **CZ** = RL × COUT ÷ RZ, which puts the zero at or below the pole of the load resistance and COUT.
4. **CP** = RESR × COUT ÷ RZ, to cancel the output capacitor's ESR zero (the Type 2 network). Without CP it is the Type 2a network, for when the ESR doesn't matter.

What the typical values give, calculated here with COUT = 66 µF nominal and 12 V out:

| Quantity | Value | How |
| --- | --- | --- |
| fRHPZ at 5 V in, 1.5 A | 100 kHz | (0.417² × 12) ÷ (2π × 2.2 µH × 1.5 A) |
| fc recommended | 20 kHz | 100 kHz ÷ 5 (below 500 kHz ÷ 10) |
| RZ for that fc | 270 kΩ | 12 ÷ (88 µS × 0.50 × 1 V) |
| fc with the datasheet's 82 kΩ | 6.0 kHz | 82 kΩ × 88 µS × 0.417 ÷ (2π × 66 µF × 0.1 Ω × 12) |
| Zero, RZ and CZ | 880 Hz | 1 ÷ (2π × 82 kΩ × 2.2 nF) |
| Pole, RZ and CP | 194 kHz | 1 ÷ (2π × 82 kΩ × 10 pF) |

So the typical network crosses over at about 6 kHz at 5 V in (4.8 kHz at 4 V, 9.7 kHz at 8 V), a third of what the procedure would aim for. That is a conservative, slower loop. With the output capacitance reduced by DC bias, the real crossover is higher. These are first-order estimates from the datasheet's equations and its typical Gm and RS, which aren't in the electrical table.

### Worked example: 5 V to 12 V at 1.5 A (boost)

The datasheet shows this case in its ripple captures, and its PSM efficiency graph reads about 88% at 1.5 A, where the 5 V curve ends. Everything below is calculated here, and nothing was measured.

| Quantity | Value | How |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Duty cycle (boost, ideal) | 0.58 | 1 − 5 ÷ 12 |
| Input current | 4.09 A | 12 × 1.5 ÷ (5 × 0.88) |
| Ripple (2.2 µH) | 2.65 A p-p | 5 × 0.583 ÷ (500 kHz × 2.2 µH) |
| Peak inductor current | 5.42 A | 4.09 + 2.65 ÷ 2 |
| Margin to the 6 A minimum peak limit | 0.58 A | 6 − 5.42 |
| Output power and total loss | 18 W, 2.45 W | 12 × 1.5; 18 ÷ 0.88 − 18 |
| Shunt loss | 45 mW | 1.5² × 20 mΩ |

**The peak limit is close.** The peak current is 5.42 A against a 6 A guaranteed minimum, so 1.5 A at 5 V in is about as much as the typical design can promise, and at 4 V in the same limit gives about 1.44 A (design table above).

**Heat.** In boost mode SW1 is on all the time and SW3 and SW4 take turns (our reading of the scheme, see How it works), so two switches always carry the input current. With an RMS inductor current of 4.16 A, the conduction loss is 2 × 4.16² × RDS(ON): 1.2 W at the typical 35 mΩ and 2.1 W at the 60 mΩ maximum. On the datasheet's θJA of 28 °C/W (a four-layer evaluation board) that is a 34–58 °C rise, before switching loss. If all 2.45 W landed in the package the rise would be 69 °C, so the real figure is in between (the inductor and shunt take part of it). At 85 °C ambient that means about 120–155 °C, close to the 150 °C thermal shutdown minimum, so watch the board copper and the ambient at low input.

### Worked example: 20 V to 12 V at 2 A (buck)

The efficiency graph reads about 94% at 2 A for 20 V in.

The ripple is larger than the load, so at 2 A in forced PWM the inductor current dips slightly below zero each cycle, and at light load in forced PWM the valley reaches about −2.2 A, which is at the edge of the −2 A minimum reverse current limit (our calculation). At 36 V in the light-load valley would be about −3.6 A, beyond the −3.5 A maximum. The datasheet doesn't describe what the part does when it hits the reverse limit every cycle, only that SW4 turns off and SW3 turns on.

## Layout

Silergy gives these rules and a layout drawing (Figure 8).

- Place **CIN, CVCC, COUT, CBS, L and the feedback resistors close to the IC**.
- Give the GND pins as much copper as the board allows, ideally a ground plane, with **vias under the exposed pad** for soldering and heat.
- **Keep the input loop small**: CIN close to IN and GND.
- **Keep the output loop small**: COUT close to OUT and GND.
- **BS is sensitive**: put each bootstrap capacitor between its BS and LX pins as close as possible.
- **Return the small-signal grounds separately**, including the feedback divider, so that the currents circulating in the ground plane don't disturb the regulator.
- If the chip driving EN is high-impedance in shutdown and IN is wired directly to a battery, **add a 1 MΩ pull-down from EN to ground.**

The IN pins are 1–4, OUT 19–22, LX1 5, 28 and 29, LX2 18, 24 and 25, and GND 12, 26, 27 and the exposed pad. Pin 8 is NC (tie it to GND or leave it open).

## Things to watch

- **No output current rating.** The "7 A" in the feature list and Silergy's selection guide is the peak switch limit. The load current you get depends on the input, the output and the inductor ripple, and at 4–5 V in with the typical 2.2 µH it is only about 1.4–1.75 A at the 6 A minimum limit (design table above).
- **The typical inductor is far below the datasheet's own equations.** At 40% ripple, the buck equation asks for about 20 µH at 36 V in and 2 A, and the boost equation for about 3.6 µH at 5 V in and 1.5 A. With 2.2 µH the ripple is 7.3 A p-p at 36 V in, which costs load current at the peak limit and puts the light-load valley in forced PWM beyond the reverse current limit (see the buck worked example). The datasheet's graphs stop at 20 V in, apart from the output-voltage-against-input curve.
- **The feedback resistor advice only reaches 11 V.** "10 kΩ to 100 kΩ for both" gives at most 1 V × 11 = 11 V, and the typical design uses 110 kΩ for 12 V. Up to 20 V needs a ratio of 19.
- **Little margin above the top of the output range.** The output goes up to 20 V, the OUT pin overvoltage threshold is 22–26 V, and OUT, LX2, ISP and ISN are rated to 28 V. Choose the output capacitors' voltage rating with that in mind.
- **Little margin at the bottom of the input range.** The lowest input is 4 V, and the undervoltage lockout's rising threshold can be as high as 3.9 V. It turns the part off again about 0.3 V lower (typical hysteresis).
- **The typical design is always on.** EN is tied to VIN through 10 kΩ and 1 MΩ, so it never goes low on its own. The 1 µA shutdown current (1 µA typical at 25 °C, 5 µA maximum over temperature) only applies with EN pulled down, and pulling it down through the 10 kΩ REN1 adds VIN ÷ 10 kΩ, up to 3.6 mA at 36 V (our calculation). Use a larger REN1 if the part has to switch off.
- **The compensation values give a slow loop.** The datasheet's procedure aims for 1/5 of the right-half-plane zero, about 20 kHz at 5 V in and 1.5 A, which needs RZ near 270 kΩ, while its 82 kΩ gives about 6 kHz (calculated with nominal capacitance). The Gm (88 µS) and current-sense gain (100 mΩ) it uses are typical values from the text, not the electrical table.
- **Heat at low input.** At 5 V in and 1.5 A the conduction loss alone is 1.2–2.1 W, a 34–58 °C rise on the datasheet's four-layer board (see the boost worked example).
- **Several timing limits are from simulation.** The minimum on-time and off-time, the power-good delays, the overvoltage delay and the undervoltage delay carry Note 4, "based on simulation result".
- **Two output-voltage graphs disagree.** The output against load sits at about 12.11–12.12 V, and the output against input at about 12.00–12.01 V, for the same 12 V setting. Both are within the reference tolerance, so they are probably different boards or resistors (our reading). The output-against-input graph also runs to 40 V in, beyond the 36 V operating range.
- **Units have dropped out of the PDF.** "2.2 H", "22 F", "3×22 F", "2.2 F" and "200 s" are read as µH, µF and µs, which is what the text and the rest of the datasheet use.
- **The revision label is mixed.** The first page footer says Rev. 1.0A and the others Rev1.0A, and the revision history lists 1.0A (June 13, 2025), 1.0 (July 17, 2024) and 0.9 (August 1, 2022). Rev 1.0 changed the block diagram, the peak current limit description and the short-circuit hiccup timing, so older copies differ on those.
