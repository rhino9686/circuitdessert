## How it works

The SY24640 puts a current-sense amplifier and a comparator in one chip. It measures VIN = VIN+ − VIN−, the voltage across a shunt resistor in the supply line, and compares it with the voltage on the LIMIT pin. When VIN is above the limit, the open-drain ALERT pin pulls low, and a processor input or a protection circuit takes it from there. There is no analog output. What you get is one yes-or-no answer with a threshold you choose.

**The threshold comes from one resistor.** An internal 20 µA current source drives the LIMIT pin, so a resistor RLIMIT to ground makes VLIMIT = 20 µA × RLIMIT. The range is 0 mV to 250 mV (up to 12.5 kΩ), and the source is within ±1% over temperature. The threshold is also the shunt voltage at the trip point: RLIMIT = RSENSE × ITRIP ÷ 20 µA. Or a DAC can drive the LIMIT pin, to change the limit while the system runs (Figure 2 of the datasheet).

**Common mode to 36 V, independent of the supply.** The inputs take 0 V to 36 V with a 2.7 V to 5.5 V supply, so the part can watch a shunt on a 12 V, 24 V or 36 V rail (high side) or in the ground return (low side) while it runs from a 3.3 V logic supply. The datasheet's common-mode range starts at 0 V (and −0.3 V is only an absolute maximum), so unlike the SY24642 it can't sense a shunt below ground.

**One direction only.** The differential input range is 0 mV to 250 mV. The comparator looks for current flowing from the supply side (IN+) to the load side (IN−), and the datasheet doesn't cover current in the other direction (the absolute maximum differential is ±40 V).

**The response time is selectable, and longer is quieter.** The part compares the average input over a 10 µs window. With DELAY open the comparator looks at one window, and if the average is above the limit at the end of it, ALERT goes low (10 µs). The 50 µs and 100 µs settings keep a counter: a window above the limit counts up, a window below it counts down by one, and ALERT goes low when the counter reaches 5 (50 µs) or 10 (100 µs). A noise spike that is too short to fill the count is ignored, and the offset is better at the longer settings (see the specifications). In the transparent mode ALERT returns high when any 10 µs window has no overcurrent, whatever the delay setting.

**Hysteresis.** ALERT returns high when VIN falls below VLIMIT minus the hysteresis, which is 2, 4 or 8 mV depending on the HYS pin.

**Transparent or latched.** With LATCH low ALERT follows the current: low while it is above the limit, high when it is not. With LATCH high, ALERT stays low after the overcurrent has gone, until the controller pulls LATCH low for at least 20 µs. If the current is still above the limit when LATCH goes low, ALERT stays low. After that, the controller sets LATCH high again to go back to latch mode.

**Disable mode.** ENABLE low puts the part in a mode with a 1.2 µA maximum quiescent current (0.6 µA typical), for battery systems that don't need to monitor all the time. It takes about 20 µs to enter that mode, and 150 µs (the table) or about 300 µs (the text) to leave it.

### Setting the threshold (RLIMIT, RSENSE)

Pick the trip current ITRIP and the shunt first, then the resistor.

- **Shunt voltage at the trip point:** VLIMIT = ITRIP × RSENSE. It has to be inside 0 mV to 250 mV.
- **Resistor:** RLIMIT = VLIMIT ÷ 20 µA, from 0 Ω to 12.5 kΩ. 2.5 kΩ gives 50 mV.

A bigger limit voltage makes the offset and the hysteresis a smaller part of the threshold, and costs more power in the shunt and more voltage lost in the supply line (VLIMIT × ITRIP at the trip point). The datasheet says the 250 mV range lets you use larger shunt resistors for lower currents. The second design table above shows how much of a 10 mV to 250 mV limit the offset and the hysteresis take up, and the third one gives the trip current for common shunt values. At a 10 mV limit the 2 mV hysteresis alone is 20% and the 500 µV offset is 5%, so the datasheet's range is best used above a few tens of millivolts (our reading; it gives no smallest usable limit).

**With a DAC.** The LIMIT pin can be driven from a DAC instead (Figure 2), which lets the threshold change with the operating conditions. The 20 µA source still pushes its current out of the pin, so a DAC voltage on the pin has to take 20 µA (our reading; the datasheet doesn't say, and its figure shows the DAC and RLIMIT on the same node).

### Response time and hysteresis (DELAY, HYS)

DELAY and HYS each have three states: open, tied to GND, or tied to VS. They have to be tied straight to the pin or left completely open, and if a resistor is used, it has to be 1 kΩ or less, so as not to disturb the internal level detection. Use the first design table above for the settings.

- **Short delay (10 µs):** the fastest alert, from one window. A short spike above the limit can set it off, as long as it is above the limit on average for the whole window.
- **Longer delays (50 and 100 µs):** a count of 5 or 10 windows, which ignores noise spikes but takes at least 50 or 100 µs to react. The offset is better too (−75 µV typical at 100 µs, against −350 µV at 10 µs).
- **Hysteresis:** the wider settings keep ALERT from chattering when the current sits at the limit. The right choice depends on the limit: 8 mV is 16% of a 50 mV limit and 80% of a 10 mV one.

### ALERT, ENABLE and LATCH

ALERT is open-drain and active low. The text recommends a 10 kΩ pull-up to the supply, and says it may be pulled above VS but never above 5.5 V. At 3.3 V and 10 kΩ it draws 0.33 mA while it is low, and the low level is 100 mV maximum at 3 mA. For a hardware interrupt, use the transparent mode so that the processor sees the edge in both directions. In latch mode, the processor needs a GPIO for LATCH to clear and re-arm the alert, and a clear takes a low pulse of at least 20 µs.

ENABLE and LATCH are logic inputs, with 1.4 V as the minimum high level and 0.4 V as the maximum low level, so a 3.3 V GPIO can drive them. DELAY and HYS are three-level pins whose high level is within 0.5 V of VS.

### Worked example: a 5 A trip on a 12 V rail

These numbers are calculated here from the datasheet's values, and nothing was measured. The choices are ours: a 10 mΩ shunt, a 50 mV limit, VS = 3.3 V, DELAY = GND (50 µs) and HYS = GND (4 mV).

- **Resistor:** RLIMIT = 50 mV ÷ 20 µA = 2.5 kΩ.
- **Trip current:** 50 mV ÷ 10 mΩ = 5 A. It releases when VIN falls below 50 − 4 = 46 mV, which is 4.6 A (the hysteresis is 0.4 A).
- **Shunt:** 0.25 W at 5 A, and a 50 mV drop in the 12 V line.
- **Timing:** the current has to be above 5 A for at least five 10 µs windows in a row (50 µs). A clean window takes one off the count, and in the transparent mode a single clean window clears ALERT.
- **Supply:** 135 µA at 3.3 V is 0.45 mW when enabled, and 0.6 µA typical (2 µW) when disabled.

The trip level has several contributors, and they add:

| Contributor | Source | Error at 5 A |
| --- | --- | --- |
| LIMIT current | 20 µA ±1% over temperature | ±1.0% |
| RLIMIT tolerance | A 1% resistor (our choice) | ±1.0% |
| Comparator offset | 500 µV maximum, over 50 mV | ±1.0% (50 mA) |
| Shunt tolerance | A 1% shunt (our choice) | ±1.0% |
| Sum of the worst cases | | ±4.0% (4.8 A to 5.2 A) |
| Root of the sum of squares | | ±2.0% (4.9 A to 5.1 A) |

The common-mode, supply and drift terms are left out: the offset limit is for VS = 3.3 V and the datasheet gives its drift as 0.5 µV/°C maximum (up to 50 µV over 100 °C, or 0.1% of 50 mV), and the common-mode and supply terms are 110 dB and 60 µV/V at worst. The limit is the larger part of the error, since a 1% resistor and a 1% shunt are each as big as the datasheet's whole offset budget at 50 mV.

## Layout

The datasheet has no layout section and no layout figure, only the recommended land patterns in the package outline drawings. These notes are ours, not the datasheet's:

- Connect IN+ and IN− to the shunt with a Kelvin connection (traces from the shunt's own pads, not from the high-current trace), keep them close together and short, and keep the loop between them small. With a few milliohms, the trace resistance is a real part of the sensed voltage.
- Put the bypass capacitor close to VS and GND (the datasheet's Figure 1 shows one but gives no value).
- Keep RLIMIT close to the LIMIT pin, with its ground return short, since its voltage is the threshold and it is only tens to hundreds of millivolts.
- Leave DELAY and HYS open or hard-tied. A floating pin has 1 µA typical of leakage (the digital-input spec), so keep it clear of other nets.
- The DFN thermal pad may be connected to ground or left floating.

## Things to watch

- **It senses one direction only.** The recommended differential range is 0 mV to 250 mV, with no reverse-current detection, and the common-mode range starts at 0 V.
- **The offset has three numbers.** The feature list says ±500 µV maximum. The table gives −75 and −500 µV at the 100 µs setting, −125 and −500 µV at 50 µs, and −350 and −650 µV at 10 µs, all negative with no minimum column, and the 10 µs maximum is above the ±500 µV in the feature list. The sign convention isn't explained. The fastest setting is the least accurate.
- **The delay figures are typical only, at 1 mV overdrive.** The table has no minimum or maximum for the propagation delay and no figure for a larger overdrive. The 10 µs figure is a window average at the end of a 10 µs window, so it is a response to a sustained overcurrent and not a guaranteed short-circuit trip time (our reading).
- **The hysteresis values are typical only,** and compared with a small limit they are large (2 mV is 20% of 10 mV). The datasheet gives no smallest usable limit.
- **The enable time is given two ways.** The table says 150 µs, and the text says about 300 µs to leave the low-power state. The start-up time (1 ms typical) isn't defined. What ALERT does while the part is disabled, or during start-up, isn't stated.
- **The latch description is confusing.** The text under Figure 7 says the LATCH pin "will be pulled high" and "pulled low" as if the part drove it. LATCH is an input (it has VIH and VIL specs and the pin table says selection input), and the behaviour on this page is our reading of the rest of the text and of Figure 7.
- **The logic pins and the absolute maximum disagree.** The high level has a 6 V maximum in the table, but the absolute maximum for any pin is VS + 0.3 V. Drive the logic pins from the same supply as VS, or at most 0.3 V above it.
- **The ALERT pull-up limit is given two ways.** The text says it must not exceed 5.5 V, and the absolute maximum on ALERT is 6 V. The recommended supply maximum is 5.5 V and the absolute maximum on VS is 6 V.
- **Open means floating.** DELAY and HYS have 1 µA typical (2 µA maximum) of leakage, and the defaults (10 µs, 2 mV) depend on the pin not being pulled by anything on the board.
- **The disabled current is given two ways.** The table has 1.2 µA maximum, and the application text says less than 10 µA for all pins together.
- **No bypass capacitor value, no layout rules.** Figure 1 shows a capacitor on VS with no value, and there is no layout section.
- **Limit-pin details are missing.** There is no LIMIT pin input range or noise guidance, no statement of whether RLIMIT may stay when a DAC drives the pin, and no filtering advice for the shunt inputs. The input bias current is 3.5 µA typical and 8 µA maximum (an average of IN+ and IN−), so a series filter resistor would shift the threshold.
- **The thermal table has a stray "Min".** The DFN θJA row reads "Junction-to-ambient Thermal Resistance(DFN2×2-10)Min", under a "Max" column header.
- **Wording.** The text says "common modulus voltage", "a possibility of a weighing", and calls the Figure 4 and 5 settings "responses".
- **The revision date.** The revision history gives Rev 1.0 (production release) as July 17, 2024, and the page footers say © 2025.
