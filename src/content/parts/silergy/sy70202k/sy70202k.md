## How it works

The SY70202K is a power-management IC for solid-state drives and microcontroller boards. From one 2.8–3.7 V supply it makes six rails: four synchronous buck converters with built-in FETs and two LDOs. Each buck switches at 2 MHz and needs three external parts: an input capacitor, an inductor and an output capacitor. The rails are sized for a typical SSD: Buck1 (4 A, 1.6–3.0 V) for NAND core, Buck2 (3 A, 0.75–1.85 V) for NAND I/O, Buck3 (4 A, 0.5–1.2 V) for the SoC core, Buck4 (2 A, 0.807–1.907 V) for a SoC PHY or DRAM I/O, LDO1 (0.4 A) for a SoC PHY or DRAM core and LDO2 (0.4 A) for SoC I/O (the datasheet's Figure 1 labels them this way).

**One part, many defaults.** The part is programmed at the factory with default voltages, delays and modes, and three pins pick between three factory banks while VIN is coming up: GPIO1 (Buck3 voltage), GPIO3 (the power-on sequence) and GPIO4 (Buck4 and LDO1). Tied high, floating or low, each pin changes the defaults without any I²C traffic (the tables below). After start-up, every voltage, mode, delay and sleep setting can be changed over I²C at up to 3.4 MHz.

**Buck and bypass modes.** Buck1 can run as a buck or as a load switch that connects its output straight to VIN (bypass mode, 40 mΩ pass FET, 4.5 A typical limit). LDO1 and LDO2 can run as LDOs or as load switches to VIN_LDO1. LDO2's mode is set by the GPIO6 pin and latched after its power-on delay; LDO1's by GPIO4 and the LDO1_Mode register.

**Sequencing.** When VIN passes the 2.6 V UVLO threshold, the part waits about 1 ms (tSYS,ON, 1.5 ms maximum), reads the GPIO banks, then turns each rail on after its own delay of 0 to 7.5 ms in 0.5 ms steps. Two more signals follow the same schedule: EXT_EN (GPIO7), which can enable an outside regulator, and nRESET, an open-drain output released after a delay of 0.5 to 16 ms. Before a rail is enabled, its discharge resistor is on. Turn-off has its own delays (0, 0.25, 0.5 or 1 ms).

**Sleep and deep sleep.** Two low-power states, SLEEP (the GPIO8 pin or a register bit) and deep sleep (GPIO5 or a register bit), each have a per-rail choice of staying on or turning off, set over I²C. Buck3 can also drop to a second voltage in SLEEP (its VSET1). Rails follow their power-off and wake-up delays going in and out, I²C stays alive, and nRESET stays high.

**Low-power operation.** Each buck can run in a low-power mode at light load or be forced into PWM (register 0x0A); the default is low-power mode. In the datasheet's no-load figures the whole device draws 20 µA with every regulator disabled and 120 µA with all enabled (typical, not production tested).

### Choosing the defaults with GPIO1, GPIO3 and GPIO4

The three input pins are read once, during the system delay after VIN passes UVLO, so they must be in their final state before VIN rises and cannot be changed while the converters run (floating means unconnected). The default tables are in the design tables above. Three details that matter:

- **GPIO1** picks two voltages for Buck3: VSET0 for normal operation and VSET1 for SLEEP. Its Figure 7 shows that pulling GPIO1 from high to low after start-up does not change Buck3, and that a move from 0.75 V to 0.7 V is made over I²C.
- **GPIO3** picks the order in which the rails come up. Sequences B and C have the same numbers in the datasheet's table, so GPIO3 floating and low are the same in the table.
- **GPIO4** picks Buck4's default voltage (0.907 V in every bank) and whether LDO1 starts as an LDO at 1.8 V (pin high) or as a load switch that follows VIN (floating or low). Its Figure 11 shows that Buck4 and LDO1 do not react to a later change of the pin either.

### Power-on sequence in numbers

With sequence A, the rails come up as follows. Times are from SYS_OK (our reading of the datasheet's Figure 8), which is about 1 ms after UVLO.

| Time after SYS_OK | Event |
| --- | --- |
| 0.5 ms | LDO1 and LDO2 turn on (soft start 0.2 ms) |
| 1.0 ms | Buck3 turns on (soft start 0.25 ms) |
| 1.5 ms | Buck4 (soft start 0.15 ms) and EXT_EN |
| 2.0 ms | Buck2 (soft start 0.25 ms) |
| 3.0 ms | Buck1 (soft start 0.25 ms) |
| 4.0 ms | nRESET released (the default trigger is SYS_OK; Buck2 power-good can be chosen instead) |

Sequence B and C swap the Buck1 and Buck2 times (2.0 ms and 3.0 ms). In the datasheet's power-on scope capture (VIN 3.3 V, outputs 2.5, 1.2, 0.75, 0.9, 1.8, 1.8 V) the rails step up in the order LDO1 and LDO2, Buck3, Buck4, Buck2, Buck1, at 0.5 ms per division.

### Setting voltages over I²C

The slave address is 0100101 followed by the read/write bit, so the address bytes are 0x4A to write and 0x4B to read. A write sends the starting register address and then as many data bytes as needed, which go to consecutive registers. Each output voltage register holds a code, and the output is a base voltage plus the code times the step (the output voltage codes table).

### Worked example (calculated): Buck3 at 0.9 V with 0.7 V in sleep

Suppose the SoC core wants 0.9 V when running and 0.7 V while the system sleeps. Buck3's registers VSET0 (0x02) and VSET1 (0x03) are 0.5 V plus 10 mV per code:

- VSET0: (0.9 − 0.5) ÷ 0.01 = 40, which is 28h
- VSET1: (0.7 − 0.5) ÷ 0.01 = 20, which is 14h

One write of four bytes does both: 0x4A (address, write), 0x02 (first register), 0x28, 0x14. Bit 7 of register 0x08 (B3_VID@SLEEP_EN) is set by default, so Buck3 moves to VSET1 in SLEEP; Buck3's SLEEP_EN bit (bit 2 of the same register) is zero by default, so Buck3 stays on. The datasheet says these values are lost on any protection shutdown, so the host must write them again after a restart (see Things to watch).

### Input current at full load (calculated)

Adding up the maximum loads (4 A at 2.5 V, 3 A at 1.2 V, 4 A at 0.75 V and 2 A at 0.907 V) gives 18.4 W of buck output. At 3.3 V in and with 100% efficiency that is 5.6 A, and the two LDOs at 0.4 A each add 0.8 A more because an LDO draws its output current from the input: 6.4 A in total at best. With a 90% buck efficiency (our assumption; the datasheet's curves are in the by-eye readings below) it is about 7.0 A. All four rails at full load is a worst case that a real SSD board will not reach, but it sets the size of the VIN source and the input capacitance.

### Choosing the inductor and capacitors (calculated)

The datasheet's design procedure gives formulas for the inductor, the capacitors and the ripple. The inductor-and-capacitor table applies them to the four bucks with the BOM's 0.47 µH. Two points stand out:

- **The 0.47 µH inductor gives 16% to 36% ripple at 3.7 V in, against the 40% the procedure targets.** The datasheet says the part tolerates the variation.
- **The 4 A inductor is below the Buck1 peak.** Buck1 at 4 A load and 3.7 V in has about 4.43 A of peak current, and Buck3 has about 4.32 A. The BOM says the DFE201612E-R47M is "4A", without saying whether that is its saturation current or its heating current. The procedure requires a saturation rating above the peak at full load, and Buck1's current limit is 5.6 A, so check the inductor's own datasheet before using it at full load. A single 10 µF input capacitor gives a small ripple at the nominal value (25 to 50 mV in the table), but ceramic capacitance falls with DC bias.

## Layout

The layout notes are the datasheet's (page 42) plus the suggested layout in its Figure 28:

- Place each input capacitor, output capacitor and inductor as close to the device as possible. Place the VIN_B1 decoupling capacitor next to the VIN_B1 and PGND12 pins and minimise the loop formed by the input capacitors, input pins and PGND pins; use the same rule for every buck and the LDOs.
- Maximise the copper on the ground pins for thermal and noise performance, and keep the copper on each LX pin small to reduce noise coupling.
- AGND is described as an analog ground that is Kelvin-connected to the other ground pins.
- The suggested layout shows each buck's input capacitor, inductor and output capacitors around its side of the BGA with vias for VIN, the switch nodes and ground, on a four-layer board (top, two middle layers and bottom). The datasheet's θJA of 35 °C/W is measured on a 4-layer JEDEC board.
- In PWRDIS use, GPIO7 needs an external 10 kΩ pull-down (note under the GPIO initial status table).

## Things to watch

- **Programmed values are lost on a fault.** The datasheet says the device returns to its defaults after a VIN overvoltage, overtemperature, output overvoltage, short-circuit or overcurrent shutdown, and each of those restarts the power-up sequence. Voltages, modes and sleep settings written over I²C must be written again after any restart, so the host has to watch nRESET or the fault flags.
- **GPIO6 and GPIO8 have an internal 1 MΩ pull-down.** A floating GPIO8 reads as low, which is the SLEEP command, and a floating GPIO6 selects load-switch mode for LDO2. The BOM's 100 kΩ pull-ups to VIN override both. GPIO5, the deep-sleep input, has no internal pull listed and low means deep sleep, so it needs a pull-up too if it is used as the DPSLP input.
- **GPIO7 starts as an I²C-controlled output, not as EXT_EN.** The register default for GPIO7's function is "I²C controlled output" (01), but the sequencing figures and tables show EXT_EN on GPIO7. If you want EXT_EN to enable an outside regulator, change bits 5:4 of register 0x15 or confirm that your factory configuration does it already.
- **The supply window is narrow.** The recommended VIN range is 2.8 V to 3.7 V, UVLO is 2.6 V rising with 200 mV hysteresis, and VIN overvoltage shuts the part down at 3.9 V (restart at 3.6 V), only 0.2 V above the top of the recommended range. The absolute maximum on any pin is 6 V.
- **Buck1 and Buck3 can peak above a 4 A inductor.** See the calculation above: a 4 A inductor on a 4 A rail has no margin at 3.7 V in.
- **The GPIO banks cannot be changed while running.** GPIO1, GPIO3 and GPIO4 must be stable before UVLO. GPIO6 is latched after LDO2's power-on delay, and unlocking it (bit 2 of 0x15) is "not recommended due to trigger protection".
- **Light-load efficiency is modest.** With the default low-power mode, the curves fall to about 70% to 88% at 1 mA. If a rail should stay in PWM at light load, set its forced-PWM bit in register 0x0A.
- **Sleep entries need spacing.** The interval between entering two SLEEP or deep-sleep states must exceed 4 ms, because the internal clock blocks the next instruction for 4 ms. The datasheet says the SLEEP and DPSLP signals are masked before nRESET goes high (a factory trim bit selects masking), and that the host should set the sleep options over I²C after nRESET goes high.
- **Several thresholds are design-only.** The system-warning (SYSWARN) threshold is 2.9 V typical (±3.5%) with 50 mV hysteresis, and the VIN overvoltage, thermal, buck short-circuit and LDO current-shutdown values are typical figures that are "guaranteed by design" and not production tested (note 6).
- **Output capacitor advice differs between sections.** The design procedure asks for two X5R 10 V capacitors of at least 22 µF on each output, the BOM lists two 22 µF 6.3 V capacitors per buck (C7 to C14), and Figure 5 draws one 22 µF per output, two on Buck1 above 2.5 A.

## Datasheet notes

- **The Buck4 formula and its table disagree.** The formula is B4_VSET × 0.05 + 0.907 V, but the table gives 0.807 V at code 00h and 0.907 V at 02h, so the base must be 0.807 V. The electrical table's range (0.807 V to 1.907 V) agrees with the table.
- **Figures 3 and 6 mark a "Logic off" level that the text does not explain.** The text puts the falling UVLO threshold at 2.4 V typical (2.6 V rising minus 0.2 V hysteresis), while Figure 3 marks "Logic off" at 2.3 V and Figure 6 at 2.2 V.
- **The capacitor sizes in the BOM do not agree.** The 1 µF capacitors are described as 0603 in the BOM, as 0402 in Figure 5, and the part number GRM033C80G105MEA2D has Murata's "03" size code, which we take to mean 0201 (from Murata's usual naming, not checked against its datasheet).
- **R0 is not in the BOM.** The schematic draws a resistor in series with the AVIN pin with a 1 µF capacitor C0, and R0 has no value. The capacitor "C6" appears in the layout figure but not in the BOM or the schematic.
- **Application B's Buck4 output capacitor.** Figure 5(b) shows a 1 µF capacitor on Buck4 where the other two show 22 µF. It looks like a drawing error but the datasheet does not say.
- **A floating input disables its channel (Figure 6, note 3), yet floating is a valid bank setting.** Floating is one of three valid states for GPIO1, GPIO3 and GPIO4, and Application B uses all three floating. The note presumably refers to a different pin or condition, but does not say which.
- **B3_VID@SLEEP_EN is described confusingly.** Its 00h description says Buck3 "keeps VSET1" when entering SLEEP, which presumably should read VSET0.
- **Register field names.** The 0x04 description labels the LDO_OVP bit "DDR SEL" and the B4_Add bit "Buck4 Operating Mode B4_Mode".
- **LDO2's input-range rows use the symbol VIN_LDO1.** The two LDOs share the VIN_LDO1 pin, so this is consistent with the pinout, but the table does not say so.
