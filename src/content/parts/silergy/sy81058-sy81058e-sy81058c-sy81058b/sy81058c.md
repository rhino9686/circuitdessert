## How it works

The SY81058C is a synchronous buck regulator with both power switches inside a SOT23-6 package. It regulates with constant on-time, ripple-based control. The controller builds an internal copy of the inductor current, adds it to the feedback voltage, and starts a new on-time whenever that sum falls below the 0.6 V reference. The on-time is worked out inside the chip from the input voltage, the output voltage and the target frequency (tON = (VOUT ÷ VIN) ÷ fSW), so the switching frequency stays close to 1.2 MHz as VIN changes. The compensation is built in, and the part is stable with a 10 µF output capacitor.

At light load the C version uses pulse-frequency modulation (PFM). When the load falls below half the inductor ripple current, IOUT = VOUT × (1 − D) ÷ (2 × fSW × L), the bottom switch turns off before the current can reverse, and the controller skips cycles, so the switching frequency falls with the load. In this circuit at 12 V in, with a 2.2 µH inductor, that crossover is about 0.45 A. Above it, the part runs in continuous conduction at nearly constant frequency. In PFM the part stops switching as soon as FB rises above the reference, so no separate over-voltage protection is needed. The E and B versions behave differently: they keep the bottom switch on even when the inductor current crosses zero, so they stay at constant frequency down to no load, at the cost of light-load efficiency.

Enabling the part (EN rising through about 1.2 V) starts a 180 µs turn-on delay, then an internal soft-start of about 1 ms. The part can start into an output that is already pre-biased. Current limiting is cycle by cycle: the top switch turns off at its peak limit (at least 4 A) and the bottom switch stays on until the current falls below its valley limit (at least 3 A). If the output drops below 33% of its regulated level, the part enters hiccup mode, 1.5 ms on and 4.5 ms off, and recovers by itself when the short is removed. Over-temperature protection shuts the part down at 150 °C and restarts it, after a full soft-start, once the junction has cooled by about 15 °C. Input under-voltage lockout keeps switching off until VIN is above its rising threshold.

The datasheet states a maximum duty cycle of up to 75% across temperature, so the output can't sit above roughly 75% of the input. At 3.3 V that is about 4.4 V in, which is already below the part's own 4.5 V minimum.

## Example application

A 12 V to 3.3 V buck converter built from the circuit in Silergy's datasheet. The 1.2 MHz switching frequency allows a 2.2 µH inductor and a single 10 µF output capacitor. This section keeps the datasheet's component values, adds the reasoning behind each one, and works the numbers for 12 V in.

The datasheet gives one reference circuit for the 1.2 MHz parts, at 3.3 V, and a table of values for 1.2 V, 1.8 V and 5 V. The 500 kHz versions (SY81058 and SY81058E) have their own circuit with a 4.7 µH inductor. The datasheet does not name a nominal input voltage, so the worked example below uses 12 V, with 18 V as the worst case for ripple and peak current.

### Output voltage

The output is set by two resistors: VOUT = 0.6 V × (1 + RH ÷ RL). The datasheet recommends 1 kΩ to 1 MΩ for both, to keep light-load current low. With RH = 100 kΩ and a 3.3 V target, RL = 0.6 ÷ (3.3 − 0.6) × 100 kΩ = 22.2 kΩ. The nearest 1% value, 22.1 kΩ, gives 3.315 V nominal. With the reference's ±1.5% tolerance the output lands between 3.265 V and 3.365 V, before the resistors' own 1% tolerance is added.

### Inductor

Choose the inductance for a ripple current of about 40% of the maximum load at the highest input voltage: L = VOUT × (1 − VOUT ÷ VIN,MAX) ÷ (fSW × IOUT,MAX × 0.4). For 3.3 V, 18 V in, 3 A and 1.2 MHz that gives 1.87 µH, and the datasheet uses 2.2 µH. The part tolerates a wide range of ripple, so the final value can differ from the calculation with little effect.

Three more checks apply. The saturation current must exceed the peak current, ISAT > IOUT,MAX + VOUT × (1 − VOUT ÷ VIN,MAX) ÷ (2 × fSW × L). The winding resistance (DCR) should be below 50 mΩ for good efficiency, along with low core loss at 1.2 MHz. And for the forced-continuous versions (E and B) the inductance must be high enough that the reverse current limit isn't hit at zero load; the PFM C version doesn't have that concern.

### Output capacitor

Use an X5R or better ceramic rated for 16 V, with at least 10 µF. Output ripple has two parts: the inductor ripple current across the capacitor's ESR (ΔIL × ESR), and the charge on the capacitance (ΔIL ÷ (8 × COUT × fSW)). Add them for a total. Ceramic capacitance falls as the DC voltage across it rises, so measured ripple is usually higher than the calculation; read the effective capacitance off the capacitor's DC-bias curve and recalculate.

### Input capacitor

Use an X5R or X7R ceramic rated 25 V (at least 20% above the highest input voltage), 10 µF or more, as close as possible to the IN and GND pins with the smallest possible loop. The RMS current it carries is IOUT × √(D × (1 − D)), at most IOUT ÷ 2 at 50% duty, so choose one with an RMS rating above half the load current, and add capacitors in parallel if the calculation needs it. The datasheet says capacitance matters less than RMS rating, and that a single 10 µF X5R is enough for most applications. If VIN arrives over long wires, add bulk capacitance (electrolytic, tantalum or polymer) to damp overshoot and ringing from lead inductance. The reference circuit's 47 µF electrolytic, CIN1, is that kind of bulk capacitor.

### Bootstrap, feedforward and enable

The bootstrap capacitor CBS is a 0.1 µF low-ESR ceramic between BS and LX; it supplies the gate drive for the top switch. The feedforward capacitor CFF sits across RH and can speed up the load-transient response; the datasheet recommends at least 22 pF for most applications and a larger value when the output LC is large. This circuit uses 47 pF.

Don't connect EN straight to VIN or another voltage source; the datasheet asks for a resistor between 1 kΩ and 1 MΩ, and EN must not float. The reference circuit uses RENH = 10 kΩ and RENL = 1 MΩ, which puts EN at 99% of VIN, so the part starts when VIN passes its own under-voltage threshold and the divider only limits current. To start at a higher voltage, size the divider from the EN thresholds (1.2 V rising, 1.0 V falling, typical): VIN,start = 1.2 V × (1 + RENH ÷ RENL). As an example, RENH = 510 kΩ with RENL = 100 kΩ starts the part at about 7.3 V and stops it at about 6.1 V.

The conduction loss counts only the I²R drop in the two switches, so it is a floor. Switching loss and inductor loss come on top of it. At θJA = 55 °C/W the conduction loss alone is about 25 °C of temperature rise on the datasheet's test board.

## Layout

Silergy gives these layout rules, along with a suggested placement drawing (Figure 10 in the datasheet). With a package this small and fast switching edges, layout decides how well the converter works.

- Put the **bootstrap capacitor** on the same layer as the IC and keep the BS, LX and CBS path as short as possible.
- Put the **input capacitors** very close to the IN and GND pins, with the smallest loop, joined by a wide copper area.
- Connect the **output capacitor's** negative terminal to the GND pin with wide copper traces, not vias.
- Place **RH, RL and CFF** as close to the FB pin as possible and route the feedback line away from LX and other fast signals. Take the feedback from the output capacitor with a Kelvin connection, not from the inductor's output terminal.
- Keep the **LX copper** small to limit EMI, but wide enough to keep its resistance and inductance low.
- Use a **ground plane** if you can, with several GND vias around the device for heat. The datasheet's thermal resistance (θJA) is 55 °C/W on a 6 × 6 cm, two-layer, 2 oz board.

## Things to watch

- **The schematic's "4.5–18 V" is the part's range, not the range of every output.** The 75% duty limit means 5 V out needs about 6.7 V in or more.
- **The top-switch current limit is only 4 A minimum on a 3 A part.** With the datasheet's 2.2 µH inductor at 18 V in, the calculated peak is 3.51 A, leaving about 0.5 A. Tolerance on the inductance and any extra ripple eat into that.
- **The efficiency plots use 2.2 µH, but the electrical-characteristics table is specified with 4.7 µH.** The table's conditions (L = 4.7 µH, COUT = 10 µF) are the same for all four versions, even though the C version's own reference design uses 2.2 µH.
- **Quiescent current is only given for the PFM parts.** The E and B versions switch continuously, so expect a no-load input current well above the 200 µA figure here; the datasheet gives no number for them.
- **The CFF table dips below the text.** The text recommends at least 22 pF for most applications, but the C and B table lists 10 pF for the 1.2 V and 1.8 V outputs.
- **Pick the version before you pick the inductor.** The SY81058 and SY81058E need 4.7 µH at 3.3 V, the SY81058C and SY81058B only 2.2 µH. The parts are pin-compatible, but the inductor and CFF values are not interchangeable.

## Datasheet notes

- The layout section talks about connecting "the exposed GND pad" to a large copper area, but the SOT23-6 pinout shows no exposed pad. This is most likely carried over from a related datasheet with a different package, so just give the GND pin plenty of copper.
- The thermal table says case temperature θJC is measured "at Pin 6", which is the BS pin. The datasheet doesn't explain this.
- The BOM lists the manufacturer of the Murata parts as "mμRata", an obvious typo.
- Silergy's download link for this datasheet redirects to a sign-in page, so the datasheet field points to the product page instead, and the revision shown above (Rev 1.0B, 2025) is from the copy of the datasheet used to write this page, which hasn't been compared with the current one.
