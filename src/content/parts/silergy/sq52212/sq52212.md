abstract: "A two-channel digital power monitor for rails up to 70 V. For each channel a 16-bit delta-sigma ADC measures the shunt voltage and the bus voltage, and the chip calculates current, power and energy and stores them in registers that the host reads over I2C or SMBus. The shunt range is selectable, ±81.92 mV at 2.5 µV per step or ±20.48 mV at 625 nV per step, and the bus range is 52.4 V or 78.6 V (the datasheet limits use to 70 V). Shunt gain error is 0.02% typical and 0.25% maximum, shunt offset ±2 µV typical and ±10 µV maximum, and the input bias current is 0.1 nA typical and 5 nA maximum, so the part can use large shunts for small currents. Two alert registers can each be assigned to either channel, with five limit types. The interface is 1.2 V compatible with 16 addresses and high-speed mode to 3.4 MHz. The part draws 335 µA typical, 6 µA in shutdown and 5 nA when disabled by the EN pin, and comes in a 1.51 mm square 16-bump chip-scale package. The datasheet has no typical-performance plots and gives no component values for its application circuit other than 2 kΩ pull-ups."

## How it works

The SQ52212 monitors two rails at once. Each channel has its own shunt (measured between IN+ and IN−) and its own bus voltage (the VBUS pin). One 16-bit delta-sigma ADC is shared: it converts the shunt and then the bus of channel 1, then the same for channel 2, and goes round again. For each channel the chip calculates current, power and energy in the background and puts them in registers that the host reads over I2C or SMBus. Per channel there are five result registers (shunt voltage, bus voltage, current, power and a 32-bit energy count), a calibration register and an alert limit register.

**The bus is independent of the supply.** The inputs take −0.3 V to 70 V (72 V absolute maximum) while the part runs from VS at 2.7 V to 5.5 V. The datasheet says no supply sequencing is needed and that the bus can be present with the supply off. Either shunt can be on the high side or the low side, the shunt voltage is signed, and the bus voltage register is never negative.

**Two ranges per channel.** The shunt range is ±81.92 mV (2.5 µV per step) or ±20.48 mV (625 nV per step), chosen in CONFIG2. The bus range is 52.4 V (1.6 mV per step) or 78.6 V (2.4 mV per step), chosen in CONFIG3, and the datasheet says to keep the 78.6 V range's use to the recommended 70 V. Both reset to the lower range, so a bus above 52.4 V is outside the reset range until the bus range bit is set.

**Calibration sets current, power and energy.** The host writes a calibration value for each channel; until it does, current, power and energy read zero. The values come from the datasheet's equations:

- CAL = 2048 × Shunt_LSB ÷ (Current_LSB × RSHUNT), with Shunt_LSB 2.5 µV or 625 nV by range.
- Current_LSB must not be below Imax ÷ 2^15 and must stay under 8 times that, to avoid losing resolution.
- Current [A] = Current_LSB × CURRENT; Power [W] = 32 × Current_LSB × POWER (bus range 0) or 48 × (bus range 1); Energy [J] = 32 × Current_LSB × ENERGY, or 48 × for bus range 1.

Energy is accumulated by multiplying power by the elapsed time from an internal 500 kHz oscillator (±0.5% at 25 °C, ±1% over temperature). The energy error limit (1.2% maximum) is wider than the power error limit (0.7%).

**Averaging, conversion time and alerts.** The shunt and bus conversion times are set separately, 140 µs to 8.244 ms, and apply to both channels. Averaging of 1 to 1024 samples applies to both too, and registers update once the count is reached. An alert, however, is compared against each conversion as it completes, and averaged values do not trigger alerts. The datasheet presents this as a feature: the alert reacts faster than the averaged registers, while software watches the averaged values for slower limits.

**Two alert registers.** Each of the two alert configuration registers is assigned to a channel and set to one of five functions: shunt over, shunt under, bus over, bus under or power over. Two functions can be live at the same time. The pin is open drain, active low by default, with a latch option, and can also signal conversion ready or an energy overflow. The FLAGS register says which limit tripped. The part also answers the SMBus alert response address (0001 100) and the general-call reset (0000 000 followed by 06h).

**Power modes.** Shutdown (mode bits 000 or 100) keeps the I2C interface alive, takes 6 µA typical and 40 µs to leave. The EN pin disables the part completely: 5 nA typical, no bus response, the configuration is lost, and 100 µs to recover. A supply below 2.2 V triggers power-on reset.

### Choosing the shunt and the range

- **Range.** The full-scale current is 81.92 mV ÷ RSHUNT (or 20.48 mV ÷ RSHUNT in the low range): 41 A at 2 mΩ, 8.2 A at 10 mΩ (calibration table).
- **Small currents.** The 10 µV maximum offset is a fixed error: 5 mA at 2 mΩ, and 1% of the reading at 0.5 A (offset table). The low bias current lets the shunt be large, which the datasheet says allows sub-milliampere currents to be measured accurately.
- **Which shunt range.** The ±20.48 mV range gives finer steps (625 nV), not a lower offset limit: the datasheet gives one 10 µV maximum offset (at VCM = 12 V) with no range dependence.
- **Choosing CAL.** Round Current_LSB up from Imax ÷ 2^15 and use CAL = 2048 × Shunt_LSB ÷ (Current_LSB × RSHUNT), which for a step 1 to 8 times the smallest gives a CAL from 2048 down to 256 (our arithmetic).
- **Heat.** Full scale dissipates (81.92 mV)² ÷ RSHUNT: 3.4 W at 2 mΩ.

### Input filters

The datasheet says the low bias current allows input filters without the usual accuracy cost. That is true of the offset: 5 nA maximum through 100 Ω is 0.5 µV. But the input differential impedance is only 140 kΩ, so a resistor in each input divides the shunt voltage: 100 Ω in each is 0.14% gain error and 1 kΩ is 1.4%, against a 0.25% maximum gain error (filter table, calculated here). The bias figure is also given only for zero shunt voltage; with 140 kΩ across the inputs, the current into the pins at 20 mV across the shunt is about 143 nA (our arithmetic).

### Worked example: the datasheet's 12 V, 2 mΩ, 10 A case

The register values are the datasheet's; the extra comments are calculated here.

- **Current_LSB.** With a maximum current of 15 A, equation 2 gives 457.7 µA per bit. The datasheet uses 1 mA.
- **CAL.** 2048 × 2.5 µV ÷ (1 mA × 2 mΩ) = 2560 (0A00h).
- **Shunt register.** 10 A × 2 mΩ = 20 mV, which is 8000 counts (1F40h).
- **Current register.** 8000 × 2560 ÷ 2048 = 10000 (2710h), which is 10 A.
- **Bus register.** 12 V at 1.6 mV per step is 7500 (1D4Ch).
- **Power register.** 3750 (0EA0h), and at 32 mW per step that is 120 W.

Worked out here, beyond the datasheet's example:

- **The power register's arithmetic.** 10000 × 7500 ÷ 20000 = 3750 gives the datasheet's value. The same divisor fits the 78.6 V range, where 10000 × 5000 ÷ 20000 = 2500 and 2500 × 48 mW is again 120 W. The datasheet gives no formula for the divisor, so this is our inference from the two step ratios (1.6 mV × 20000 = 32 and 2.4 mV × 20000 = 48).
- **Range of the current register.** At 1 mA per step the signed 16-bit register ends at 32.767 A, which is 65.5 mV across 2 mΩ, below the shunt's 40.96 A. The OVF flag warns of an overflow. A 2 mA step covers the whole range.
- **Accuracy at 10 A.** The shunt voltage is 20 mV, so the 10 µV maximum offset is 0.05% and the 0.25% gain error brings it to 0.30% (error budget table). On the bus, 20 mV maximum offset is 0.17% of 12 V and the gain error 0.35%, 0.52% together. The datasheet's maximum power error, 0.7% at full-scale voltage and current, is in line with the two gain errors added (0.6%) plus offsets; a real shunt's tolerance adds to it.
- **Energy.** One count is 32 mJ at 1 mA per step. At 120 W the register overflows after about 13 days (energy table), so a host that totals energy over longer has to handle the overflow flag; ACC_RST in CONFIG2 resets the count and clears the flag.
- **Shunt dissipation.** 0.2 W at 10 A; 0.45 W at the 15 A used to find Current_LSB.

## Layout

The datasheet gives no layout figure beyond the package outline. On the digital lines it advises minimising coupling between SDA and SCL and from switching noise (coupled edges can be read as start or stop conditions), which routing the lines in parallel with ground between layers typically reduces, and shielding them. The points below are our notes, not the datasheet's:

- Bring IN+ and IN− to each shunt with a Kelvin connection and keep the loop small; the shunts are only milliohms.
- Put a bypass capacitor close to VS and GND (Figure 1 draws one but gives no value).
- With 70 V on the inputs, keep the creepage and clearance of the PCB in mind between the bus pins and the low-voltage pins of the chip-scale package.
- Keep the A0 and A1 straps short; the part samples them at every bus transaction.

## Things to watch

- **The reset bus range is 52.4 V.** A bus from 52.4 V up to 70 V needs CONFIG3 bit 14 (channel 1) or 15 (channel 2) set, which changes the step to 2.4 mV and the power step to 48 × Current_LSB.
- **Nothing works until it is calibrated.** Each channel's current, power and energy read zero until its CAL is written, again after any reset, power cycle or disable (the datasheet says the calibration registers must be programmed after power-up, power-cycle events or when the device is enabled).
- **The disabled state wipes the configuration.** Taking EN low resets everything; the part ignores the bus, and needs 100 µs after EN goes high before it is used. Shutdown by the mode bits keeps the registers and the bus, and recovers in 40 µs.
- **EN needs a higher logic level than the bus.** The I2C inputs accept a 0.9 V high level (a 1.2 V host works at any supply), but EN's high level is 1.4 V minimum, so a 1.2 V GPIO can't drive it.
- **Alerts act on each conversion, not on the averaged value.** That makes them fast, but a noisy shunt reading can trip a limit that the averaged registers would not show. See the update-interval table for the time between checks.
- **The flags name the limit, not the channel.** FLAGS bits 12 and 13 say which of the two limits tripped, independent of channel; the channel is whatever that limit register was assigned to.
- **The energy register overflows.** At 1 mA per step and 120 W it takes about 13 days (energy table). The overflow flag and the ENOF_MASK alert exist, and ACC_RST clears the count.
- **The 140 kΩ differential input impedance matters with filters.** 100 Ω resistors in each input cost 0.14% of the shunt voltage, as large as half the maximum gain error. The bias current figure is only for zero shunt voltage.
- **Bus offset is large on low rails.** The 20 mV maximum is 0.6% of 3.3 V and 2% of 1 V (bus error table).
- **Little margin above the bus range.** The inputs stop at 72 V, 2 V above the 70 V recommended maximum, so transients on the bus need to stay under that (our note). The differential limit is ±40 V.
- **Offset and its drift.** The shunt offset drifts up to 100 nV/°C maximum, which is up to 10 µV over 100 °C, as much as the 10 µV limit itself (calculated). The bus offset drifts up to 40 µV/°C, which is 4 mV over 100 °C.
- **Quiescent current over temperature.** 370 µA maximum at 25 °C and 500 µA over the temperature range; at 5.5 V that is up to 2.8 mW (our arithmetic).
- **Chip-scale package.** The datasheet's thermal figures (θJA 38 °C/W, 2.63 W) are for a four-layer board with thermal vias; the part's own dissipation is a few milliwatts, so this matters little.

## Datasheet notes

- **The register list is pointed at Table 1 but is Table 2.** The text says "See Table 1 for a complete list of registers"; Table 1 is the address table and the register map is Table 2.
- **Energy registers "address = 09h".** The heading says 09h, which is the channel 2 bus voltage register; the table gives 04h and 0Ch.
- **"Three modes: continuous and triggered".** The text lists two, then describes two low-power modes.
- **Range fields are in two places.** The text says both the shunt range and the bus range are in CONFIG2; the register tables put the shunt range in CONFIG2 (bits 1 and 0) and the bus range in CONFIG3 (bits 15 and 14).
- **ACC_RST's default reads "00b = All channels active".** It looks copied from the channel-enable field; the bits reset to 0 after any write.
- **Shutdown current.** The text says less than 4 µA; the table says 6 µA typical and 10 µA maximum.
- **The example uses the wrong addresses in places.** The text calls the shunt voltage register "01h" and the bus voltage register "01h", while the table gives 00h and 01h. It also says the VBUS pin "is connected to the VBUS pin"; the test conditions and the application text say the bus is measured at the IN− pin (VBUS = VIN− = 12 V), while Figure 1 draws VBUS on the bus side of the shunt.
- **Shunt_LSB is written "2.5uA" and "625nA".** They are 2.5 µV and 625 nV.
- **"Four possible addresses".** The address text says four; the table gives sixteen.
- **Full scale values.** The feature list says 52.42 V and 78.64 V, the tables say 52.4 V and 78.6 V (32768 steps at 1.6 mV is 52.43 V and at 2.4 mV is 78.64 V).
- **"Return to the Summary Table"** is left in the text after the two's-complement example.
- **The recommended differential input range is not listed.** Only the absolute maximum (±40 V) and the ADC full scales are given.
