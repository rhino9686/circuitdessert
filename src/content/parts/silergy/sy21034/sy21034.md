## How it works

The SY21034 is a buck converter for input voltages up to 100 V. The power switch, an N-channel FET with 1 Ω on-resistance, is inside the TSOT23-6 package; the rectifier is not. It is asynchronous: a Schottky diode outside the chip carries the inductor's current whenever the switch is off. At a low duty cycle, such as 5 V from 48 V, that diode conducts about 90% of the time, so its forward drop becomes the largest single loss (see the worked example).

**Control.** It uses constant off-time with peak-current mode. When the switch current reaches an internal threshold, VCOMP (our reading of the block diagram: the error amplifier's output, which compares FB with the 0.6 V reference), the switch turns off for a fixed time, tOFF = (1 − VOUT ÷ VIN) ÷ fSW, which the chip calculates from the input voltage, the output voltage and the target frequency. Then it turns on again. At 200 kHz that gives a pseudo-constant frequency (160 to 240 kHz in the electrical table) and a peak inductor current that follows the load. Compensation is inside the chip, so the parts around it are the inductor, the capacitors, the diode and the feedback divider. For large load steps the datasheet suggests 47 pF in parallel with RH.

**Duty-cycle limits.** The minimum on-time and off-time are both 80 ns typical. At 200 kHz that means VOUT can't go below about 1.6% of VIN (1.6 V from 100 V, 0.77 V from 48 V) and can't go above about 98.4% of VIN. The datasheet states neither limit; they are our arithmetic from the two minimum times, and the on-time one matters for low outputs at high inputs (the feedback section below). Silergy's product page lists a maximum duty cycle of 80%, which the datasheet doesn't repeat and which we can't reconcile with an 80 ns off-time; if it applies, a 12 V output needs at least 15 V in. Test low-dropout operation before relying on it.

**Enable and shutdown.** EN above 1.2 V (1.14 to 1.26 V) turns the part on and below 1.0 V (0.94 to 1.06 V) shuts it down, to 9 µA typical (20 µA maximum). The datasheet asks that EN not be left floating and not be tied straight to IN: if it is pulled up from IN, use a resistor of 1 kΩ to 1 MΩ. The feature list calls the threshold accurate, so a divider from VIN can serve as an input undervoltage lockout; the table *Enable divider for an input turn-on level* above sizes one.

**Start-up and protection.** An internal soft-start takes 800 µs typical. The switch's peak current is limited cycle by cycle, at 0.9 A minimum and 1.3 A maximum. If the load keeps rising past the limit the output voltage falls, and a short circuit sends the part into hiccup mode (the datasheet gives no timing; its short-circuit captures are at 20 ms per division). Over-temperature protection stops the part at 150 °C and restarts it, after a complete soft-start, about 15 °C lower.

**Bootstrap.** The high-side gate driver is supplied by a 100 nF capacitor between BS and LX, which the datasheet's circuits show with a 10 Ω resistor in series. The block diagram also shows a "Refresh FET" from LX to ground that the text never mentions. Our guess, which the datasheet doesn't confirm, is that it recharges the bootstrap capacitor when the diode isn't carrying current, as at light load.

**Light load.** The datasheet doesn't say what the part does at light load (Silergy's product page lists Auto PFM). Its efficiency plots start at 1 mA, and at high input voltage they show that a lot of power is lost there; the table *Light-load loss read from the efficiency plots* above puts numbers on it.

### Output voltage (RH, RL, CFF)

VOUT = 0.6 V × (1 + RH ÷ RL), so RL = 0.6 V × RH ÷ (VOUT − 0.6 V). The datasheet suggests 10 kΩ to 1 MΩ for both resistors to keep the light-load drain down, and uses 100 kΩ for RH. The table *Feedback resistors for common outputs* above gives E96 values for other outputs. That divider is not free: it draws 44 µA from a 5 V output and 115 µA from a 12 V one (calculated), which is as much as the converter's own 100 µA quiescent current, though at the output voltage rather than the input.

**Accuracy.** The reference is 0.588 to 0.612 V in the electrical table, which is ±2%, not the ±1% of the feature list. For the 5 V circuit that gives 4.88 to 5.08 V, and 4.80 to 5.17 V with 1% resistors (calculated). The FB bias current, at most 50 nA, shifts the output by 0.1% or less with these resistors (table above), so 100 kΩ-class dividers are fine here.

**Feedforward capacitor.** CFF is marked optional in the datasheet and recommended for large load steps. With RH = 100 kΩ it adds a zero at about 34 kHz (calculated), below the 51 and 59 kHz bandwidths on the Bode plots.

**Low outputs from high inputs.** Because tON = (VOUT ÷ VIN) ÷ fSW must be at least 80 ns, the lowest output the part can regulate at 200 kHz is about 0.016 × VIN: 0.77 V at 48 V, 1.2 V at 75 V and 1.6 V at 100 V. At the 240 kHz maximum frequency the 100 V figure becomes 1.9 V. A 1.8 V output from 100 V is therefore marginal, and from 48 V it has plenty of room (calculated).

### Inductor (L)

The datasheet's rule is a ripple current of about 40% of the maximum load: L = VOUT × (1 − VOUT ÷ VIN,MAX) ÷ (fSW × IOUT,MAX × 0.4). At 100 V in and 0.6 A that is 99 µH for 5 V and 220 µH for 12 V, yet its bill of materials uses 47 µH and 68 µH, about half and a third of that. It adds that the part tolerates a wide range of ripple. The table *Inductor ripple and peak current* above shows what the smaller values do:

- **5 V with 47 µH:** the ripple is 0.42 to 0.51 A (70% to 84% of 0.6 A) and the peak at full load stays at 0.85 A or less at 200 kHz. That is under the 0.9 A minimum limit, by 0.05 A at 100 V in. At the 160 kHz minimum frequency the peak reaches 0.92 A at 100 V.
- **12 V with 68 µH:** at 48 V in and above, the peak at 0.6 A is 0.93 to 0.99 A at 200 kHz, over the 0.9 A minimum limit. At the minimum limit this circuit supplies only about 0.57 A at 48 V and 0.51 A at 100 V (calculated). The limit's maximum is 1.3 A, so most parts have more room, but the datasheet only guarantees 0.9 A. A larger inductor fixes it, at the cost of size.

The inductor's saturation current must exceed the peak, IOUT,MAX + VOUT × (1 − VOUT ÷ VIN,MAX) ÷ (2 × fSW × L), which is 0.85 A for the 5 V circuit and 0.99 A for the 12 V one at 100 V in (calculated). The datasheet's inductor column has no current ratings, so check the Sumida parts' own datasheets. It also asks for a DCR below 50 mΩ for good efficiency, which is a tall order for a 47 to 68 µH inductor of the BOM's size; check the DCR of the inductor you pick.

### Capacitors (CIN1, CIN2, COUT, CBS)

**Input.** The datasheet is inconsistent about the size: the Application Information says X5R or better, 1 µF, and a voltage rating at least 20% above the highest input; the pin table says at least 4.7 µF; a later sentence says a single 4.7 µF X5R is enough in most applications; the bill of materials uses 2.2 µF (100 V, X7R) plus a 22 µF / 200 V electrolytic. The RMS current is IOUT × √(D × (1 − D)), at most half the load current. At 48 V in and 5 V out at 0.5 A it is 0.15 A, and 2.2 µF ripples by about 0.11 V (calculated, before the capacitance lost to DC bias, which at 48 V can be much of it for a ceramic; check the capacitor's own bias curve). The 20% rule asks for 120 V at a 100 V input, which the 100 V ceramic doesn't meet. The 200 V electrolytic is the kind of bulk capacitor the datasheet suggests where long supply cables can ring at the input. The part's rating is 100 V, with no allowance above it.

**Output.** The text asks for X5R or better with more than 22 µF, the bill of materials uses 22 µF, and the electrical table was measured with 10 µF. With 47 µH at 48 V in, the capacitive ripple (ΔIL ÷ (8 × COUT × fSW)) is about 14 mV with 22 µF and 30 mV with 10 µF (calculated, before ESR and the loss of capacitance under DC bias). The datasheet's ESR term is ΔIL × ESR, a few millivolts for a ceramic.

**Bootstrap.** CBS is 100 nF, X7R, 50 V, with the 10 Ω RBS in series. A 10 Ω resistor was added to the datasheet's circuits in Rev 0.9B, according to its revision history; it doesn't say why.

### Rectifier diode (D)

The datasheet asks for a Schottky diode with a low forward voltage and fast switching, and says its voltage rating must be higher than the maximum output voltage. That can't be right for a buck converter: when the switch is on, the LX node is at VIN, so the diode's reverse voltage is the input voltage. Rate it above the highest input with margin; the bill of materials' SS320 is 200 V. Its average current is IOUT × (1 − D), up to 0.57 A at 0.6 A with 5 V from 100 V, and its peak is the inductor's peak current (0.85 to 0.99 A for the two circuits at 100 V). Its dissipation is the biggest loss in the circuit at a low duty cycle: with an assumed forward voltage of 0.7 V (check the diode's own datasheet), 0.31 W at 5 V and 0.5 A from 48 V, more than ten times the conduction loss in the chip (calculated).

### EN: jumper, undervoltage lockout (Jump_EN, RENH, RENL)

With the jumper fitted, EN sits at VIN × 1 MΩ ÷ (10 kΩ + 1 MΩ), about 99% of VIN. EN passes 1.2 V when VIN is only 1.2 V, so the part turns on as soon as the input passes its own 4.5 V undervoltage lockout. That divider draws VIN ÷ 1.01 MΩ: 48 µA at 48 V and 99 µA at 100 V, with 2.3 mW at 48 V (calculated). Remove the jumper and RENL pulls EN to ground, so the part is off. The datasheet's Figure 1 shows EN as an ON/OFF input instead.

For a real input undervoltage lockout, make RENH larger (table above). The falling threshold is 1.0 V against 1.2 V rising, so with ideal resistors the part turns off at 83% of its turn-on voltage, and over the datasheet's spread the ratio could be as low as 75% (0.94 ÷ 1.26). A 48 V turn-on gives a turn-off near 40 V, and a 36 V turn-on near 30 V. EN's absolute maximum is IN + 0.3 V, so a divider from IN is safe at any input. The datasheet gives no EN input current, so the divider's accuracy at high resistance is unknown.

### Worked example: a 5 V rail from a 48 V telecom bus

A telecom bus of 36 to 75 V feeds a 5 V rail that needs up to 0.5 A, using the datasheet's circuit (47 µH, 22 µF, the diode SS320). The efficiency is read by eye from the 5 V plot and is good to a few points; everything else is calculated.

| Quantity | Value | How |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Duty cycle, 36 V to 75 V in | 14% to 6.7%, 10.4% at 48 V | VOUT ÷ VIN |
| On-time | 694 ns at 36 V, 521 ns at 48 V, 333 ns at 75 V | D ÷ 200 kHz; well above the 80 ns minimum |
| Off-time | 4.5 µs at 48 V | (1 − D) ÷ 200 kHz |
| Inductor ripple, 47 µH | 0.46 A at 36 V, 0.48 A at 48 V, 0.50 A at 75 V | Table above; about 92% to 99% of the load |
| Peak current at 0.5 A | 0.73 to 0.75 A, or 0.79 to 0.81 A at 160 kHz | Half the ripple above the load; the minimum limit is 0.9 A |
| Light-load boundary | Below about 0.24 A the inductor current reaches zero | Half the ripple (our reading of how a diode-rectified converter behaves) |
| Input capacitor | 0.15 A RMS; about 0.11 V ripple with 2.2 µF at 48 V | IOUT × √(D × (1 − D)); IOUT × D × (1 − D) ÷ (fSW × CIN) |
| Output ripple | About 14 mV capacitive with 22 µF | ΔIL ÷ (8 × COUT × fSW), before ESR and DC-bias loss |
| Efficiency at 0.5 A | About 81% at 48 V (84% at 24 V, 76% at 85 V) | Read by eye from the datasheet's 5 V plot |
| Total loss at 48 V | 0.59 W (3.09 W in for 2.5 W out) | From the efficiency above |
| Diode | 0.31 W, about half of the loss | 0.45 A average × an assumed 0.7 V |
| Conduction loss in the chip | 28 mW | 0.27 A² (RMS current squared) × 1 Ω × D |
| Inductor | About 13 mW | 0.27 A² × 50 mΩ (the datasheet's DCR target) |
| Quiescent and EN divider | 4.8 mW and 2.3 mW | 100 µA × 48 V; 48 V × 48 µA |
| Left over | About 0.22 W: switching, core and capacitor losses | Total loss minus the lines above |
| Die temperature rise | 3 °C to 26 °C | θJA 100 °C/W times the chip's conduction and quiescent loss (3 °C), or times all of the left-over loss as well (26 °C) |

**Where the heat goes.** The diode dissipates more than the chip's switch at this duty cycle. The datasheet doesn't split out the chip's switching loss, so the chip's share of the 0.59 W is somewhere between 33 mW and 0.26 W; the upper figure is a bound, not an estimate. At 25 °C ambient even the bound leaves the junction near 50 °C. Hot enclosures are the concern: at 85 °C ambient the bound is 111 °C against the 125 °C limit, and at 85 V in it grows to 45 °C (130 °C at 85 °C ambient, over the 125 °C limit). Measure the chip's temperature on your board, whose θJA won't be the datasheet's evaluation board's 100 °C/W.

**The peak current margin is thin at the top of the range.** With 47 µH the peak at 0.5 A is 0.75 A at 75 V, 0.15 A under the 0.9 A minimum limit. At 0.6 A and 100 V it is 0.85 A, and a part running at the 160 kHz minimum frequency reaches 0.92 A. Move to 68 to 100 µH if the rail must supply the full 0.6 A across the input range.

**The bus must stay at or below 100 V.** There is no headroom between the recommended maximum and the 100 V absolute maximum on IN, and a 48 V telecom feed can ring above 75 V on a hot-plug or a switched load. Add a transient suppressor and the bulk capacitor, and check the capacitor's DC-bias curve at the real input voltage.

**Light-load rail.** If the 5 V rail idles at a few milliamps, the loss in the light-load table dominates: about 21 mW at 48 V (calculated from the plot at 1 mA), roughly three times the 7 mW that the quiescent current and the EN divider account for.

## Layout

From the datasheet's layout guide and Figure 4:

- **Input capacitors** very close to IN and GND, joined by wide copper to minimise the loop. The datasheet also says to put the capacitor close to the negative end of the rectifier; the switching current loop runs through the input capacitor, the switch and the diode, so keep that loop small (our note).
- **Output capacitor's** ground terminal to GND with wide copper, not vias.
- **RH, RL and CFF** as close to FB as possible, away from LX and other fast signals. Connect the divider to the output capacitor with a Kelvin connection, not to the inductor's output terminal. Figure 4 shows them stacked under the FB pin with a separate trace to the output capacitor.
- **LX copper** small for EMI, but wide enough for low resistance and inductance.
- **Control signals** not tied straight to VIN: use a resistor of 1 kΩ to 1 MΩ.
- **Ground vias** around the part for heat, and a large ground copper area connected to the GND pin; a ground plane if space allows. The datasheet's text speaks of an exposed pad; the TSOT23-6 has none (see the datasheet notes).
- **In Figure 4** the inductor is above the IC, the diode D sits beside COUT on the output side, RBS and CBS are next to the BS and LX pins, and CIN is at the IN pin.

## Things to watch

- **Light-load efficiency is poor at high input voltage.** At 1 mA the plots show about 20% (5 V out) or 35% (12 V out) at 48 V in, and about 8% or 17% at 85 V in. That is a loss of roughly 21 mW at 48 V and 58 mW at 85 V for either output, of which the quiescent current explains only 5 and 9 mW. The part is a poor fit for a rail that idles at low current on a 48 V or higher bus; measure before committing.
- **The datasheet's 12 V circuit may reach the current limit before 0.6 A.** With the 68 µH inductor at 48 V in and above, the calculated peak at 0.6 A is over the 0.9 A minimum limit. See the inductor section and the table.
- **The diode needs a voltage rating above the input.** The datasheet's text says "output". Its 200 V SS320 is right for a 100 V input.
- **The input rating has no margin.** 100 V is both the recommended and absolute maximum, so any spike above it is a violation. Use ceramics rated for at least 120 V (the datasheet's own rule), check the bias curve, and protect the input.
- **Don't tie EN straight to IN.** Use a resistor of 1 kΩ to 1 MΩ. With the datasheet's 10 kΩ / 1 MΩ the divider draws about 1 µA per volt of VIN even when the converter is idle.
- **Low outputs from high inputs run into the minimum on-time.** The lowest output the part can regulate is about 0.016 × VIN, so a 1 V output works only up to about 62 V in; see the output-voltage section.
- **Several behaviours are not documented.** The datasheet gives no light-load mode, no hiccup timing, no typical current limit, no maximum duty cycle, no EN input current and no absolute maximum for BS.
- **The heat budget is small.** θJA is 100 °C/W on the evaluation board and the datasheet's power dissipation is 1 W at 25 °C. Much of the loss is in the diode and the inductor, but the chip's own share, switching loss included, is not given.

## Datasheet notes

- **The detailed description reads as if it were for a different part.** It says the part delivers 2 A and switches at 800 kHz. The feature list, electrical table, plots and bill of materials all say 0.6 A and 200 kHz, and we follow those. The heading of the Typical Performance Characteristics gives "VIN = 12 V, VOUT = 5 V, L = 6.8 µH, COUT = 22 µF, IOUT = 2 A", which matches none of the plots under it (each gives its own conditions, with 47 µH or 68 µH inductors and up to 85 V in).
- **The current limit is printed in mA.** The electrical table gives 0.9 (minimum) and 1.3 (maximum) mA, which can't be right for a 0.6 A part; we read them as amps. There is no typical value.
- **Reference tolerance.** The feature list says ±1%; the electrical table's 0.588 to 0.612 V is ±2%.
- **The FB input current test condition is VFB = VIN,** with VIN = 48 V in the table's conditions, which is over the 24 V absolute maximum on FB.
- **Shutdown current.** The text says it drops below 10 µA; the electrical table's maximum is 20 µA (9 µA typical).
- **Capacitor sizes disagree.** Input: 1 µF in one place, 4.7 µF in the pin table, 2.2 µF in the bill of materials. Output: more than 22 µF in the text, 22 µF in the bill of materials, 10 µF in the electrical table's conditions.
- **The diode's voltage rating** is given against the maximum output voltage; it should be the maximum input voltage.
- **The layout text mentions an exposed GND pad.** The TSOT23-6 has no exposed pad (the pinout and package drawing show six leads only), so we take it to mean the ground copper around the GND pin.
- **The over-temperature paragraph** calls the control scheme "Instant-PWM architecture", a name used nowhere else in the datasheet.
- **BS has no absolute maximum rating,** and Jump_EN, which is on the application schematic, is not in the bill of materials.
