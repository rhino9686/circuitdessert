## How it works

The SY20011 is a synchronous buck converter with both switches inside a 2 × 2 mm package, built for products that sit asleep on a small battery and wake up for short bursts of work. It uses constant on-time (COT) control: the output voltage is compared with the reference directly, with a ramp built from the inductor's ripple current, and each time the output dips the top switch turns on for a fixed time, tON = VOUT ÷ (fSW × VIN), which the part calculates to give about 1 MHz. Then the bottom switch conducts until the output needs the next pulse. Compensation is internal, so the only external parts are the inductor, the capacitors and the feedback divider.

**Light load.** Because a pulse is issued only when the output needs one, the switching frequency falls with the load, and with no load the part spends nearly all its time idle. That is where the quiescent current matters, and the STB pin sets it.

**STB: 400 nA or 15 µA.** With STB low, the part runs in deep standby and draws 400 nA typical (600 nA maximum) from the input while it isn't switching. The price is response time: waking from standby adds about 3 µs, so a sudden load step sees a larger dip before the converter catches it (Figure 4). With STB high, the part draws 15 µA but answers load steps at once. STB can come from a microcontroller pin, so firmware can pick the fast mode while it is busy and deep standby while it sleeps.

**EN and shutdown.** EN above 1.1 V turns the part on, and switching starts about 0.4 ms later, followed by an internal soft-start. Below 0.4 V it shuts down to 10 nA typical (100 nA maximum), and a 10 Ω switch discharges the output. EN must not float.

**OUT and FB.** The OUT pin is a separate sense connection to the output, which the datasheet asks to be taken from the output capacitor rather than the inductor. The feedback divider sets VOUT = 1.2 V × (1 + RH ÷ RL), from 1.2 V up to the input. The datasheet recommends 10 to 50 MΩ for both resistors, so the divider itself draws only tens of nanoamps; a 100 kΩ divider would draw more than the whole converter.

**Dropout.** When the input falls close to the output, the top switch stays on (100% duty cycle) and the output follows the input, less the drop in the top switch and the inductor. The datasheet gives the threshold as VOUT plus IOUT × (inductor DCR + top-switch resistance), and leaving bypass needs about 3% more (our reading of its formulas). This matters for a 3.3 V output from a lithium cell, which runs in bypass for the last part of the discharge.

**Protection.** The top switch's peak current is limited to 0.9 A minimum, and the bottom switch's valley current to 0.5 A minimum; after two peak-limit trips the valley limit folds back to 65%. If the output stays below 1 V for about 20 µs, the part goes into hiccup mode, 0.25 ms on and 0.25 ms off, and restarts with a soft-start when the fault clears. Over-temperature protection stops the part at 150 °C and restarts it 15 °C lower. The power-good output is released when FB is above 90% of the reference and below the over-voltage threshold, and pulls low otherwise.

### Output voltage (RH, RL, CFF)

VOUT = 1.2 V × (1 + RH ÷ RL). With RL = 20 MΩ, the datasheet's example, RH = (VOUT − 1.2 V) × RL ÷ 1.2 V, so 10 MΩ for 1.8 V. The reference's ±1.5% gives 1.773 to 1.827 V, and 1% resistors widen that to 1.761 to 1.839 V worst case (calculated). The table *Feedback resistors for common outputs* above gives E24 pairs for other outputs, all within the 10–50 MΩ range.

At these values, small leaks matter. The datasheet doesn't give the FB pin's input current, and a board leakage of a few hundred megaohms across RL or from FB to a nearby trace shifts the output measurably; keep the board clean around FB and the divider (our note). The 10 pF CFF across RH speeds up the load response; the datasheet recommends it where load steps are large, and the layout guide puts RH, RL and CFF close to the FB pin.

### Inductor (L)

The datasheet's rule is a ripple current of about 40% of the maximum load: L = VOUT × (1 − VOUT ÷ VIN,MAX) ÷ (fSW × IOUT,MAX × 0.4). For 1.8 V from 5.5 V at 500 mA that is 6.1 µH, yet its bill of materials, its efficiency plots and its Table 1 all use or allow 2.2 µH, which gives about 105% ripple at 5 V (calculated). The datasheet says the part tolerates a wide range of ripple. The table *Inductor ripple and peak current* above shows what each choice does: with 2.2 µH the peak reaches 0.78 A at 5.5 V and full load, still below the 0.9 A minimum peak limit, with 0.12 A to spare. A 4.7 µH inductor cuts the ripple in half and the output ripple with it, at the cost of size and DCR.

The inductor's saturation current must exceed the peak, IOUT,MAX + ΔIL ÷ 2, and its DCR should be under 50 mΩ for good efficiency.

### Capacitors (CIN1, CIN2, COUT)

Input: an X5R or better ceramic of more than 10 µF rated for 6.3 V, close to IN and GND with the smallest loop. Its RMS current is IOUT × √(D × (1 − D)), at most half the load current; at 5 V and 500 mA that is 0.24 A, and a 10 µF ceramic ripples by about 12 mV (calculated). The 100 µF electrolytic in the bill of materials is the bulk capacitance the datasheet recommends when the input arrives on long wires; a battery soldered to the board doesn't need it.

Output: the text asks for more than 22 µF, but the bill of materials, the electrical table and Table 1 all use 10 µF. With 2.2 µH at 5 V in, 10 µF gives about 6.5 mV of capacitive ripple, 22 µF about 3 mV, before ESR and the loss of capacitance under DC bias (calculated). Table 1 allows up to 350 µF with 2.2 µH.

### EN, STB and PG (RENH, RENL, RSTBH, RSTBL, RPG)

The datasheet doesn't want control pins tied straight to VIN: it asks for a resistor of 1 kΩ to 1 MΩ when they are pulled up to the input. Its circuit uses a 1 kΩ / 1 MΩ divider on each of EN and STB, which holds both high. That has two consequences for battery life:

- **STB is high, so the part runs in its 15 µA mode, not the 400 nA one.**
- **The two dividers draw VIN ÷ 1 MΩ each**, 10 µA together at 5 V, 7.4 µA at 3.7 V.

So the datasheet's own circuit draws about 22 to 25 µA with no load, some 50 times the headline figure (calculated). For the 400 nA mode, tie STB to ground (or drive it from a microcontroller), and pull EN up through 1 MΩ with no resistor to ground, or drive it too. The table *Standby drain from a 3.7 V cell* above compares the options.

The 100 kΩ PG pull-up draws nothing while PG is released (output good) but VIN ÷ 100 kΩ, 37 µA at 3.7 V, while PG pulls low. The datasheet doesn't say what PG does with EN low; if it pulls low in shutdown, as its description suggests, that current dwarfs the 10 nA shutdown figure. Pull PG up to a rail that is off in shutdown, such as VOUT or the microcontroller's own supply, or use a higher value (our suggestion).

### Worked example: a wearable on a lithium cell

A 100 mAh lithium-polymer cell (4.2 V full, 3.0 V empty, 3.7 V nominal) feeds a 1.8 V rail for a microcontroller that sleeps most of the time and wakes for radio bursts of up to 200 mA. The circuit is the datasheet's, with STB driven from a microcontroller pin and EN pulled up through 1 MΩ. Everything here is calculated.

| Quantity | Value | How |
| --- | --- | --- |
| On-time, 4.2 V to 3.0 V in | 429 ns to 600 ns | 1.8 V ÷ (1 MHz × VIN); well above the 80 ns minimum |
| Inductor ripple, 2.2 µH | 0.47 A at 4.2 V, 0.33 A at 3.0 V | Table above |
| Peak current at a 200 mA burst | 0.43 A at 4.2 V | Half the ripple above the load; under the 0.9 A limit |
| Light-load operation | Pulse skipping below about 0.23 A at 4.2 V | Half the ripple; the frequency falls with the load (our reading of COT operation) |
| Sleep drain, STB low | About 0.43 µA typical, 0.63 µA maximum | 400 nA (600 nA) plus the divider's 60 nA reflected to the cell |
| Same, with the datasheet's EN and STB dividers | About 22 µA | 15 µA plus 7.4 µA |
| Converter's own share of the 100 mAh, per year | 3.8 mAh (5.5 mAh maximum) | 0.43 µA × 8760 h |
| Conduction loss at 500 mA, 5 V in | 48 mW in the switches, 14 mW in a 50 mΩ inductor, 4 °C die rise | I²R with 280 mΩ and 120 mΩ, θJA 85 °C/W |

**Switch STB with the workload.** In deep standby the converter costs under 4 mAh a year, about 4% of the cell; with STB high it would cost 130 mAh, more than the whole cell. Driving STB low when the microcontroller sleeps and high just before a radio burst keeps both the drain and the dip on the load step small. If STB stays low during bursts, check the 1.8 V rail's dip on the first load step against the microcontroller's brown-out level.

**The battery covers the whole range.** At 3.0 V the converter still has 1.2 V of headroom over 1.8 V, so it never reaches dropout. A 3.3 V output would: from a 0.5 A load it enters bypass below about 3.47 V (VOUT + 0.5 A × (280 + 50) mΩ, calculated), and then the rail falls with the cell.

## Layout

From the datasheet's layout guide (Figure 6):

- **Input capacitors** as close as possible to IN and GND, joined by wide copper, with the smallest loop.
- **Output capacitor's** ground terminal to GND with wide copper, not vias.
- **RH, RL and CFF close to FB**, away from LX and other fast signals. Take OUT and the divider from the output capacitor with a Kelvin connection, not from the inductor.
- **LX copper** small for EMI but wide enough for low resistance and inductance.
- **Control pins** pulled up to VIN through 1 kΩ to 1 MΩ, not directly.
- **Ground vias** around the part and in the exposed pad, which goes to a copper area larger than itself; a ground plane if space allows.

## Things to watch

- **The datasheet's circuit doesn't run in the 400 nA mode.** Its dividers hold STB high (15 µA) and draw about 7 to 10 µA more themselves. Tie STB low or drive it, and drop the resistors to ground on EN and STB, to get the headline figure.
- **Deep standby trades away load response.** With STB low, waking adds about 3 µs and a bigger dip on fast load steps.
- **Watch the PG pull-up in shutdown.** A 100 kΩ pull-up to VIN draws 37 to 50 µA whenever PG is low, which may include shutdown; pull it up to a switched rail.
- **High-value feedback resistors need a clean board.** The divider is 10–50 MΩ, and the FB input current isn't specified.
- **The 2.2 µH inductor runs at about 100% ripple** at 5 V, against the datasheet's own 40% rule; the peak still stays under the 0.9 A minimum limit at 500 mA.
- **Small errors and inconsistencies in the datasheet.** The feature list gives the top switch as 170 mΩ, the electrical table as 280 mΩ. The minimum input is 2.5 V in the feature list and recommended conditions, but 2.2 V in the electrical table, the typical-application figure ("VIN : 2.2 ~ 5.5V"), the detailed description and Silergy's product page. The output-capacitor text asks for more than 22 µF, the bill of materials uses 10 µF. The bypass formula reads "VOUT_SET × 1.03%", which only makes sense as 103%. EN is called "high-voltage-capable", but its absolute maximum is IN + 0.6 V. The over-voltage threshold that bounds power good isn't given (the block diagram shows 120% of VREF).
- **Absolute maximum is 6 V on IN.** A 5 V USB rail with spikes is close; the recommended maximum is 5.5 V.
