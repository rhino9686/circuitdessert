## How it works

The SQ82550 is a shunt reference: a two-terminal part that behaves like a very precise Zener diode. Fed from a supply through a resistor, it conducts whatever current is needed to hold its cathode at its breakdown voltage, and the resistor drops the rest of the supply. The load connects across it and takes its share of the resistor's current; the reference takes what's left (Figure 1).

**Its own supply doesn't matter, its current does.** Since the reference sets its voltage by conducting, any supply above the reference voltage works, as long as the resistor gives the reference at least its minimum current in the worst case (lowest supply, highest load) and no more than 15 mA in the other (highest supply, no load). The minimum is 88 to 100 µA at 25 °C and 115 to 128 µA over temperature, depending on version. Within that window, the voltage moves by about 1 mV from the minimum to 1 mA, and by up to 9 to 12 mV from 1 mA to 15 mA (over temperature, excluding die heating). So a reference run at a few hundred microamps is more accurate and cooler than one run at several milliamps.

**Accuracy.** The voltage is trimmed in the package to ±0.1% at 25 °C, and the whole −40 to 125 °C range stays within ±0.5%. That is what the temperature coefficient (37 to 47 ppm/°C at most) allows over 165 °C, with the initial error. Long-term drift is 70 to 110 ppm over 1000 hours and thermal hysteresis 0.4 to 0.8 mV, both typical.

**No capacitor needed.** Unlike many shunt references, it is stable with no output capacitor, and with one. A capacitor lowers the output impedance at high frequencies; the datasheet's plots use 1 µF and 10 µF.

**Where it fits.** A shunt reference is the simple, cheap and compact way to get a precise voltage: a SOT-23 and a resistor, from any supply, drawing a few hundred microamps. Its weaknesses against a series reference such as Silergy's [SQ82535](site:parts/silergy/sq82535/) are accuracy (±0.1% against ±0.025%, and up to 40 ppm/°C against 3) and efficiency at high or varying load, since the resistor must carry the full load current plus the reference's minimum all the time.

### The series resistor (R2)

The datasheet gives the two limits:

- **RS maximum = (VS minimum − VR maximum) ÷ (IL maximum + IQ minimum)**, so the reference still gets enough current at the lowest supply and the highest load.
- **RS minimum = (VS maximum − VR minimum) ÷ (IL minimum + IQ maximum)**, so it gets no more than 15 mA at the highest supply and the lowest load.

Pick a value between them, then check the resistor's power, (VS − VR) × (IL + IQ), and its tolerance and drift, which move the current. The table *Series resistor for the 2.5 V version* above works these out for common supplies (calculated).

The datasheet's own example suggests designing for 3 to 10 mA of reference current, and its board's 510 Ω from 5 V gives 4.9 mA (calculated): 12.3 mW in the resistor, 12.3 mW in the reference and a 3.8 °C die rise. That works, but the current-change and temperature limits favour less: from 1 to 15 mA the voltage can move by up to 9 mV (2.5 V version, over temperature, excluding die heating), against 1.2 mV from the minimum to 1 mA. Unless the load needs it, a few hundred microamps above the minimum is the better design point (our reading).

### Capacitors (C1, C3)

No output capacitor is needed for stability, and any value is tolerated; the board fits 10 µF. The datasheet's layout notes recommend 0.1 µF or more of ceramic on the supply side of RS, because supply noise passes through to the output, and the board fits 1 µF there. With RS and the output capacitor, the reference's output also forms an RC filter against supply noise (our note).

### NC pin (R1)

The pin description says NC must be left open or connected to ANODE, and its absolute maximum is ±0.3 V. The board's unfitted R1 from NC to ground is consistent with that, as an option to tie it to ground, which is the anode here (our reading). Leave it open.

### Worked example: the reference for an SA89853 ADC

The same job as on the [SQ82535 page](site:parts/silergy/sq82535/): the [SA89853](site:parts/silergy/sa89853/)'s REFP, about 31 kΩ, 81 µA at 2.5 V, with its own 10 µF across REFP and REFM, from a 3.3 V ±5% rail. Here the SQ82550DAOT (2.5 V) does it with a resistor. Everything below is calculated.

| Quantity | Value | How |
| --- | --- | --- |
| RS maximum | 1.88 kΩ | (3.135 − 2.5125) V ÷ (81 + 250) µA |
| RS chosen | 1.8 kΩ | Standard value |
| Reference current | 0.265 to 0.462 mA | Over the supply range, 81 µA load |
| Current from the 3.3 V rail | 0.35 to 0.54 mA | Reference plus load |
| Dissipation | 1.2 mW in the reference (0.4 °C rise), 0.5 mW in RS | At 3.465 V |
| Error at 25 °C | ±4.1 LSB | Initial, ±2.5 mV |
| Error over −40 to 125 °C | ±20.5 LSB | Tolerance, ±12.5 mV, initial included |
| Change with current | −0.4 to +1.2 mV, up to 2 LSB | IRMIN to 1 mA limit |
| Hysteresis and long-term drift | About 0.66 and 0.45 LSB | Typical, 0.4 mV and 110 ppm |
| Noise, 10 Hz to 10 kHz | 38 µVrms, 0.06 LSB | Typical |

**Against the SQ82535C.** The series reference keeps the same ADC within about ±2 LSB over temperature; the shunt allows about ±20 LSB, ten times more. At 25 °C, after a one-point calibration, the gap narrows to the drift: up to 40 ppm/°C against 3. In return, the shunt costs a SOT-23 and a resistor, works from any rail above 2.5 V (where the SQ82535C needs 3 V minimum), and draws less from the rail, about 0.5 mA against up to 1.2 mA. For a 12-bit converter that is calibrated or used near room temperature, the shunt is enough; for accuracy over temperature, or above 12 bits, the series reference earns its cost (our reading).

## What the datasheet shows

For each version at 25 °C: output impedance against frequency at 250 µA and 1 mA with 1 µF and 10 µF; the response to an input voltage step with 1 µF and 10 µF; and, for all four together, reverse current against voltage (the knee near each voltage) and noise density against frequency. There is also a thermal hysteresis distribution, in mV/V. We haven't read values off the plots.

## Things to watch

- **Mind the minimum current over temperature.** It is up to 115–128 µA, depending on version, against 88–100 µA at 25 °C. Design RS for the worst case: lowest supply, highest load, highest VR.
- **Less current is more accurate.** The voltage moves up to 1.2 mV from the minimum to 1 mA but up to 9–12 mV from 1 to 15 mA (over temperature), before die heating. The datasheet's 3–10 mA example is far more than an ADC reference needs.
- **The current-change limits exclude die heating.** At 15 mA, the 2.5 V version dissipates 37.5 mW and its die rises about 12 °C on the test board (calculated), which moves the voltage through the temperature coefficient as well.
- **The headline figures are for one version.** The feature list's 38 µVrms noise is the 2.5 V version's (the 5 V version is 80 µVrms), and its ±40 ppm/°C is close to, but not the same as, the per-version maximums of 37 to 47 ppm/°C.
- **Leave NC open** or tie it to ANODE; it is rated to only ±0.3 V.
- **Every limit is guaranteed by design, test or statistical correlation**, with production tests at 25 °C only (Note 4).
- **Small errors in the datasheet.** The thermal table prints "Max" in the θJA value column, above the 309.5 °C/W figure. The application text refers to "Figure 4" for the typical application, which is Figure 1. The Application Schematic draws C3 polarized, though the BOM lists a ceramic 0805.
