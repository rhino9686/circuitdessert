## How it works

The SY50307 is the primary-side half of a secondary-regulated flyback converter, with its 700 V GaN switch built in. The output voltage is regulated on the secondary side: a shunt regulator (TLV431) drives the LED of an optocoupler, whose transistor pulls on the COMP pin. In a USB PD charger the protocol IC changes the divider on that TLV431, which moves the output between 3.3 V and 11 V. The controller turns the COMP voltage into a peak-current command and ends each on-time when the voltage on SOURCE, across the sense resistor, reaches it, up to a limit of 0.5 V.

The control method changes with the operating point. At low line and heavy load the transformer runs in continuous conduction at a fixed 90 kHz; the datasheet calls it quasi-fixed frequency control, and it removes the need for slope compensation. At other conditions the part switches to quasi-resonant operation, turning the GaN on at a valley of the drain voltage, with the frequency limited to 90 kHz. At high line it is forced into QR, because CCM at high line costs efficiency and, with a synchronous rectifier on the secondary, creates higher voltage transients. At light load the frequency falls toward a 28 kHz floor so the converter stays out of the audible range, and below that the part goes to sleep in burst mode at 70 µA. The lighter the load, the longer it sleeps.

One pin, VSEN, does several jobs. It connects through a resistor divider to the auxiliary winding. While the GaN is on, the winding voltage is negative and proportional to the bus voltage, so the current flowing out of VSEN measures the input: the part compares it with a brown-out current (100 µA), a brown-in current (111 µA) and a high-line current (300 µA). While the GaN is off, the winding voltage follows the output voltage, so VSEN also sets the output over-voltage and under-voltage points, 2.0 V and 0.3 V on the pin. The same winding gives the valley timing for QR. The pin is blanked for 1.45 µs after turn-off so the leakage spike doesn't trigger a false over-voltage.

VCC takes 10 to 85 V, which covers the auxiliary winding's voltage across a wide output range, so no extra regulator is needed. The part starts from a high-voltage current source on the DRAIN pin (0.55 mA), starts switching at 18 V on VCC, and its first pulse checks the input voltage against the brown-in level. If VSEN current is below that, it stops and retries after the auto-recovery timer of 2 s. In normal operation the part also checks VSEN during start-up: it only allows forced QR when VSEN is above 400 mV and the high-line condition is detected, because at the start of a start-up the output is near zero and valley detection doesn't work.

Faults all stop switching and restart after the 2 s timer, or when VCC recovers: brown-out for 64 ms, overload (COMP high for 64 ms), output OVP or UVP, VCC over-voltage (above 94 V), over-temperature at 150 °C (resuming 24 °C lower), a short on SOURCE found during start-up, and a short in the secondary synchronous rectifier, found when the SOURCE voltage exceeds 650 mV for four cycles in a row.

### Bus capacitor

The datasheet's rule is 1 to 2 µF per watt of input power; at 88% efficiency the 25 W output needs about 28.4 W in, which gives 28 to 57 µF. A more exact calculation from a chosen ripple, here 54.8 V at the lowest line, gives 36 µF, and the circuit uses two 18 µF capacitors (C1 and C2) on each side of the L2 filter. The minimum bus voltage is √2 × 90 V − 54.8 V = 72.5 V.

### Turns ratio, duty cycle and inductance

The turns ratio is limited by the stress on the GaN: NPS ≤ (700 V × 0.9 − √2 × 264 V − 80 V) ÷ (VOUT,MAX + VDF) = 16.06 with VOUT,MAX = 11 V and VDF = 0 V. The datasheet picks 11. The maximum duty cycle at the minimum bus voltage, evaluated at the rated 25 W and the 3 A that goes with 8.33 V, is DMAX = 11 × 8.33 ÷ (72.5 + 11 × 8.33) = 55.84%. The primary inductance for a ripple factor KRP of 0.42 is LM = VBUS,MIN² × DMAX² × η ÷ (2 × POUT × fSW × KRP) = 763 µH, set to 760 µH. The datasheet suggests a KRP of 0.3 to 0.5 for full load at the lowest line.

### Primary and secondary current

At the minimum bus voltage and rated power the primary peak current is 0.998 A and the RMS current is 0.54 A. The secondary peak current is NPS × IP,PK = 10.98 A and the RMS current is 4.68 A. At the OCP point (output current up 30%, at the lowest AC input and the highest bus) the primary peak is 1.083 A.

### Current sense resistor

RSOURCE = VSOURCE,MAX ÷ IP,PK,OCP = 0.5 V ÷ 1.083 A = 0.462 Ω. The datasheet then says "RSOURCE is set to 0.392 Ω", which is the 0.75 Ω and 0.82 Ω pair in parallel in the circuit. That is lower than the calculated value, which raises the current limit; see Things to watch. It also says to adjust the resistor to the converter's measured OCP point.

### Transformer turns

With the 57.2 mm² core and a flux swing ΔB of 0.3 T, the datasheet gets NP = 44.2 and chooses 44. Then NS = NP ÷ NPS = 4. The auxiliary turns are set from the lowest VCC wanted, NA = NS × 10 V ÷ 3.3 V = 12.1, set to 12. It says to check VCC at the lowest output voltage and no load and to adjust NA from the measured result.

The datasheet suggests a current density of 4 to 10 A/mm² for the wire. It uses Φ0.3 mm for the primary and Φ0.7 mm for the secondary.

### Voltage sense divider

RH sets the brown-out point: RH = √2 × VIN,BO ÷ IBO × NA ÷ NP = 239 kΩ for 62 Vac, set to 240 kΩ. RL then sets the output OVP: RL = RH ÷ ((NA ÷ NS) × VOUT,OVP ÷ VVSEN,OVP − 1) = 12.77 kΩ for 13.2 V, set to 12.7 kΩ. The datasheet says the OVP point should come from the highest output voltage, with 120% as the example (20 V × 1.2 = 24 V). The thresholds that these resistors give are in the *Thresholds* table above.

### Output divider and feedback

The TLV431's reference is 1.24 V. For a 3.3 V sleep-mode output, the datasheet takes RFBU = 100 kΩ, from the protocol IC's requirement, and calculates RFBD = 100 kΩ × 1.24 ÷ (3.3 − 1.24) = 60.2 kΩ; the circuit uses 59 kΩ. A 59 kΩ lower resistor gives 3.35 V.

### Secondary rectifier

The maximum reverse voltage on the secondary MOSFET is √2 × 264 V ÷ NPS + VOUT,MAX + spike = 51.9 V, so the datasheet recommends a 100 V part. The peak current is 10.98 A. The circuit uses Silergy's SY52341FAP synchronous rectifier controller, which has the MOSFET integrated.

## Layout

The datasheet gives these layout rules, plus a suggested layout drawing (Figure 21).

- Connect the **bridge rectifier output to the bus capacitor first**, then to the switching circuit, for EMI and to lower the line-frequency ripple.
- Keep the **switching loops small**: the primary power loop, the secondary loop and the auxiliary loop.
- Connect the **bias supply trace to the bias capacitor first**, not to the GND pin, and put that capacitor as close as possible to the device.
- Keep the loop from the **SOURCE pin through the sense resistor to the GND pin** as small as possible.
- Place the **VSEN resistor divider** next to the IC.
- Connect the **NC pin to the SOURCE pin** for better thermal performance.
- Follow the datasheet's **primary ground scheme**, which numbers eight ground points: the bus capacitor (①), the IC (②), the auxiliary winding (③), the VSEN divider (④), the Y capacitor (⑤), the sense resistor (⑥), the VCC capacitor (⑦) and the optocoupler receiver (⑧).
- Place the **VCC ceramic capacitor** as close as possible to the VCC and GND pins.

## What the datasheet shows

The typical-performance pages use a 25 W PD output (5 V / 3 A, 9 V / 2.77 A, 3.3–5.9 V / 3 A, 3.3–11 V / 2.25 A) with a 1.2 m PD cable at 25 °C. They show steady-state captures of SOURCE and drain voltage at 90 Vac and 264 Vac with an 11 V, 2.25 A load and with no load, and two plots of average efficiency against input voltage (90, 115, 230 and 264 Vac). The plots label two curves, "Average Board" and "Average (Cable)", with limit lines for COC Tier 2 and Level 6, but they don't say which output each plot is for.

Reading the plots by eye, to about one point: in the first, board efficiency is about 90% across the range, and the cable-end figure is about 88–89%. In the second, board efficiency is about 89.5% at 90 and 115 Vac, falling to about 87.5% at 264 Vac, and the cable-end figure goes from about 85.5% to about 84%. Both curves are above the limit lines in the plot.

## Things to watch

- **The 0.392 Ω sense resistor doesn't match the calculation.** The datasheet's calculation gives 0.462 Ω for a 1.083 A limit, but the circuit uses 0.392 Ω, which sets about 1.28 A, 18% higher. That raises the OCP point to about 153% of the rated current instead of 130%. The datasheet tells you to tune the resistor on a real converter anyway, but don't take 0.392 Ω as the calculated value.
- **The transformer turns use the wrong current.** The text gives NP = LM × IP,PK,OCP ÷ (ΔB × AE) with 1.083 A, but the printed answer of 44.2 turns matches 0.998 A. With 1.083 A the result is 48 turns. With 44 turns the flux is 0.33 T at the design OCP point and about 0.39 T at the 1.28 A limit, above the datasheet's own 0.2–0.3 T. The core material isn't named, so check saturation at hot temperature.
- **The auxiliary winding gives under 10 V at 3.3 V out.** NA = 12 and NS = 4 give VCC = 3 × VOUT, which is 9.9 V at 3.3 V before the D1 and R2 drops. The text asks for a minimum VCC above 10 V in one place and above 8 V in another, and the recommended operating range starts at 10 V. It says to adjust NA after testing at the minimum output and no load.
- **The wire current densities don't match the text.** The text sets 5 A/mm² for the primary wire, but the arithmetic uses 8 A/mm² (0.54 A ÷ 0.0675 mm²). The secondary's Φ0.7 mm carries about 12 A/mm², above the stated 4–10 A/mm² range.
- **The VCC over-voltage trip is close to the recommended maximum.** The minimum trip is 86 V and the recommended maximum VCC is 85 V.
- **The parts list and the schematic disagree on the synchronous rectifier.** The schematic and description say SY52341FAP; the part number column says SY52441FAP. The secondary MOSFET's 100 V rating in the text is also not QS1, the 30 V load switch.
- **The efficiency plots don't say which output they are for.** Both are titled "Average Efficiency vs. Input Voltage", and their vertical scales differ.
- **VSEN must stay blanked at the right time.** The RCD clamp has to be tuned so that the drain spike doesn't last longer than the 1.45 µs blanking time, and at no load the secondary diode conduction must be longer than the maximum blanking time.
