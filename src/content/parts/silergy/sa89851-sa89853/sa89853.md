## How it works

The SA89853 is a successive-approximation (SAR) ADC behind a 16-channel multiplexer. A SAR converter samples its input onto a capacitor array and then finds the 12-bit result one bit at a time, so each conversion is a single sample, taken at a known moment, with no averaging and no latency beyond the frame. The SA89851 is the same part with 8 channels.

**One frame, one sample.** Everything happens in 16-bit SPI frames. The falling edge of CS_N samples the channel that the multiplexer is on and starts the conversion, which the 16 SCLK cycles clock along. During those cycles the part shifts out the result (a 4-bit channel address followed by the 12-bit code, MSB first) and shifts in a 16-bit command. On the second SCLK falling edge the multiplexer moves to the next channel, which then has from the 14th rising edge to the next CS_N falling edge to settle (at least 325 ns). So the sample rate is set by how often CS_N falls, up to 1 MSPS with a 20 MHz clock.

**A two-frame pipeline.** In manual mode, a channel chosen in frame N is connected in frame N+1 and sampled at the start of frame N+2, whose output carries its result (Figure 7). The channel address in DO15–DO12 says which channel each result belongs to, so code can check it. The first frame after power-up or a reset is invalid.

**Three ways to pick channels.**

- *Manual mode* (the default): each frame names the next channel.
- *Auto-1 mode*: the part steps through a list of channels programmed once with a 16-bit mask, one per frame, and wraps around.
- *Auto-2 mode*: the part steps from channel 0 to a programmed last channel and wraps around.

Once a mode is running, frames of all zeros keep it going (the "continue" command), so a controller can hold SDI low and just read. The table *Frame commands* above lists the command codes.

**Inputs and ranges.** Every channel is single-ended. The converter measures AINP against AINM, AINM goes to ground, and the multiplexer output MXO comes out on a pin: tie it straight to AINP, as the datasheet's schematics do, or put a buffer amplifier between them to drive the sampling capacitor from a high-impedance source. The range is 0 to VREF (range 1) or 0 to 2 × VREF (range 2), but range 2 needs 2 × VREF ≤ VA: with a 2.5 V reference, VA must be at least 5 V. Codes are straight binary, 0 at 0 V and 4095 at full scale less 1 LSB.

**External reference.** The part has no reference of its own. REFP takes 2 to 3 V (2.5 V typical) against REFM, with 10 µF between them, and presents about 31 kΩ, so a 2.5 V reference supplies about 81 µA. The datasheet suggests a low-noise band-gap reference such as Silergy's SA89550.

**Alarms and GPIOs.** Each channel has a high and a low threshold (10 bits, compared with the top 10 bits of the result). GPIO0 can signal a high alarm, a high-or-low alarm, and GPIO1 a low alarm, as active-high outputs that set on the 12th SCLK of the frame that crosses and clear on the 10th SCLK of the next. GPIO2 can instead select the range from a pin, and GPIO3 can act as an active-low power-down input. Any GPIO not given one of those jobs is a general-purpose input or output, read through DO15–DO12 when DI04 is set and written through DI03–DI00.

**Power-down.** Setting DI05 in a mode frame powers the part down at the end of the next frame, and GPIO3, configured as PD_N, powers it down at once when driven low. It draws 0.7 µA powered down, against 1 mA static and 2.1 mA at 1 MSPS (VA 2.7–3.6 V). The power-up time is 200 µs.

### Input filters (R2, C3)

The 33 Ω and 270 pF on each input are a charge reservoir more than a filter: their corner is about 18 MHz (calculated). When the part samples a channel, its 16 pF input capacitance, still charged to the previous channel's voltage, shares charge with C3. C3 is 17 times larger, so the step is at most about 6% of the difference, and it recovers through the 33 Ω within about 60 ns, well inside the 325 ns acquisition time (our calculation, from Figure 6's capacitances). The datasheet's layout notes give the same reason: the capacitors are there to charge the internal capacitor during sampling.

What the reservoir can't fix is the source behind it. Between two samples of the same channel, the source must put the borrowed charge back through its own resistance. Scanning 16 channels at 1 MSPS, each channel is sampled every 16 µs, which a 10 kΩ source manages but a 33 kΩ one doesn't. The table *Error from source resistance, worst case* above gives figures from a simplified model (calculated). For high-impedance sources, such as high-voltage dividers, scan more slowly or put a buffer between MXO and AINP.

### Supplies and reference (C1, C2, R1)

10 µF and 0.1 µF at every VA and VBD pin, as close as possible; 10 µF across REFP and REFM (at least 10 µF, per the layout guide). VBD must never be above VA, which matters at power-up when the two come from different supplies. The SNR is 69 dB with VBD at 3.3 V or less but 64 dB at 5 V or more, so a 3.3 V VBD is the better choice even with VA at 5 V.

The 47 kΩ pull-ups on GPIO2 and GPIO3 keep those inputs defined. If GPIO2 is configured as the range input, the pull-up selects range 2, which needs VA ≥ 2 × VREF; if GPIO3 is configured as PD_N, the pull-up keeps the part running (our reading).

### Worked example: an 800 V battery pack through a divider

The datasheet's application measures a high-voltage pack through a divider (R2 and R3 in its Figure 14), with no values. Ours: a full scale of 1000 V on range 1 with a 2.5 V reference, so a ratio of 400. Everything here is calculated, nothing was measured.

| Quantity | Value | How |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Divider | 3.99 MΩ over 10 kΩ, ratio 400 | 1000 V → 2.5 V; the top resistor made of several in series for the voltage |
| ADC input at 800 V | 2.00 V | 800 ÷ 400 |
| Divider current and top-string dissipation at 800 V | 200 µA, 0.16 W | 800 V ÷ 4 MΩ |
| Source resistance seen by the input | 9.98 kΩ | 10 kΩ ∥ 3.99 MΩ |
| LSB at the pack | 0.244 V | 2.5 V ÷ 4096 × 400 |
| Offset error at the pack | ±1.2 V | ±5 LSB maximum (range 1) |
| Gain error at the pack | −1.0 to +0.6 V | −4 to +2.5 LSB (range 1) |
| A 0.1% reference | ±0.8 V at 800 V | Proportional to the reading |
| Divider ratio, two 0.1% resistors | up to ±1.6 V at 800 V | 0.2% worst case |
| Charge-sharing error, full scan at 1 MSPS | about 0.6 LSB (0.15 V) | Table above, 10 kΩ |

**The divider and the reference set the accuracy.** The ADC's own offset and gain limits add about ±1.2 V and ±1 V, while 0.1% parts in the divider and reference add up to ±2.4 V at 800 V. A calibration at one or two known voltages removes most of all of these. With a 10 kΩ bottom resistor the input stays within the charge-sharing limit even at full scan rate; a higher bottom resistor would save divider current but would need a slower scan or a buffer.

**Isolation and redundancy.** The datasheet draws digital isolation between the ADC and the controller, and marks the measurement "x2 for functional safety", meaning two independent channels. Neither is part of the ADC.

## Talk to it from an ESP32-S3

This section wires an ESP32-S3 dev board to the SA89853 over SPI. The sketch reads any channel in manual mode, scans every channel in auto-2 mode, switches the range, resets the part, and can send any raw frame. It is built from the [Arduino interface template](site:contribute/arduino/), with block 2 rewritten for the part's 16-bit frames. It works for the SA89851 too, with `NUM_CH` set to 8.

> **Status:** Compile-checked for the ESP32-S3 (arduino-esp32 3.3.12, board ESP32S3 Dev Module), with no warnings (`-Wall -Wextra`). | Ground | GND | 6, 10, 19, 20, 30 (AGND), 35 (BDGND), 3 (REFM), 9 (AINM) | One ground |
| SCLK | GPIO12 (SCK) | 32 | |
| SDI | GPIO11 (MOSI) | 33 | |
| SDO | GPIO13 (MISO) | 34 | |
| CS_N | GPIO10 | 31 | |
| Reference | 2.5 V source | 4 (REFP) | 10 µF from REFP to REFM |
| MXO to AINP | | 7 to 8 | A short link |
| Inputs | | 28 (CH0), 27 (CH1) … | 33 Ω in series and 270 pF to ground at each used input. Keep them between 0 V and 2.5 V |

- **VA and VBD both at 3.3 V** keeps the wiring simple, and VBD is never above VA. The cost is range 2, which needs VA of at least 5 V with a 2.5 V reference. To use it, feed VA from a clean 5 V and keep VBD at 3.3 V.
- **The reference** can be a 2.5 V reference IC powered from 3.3 V, such as the SA89550 the datasheet suggests, or a precision shunt reference with a resistor from 3.3 V (our suggestion). It must supply about 81 µA into REFP. Without a reference, the part has nothing to convert against.
- **GPIO0 to GPIO3** start as inputs, so they can stay unconnected for a first test, or take the datasheet's 47 kΩ pull-ups to VBD.
- **Unused channels** can be tied to ground, so an auto-2 scan reads them as 0.

### The sketch

Paste it into a new sketch, choose the board *ESP32S3 Dev Module* (or your own ESP32-S3 board), and open the Serial Monitor at 115200 baud with line ending *Newline*. The first block is the only one to edit: set `NUM_CH` to 8 for the SA89851, and `VREF_MV` to your reference.

```cpp
/*
  SA89853 (or SA89851) console for an ESP32-S3, over SPI.
  Wiring (ESP32-S3): GPIO12 = SCLK, GPIO11 = SDI, GPIO13 = SDO,
  GPIO10 = CS_N. VA and VBD at 3.3 V; a 2.5 V reference on REFP.
  Register 0 reads the next frame's result, register 1 sends a raw
  16-bit frame. Extra commands: read <ch>, all, range <1|2>, swreset.
*/
#include <SPI.h>

// ===== 1. DEVICE CONFIG: edit this block ==================================
// From the SA89851/3 datasheet (Rev 1.0): every frame is 16 SCLK cycles.
// The controller sends a 16-bit command on SDI while the part returns a
// 4-bit channel address and the 12-bit result of the previous sample.

// ESP32-S3 pins: the board package's default SPI pins. Any free GPIO works.
const int8_t   SCK_PIN       = 12;
const int8_t   MISO_PIN      = 13;         // SDO
const int8_t   MOSI_PIN      = 11;         // SDI
const int8_t   CS_PIN        = 10;         // CS_N (active low)

const uint32_t BUS_HZ        = 2000000;    // up to 20 MHz in the datasheet
const uint8_t  SPI_DATA_MODE = SPI_MODE0;  // SCLK idles low; SDI is clocked in
                                           // on the rising edge, SDO changes
                                           // on the falling edge
const uint8_t  WORD_BYTES    = 2;          // every frame is 16 bits
const uint16_t REG_MAX       = 1;          // 0 = next result, 1 = raw frame

// The SA89851/3 has no reset pin (swreset resets it through SPI).
const int8_t   RESET_PIN     = -1;
const uint16_t BOOT_MS       = 1;          // power-up time is 200 us

struct NamedReg { const char *name; uint8_t reg; };
const NamedReg REGS[] = {
  {"RESULT", 0},
};

const uint8_t  NUM_CH  = 16;     // 16 for the SA89853, 8 for the SA89851
const float    VREF_MV = 2500.0f; // the voltage on REFP, in mV (2000 to 3000)
uint8_t rangeSel = 1;            // 1 = 0 to VREF, 2 = 0 to 2 x VREF
                                 // (range 2 needs 2 x VREF <= VA)

// Commands (datasheet Tables 1, 5, 6, 9).
const uint16_t CMD_CONTINUE = 0x0000;  // keep the current mode and settings
const uint16_t CMD_MANUAL   = 0x1000;  // manual mode
const uint16_t CMD_AUTO2    = 0x3000;  // auto-2 mode
const uint16_t CMD_GPIO_PRG = 0x4000;  // GPIO program register
const uint16_t CMD_AUTO2_PRG = 0x9000; // auto-2 program register (last channel)
const uint16_t BIT_PROGRAM  = 0x0800;  // DI11: take bits DI10 to DI00 from this frame
const uint16_t BIT_RESET_CH = 0x0400;  // DI10 in auto modes: restart at the first channel
const uint16_t BIT_RANGE2   = 0x0040;  // DI06: 0 to 2 x VREF
const uint16_t BIT_SWRESET  = 0x0200;  // DI09 in the GPIO program register

bool parseNum(const char *s, uint32_t &out);
void printHex(uint32_t v, uint8_t digits);

// ===== 2. BUS LAYER: SPI, SA89851/3 16-bit frames =========================
// There are no register addresses. CS_N falling samples the input; the
// 16 SCLK cycles that follow shift the command in and the result out. The
// channel chosen in one frame is sampled at the start of the frame after
// next, so a single reading takes three frames.

const __FlashStringHelper *lastError = F("SPI has no error codes: check wiring and mode");
uint16_t lastSdo = 0;   // SDO word of the most recent frame

bool busBegin() {
  pinMode(CS_PIN, OUTPUT);
  digitalWrite(CS_PIN, HIGH);
  SPI.begin(SCK_PIN, MISO_PIN, MOSI_PIN);   // chip select is driven by hand
  return true;
}

bool busReady() { return true; }
const __FlashStringHelper *busError() { return lastError; }

static uint16_t frame16(uint16_t cmd) {
  SPI.beginTransaction(SPISettings(BUS_HZ, MSBFIRST, SPI_DATA_MODE));
  digitalWrite(CS_PIN, LOW);
  uint16_t hi = SPI.transfer((uint8_t)(cmd >> 8));
  uint16_t lo = SPI.transfer((uint8_t)(cmd & 0xFF));
  digitalWrite(CS_PIN, HIGH);
  SPI.endTransaction();
  lastSdo = (uint16_t)((hi << 8) | lo);
  return lastSdo;
}

bool devRead(uint16_t reg, uint32_t &value) {
  value = (reg == 0) ? frame16(CMD_CONTINUE) : lastSdo;
  return true;
}

bool devWrite(uint16_t reg, uint32_t value) {
  if (reg != 1) {
    lastError = F("Write to register 1 to send a raw frame.");
    return false;
  }
  frame16((uint16_t)value);
  Serial.print(F("SDO "));
  printHex(lastSdo, 4);
  Serial.println();
  return true;
}

static uint16_t rangeBits() { return rangeSel == 2 ? BIT_RANGE2 : 0; }

static void printResult(uint16_t sdo) {
  uint16_t code = sdo & 0x0FFF;
  float fs = (rangeSel == 2 ? 2.0f : 1.0f) * VREF_MV;
  Serial.print(F("CH")); Serial.print(sdo >> 12);
  Serial.print(F("  code ")); Serial.print(code);
  Serial.print(F("  ")); Serial.print(code * fs / 4096.0f, 1); Serial.println(F(" mV"));
}

// Read one channel in manual mode: select it, let the multiplexer switch,
// then read the result, which carries the channel address in its top bits.
static bool readChannel(uint8_t ch, uint16_t &sdo) {
  frame16((uint16_t)(CMD_MANUAL | BIT_PROGRAM | (uint16_t)(ch & 0x0F) << 7 | rangeBits()));
  frame16((uint16_t)(CMD_MANUAL | BIT_PROGRAM | (uint16_t)(ch & 0x0F) << 7 | rangeBits()));
  sdo = frame16((uint16_t)(CMD_MANUAL | BIT_PROGRAM | (uint16_t)(ch & 0x0F) << 7 | rangeBits()));
  if ((sdo >> 12) != ch) {
    lastError = F("Channel address in the result doesn't match: check SDO and the mode.");
    return false;
  }
  return true;
}

// Scan every channel once in auto-2 mode and print each result.
static void readAll() {
  frame16((uint16_t)(CMD_AUTO2_PRG | (uint16_t)((NUM_CH - 1) & 0x0F) << 6));
  frame16((uint16_t)(CMD_AUTO2 | BIT_PROGRAM | BIT_RESET_CH | rangeBits()));
  bool seen[16] = {false};
  uint8_t count = 0;
  for (uint8_t i = 0; i < NUM_CH + 2 && count < NUM_CH; i++) {
    uint16_t sdo = frame16(CMD_CONTINUE);
    uint8_t ch = (uint8_t)(sdo >> 12);
    if (i == 0 || ch >= NUM_CH || seen[ch]) continue;  // the first is from before the scan
    seen[ch] = true;
    count++;
    printResult(sdo);
  }
  if (count < NUM_CH) Serial.println(F("Not every channel answered: check SDO and NUM_CH."));
}

bool busCommand(const char *cmd, const char *a1) {
  uint32_t v;
  if (!strcmp(cmd, "help") || !strcmp(cmd, "?")) {
    Serial.println(F("read <ch>          read one channel (manual mode)"));
    Serial.println(F("all                read every channel once (auto-2 mode)"));
    Serial.println(F("range <1|2>        0 to VREF, or 0 to 2 x VREF"));
    Serial.println(F("swreset            reset the part through SPI"));
    Serial.println(F("Register 0 reads the next result, w 1 <word> sends a raw frame."));
    return false;                       // the console prints the rest
  }
  if (!strcmp(cmd, "read")) {
    uint16_t sdo;
    if (!a1 || !parseNum(a1, v) || v >= NUM_CH) {
      Serial.print(F("Give a channel from 0 to ")); Serial.println(NUM_CH - 1);
      return true;
    }
    if (readChannel((uint8_t)v, sdo)) printResult(sdo);
    else { Serial.println(busError()); printResult(sdo); }
    return true;
  }
  if (!strcmp(cmd, "all")) { readAll(); return true; }
  if (!strcmp(cmd, "range")) {
    if (!a1 || !parseNum(a1, v) || v < 1 || v > 2) { Serial.println(F("Give 1 or 2")); return true; }
    rangeSel = (uint8_t)v;
    Serial.print(F("Range ")); Serial.print(rangeSel);
    Serial.print(F(": 0 to ")); Serial.print((rangeSel == 2 ? 2.0f : 1.0f) * VREF_MV, 0);
    Serial.println(F(" mV"));
    return true;
  }
  if (!strcmp(cmd, "swreset")) {
    frame16((uint16_t)(CMD_GPIO_PRG | BIT_SWRESET));
    delayMicroseconds(1);
    frame16(CMD_CONTINUE);              // the first frame after a reset is invalid
    rangeSel = 1;
    Serial.println(F("Reset: manual mode, channel 0, range 1."));
    return true;
  }
  return false;
}

// ===== 3. CONSOLE (the same for every bus) ================================
// Serial Monitor at 115200 baud, line ending "Newline". Commands:
//   help | scan | addr 0x2A | r <reg> | w <reg> <value> | d <first> <count>
//   | t | reset
// Numbers can be decimal or 0x hex.

bool parseNum(const char *s, uint32_t &out) {
  char *end;
  out = strtoul(s, &end, 0);
  return end != s && *end == 0;
}

void printHex(uint32_t v, uint8_t digits) {
  for (int8_t i = digits - 1; i >= 0; i--) {
    Serial.print((uint8_t)((v >> (4 * i)) & 0xF), HEX);
  }
}

void printWord(uint32_t v) {
  const uint8_t bits = WORD_BYTES * 8;
  int32_t s = (int32_t)v;
  if (bits < 32 && (v & (1UL << (bits - 1)))) {
    s = (int32_t)(v | (0xFFFFFFFFUL << bits));      // sign-extend
  }
  Serial.print(F("0x")); printHex(v, WORD_BYTES * 2);
  Serial.print(F("  unsigned ")); Serial.print(v);
  Serial.print(F("  signed ")); Serial.println(s);
}

void deviceReset() {
  if (RESET_PIN < 0) {
    Serial.println(F("RESET_PIN is not set."));
    return;
  }
  // Only ever pulled LOW. The 3.3 V pull-up on the part makes it high.
  pinMode(RESET_PIN, OUTPUT);
  digitalWrite(RESET_PIN, LOW);
  delay(2);
  pinMode(RESET_PIN, INPUT);
  delay(BOOT_MS);
  Serial.println(F("Reset done."));
}

void showRead(uint16_t reg) {
  uint32_t v;
  if (devRead(reg, v)) printWord(v);
  else Serial.println(busError());
}

void help() {
  Serial.println(F("r <reg>            read one register"));
  Serial.println(F("w <reg> <value>    write one register (this can change"));
  Serial.println(F("                   the device's setup: be careful)"));
  Serial.println(F("d <first> <count>  read a run of registers"));
  Serial.println(F("t                  read the named registers in REGS[]"));
  Serial.println(F("scan / addr <a>    I2C only: find the device / set address"));
  Serial.println(F("reset              pulse the reset pin, if RESET_PIN is set"));
}

void handle(char *ln) {
  char *cmd = strtok(ln, " \t");
  char *a1 = strtok(NULL, " \t");
  char *a2 = strtok(NULL, " \t");
  uint32_t reg, val;
  if (!cmd) return;
  if (busCommand(cmd, a1)) return;
  if (!strcmp(cmd, "help") || !strcmp(cmd, "?")) { help(); return; }
  if (!strcmp(cmd, "reset")) { deviceReset(); return; }
  if (!busReady()) {
    Serial.println(F("No device address yet: type 'scan' or 'addr 0x..'."));
    return;
  }
  if (a1 && parseNum(a1, reg) && reg > REG_MAX) {
    Serial.println(F("Register number is above REG_MAX."));
    return;
  }
  if (!strcmp(cmd, "r") && a1 && parseNum(a1, reg)) {
    showRead(reg);
  } else if (!strcmp(cmd, "w") && a1 && a2 &&
             parseNum(a1, reg) && parseNum(a2, val)) {
    if (devWrite(reg, val)) Serial.println(F("written"));
    else Serial.println(busError());
  } else if (!strcmp(cmd, "d") && a1 && a2 &&
             parseNum(a1, reg) && parseNum(a2, val)) {
    for (uint32_t i = 0; i < val && reg + i <= REG_MAX; i++) {
      Serial.print(F("[0x")); printHex(reg + i, 2); Serial.print(F("]  "));
      showRead(reg + i);
    }
  } else if (!strcmp(cmd, "t")) {
    for (uint8_t i = 0; i < sizeof(REGS) / sizeof(REGS[0]); i++) {
      Serial.print(REGS[i].name);
      Serial.print(F("  [0x")); printHex(REGS[i].reg, 2); Serial.print(F("]  "));
      showRead(REGS[i].reg);
    }
  } else {
    Serial.println(F("?  (type help)"));
  }
}

char lineBuf[40];
uint8_t lineLen = 0;

void setup() {
  Serial.begin(115200);
  // On the native USB port, text sent before the Serial Monitor connects is
  // lost, so wait up to 3 s for it. A UART port is ready at once.
  for (uint32_t t0 = millis(); !Serial && millis() - t0 < 3000; ) delay(10);
  // High-impedance until a reset is asked for.
  if (RESET_PIN >= 0) pinMode(RESET_PIN, INPUT);
  delay(BOOT_MS);                     // let the device finish starting up
  if (!busBegin()) Serial.println(F("The bus did not start. Check the pin numbers."));
  Serial.println(F("Register console ready. Type help."));
  if (!busReady()) Serial.println(F("Type scan to find the device."));
}

void loop() {
  while (Serial.available()) {
    char c = Serial.read();
    if (c == '\n' || c == '\r') {
      if (lineLen) {
        lineBuf[lineLen] = 0;
        handle(lineBuf);
        lineLen = 0;
      }
    } else if (lineLen < sizeof(lineBuf) - 1) {
      lineBuf[lineLen++] = c;
    }
  }
}
```

### Using it

1. Build the wiring with the power off, including the reference, and connect a test voltage between 0 V and 2.5 V to CH0.
2. Upload the sketch and open the Serial Monitor.
3. Type `read 0`. The sketch selects channel 0, waits out the two-frame pipeline, checks that the result says channel 0, and prints the code and the voltage.
4. Type `all` to scan every channel once in auto-2 mode. Floating inputs read anything; tie unused ones to ground.
5. `range 2` doubles the full scale to 2 × VREF, but only works with VA ≥ 2 × VREF; with VA at 3.3 V, keep range 1.
6. `w 1 <frame>` sends any 16-bit frame and prints what came back (see *Frames for common tasks* above), `r 0` sends a continue frame and shows the raw result, and `swreset` resets the part.

If `read` reports that the channel address doesn't match:

- SDO must reach GPIO13. If every result reads `0xFFFF` or `0x0000`, check SDO and CS_N.
- Check the SPI mode (mode 0) and that VBD is powered: the SPI pins run from VBD, not VA.
- A wrong address with sensible codes usually means the part is in an auto mode from an earlier command. `read` switches it back to manual; try it twice.

If the codes are wrong but the address is right, check the reference on REFP and that MXO is linked to AINP.

### Pitfalls with the ESP32-S3

- **Two USB ports.** On boards with two, the one marked USB is the chip's own port: set *USB CDC On Boot* to *Enabled* to make `Serial` use it. The sketch waits up to 3 s for the Serial Monitor on that port.
- **Don't expect 1 MSPS from this sketch.** It drives CS_N with `digitalWrite()` and runs SPI at 2 MHz, so a frame takes several microseconds plus the software overhead. For full speed, let the SPI peripheral drive CS_N (hardware chip select) and use DMA; that is beyond this console.
- **20 MHz needs care at VBD = 3.3 V.** SDO becomes valid up to 27 ns after the falling SCLK edge, which is longer than the 25 ns half-period at 20 MHz, so a controller that samples on the rising edge would read too early. Below about 16 MHz there is margin (our calculation, before the controller's own setup time), or run VBD at 5 V (17 ns) if the controller tolerates 5 V, which the ESP32-S3 doesn't.
- **Pick free pins.** Avoid GPIO0, 3, 45 and 46 (boot strapping), GPIO19 and 20 (the USB port) and GPIO26 to 37 (flash and PSRAM on many modules).
- **The part stays in the mode you leave it in.** After `all` it is in auto-2 mode, so `r 0` (a continue frame) steps through the channels. `read` returns it to manual mode.

## Layout

From the datasheet's PCB layout guide, with drawings for both packages:

- **At least 10 µF between REFP and REFM**, as close to the pins as possible.
- **Decoupling capacitors at every supply pin**, as close to the power and ground pins as possible.
- **A capacitor at every channel input**, to supply the charge the sampling capacitor takes.
- **A small resistor in series with SDO, close to the ADC**, if the trace rings.
- **Series resistors on SCLK and SDI, close to the controller**, when the controller's outputs are strong.

## Things to watch

- **Range 2 needs VA ≥ 2 × VREF.** The feature list advertises 0 V to 5 V, which needs a 2.5 V reference and VA of at least 5 V. At VA = 3.3 V, range 2 is out of reach with any allowed reference (2 V minimum).
- **An external reference is required.** The part has none of its own. REFP takes 2 to 3 V and draws about 81 µA at 2.5 V.
- **The result arrives two frames after you ask for it** in manual mode, and the first frame after power-up or a reset is invalid. Use the channel address in DO15–DO12 to match results to channels.
- **High source impedance needs a slow scan or a buffer.** The 270 pF input capacitors cover the sampling itself, but the source must recharge them between samples. See the source-resistance table.
- **No missing codes only to 11 bits**, and the INL limit is up to 4 LSB (3.6 LSB on the SA89851).
- **VBD must never exceed VA**, including at power-up, and a 5 V VBD costs about 5 dB of SNR.
- **20 MHz at VBD = 3 V is tight** for a controller that samples SDO on the rising edge (td2 is 27 ns against a 25 ns half-period).
- **Auto-1 with channel 15:** the datasheet's Note 2 says that if CH15 is in the list, at least one of CH12 to CH14 must be too.
- **The dynamic figures and all the SPI timing are "guaranteed by design"** (Note 5), not production tested.
- **Small errors in the datasheet.** The SA89851 table puts the 0.4 V VOL limit in the minimum column. Both revision-history rows say "Revision 1.0". The ordering table's "Future product" footnote marks neither part. Figure 16 labels its last two pins GND and leaves them unconnected, though the pin table calls them AGND.
- **A familiar command set.** The frame commands match those of TI's ADS795x multichannel ADCs, as far as we can tell (our observation, not stated by Silergy). Code written for those parts may work, but check every field and the timing first.
