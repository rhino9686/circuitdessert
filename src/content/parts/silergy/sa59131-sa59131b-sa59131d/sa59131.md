## How it works

The SA59131 is a fixed-gain differential amplifier built to sit across a shunt resistor. It measures VSENSE = VIN+ − VIN−, multiplies it by the gain G (50 for the SA59131) and adds a level set by the two reference pins:

OUT = G × VSENSE + VREF

Current in the shunt makes VSENSE, so the output is a voltage that follows the current, and the reference pins decide where the output sits at zero current. In general VREF is the average of the REF1 and REF2 voltages (our reading of the datasheet's reference text, see "Setting the output reference").

**A common-mode range that doesn't depend on the supply.** The inputs work from −4 V to 80 V whatever VS is (2.7 V to 5.5 V). That lets a 3.3 V or 5 V supply watch a shunt on a rail up to 80 V (high side), lets the shunt sit in the ground return (low side), and, because the range goes below ground, lets the part keep measuring when the shunt's common-mode voltage swings negative. The amplifier does the translation from the shunt's common-mode voltage to a ground-referenced output.

**Enhanced PWM rejection.** In a motor drive, a switching converter or a battery pack with switched loads, the common-mode voltage at the shunt steps by tens of volts in microseconds. The datasheet says the part attenuates these steps before they reach the output, instead of relying on a wide amplifier bandwidth, and it quotes 93 dB of common-mode rejection at 50 kHz (typical, by design) against 132 dB at DC. Its two transient-response plots (a step from −4 V to 80 V and one from 80 V to −4 V, 8 µs per division) show the output moving by roughly 50 mV one way and 100 mV the other for a few microseconds (read by eye, on a 100 mV per division scale). The datasheet gives no limit for these spikes.

**Zero-drift input stage.** The shunt voltage is small, so the offset is what limits the low end. The datasheet gives ±25 µV maximum at 25 °C (±5 µV typical), with 250 nV/°C maximum drift, and the maximum gain error is ±0.15% (±0.02% typical).

**Unidirectional or bidirectional.** Which direction the output moves for which current, and where it sits at zero current, is set by how REF1 and REF2 are connected. The application schematic uses the mid-supply connection, so the output starts at VS/2 and moves up or down with the current. See "Setting the output reference".

**Three versions.** The SA59131B, SA59131 and SA59131D differ in gain (25, 50 and 100 V/V). A larger gain gives a bigger output for the same shunt voltage, which allows a smaller shunt voltage (and less dissipation) for the same output, and it is paid for with a smaller input range. The datasheet lists different offset-voltage supply rejection and reference rejection for them, and every other electrical row once.

## Setting the output reference

REF1 and REF2 are the two ends of an internal divider (two 50 kΩ resistors in the datasheet's Figure 2, with the midpoint feeding the amplifier). Each accepts any voltage from 0 V to VS. The datasheet shows six ways to connect them (the figures are its Figures 3 to 8, where the divider figure is the first of two figures numbered 8):

| Connection | REF1 | REF2 | OUT at zero current | Current direction |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Figure 3, ground reference | GND | GND | 0 V | One way: VSENSE must be positive to lift OUT |
| Figure 4, supply reference | VS | VS | VS | One way: VSENSE must be negative to pull OUT down |
| Figure 5, external reference | An external reference | The same reference | That reference | Both |
| Figure 6, mid-supply | GND | VS | VS/2 | Both |
| Figure 7, mid-external reference | GND | An external reference | Half the reference | Both |
| Figure 8, divider (R1 from VS, R2 to GND) | The divider tap | The divider tap | The tap voltage | Both, with the tap read as a second ADC input |

In the divider connection the datasheet says to take the output as a two-ended signal, OUT against the tap (the figure labels them ADC+ and ADC−), because the internal impedance of the reference pins affects the external division.

**The general rule is our reading.** The output at zero current is the average of the two reference voltages, (VREF1 + VREF2) ÷ 2, which gives every row above. The datasheet writes its reference-divider-accuracy condition as VOUT = |VREF1 − VREF2| ÷ 2, which gives the same value only when one reference pin is on ground, so it is a special case.

**The reference pins are not high-impedance.** The datasheet gives no reference input current or resistance, only the 50 kΩ values in Figure 2. With REF2 on VS and REF1 on ground at 5 V, those two resistors alone would draw 5 V ÷ 100 kΩ = 50 µA from the supply (calculated from Figure 2, ignoring the amplifier's own input path). A reference source or a divider that feeds REF1 or REF2 has to be low-impedance, as the datasheet's Figure 8 text warns.

### Choosing RSENSE

The datasheet gives no formula and no table for the shunt. Its text says the range of current is set by the shunt value and the gain, that a larger resistor makes a bigger signal, a smaller share of fixed error and better accuracy but dissipates more power and drifts with its own temperature, and that with a gain of 50 V/V and very low offset a low-value resistor is recommended.

The limits below are ours. The output has to stay inside the window where the gain error is specified, from 50 mV above ground to 200 mV below VS (the condition of the gain-error row). With the mid-supply reference that allows VSENSE up to (VS/2 − 0.2 V) ÷ 50, which is 46 mV at VS = 5 V and 29 mV at 3.3 V, and the shunt is that voltage divided by the largest current. The first design table gives the shunt-voltage window for every reference connection and gain, and the second shows what a shunt of each size gives for the SA59131.

A bigger shunt gives a bigger signal and a smaller error from the offset, and costs more dissipation (I²R) and more voltage lost in the supply rail. The second design table (calculated): a 10 mΩ shunt covers ±4.6 A at 5 V with 0.21 W at full scale, and a 1 mΩ shunt covers ±46 A with 2.1 W.

### Supply bypass

The datasheet recommends a 0.1 µF MLCC as close as possible to the VS and GND pins, and its bill of materials has a 100 nF, 50 V, X7R in 0603. It gives no stability requirement for it.

### Worked example: the datasheet circuit, ±4.6 A at 5 V

These numbers are calculated here from the datasheet's values, and nothing was measured. The circuit is the one in the application schematic: SA59131 (gain 50), RSENSE = 10 mΩ, VS = 5 V, REF2 on VS and REF1 on ground.

- **Range.** VSENSE from −49 mV to +46 mV, which is −4.9 A to +4.6 A. The upper limit comes from the 4.8 V (VS − 0.2 V) end of the window and the lower from the 50 mV end.
- **Transfer.** OUT = 2.5 V + 50 × 10 mΩ × I = 2.5 V + 0.5 V per amp. At 4.6 A the output is 4.8 V.
- **Dissipation.** 0.21 W in the shunt at 4.6 A, well inside its 1 W rating (which would be reached at 10 A, where VSENSE is 100 mV and the output would have saturated long before). The voltage lost in the rail at full scale is 46 mV.

The error depends on the current, because the offset is fixed and the gain error is a percentage. The 25 °C columns use the datasheet maximums (25 µV offset, 0.15% gain error). The 125 °C column adds the maximum drifts over the 100 °C from 25 °C: 250 nV/°C × 100 = 25 µV more offset, so 50 µV, and 2.5 ppm/°C × 100 = 0.025% more gain error, so 0.175%. In the table, OUT is the ideal output, "Offset" is the 25 µV offset as a percentage of the reading, "Offset + gain" adds the 0.15% gain error (25 °C), "At 125 °C" is the same sum with the drifts, and "With 1% shunt" adds the shunt's 1% tolerance to the 25 °C sum.

| 0.05 A | 0.5 mV | 2.525 V | 5.00% | 5.15% | 10.18% | 6.15% |
| 0.1 A | 1 mV | 2.550 V | 2.50% | 2.65% | 5.18% | 3.65% |
| 0.25 A | 2.5 mV | 2.625 V | 1.00% | 1.15% | 2.18% | 2.15% |
| 0.5 A | 5 mV | 2.750 V | 0.50% | 0.65% | 1.18% | 1.65% |
| 1 A | 10 mV | 3.000 V | 0.25% | 0.40% | 0.68% | 1.40% |
| 2 A | 20 mV | 3.500 V | 0.13% | 0.28% | 0.43% | 1.28% |

These are worst-case sums of the datasheet's maximum values. They leave out the terms below, the ADC's own error and the nonlinearity (±0.01% typical), and in practice errors rarely all add in the same direction. Below about 1.7 A the maximum offset error is larger than the maximum gain error (calculated), and the 1% shunt (the bill of materials part) is a bigger error than the amplifier's gain error.

**Terms left out above (our arithmetic):**

- **Common mode.** The CMRR is 114 dB minimum, 2.0 µV/V, so across the whole −4 V to 80 V range the offset can move by up to 168 µV at the minimum (21 µV at the 132 dB typical), more than the offset limit. The third design table gives the shift for smaller changes. On a fixed rail the shift is a constant that a zero-current calibration removes.
- **Supply.** The offset-voltage supply rejection of ±10 µV/V maximum for the SA59131 means a 2.3 V change in VS (5 V down to 2.7 V) moves the offset by up to 23 µV.
- **Reference.** The reference divider accuracy is 0.12% maximum. If it is read as a percentage of the 2.5 V mid-supply level (our reading), the zero-current output can be off by up to 3 mV, which is 60 µV referred to the input and 6 mA at 10 mΩ, and a zero-current calibration removes it too.
- **Temperature at the shunt.** The datasheet gives no shunt temperature coefficient (our note).

**The same shunt with REF1 and REF2 both on ground (unidirectional).** The window is 1 mV to 96 mV, so 0.1 A to 9.6 A, and the output runs from 0.05 V to 4.8 V (0.5 V per amp). The first design table gives the other combinations.

## Layout

The datasheet's three rules, with the layout figures for the two packages (top layer only):

- **Use a Kelvin connection from IN+ and IN− to the shunt.** With a few milliohms, the resistance of the traces between the shunt pads and the amplifier pins is large next to the shunt itself, and it adds a significant error. A Kelvin connection (the two input traces taken from the shunt's own pads, not from the high-current trace) means only the shunt's resistance is measured.
- **Minimise the loop formed by these two connections.**
- **Put the 0.1 µF bypass capacitor as close as possible to VS and GND.**

In the TSSOP8 figure the four pins on the left are NC, IN+, IN− and GND from top to bottom, and OUT, REF1, REF2 and VS run along the right side from top to bottom, with the capacitor under the package between the GND and VS copper. In the SOP8 figure, NC has a via to the ground copper, and the REF1 and REF2 pins are joined to the ground and VS copper by short strips (our reading of the figures, which agrees with the application schematic).

**The package drawings.** TSSOP8: body 2.90–3.10 mm long and 4.30–4.50 mm wide, 6.20–6.60 mm lead span, 1.20 mm maximum height, 0.65 mm pitch, 0.19–0.30 mm leads. Its recommended PCB layout shows pads 0.4 mm wide and 1.2 mm long on a 0.65 mm pitch, with the two rows 5.8 mm apart (we read the dimension as centre to centre). SOP8: body 4.70–5.10 mm long and 3.80–4.00 mm wide, 5.80–6.20 mm lead span, 1.30–1.75 mm height, 1.27 mm pitch, 0.30–0.51 mm leads. Its pad layout is marked "Reference Only" and shows pads 0.60 mm wide and 1.55 mm long on a 1.27 mm pitch, 5.40 mm between the two rows (we read the dimension as centre to centre, as for the TSSOP8).

**The KiCad files.** The SOP8 footprint is on the SOIC-8 package page (the same package under TI's name): its pads equal the SOP8 layout above, and it is listed as compared. The TSSOP8 footprint is on the TSSOP-8 page, built from the pad drawing of the pin-compatible INA240-Q1, whose pads (1.5 × 0.45 mm) are larger than the 1.2 × 0.4 mm shown above, so the SA59131 is listed there as "Assumed". ## What the datasheet shows

The data is the electrical characteristics table (VS = 5 V, VCM = 12 V, VREF1 = VREF2 = VS/2, with the limits over temperature guaranteed by design, test or statistical correlation and several rows by design only), twelve typical performance plots, two common-mode transient plots, the application text with its Figures 2 to 8, the application schematic with its bill of materials, two layout figures, the two package outlines with recommended pads, and the tape-and-reel pages.

What the plots show, read by eye (typical units at 25 °C unless stated):

- **Offset, gain error and bias current against temperature** are nearly flat from −40 to 125 °C: offset about −2 to −3 µV, gain error within about 0.01%, bias current about 80 to 81 µA.
- **Quiescent current** is about 1.64 mA at 2.7 V, 1.68 mA at 3.3 V and 1.74 to 1.78 mA at 5 V, and falls slightly with temperature.
- **Input bias current against common-mode voltage** is about −10 µA near −4 V and rises to about 70 µA by 6 V (a step), then to about 195 µA at 80 V. **Against the differential input voltage** (±0.5 V), IB+ rises from about −35 µA to 210 µA while IB− falls the same way, and they cross at 85 µA at zero.
- **Common-mode rejection against frequency** is about 138 to 140 dB at the low end of the plot, falls past about 1 kHz (the table gives 93 dB at 50 kHz), and is about 65 dB at 1 MHz. **Power-supply rejection** is about 120 dB to 1 kHz and about 25 dB at 1 MHz.
- **Gain against frequency** for all three versions is flat to a few hundred kilohertz, with the 500 kHz bandwidth in the table, and the noise density is about 35 to 40 nV/√Hz up to a few hundred kilohertz.

There is no formula or table for choosing the shunt, no guidance on filtering the inputs, no output current or short-circuit figure, no start-up or overload-recovery time, no reference input current or resistance, no minimum or maximum for the bias current, and the electrical table is given at VS = 5 V only (the plots add 2.7 V and 3.3 V for the quiescent current).

## Things to watch

- **It is one datasheet for three versions, and the SOP8 exists only for the 50 V/V one.** Check the gain in the part number (SA59131BHMP, SA59131HMP, SA59131FAP, SA59131DHMP) and the top mark. The input range follows the gain.
- **The KiCad files are ours and not yet opened in KiCad.** The symbol file holds two symbols, SA59131_SOIC-8 (Silergy's SOP8) and SA59131_TSSOP-8, each with its own footprint and pin numbers; the SA59131B and SA59131D (TSSOP8 only) use the TSSOP-8 symbol. The pin names and numbers come from the pin table; the layout and pin types are ours. The TSSOP-8 footprint uses TI's larger pads, not Silergy's (see Layout).
- **The TSSOP8 and SOP8 pin numbers differ.** See the pin table: IN+ is pin 2 or pin 8, IN− is pin 3 or pin 1. Only REF1 is pin 7 in both.
- **Ground NC.** The pin table says to connect it to ground, the application schematic and the SOP8 layout do, and the Figure 1 drawing omits it. The absolute maximum for it is GND − 0.3 V to VS + 0.3 V.
- **Both reference pins must be connected, and the output reference follows what they are tied to.** With REF2 on VS the mid-supply level moves with the supply (half of any change in VS shows up at OUT, our arithmetic from the average rule), which suits an ADC that uses the same supply as its reference. An ADC with its own reference needs the REF pins driven from that reference (Figures 5 and 7), from a low-impedance source.
- **Offset and drift set the low end.** The ±25 µV limit is for 25 °C and grows by up to 25 µV by 125 °C. The common-mode term can be larger still (third design table).
- **The inputs draw a large and varying bias current.** 85 µA typical at 25 °C and 12 V common mode, rising with the common-mode voltage (about 195 µA at 80 V by eye) and moving in opposite directions at the two pins with the shunt voltage. A series resistor in the inputs therefore shifts the gain and the offset, and the datasheet gives no guidance. If the slope of the plot (about 245 µA per volt of differential input, read by eye) is read as an input resistance of roughly 2 kΩ per pin (our reading, not a datasheet figure), every ohm of series resistance in both inputs lowers the gain by about 0.05%. Keep the shunt traces short and unfiltered unless you have measured it.
- **Near ground the gain error isn't specified.** The gain-error limit starts 50 mV above ground and the output can reach GND + 10 mV at most, so with a ground reference the lowest currents sit outside it (1 mV across the shunt for the SA59131, from the first design table).
- **The output drive isn't specified.** The swing is tested into 10 kΩ to ground, there is no output current or short-circuit figure, and the maximum capacitive load is 2.2 nF (no sustained oscillation). An ADC sampling capacitor larger than that directly on OUT needs a series resistor or a buffer (our note).
- **The bandwidth is 500 kHz for small signals but 100 kHz for 2% THD+N.** Settling to 0.5% takes 7 µs and the slew rate is 2 V/µs, so a swing from 0.05 V to 4.8 V needs at least about 2.4 µs (calculated). Large fast current waveforms are filtered and distorted above about 100 kHz.
- **The common-mode ratings are −6 V to 90 V absolute and −4 V to 80 V recommended, and ±80 V differential.** The datasheet gives no surge or spike information beyond them.
- **It isn't a low-power part.** 1.8 mA typical and 2.4 mA maximum at 25 °C (2.6 mA over temperature). At 5 V that is about 9 mW, which warms the TSSOP8 by under 2 °C at 190 °C/W (calculated).
- **The SOP8 is MSL3 and the TSSOP8 MSL1.** Dry-pack handling applies to the SOP8 only.
- **Shunt tolerance is the largest error in the example.** The bill of materials shunt is 1%, several times the amplifier's gain error.

## Datasheet notes

- **The reference-accuracy condition is a special case.** The table writes VOUT = |(VREF1 − VREF2)|/2 for the divider accuracy. That equals the average of the two references only when one is on ground. With both pins tied to the same voltage (Figures 4 and 5) it would give 0 V, where the text puts the output at VS or at the reference. This page uses the average rule (our reading).
- **Figure 8 appears twice.** The reference-divider figure and the TSSOP8 recommended-layout figure are both numbered Figure 8, and the SOP8 layout is Figure 9.
- **The common-mode rejection plot reads higher than the table.** The plot shows about 138 to 140 dB at low frequency (by eye) against 132 dB typical in the table. 
- **The quiescent-current headline is the 25 °C limit.** The front page says 2.4 mA maximum. The table gives 2.4 mA at 25 °C and 2.6 mA over temperature.
- **The power-dissipation row uses a 125 °C junction.** 0.53 W for the TSSOP8 and 0.88 W for the SOP8 are (125 − 25) ÷ θJA with θJA of 190 and 114 °C/W. The absolute maximum junction temperature is 150 °C, which would give 0.66 W and 1.10 W (our arithmetic).
- **The offset limit is stated at 25 °C only.** The table header says limits cover −40 to 125 °C unless noted, and the offset row notes 25 °C. The drift row is what covers temperature.
- **The general description says "There fixed gains are available"** (it means three).
- **The application text is written for the 50 V/V version** ("Because the SA59131 has a gain of 50V/V …") although the datasheet covers three.
- **The SQ52131 name.** Silergy's site also lists SQ52131 (SQ52131HMP, SQ52131FAP) with the same title, description and package options (seen in search-result snippets: the SQ52131HMP page itself returned an error when opened). Neither the SA59131 datasheet nor the SA59131 product page mentions it, so we can't tell whether it is the same part under a new number. Ask Silergy before treating them as one.
- **The product page's datasheet is dated 2026-03-10 with no revision,** and this page uses Rev. 1.0A of October 15, 2025.
- **Revision history.** Rev. 1.0 was the initial release (February 25, 2025). Rev. 1.0A (October 15, 2025) added the SA59131B and SA59131D and changed the gain-against-frequency plot range from 10 Hz to 10 MHz to 10 Hz to 1 MHz.
- **Status.** Production, from Silergy's SA59131 product page.
