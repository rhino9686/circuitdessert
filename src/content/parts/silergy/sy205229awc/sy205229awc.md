## How it works

The SY205229AWC is a low-capacitance transient voltage suppressor (TVS) for high-speed data interfaces. Each of its two I/O pins protects one data line against over-voltage and over-current transients. The typical 1.2 pF from I/O to GND keeps the insertion loss on the data line small.

**Discharge paths.** The datasheet draws the internal network three times: on the cover (page 1), in the pinout (page 2) and inside the body of both application figures (pages 5 and 6). Each I/O pin sits between two steering diodes: one goes up to VCC (pin 4) and one comes up from GND (pin 1). A Zener diode joins VCC to GND, with its cathode on VCC. The text on page 5 describes three paths:

- A positive line-to-ground transient is discharged through the upper steering diode and the Zener.
- A negative line-to-ground transient is discharged directly to ground through the lower steering diode.
- A line-to-line discharge is handled by the Zener together with two forward steering diodes.

Figure 1 on page 3 plots the unidirectional V-I characteristic, labelled VC, VBR, VRWM, IR, IT, IPP, VF and IF.

**Electrical behaviour (TA = 25 °C, page 2).** The working voltage is 5.0 V (listed as a maximum), and the leakage at that voltage is 0.01 µA typical and 0.1 µA maximum. Breakdown at 1 mA is 6.0 V minimum, 8.0 V typical and 10.0 V maximum; forward voltage at 1 mA is 0.4, 0.7 and 1.2 V. The clamping voltage is 12.5 V typical and 13.5 V maximum at 7.5 A over 8/20 µs, and 12 V typical at 16 A over 10/100 ns, with a dynamic resistance of 0.25 Ω typical. Capacitance at 2.5 V reverse and 1 MHz is 1.20 pF typical and 1.50 pF maximum from I/O to GND, and 0.60 pF typical and 0.75 pF maximum between the two I/Os. The datasheet says the clamping voltages, dynamic resistance and capacitances are guaranteed by design and not production-tested.

**ESD and surge robustness.** The part is rated ±30 kV air and ±30 kV contact per IEC 61000-4-2, and 7.5 A (8/20 µs) per IEC 61000-4-5. Both the 7.5 A and the 100 W peak pulse power are also absolute maximum ratings. The cover says each I/O pin withstands over 1000 ESD strikes of ±8 kV contact discharge, and page 4 plots the ±8 kV contact clamping. Our readings of those plots are in the design tables below.

## Example application

The datasheet's application (pages 5 and 6) protects the D+ and D− lines of a USB 2.0 port. The part sits between the USB controller and the port. Pin 2 goes to D+, pin 3 to D−, pin 4 to VBUS and pin 1 to ground. Each data row carries a shunt capacitor C_T to ground and a series resistor R_T, and the datasheet gives no value for either.

## Layout

Silergy's PCB layout guidelines (page 5):

- Place the part as close to the connector port as possible.
- Keep the distance between the ground pin and the GND reference path as short as possible.
- Use large vias to connect the VCC and GND pins to the PCB VCC and GND.
- Avoid running critical signals near board edges.
- Keep the connection wires as short as possible to minimise parasitic inductance.

## Things to watch

- **No component values.** C_T and R_T have no value, part number or maker, and the datasheet has no recommended-components table. What to put on each data line is the designer's choice, and the figure gives no guidance.
- **A 5 V rail sits at the working-voltage maximum.** VRWM is 5.0 V, listed as a maximum, and leakage is specified only at 5 V. VBR starts at 6.0 V at 1 mA. The datasheet does not give leakage between 5 and 6 V. The application ties VCC to VBUS, and the USB 2.0 specification allows VBUS up to 5.25 V (that limit is from the USB specification, not this datasheet), so a rail at 5.25 V is above the point where leakage is guaranteed and below the minimum breakdown. Our reading: the part is still blocking there, but no number backs that up.
- **Wide breakdown spread.** VBR at 1 mA is 6.0 V minimum, 8.0 V typical and 10.0 V maximum. The clamping voltage at 7.5 A (12.5 V typical, 13.5 V maximum) is the figure that protects the downstream IC.
- **The maximum clamp at the rated current exceeds the rated power.** 13.5 V × 7.5 A is 101.3 W against a 100 W PPK; at the typical clamp it is 93.8 W (see the calculated checks). Treat 7.5 A as the limit.
- **The first ESD spike is far above the clamp.** The ±8 kV contact plots on page 4 show first-spike peaks of about 65 V and −63 V (read by eye) against a 12 V clamp at 16 A. The downstream IC sees that spike for a few nanoseconds, which is why the layout rules (short wires, close to the connector) matter.
- **Capacitance budget.** 1.2 pF typical and 1.5 pF maximum per line to ground is the point of the part for USB 2.0 and VGA-class lines. The applications list is USB 2.0, FireWire, video graphics cards and SIM ports, so check the budget before using it on a faster interface.
- **Surge rating is per pulse.** 7.5 A (8/20 µs) with a 12.5 V typical clamp. The datasheet gives no number of surge strokes; "over 1000 strikes" is for ±8 kV contact ESD.
- **Unidirectional.** Figure 1 is titled "Uni-directional TVS". Pin 1 must go to ground and pin 4 to the supply or VBUS. VF is given at 1 mA only (0.4 to 1.2 V), so the datasheet does not say how high a negative line transient is clamped at high current; the −8 kV plot shows a spike of about −63 V.
