## How it works

The SY80082x is a synchronous buck regulator with both power switches inside a six-pin SOT23-6 package. It uses a constant off-time, peak current mode control scheme. The top switch turns on, and turns off when its sensed current reaches an internal control level. The bottom switch then turns on for a fixed off-time, which the chip works out from the input voltage, the output voltage and the target frequency: tOFF = (1 − VOUT ÷ VIN) ÷ fSW. Because the off-time is fixed and the on-time is whatever the loop asks for, the switching frequency stays close to 1.5 MHz while the controller can react within a single cycle. The compensation is built in, so the circuit needs no external compensation network.

At light load the part switches to pulse-frequency modulation (PFM). Once the load falls below half the inductor ripple current, IOUT = VOUT × (1 − D) ÷ (2 × fSW × L), the bottom switch turns off before the current can reverse, and the controller skips cycles so the switching frequency falls with the load. In this circuit at 5 V in, with a 1 µH inductor, that crossover is about 0.38 A. Above it, the part runs in continuous conduction at nearly constant frequency. In PFM the part stops switching as soon as FB rises above the reference, so no separate over-voltage protection is needed.

Raising EN above 1 V enables the part. Switching starts after a turn-on delay of about 0.5 ms, and the output then rises over about 1 ms. EN below 0.4 V shuts the part down and cuts the input current to under 1 µA. All three variants discharge the output when disabled; the datasheet lists 50 Ω for SY80082ABT and SY80082LABT and 1.8 kΩ for SY80082FABT.

The power-good pin (PG) is an open-drain output watching the feedback voltage with a window comparator. After VIN comes up and the internal supply is ready, PG is driven low until the output is ready. It goes high about 1 µs after FB rises past 92% of the reference, and goes low again, after about 20 µs, if FB falls below 90% or rises above 120%; on the way back down from an over-voltage it recovers at 110%. Pull it up to VIN, or to another supply, through a resistor; the datasheet suggests 100 kΩ.

Current limiting is cycle by cycle: the top switch turns off at its current limit (3.5 A minimum, from the electrical table) and the bottom switch takes over. If the output falls below 50% of its regulated level, the output under-voltage protection (UVP) acts.


The three variants differ only in this fault behavior and in the output discharge:

| Part | Output discharge | Output short-circuit (UVP) | Over-temperature |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| SY80082ABT | 50 Ω | Hiccup: 2.5 ms on, 3 ms off, recovers by itself | Restarts after cooling, with a full soft-start |
| SY80082FABT | 1.8 kΩ | Latches off | Latches off |
| SY80082LABT | 50 Ω | Latches off | Latches off |

The datasheet doesn't say how a latched part is restarted. Cycling EN or the input supply is the usual way for latch-off parts, but confirm that with Silergy before you design around it. Input under-voltage lockout keeps switching off until VIN is above about 2.4 V (2.49 V maximum), with 0.15 V of hysteresis.

The part can run at up to 100% duty cycle, so the output can follow the input down in dropout. At the other end, the minimum on-time (50 ns, guaranteed by design) limits how low the duty cycle can go. The datasheet says the part supports duty cycles down to about 10% over the full temperature range; for example, 0.6 V from 5.5 V is 10.9%.

A 5 V to 1.8 V buck converter built from the circuit in Silergy's datasheet. The datasheet draws one schematic for the whole family, with SY80082ABT as U1; SY80082FABT and SY80082LABT use the same circuit. The part has a power-good output and a six-pin SOT23-6 package. This section keeps the datasheet's component values, adds the reasoning behind each one, and works the numbers for 5 V in.

### Output voltage

The output is set by two resistors: VOUT = 0.6 V × (1 + RH ÷ RL). The datasheet recommends 1 kΩ to 1 MΩ for both, to keep light-load current low. It also asks for RL no larger than 20 kΩ. With RH = 30 kΩ and a 1.8 V target, RL = 0.6 ÷ (1.8 − 0.6) × 30 kΩ = 15.0 kΩ, and the nearest 1% value, 15 kΩ, gives 1.800 V nominal. With the reference's ±1.5% tolerance the output lands between 1.773 V and 1.827 V, before the resistors' own tolerance is added.

### Inductor

Choose the inductance for a ripple current of about 40% of the maximum load at the highest input voltage: L = VOUT × (1 − VOUT ÷ VIN,MAX) ÷ (fSW × IOUT,MAX × 0.4). For 1.8 V, 5.5 V in, 2 A and 1.5 MHz that gives 1.01 µH, and the datasheet uses 1 µH. The part tolerates a wide range of ripple, so the final value can differ from the calculation with little effect.

Two more checks apply. The saturation current must exceed the peak current, ISAT > IOUT,MAX + VOUT × (1 − VOUT ÷ VIN,MAX) ÷ (2 × fSW × L). And the winding resistance (DCR) should be below 60 mΩ for good efficiency, along with low core loss at 1.5 MHz.

### Output capacitor

Use an X5R or better ceramic with a 6.3 V rating or higher and at least 10 µF. The datasheet's selection table, reproduced below, pairs each inductor with the output capacitances that suit it; the reference circuit uses one 10 µF capacitor with 1.0 µH. Output ripple has two parts: the inductor ripple current across the capacitor's ESR (ΔIL × ESR), and the charge on the capacitance (ΔIL ÷ (8 × COUT × fSW)). Add them for a total. Ceramic capacitance falls as the DC voltage across it rises, so measured ripple is usually higher than the calculation; read the effective capacitance off the capacitor's DC-bias curve and recalculate.

| VOUT | L | 4.7 µF | 10 µF | 22 µF | 44 µF |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0.9 V | 0.68 µH | – | OK | Recommended | OK |
| 1.8, 2.5 or 3.3 V | 1.0 µH | – | Recommended | OK | OK |
| 1.8, 2.5 or 3.3 V | 2.2 µH | – | – | OK | OK |

The datasheet marks one choice per row as recommended for most applications and the others as workable. Note that a 2.2 µH inductor is only listed with 22 µF or more.

### Input capacitor

Use an X5R or better ceramic rated 10 V (the rating should be at least 20% above the highest input voltage, so 6.6 V at 5.5 V in), 10 µF or more, as close as possible to the IN and GND pins with the smallest possible loop. The RMS current it carries is IOUT × √(D × (1 − D)), at most IOUT ÷ 2 at 50% duty, so choose one with an RMS rating above half the load current, and add capacitors in parallel if the calculation needs it. The datasheet says capacitance matters less than RMS rating, and that a single 10 µF ceramic is enough for most applications. If VIN arrives over long wires, add bulk capacitance (electrolytic, tantalum or polymer) to damp the overshoot and ringing from the lead inductance. The reference circuit's 47 µF electrolytic, CIN1, is that kind of bulk capacitor; the datasheet doesn't say why it was included.

### Feedforward and enable

The feedforward capacitor CFF sits across RH and can speed up the load-transient response. The datasheet gives no minimum value, and says to raise it when the output LC is large; all four rows of its table use 22 pF.

Don't connect EN straight to VIN or another voltage source; the datasheet asks for a resistor between 1 kΩ and 1 MΩ. The reference circuit uses RENH = 10 kΩ and RENL = 1 MΩ, which puts EN at 99% of VIN, so the part starts when VIN passes its own under-voltage threshold and the divider only limits current. You can raise the start voltage with a larger RENH ÷ RENL ratio, but the datasheet gives only limits for the EN thresholds (on above 1 V, off below 0.4 V), with no typical value and no hysteresis. That makes the exact turn-on voltage uncertain, so don't rely on EN as a precise voltage monitor.

| Output ripple from the capacitance, 10 µF, 5 V in | 6.4 mV, plus ΔIL × ESR |

> **The peak current sits well inside the limit.** At 5.5 V in and a full 2 A load, the 1 µH inductor's ripple puts the peak at 2.40 A, which leaves 1.10 A below the 3.5 A minimum top-switch limit. The electrical table gives that limit as 3.5 A minimum, so design to the minimum.

> **The recommended inductor has about 40% ripple at the top of the input range.** The datasheet's 40% rule gives 1.01 µH, and the table uses 1.0 µH, so the numbers agree. At 5 V in, the PFM crossover (about 0.38 A) falls close to the peak of the efficiency curve.

## Layout

Silergy gives these layout rules, along with a suggested placement drawing (Figure 7 in the datasheet). In its drawing the pins are EN 1, GND 2, LX 3, IN 4, PG 5, FB 6.

- Put the **input capacitor** very close to the IN and GND pins, with the smallest loop, joined by a wide copper area.
- Connect the **output capacitor's** negative terminal to the GND pin with wide copper, not vias.
- Place **RH, RL and CFF** as close to the FB pin as possible and route the feedback line away from LX and other fast signals. Take the feedback from the output capacitor with a Kelvin connection, not from the inductor's output terminal.
- Keep the **LX copper** small to limit EMI, but wide enough to keep its resistance and inductance low.
- Don't tie **EN** straight to VIN or another source; use a resistor between 1 kΩ and 1 MΩ.
- Put an adequate number of **ground vias** around the device, and maximize the copper connected to the GND pin. A ground plane is strongly recommended.

The datasheet's thermal resistance (θJA) is 120 °C/W on a 6 × 6 cm, two-layer, 2 oz board, which corresponds to a power dissipation of 0.83 W at 25 °C ambient.

## What the datasheet shows

Silergy's typical-performance pages cover the SY80082ABT at 25 °C, 5 V in and 1.8 V out, with L = 1.0 µH and COUT = 10 µF. They include efficiency against load current from 1 mA to 2 A for the four output voltages at 3.3 V, 4.2 V and 5 V in (4.2 V and 5 V for the 3.3 V output), scope captures of output ripple at no load and at 2 A, load-transient steps (0 to 1 A and 0.2 to 2 A), start-up and shut-down from both EN and VIN, short-circuit captures, and Bode plots at 2 A for each output voltage.

Reading the curves by eye at 5 V in: for 1.8 V out, efficiency is about 77% at 1 mA, about 85% at 10 mA, about 89% at 0.1 A, peaks at about 91% between 0.3 and 0.5 A, and falls to about 82% at 2 A. For 3.3 V out it is about 82% at 1 mA, about 88% at 10 mA, about 92% at 0.1 A, about 95% at its peak near 0.4 A, and about 89% at 2 A. Treat these as rough, about two points either way, since they come from a graph. All four plots use the 1.0 µH inductor.

## Things to watch

- **Two over-temperature figures disagree.** The electrical table gives 150 °C with 15 °C of hysteresis; the protection text says the part shuts down above 160 °C and resumes after cooling by about 20 °C. Design to the lower figure.
- **So do two shutdown-current figures.** The electrical table gives 1 µA maximum with EN low, and the enable text says less than 2 µA. Use 1 µA if you are counting standby current, and ask Silergy if it matters.
- **The latch-off variants need a restart procedure the datasheet doesn't describe.** If you choose SY80082FABT or SY80082LABT, find out from Silergy how the latch clears, and make sure your system can do it.
- **The 2.2 µH inductor option needs 22 µF or more.** The selection table lists no 10 µF choice for 2.2 µH, although the 1.0 µH reference circuit uses 10 µF.
- **RL has an upper limit of 20 kΩ.** The datasheet recommends it without saying why. All four table rows already comply, but a design for a different output voltage needs a different RH to stay under it.
- **The full-load efficiency is in the low 80s at 1.8 V.** At 2 A the curve falls to about 82% for 1.8 V from 5 V, so check junction temperature: θJA is 120 °C/W on the datasheet's own board.
- **The EN thresholds are loose.** Only a 1 V minimum for high and a 0.4 V maximum for low are given, so don't use EN as a precise voltage monitor.
- **Older datasheets list a SY80082AAT.** Rev 1.0A removed it, so the family is now three parts.
