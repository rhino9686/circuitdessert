## How it works

The SY7070B is a synchronous boost converter: an N-channel main switch between LX and ground, and a P-channel synchronous switch between LX and VOUT. The inductor goes between IN and LX. The datasheet calls the control "current hysteresis" and the block diagram shows a transconductance amplifier comparing the output with a reference, a block of PWM control logic and a bypass control. It doesn't describe the loop beyond that. Its only clue to the current swing is the frequency equation, which uses 200 mA (our reading: the peak-to-peak ripple, so about 100 mA either side of the average).

**Switching frequency.** The datasheet gives f = VIN × (VOUT − VIN) ÷ (VOUT × L × 200 mA), so with the recommended 4.7 µH it is about 650 kHz at 0.9 V in, about 740 kHz at 1.5 V, 500 kHz at 2.2 V, and falls toward zero as the input approaches 2.8 V (table above, calculated). The datasheet doesn't describe a light-load mode or say how the frequency behaves at light load.

**Current limit.** The main FET current limit is 300 mA minimum and 350 mA typical. This is the number that limits the load: at a given input the average inductor current can't be much more than the limit minus half the ripple, which is why the 100 mA headline needs a higher input (see Things to watch).

**Input pin.** IN is a supply and sense pin, and the inductor is a separate connection to the same node in the typical circuit. The datasheet asks for a 10 µF ceramic from IN to GND.

**Bypass switch.** A bypass switch in parallel with the synchronous PMOS is off while the part is enabled. When EN is low it turns on and gives a direct low-impedance path from the input to the load. The datasheet says the bypass switch isn't affected by undervoltage lockout, overvoltage or thermal shutdown. So with the part disabled, the output is the battery voltage, up to 5.0 V, and whatever is connected to it stays powered from the cell.

**Start-up and lockout.** The minimum input voltage at start-up is 0.75 V typical, and the undervoltage lockout turns the part off at 0.6 V typical with VIN falling. The recommended operating range starts at 0.7 V.

**Protection.** There is thermal shutdown at 150 °C with 20 °C hysteresis, the undervoltage lockout and the current limit. The datasheet's text mentions overvoltage when it describes the bypass switch, but it doesn't describe or specify an overvoltage protection.

### Output voltage

The output voltage is fixed at 2.8 V, with 2.716 V minimum and 2.884 V maximum (±3%), tested at 1.2 V in and 10 mA. There is no feedback pin and no way to adjust it. If you need another voltage, this is the wrong part.

### Inductor (L)

The datasheet strongly recommends 4.7 µH and says a proper inductor between IN and LX is needed for stable operation. It explains that a different inductance changes the switching frequency in proportion to 1 ÷ L, and gives no other value that works and no equation for the ripple. Using the frequency equation, the ripple is held at 200 mA (our reading), so the inductor current in the typical circuit swings about ±100 mA around the average, and the peak is the average plus 100 mA (the table *Load the current limit allows* above, calculated). Choose a saturation current above the 350 mA typical limit, since the limit has no maximum in the datasheet (our suggestion). The DCR matters at these currents too: the datasheet doesn't give a target, but a few hundred milliohms is a loss of a few tens of millivolts at 100–200 mA (our note).

### Capacitors (CIN, COUT)

- **Input.** 10 µF X5R or better ceramic, close to IN and GND, with the loop between CIN and the IN and GND pins kept small. The datasheet recommends a low-ESR 10 µF to help the transient and EMI behaviour.
- **Output.** X5R or better, 6.3 V rated, at least 10 µF, sized for both the steady-state ripple and the transient requirements. When the part is disabled the bypass puts the input voltage on the output capacitor, up to 5 V, so the 6.3 V rating is the minimum if the input can reach 5 V (our note). DC-bias derating of a 6.3 V ceramic at 2.8 V is large, so the capacitance you get is lower than the label.

### Enable

EN turns the part on and off, and must not be left floating. The thresholds scale with the input below 1.6 V: the part is on when EN is at least 0.75 × VIN and off when EN is at most 0.2 × VIN, and above 1.6 V in the levels are 1.2 V and 0.32 V (table *EN thresholds against VIN* above). Tying EN to VIN should make the part always on (our reading; the datasheet only draws an "Enable signal"). The recommended maximum on EN is VOUT + 0.3 V, which is 3.1 V for a 2.8 V output, so an enable signal from a 3.3 V logic rail is outside that range (our note; the absolute maximum is 6 V).

### Worked example: a 2.8 V rail at 50 mA from one alkaline cell

A single alkaline cell goes from about 1.5 V when fresh to about 0.9 V at the end of its discharge (our note; 0.9 V is also the lowest curve in the datasheet's graph). The table is calculated here from the efficiency read off the graph at 50 mA, with the peak current taken as the average input current plus 100 mA (half the 200 mA ripple, our reading). Nothing here was measured.

| Input | Efficiency | Frequency | Input power | Input current | Loss | Peak inductor current | Margin to the 300 mA minimum limit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1.5 V | 91% | 741 kHz | 154 mW | 102 mA | 13.5 mW | 202 mA | 98 mA |
| 0.9 V | 84% | 650 kHz | 168 mW | 186 mA | 27.5 mW | 286 mA | 14 mA |

**The loss is small and so is the heating.** At 0.9 V in, 27 mW at θJA = 161 °C/W is about 4 °C.

**At the bottom of the range there is almost no margin.** At 0.9 V in and 50 mA out, the peak is 286 mA, 14 mA under the 300 mA minimum limit. At 0.7 V (no curve; assuming 80%) the same 50 mA needs 250 mA in, which puts the peak at the 350 mA typical limit. So the load the part can carry falls quickly as the cell runs down, and at 0.7 V it is below 50 mA.

**Efficiency at light load.** At 1 mA the graph reads 83% at 0.9 V in and 89% at 1.5 V, and at 0.1 mA 79% and 87% (table above). The quiescent current alone is a visible load on a cell: 5.5 µA at 2.8 V from the output is 15 µW, about 13 µA from a 1.2 V cell if the conversion were lossless, plus 0.5 µA on IN (our calculation).

## Layout

Silergy gives these rules and a layout drawing (Figure 4).

- Place **CIN, COUT and L close to the IC**.
- Give the GND pin as much copper as the board allows, ideally a ground plane, for the best thermal and noise performance.
- **COUT must be close to the VOUT and GND pins**, and the loop area formed by COUT and GND must be as small as possible.
- **Keep the PCB copper on the LX pin small**, to avoid noise problems.
- **Keep the CIN loop small**: CIN close to IN and GND.

The SOT23-5 pins are EN (1), GND (2), IN (3), VOUT (4) and LX (5). Figure 4 shows the placement of L, CIN and COUT around the pins.

## Things to watch

- **The 100 mA headline needs a higher input than the bottom of the range.** If the 350 mA typical limit is the top of the 200 mA ripple, 100 mA needs about 1.3 V in (1.6 V against the 300 mA minimum limit), at 86–88% efficiency (our calculation). At 0.9 V in the limit allows about 64 mA typical and 51 mA at the minimum (table above), and the datasheet's 0.9 V efficiency curve ends at about 70 mA.
- **Behaviour above 2.8 V in is not described.** The recommended input range goes to 5.0 V, and the graphs stop at 2.8 V in, so the datasheet doesn't say whether the output is regulated, follows the input or something else when the input is above the output voltage while the part is enabled.
- **The load stays connected to the battery when the part is disabled.** The bypass switch connects the input to the output, so the output is the input voltage, up to 5.0 V, and anything on the output must tolerate it. The datasheet gives no resistance or current rating for the bypass switch, and the shutdown current (1 µA maximum, with VEN = 0 and VIN = 3 V) says nothing about the load. If the load must be truly off, add a load switch (our suggestion).
- **Start-up is not guaranteed all the way down.** The part starts at 0.75 V typical, with no minimum or maximum, while the recommended range starts at 0.7 V and the lockout is at 0.6 V typical. The datasheet doesn't say if it starts under load at 0.75 V.
- **The idle current is mostly on the output side.** The 5.5 µA in the feature list is the current from VOUT, with 0.5 µA from IN (both typical, with VOUT forced to 3.0 V). From a 1.2 V cell that is about 13 µA at least (our calculation, worked example above).
- **EN thresholds depend on VIN and are one-sided.** See the table above. EN must not float, and its recommended maximum is VOUT + 0.3 V.
- **The frequency drops as the input approaches 2.8 V.** With 4.7 µH the equation gives about 285 kHz at 2.5 V and about 100 kHz at 2.7 V (calculated), which matters for the output ripple and for any noise-sensitive circuit that shares the rail.
- **Peak current has no maximum.** The 300–350 mA limit has no maximum value, so the inductor's saturation current should have headroom.
- **Heat is not a concern at these currents.** At 100 mA and 1.5 V in the loss is about 36 mW, which is 6 °C at 161 °C/W (our calculation). The datasheet's 0.6 W power dissipation limit is not close.

## Datasheet notes

- **The 100 mA is only in the title.** The electrical table doesn't have an output current rating. It has the 300–350 mA main FET limit, and the efficiency graph reaches 100 mA only for inputs of 1.5 V and above.
- **EN thresholds are one-sided.** The rising threshold has only a minimum (0.75 × VIN or 1.2 V) and the falling threshold only a maximum (0.2 × VIN or 0.32 V).
- **Overvoltage is mentioned but not described.** The bypass-switch paragraph says the switch isn't affected by overvoltage, but no overvoltage protection appears in the features, the electrical table or the text.
- **The 200 mA in the frequency equation isn't explained.** It is not called a ripple, a hysteresis band or a limit anywhere, and the equation has no condition on the input voltage, though it goes negative above VOUT.
- **Output capacitor.** The pin table says a minimum of 10 µF and the text says greater than 10 µF, with a 6.3 V rating. The graphs use 10 µF.
- **The layout text and drawing name the output "OUT"**, while the pin is called VOUT.
- **The quiescent-current test sets VOUT to 3.0 V**, above the 2.8 V setting. We read the figures as the part idle and not switching.
- **Junction temperature.** The absolute maximum table lists "Junction Temperature, Operating" as −40 to 150 °C, while the recommended operating range is −40 to 125 °C. We take 150 °C as the absolute maximum.
- **The revision is the initial release**, Rev 1.0 dated Nov 18, 2025, and the revision history has no other entry.
