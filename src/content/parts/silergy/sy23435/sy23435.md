## How it works

The SY23435 sits on the secondary side of an isolated charger. The converter's own controller regulates the output through the optocoupler, and the SY23435 decides what the target voltage and current limit are, tells the sink what the charger offers, and switches VBUS on and off.

**Detection.** At power-up it puts a 330 µA pull-up current on both CC pins and waits for a sink. A sink is attached when exactly one CC pin stays between 0.8 V and 2.6 V for 150 ms. The load switch then turns on, the pull-up on the CC pin that isn't connected through the cable is switched off, and VBUS rises to 5 V. The controller keeps the 330 µA source on to advertise 3 A to sinks that don't use PD, and moves it to 180 µA once the sink is found to speak PD. If the voltage on the monitored CC pin rises above the detach threshold the load switch turns off and the search starts again.

**Protocol choice.** After the 5 V attach it waits for BC1.2 and PD communication at the same time. PD has priority: if it succeeds, QC is disabled and the output follows the PD requests. Otherwise the part goes to QC, with PD disabled. A fault or removing the device resets it to idle, to look for a new attachment. The PD power objects come from the OTP ROM: fixed PDOs for 5, 9, 12, 15 and 20 V, and PPS ranges up to 3.3–21 V, with the currents set in the OTP (10 mA steps for fixed objects, 50 mA for PPS). QC3.0 follows Table 2 of the datasheet: DP and DN levels select 5, 9 or 12 V, and in continuous mode the sink moves the voltage in 200 mV steps from 3.6 V to 12 V for Class A, or to 20 V for Class B. The SY23435TQQ is a Class A part. Before the QC step, the part holds DP and DN shorted through about 20 Ω for BC1.2, and after the 1.2 s debounce it opens that switch and connects the 19.5 kΩ pull-down on DN, which tells the sink that it supports QC.

**Regulation.** Two loops, constant voltage and constant current, drive one open-drain OPTO output, working like a TL431 shunt regulator but with a pin range of −0.3 to 30 V, so the same part serves a wide range of output voltages. A digital controller sets the reference of each loop through a DAC, which gives the 20 mV and 50 mA steps. The current loop reads the sense resistor with a zero-offset amplifier of gain 43.5, so the sense resistor can be as small as 5 mΩ. A voltage divider is integrated, so VFB needs no external resistors.

**Cable compensation.** As the load rises, the voltage lost in the output cable is made up by lowering the voltage feedback signal. A current proportional to the output current, 43.5 × IOUT × RCS ÷ RCOMP, flows through the internal upper divider resistor (142.8 kΩ in the datasheet's example), so the output rises by IOUT × RCABLE when RCOMP = 43.5 × RCS × RVSENU ÷ RCABLE. RCOMP is one resistor from the Rcable pin to ground.

**Load switch.** The blocking MOSFET and its charge pump are inside. It turns on when a sink is detected and turns off at once, with the gate pulled to ground, on any protection trip, VCC under-voltage or a hard reset. The drain connects to the converter's output on the VDD pins and the source to VBUS.

**Discharge.** When the voltage changes to a lower level, and when the cable is pulled, the output has to get to the new level quickly; the datasheet requires 5 V within 270 ms of a disconnect. An internal current sink of 150 mA, for at least 230 ms, discharges the output.

**Protection.** Over-voltage, under-voltage, short-circuit, over-temperature, CC over-voltage and short, and DP/DN over-voltage and short all react the same way: the load switch turns off, the output is reset to its default voltage, and the protocol state is dropped. When the fault clears the part waits tRESTART (about 4 s for UVP and SCP) and runs a new detection. Over-voltage and under-voltage are disabled during voltage transitions, and each protection has a blanking time to reject switching noise.

**Sleep.** If both CC pins stay above 2.6 V for 1 s (no sink), it goes to sleep and the quiescent current falls to 280 µA. It wakes when either CC pin falls below 2.6 V, but stays above 0.8 V.

### Pull-up resistor of the optocoupler

The datasheet picks RS2 so that the OPTO pin can pull the primary-side COMP voltage to 0 V at the lowest output. The relationship is RS2 ≤ (VOUT_MIN − VF − VOPTO_MIN) ÷ ICOMP_SOURCING × CTR, with VOUT_MIN = 2.8 V, an opto-coupler forward voltage VF = 1 V, a minimum OPTO voltage of 1 V, a current ICOMP_SOURCING of 50 µA (the SY22837 default) and a current-transfer ratio of 0.4. It gives a limit of 6.4 kΩ (the datasheet prints 6.46 kΩ), and 5.1 kΩ is selected.

### Compensation

The CV loop is RS7 = 2.4 kΩ in series with CS6 = 47 nF between OPTO and VFB, and the CC loop is RS9 = 200 Ω in series with CS7 = 6.8 nF between OPTO and IFB. The datasheet calls them suggested values to confirm in system testing. RS5 in series with CS5 sits across RS2; the datasheet shows them in the figure and the BOM but doesn't describe them.

### Cable compensation and current sense

For a 150 mΩ cable and RVSENU = 142.8 kΩ the equation gives RCOMP = 43.5 × 5 mΩ × 142.8 kΩ ÷ 150 mΩ = 207.06 kΩ, and the datasheet says 200 kΩ was chosen. The current-sense resistor must be 5 mΩ, ±1%, for PD compliance, in a Kelvin connection (see Layout). The datasheet asks for 1 to 4.7 µF on both the V3P3V and VCC pins.

## Layout

The datasheet gives these layout rules and a suggested layout (its Figure 12).

- Keep the **switching loops small**: the primary power loop, the secondary power loop and the auxiliary power loop.
- Connect **RS3 with a Kelvin connection**. The sense voltage is very small, so an offset or noise on it spoils the current accuracy. The SY23435 reads the voltage between its CS and GND pins, and a poor connection of these pins gives poor performance.
- Use a **star ground**, with these grounds joined at one point: ① the output capacitor, ② the SY23435 itself, ③ the VCC capacitor, ④ the V3P3V capacitor, ⑤ the Rcable resistor and ⑥ the current-sense resistor.
- Run the **bias supply trace through its capacitor first and then to the VCC pin**, with the capacitor next to the IC. Do the same for the V3P3V supply and the GND pin. (The datasheet calls these capacitors CS7 and CS6; see Datasheet notes.)
- Make the **trace between the power ground and the device signal ground as wide as possible**, to keep the current sampling accurate.

The typical-performance page has six oscilloscope plots of VBUS stepping between 5 V and 9 V, 9 V and 15 V, and 5 V and 20 V, at no load with 20 ms per division. ## Things to watch

- **The performance plots are for a different configuration.** Their test conditions are a 30 W charger with 5 V / 3 A, 9 V / 3 A, 15 V / 2 A and 20 V / 1.5 A, while the only option listed, the SY23435TQQ, provides 5 V, 9 V and two PPS ranges at 25 W and QC3.0 Class A. A part with a 15 V or 20 V object is one of the options to request from Silergy.
- **The PPS current is high for a 25 W supply.** The 3.3–11 V range at 2.75 A reaches 30.25 W at 11 V, against 25 W on the label, and 25 W at 11 V is 2.27 A. The datasheet doesn't say how the charger limits power in that range, so check it against the converter's rating.
- **The Rcable resistor in the figure doesn't match the text.** The calculation gives 207 kΩ and says 200 kΩ was chosen, but the figure and BOM have RS8 = 160 kΩ, which compensates for about 194 mΩ instead of 150 mΩ (582 mV instead of 450 mV at 3 A). It may be deliberate, to include the switch and RS3, but the datasheet doesn't say so.
- **The V3P3V capacitor is 0.1 µF in the BOM and 1–4.7 µF in the text.** The design procedure recommends 1 to 4.7 µF for both V3P3V and VCC, and CS9 is 100 nF / 6.3 V in the BOM and figure, where CS8 on VCC is 1 µF.
- **The sleep voltage leaves no margin to the turn-on threshold.** The design specification has a 3.3 V sleep output, and the VCC turn-on threshold is 3.2 V typical, up to 3.3 V. The recommended minimum for VCC is also 3.3 V. A flyback whose sleep level droops by a few percent would be close to the worst-case threshold.
- **The over-voltage threshold at 20 V is above the recommended VCC range.** 120% of 20 V is 24 V (up to 25.6 V), against a recommended maximum of 21 V and an absolute maximum of 30 V. The datasheet's table gives the thresholds as "% of VCC" without saying that it is the programmed output.
- **The sense resistor is fixed at 5 mΩ ±1%.** The datasheet makes this a PD-compliance requirement, so the resistor's tolerance and temperature coefficient go straight into the current accuracy.
- **The discharge current limits the output capacitance.** 150 mA for 230 ms removes 34.5 mC (27.6 mC at 120 mA), which takes 15 V off at most 2.3 mF (1.8 mF at 120 mA) of output capacitance (calculated, no load). The example has 560 µF.
- **The protection restart takes about four seconds.** After an under-voltage or short-circuit trip the part waits 3.15 to 4.75 s before it tries again.

## Datasheet notes

- **The DP and DN over-voltage rows have swapped names.** The electrical table gives "DN line over-voltage protection" the symbol VOVP_DP and "DP line over-voltage protection" the symbol VOVP_DN. Both are 4.3 to 4.7 V, so the values don't change.
- **The layout rules name the wrong capacitors.** They call the bias supply capacitor CS7 and the LDO supply capacitor CS6, but in the BOM and the figure CS6 and CS7 are the 47 nF and 6.8 nF compensation capacitors, and the supply capacitors are CS8 (VCC) and CS9 (V3P3V).
- **The 2.7 W power dissipation doesn't follow from θJA.** With θJA = 31 °C/W and 25 °C ambient, a 125 °C junction allows 3.2 W and 150 °C allows 4.0 W (calculated here); the datasheet doesn't say what limits it to 2.7 W.
- **The design procedure's RS2 limit is rounded up.** The same numbers give 6.4 kΩ, not 6.46 kΩ; 5.1 kΩ is well below either.
- **The block diagram and text have typing slips.** "Charge pumb" in the block diagram and "contant" in the description.
- **Silergy's product pages list the part as SY23435TQQ (SY52021TQQ) in production.** The product page for SY52021TQQ, in a Chinese-language view, gives the same figures as the datasheet: 3.3 to 21 V, 30 V, 15 mΩ, PD3.1 with PPS and QC2.0/3.0. The datasheet is Rev 1.0, initial release June 5, 2024 (checked November 2026); whether a newer revision exists wasn't checked.
