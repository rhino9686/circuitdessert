## How it works

The SY23565 is an off-line flyback controller with its 1200 V power switch inside the same DIP-7 package. It is meant for inputs that start where ordinary mains supplies stop: the datasheet's range is 85 to 570 Vac, so the DC bus can reach about 806 V. The output voltage is regulated on the secondary side. A shunt regulator on the output drives the LED of an optocoupler, and the optocoupler's phototransistor pulls the COMP pin down. The controller turns that COMP voltage into a peak-current command, and the CS pin ends each on-time when the voltage across the current-sense resistor reaches the command, with a maximum of 1 V.

Silergy calls the control method pseudo fixed frequency. At steady state the switching frequency is a fixed value, about 30 kHz, like a conventional fixed-frequency controller, but the method avoids the sub-harmonic oscillation that normally appears in continuous conduction above 50% duty, so no slope compensation is needed. The frequency is also dithered by ±7% over a 4 ms period, which spreads the EMI peaks. When the load falls, COMP drops below 500 mV and the part enters burst mode, skipping cycles to cut switching loss and no-load power. It leaves burst mode when COMP rises above 560 mV.

Start-up is from the HV pin, which feeds a current source from the bus. The part starts switching when VCC reaches its 21 V turn-on threshold. The datasheet asks for at least a 100 kΩ resistor in series with HV. Once the part is running, the auxiliary winding supplies VCC. If VCC falls below its 9 V turn-off threshold, the part resets and starts again.

Three protections stop switching and restart by themselves. Over-load: if the output can't reach its level because the peak-current limit has been reached, the closed loop drives COMP up to about 2.5 V. When COMP stays above 2 V for 67 ms, the part stops and then retries. A hard short that pulls VCC down before the timer ends is treated the same way. VCC over-voltage: if VCC climbs above 24 V, for example after an optocoupler failure, the part stops and retries. Over-temperature: switching stops at a die temperature of 160 °C and resumes after about 17 °C of cooling.

The datasheet's design example gives numbers for the bus capacitor, transformer, current-sense resistor and output diode. It gives none for the snubber, the VCC network or the feedback network, so those are left as "not given" rather than guessed.

### Bus capacitor and minimum bus voltage

The datasheet sizes the bus capacitor at 1.5 to 2 µF per watt of input power. For 12 W out at 80% efficiency, the input power is 15 W, so 22.5–30 µF; two 47 µF capacitors in series give 23.5 µF. The lowest bus voltage occurs at the lowest line voltage and full load: VBUS,MIN = √(2 × VIN,MIN² − PO × (1 − KCH) ÷ (η × CBUS × f0)). With 85 Vac, KCH = 0.2 and a 50 Hz line, that is 65.1 V.

### Turns ratio and duty cycle

The turns ratio is limited by the stress on the power MOSFET: NPS ≤ (VMOSBR × KDR − √2 × VIN,MAX − ΔVSN) ÷ (VO + VDF). With 1200 V derated to 90%, a 570 Vac peak and 100 V of spike, the limit is 13.9, and the datasheet picks 7. Maximum duty cycle at the minimum bus voltage is DMAX = NPS × (VO + VDF) ÷ (VBUS,MIN + NPS × (VO + VDF)) = 0.57.

### Primary inductance, turns and current

At the minimum bus voltage the datasheet runs the converter at the edge of discontinuous conduction, which it says gives the smallest transformer. The inductance is LM = VBUS,MIN² × DMAX² × η ÷ (2 × VO × IO × fSW × KRP) = 1.53 mH, rounded to 1.5 mH. The primary peak current at rated output is IPK = 2 × VO × IO ÷ (VBUS,MIN × DMAX × η) = 0.8 A. The primary turns follow from a chosen peak flux density: NP = IPK × LM ÷ (BMAX × AE) with BMAX = 0.22 to 0.28 T. The datasheet picks 0.26 T with the EF20 core (33.5 mm²), which gives 137.8 turns, and uses 133. The secondary has NP ÷ NPS = 19 turns, and the auxiliary winding NA = NS × VCC(AUX) ÷ VO, 19 turns for a 12 V VCC.

### Current sense resistor

The maximum peak current at the OCP point is IPK,MAX = VO × IO × KOCP × 2 ÷ (VBUS,MIN × DMAX × η) = 0.97 A with KOCP = 120%. The sense resistor is RCS = VCS,MAX ÷ IPK,MAX = 1.0 V ÷ 0.97 A = 1.03 Ω. The datasheet says to adjust it a little if the measured OCP point comes out higher or lower than the target.

### Secondary diode

The diode's peak reverse voltage is √2 × VIN,MAX ÷ NPS + VO = 127 V, and with the turn-off spike the datasheet recommends a rating above 150 V. Peak forward current is IPK,MAX × NPS = 6.79 A and average current is IO × KOCP = 1.2 A.

> **The design example has a duty cycle of 0.57, but the same datasheet's guide asks for less than 53%.** Equation 7 notes that DMAX "should be lower than 53% due to the max Ton limitation", and the example's own DMAX is above that. A turns ratio of 5 would give a duty cycle of 0.49 (see the table of turns ratios above), at the cost of a lower inductance and a higher peak current. Check this against Silergy before copying the design.

The conduction loss uses the typical RDS(on) at 25 °C. The MOSFET's resistance rises with temperature, so the loss at full load and a hot die will be higher. At θJA = 81 °C/W, 0.65 W alone is a rise of about 53 °C.

## Layout

The datasheet gives these layout rules for the SY23565. It doesn't include a placement drawing.

- Connect the **bridge rectifier output to the bus capacitor first**, then to the switching circuit. This gives better EMI and reduces line-frequency ripple.
- Keep the **switching loops as small as possible**: the primary power loop, the secondary loop and the auxiliary loop.
- Follow the datasheet's **primary ground connection scheme**, which numbers seven ground points: the bus capacitor (①), the bias supply capacitor (②), the auxiliary winding (③), the optocoupler receiver (④), the primary-side Y capacitor (⑤), the current-sense resistor (⑥) and the controller IC (⑦). The diagram shows how they join; check it in the datasheet.
- Connect the **bias supply trace to the bias capacitor first**, then to the GND pin, and put the capacitor right beside the IC.
- Keep the **CS pin – current-sense resistor – GND pin** loop as small as possible.

At these voltages, the creepage and clearance distances between primary and secondary also matter, but the datasheet doesn't say anything about them.

## What the datasheet shows

The typical-performance pages show the drain voltage at 85 Vac and 570 Vac with a 12 V, 1 A load (100 V/div and 250 V/div, 10 µs/div), and captures of over-load protection and short-circuit behaviour at both input voltages. The over-load and short-circuit captures show the output voltage and current at 1 s per division. The last two plots are efficiency against line voltage and against load.

Reading the plots by eye, efficiency peaks at about 84.5% around 230 Vac and falls to about 79% at 85 Vac and about 80.5% at 570 Vac; the numbers are in the two *read by eye* tables above. At 85 Vac, which is also the condition that sets the minimum bus voltage, full-load efficiency is about 79%, a little below the 80% the design example assumes.

## Things to watch

- **The datasheet disagrees with itself on power.** The general description says "up to 8W", while the recommended-power table gives 12 W for 85–570 Vac and the design example is 12 W.
- **The design example's duty cycle breaks the datasheet's own rule.** See the note above: 0.57 against the stated limit of under 53%.
- **The current-limit margin is thin at the low end of the tolerance.** With RCS = 1.03 Ω and VCS,MAX at its minimum of 0.9 V, the limit is 0.87 A, only 8% above the 0.81 A rated peak. Rounding RCS to a stocked value, resistor tolerance and the delay in turning the switch off all move this.
- **The core flux goes above the datasheet's own preset at the current limit.** The datasheet presets BMAX at 0.22–0.28 T for rated output, but with NP = 133 the flux at the top of the CS tolerance is about 0.38 T. The datasheet doesn't name a core material, so check the saturation flux at hot temperature.
- **The efficiency plots don't state their conditions.** The steady-state captures are at 12 V, 1 A, but the efficiency plots don't say whether they are at that output.
- **The datasheet doesn't give values for the snubber, VCC network or feedback network.** These affect the drain spike (the example assumes 100 V), the VCC level at light load and the regulation accuracy, so they need to be designed and tested.
- **The bus capacitors are in series without balancing.** Each carries about half of the up-to-806 V bus, and nothing in the datasheet shows how the voltage is shared.
- **The name differs from Silergy's product list.** The Q3 2026 list calls this part SY50655FIC, while the datasheet and ordering table say SY23565FIC.

## Datasheet notes

- Page footers read "Silergy Corp. Confidential- Prepared for Customer Use Only", and the pages from the design example onward carry the header "AN_SY23565" rather than "SY23565", which probably means the design example was taken from an application note.
- The bus-voltage line in the design example is typeset awkwardly (the capacitance and line frequency come out as "23.5*50 *10^6"). It reproduces the 65.1 V result when read as 23.5 µF and 50 Hz.
- The datasheet is Rev 0.9, "Initial Release", from 2022. The product page for SY50655FIC wasn't checked, so the part's current status and revision are unknown.
