## How it works

The L5150BN is a fixed 5 V linear regulator with a PMOS pass transistor. The block diagram shows a start-up circuit and voltage reference, a power driver that compares its feedback against 1.25 V, a current limiter and thermal shutdown. The reference is pre-trimmed, which is how the datasheet gets ±2% on the output. There is no enable pin and no adjust pin: the part has three signal pins, VS, Vo and ground.

**It changes mode with the load.** The datasheet says the regulator adapts its quiescent current to the output current. Below a threshold the part is in a very-low-consumption mode, about 50 µA typical (the application text says 55 µA, see *Datasheet notes*), and above it the part is in the normal mode with about 3.2 mA of ground current at 150 mA. The mode thresholds are a minimum of 8 mA to be in the normal mode and a maximum of 1.1 mA to be in the very low mode, with 0.8 mA of hysteresis. Between 1.1 and 8 mA the table doesn't say which mode applies, and Figure 6 shows the current against load at light load. The output accuracy follows the mode: ±2% from 8 mA up, and ±5% from 0.1 to 8 mA (4.75 to 5.25 V at 5.6 to 31 V in).

**Dropout.** The pass device is a PMOS transistor, and the dropout is 500 mV maximum at 150 mA. The table gives no typical. The dropout curve (Figure 4) is plotted at 25 °C and 125 °C up to 150 mA. The minimum operating supply of 5.6 V leaves 0.6 V of headroom, which is above that 500 mV maximum (calculated).

**Protection.** The output is current limited, and the short-circuit current is 0.65 to 1.45 A (1.1 A typical) at 13.5 V. The datasheet also lists an "output current limitation" of 0.28 A minimum, 0.6 A typical and 0.85 A maximum, measured at the point where the output has dropped 100 mV. Thermal protection acts between 150 and 190 °C with 10 °C of hysteresis.

### Output capacitor and stability

The pin table requires C0 of 220 nF or more, and the text suggests 220 nF with an ESR of 100 mΩ or more. Figure 15 plots the stability region against Co from 0.22 to 4.7 µF and ESR from 1 mΩ to 100 Ω: the minimum ESR line is about 0.08 Ω at 0.22 µF, falls to about 0.04 Ω near 0.47 µF, and sits at about 0.005 to 0.01 Ω above 1.2 µF (read by eye). The region above the line is marked stable and below it untested. The plot draws no upper ESR limit, so none is stated. The note says a capacitor with a lower ESR than the line must be evaluated case by case, and the line comes from characterization with ceramic capacitors at 100 kHz. Our note: a very-low-ESR ceramic at 220 nF is near the edge of the tested region, so either use a capacitor with some ESR or add a small series resistor.

### Input capacitors

C1 of 100 µF or more is described as necessary to ride out negative pulses on the supply line, and C2 of 220 nF or more handles line disturbances when C1 is far from the pin. The datasheet doesn't give a voltage rating or a type. With a 40 V maximum supply the capacitor ratings and any load-dump clamp are the designer's choice (our note).

### Heat and the largest load

The datasheet gives Rthj-amb = 79 °K/W on a 2 cm² board and plots it against copper area in Figure 18 (about 135 °K/W at the footprint alone, falling to about 79 °K/W at 2 cm², read by eye). The dissipation is (VS − Vo) × Io. At 13.5 V in and 150 mA that is 1.28 W (calculated), a rise of about 101 °C on 79 °K/W, so the full 150 mA can't be used at 85 °C ambient above about 10.5 V in (calculated; see the table). The same dissipation estimate applies to the 3.2 mA of ground current: it adds 43 mW at 13.5 V (calculated).

## Things to watch

- **Heat limits the load at a high supply.** At 14 V in and 85 °C ambient the part can supply about 91 mA, and about 43 mA at 24 V (calculated, 79 °K/W). The 150 mA rating needs a low input.
- **The accuracy is wider at light load.** Below 8 mA the output band is 4.75 to 5.25 V, not 4.9 to 5.1 V.
- **A low-ESR ceramic can leave the tested region.** The ESR line is near 0.08 Ω at 220 nF. The datasheet suggests an ESR of 100 mΩ or more.
- **A 100 µF input capacitor is part of the design.** The datasheet calls C1 necessary for negative pulses on the line.
- **No enable pin.** The output is on whenever the supply is above the minimum, so a switched rail needs a switch ahead of VS.
- **Quiescent current figures depend on the mode.** 50 µA is for 0.1 to 1 mA of load, and the part draws 3.2 mA typical at 150 mA.
- **The status is the datasheet's own statement.** The front page says the product is in full production; no product page was read.
- **The package drawing and land pattern were not read.** This page doesn't use them.
- **The KiCad symbol is ours and not yet opened in KiCad.** It was generated by script and checked against the pin table, but nobody has loaded it in KiCad. The pin types and the symbol layout are our choices. The tab is ground by our reading of the front-page figure, so it has no pin of its own.

## Datasheet notes

- **Quiescent current.** The application text says the quiescent current goes to 55 µA in the low consumption mode. The table gives 50 µA typical and 80 µA maximum at 25 °C, and 95 µA maximum in a second row.
- **A row with no temperature.** The second Iqn_1 row (95 µA maximum) prints the condition "VS = 13.5 V, Io = 0.1 mA to 1 mA," and ends there, with no temperature. The first row says Tj = 25 °C. Our reading is that the second is over the full temperature range.
- **Dropout and current-limit definitions.** The footnotes say the dropout and the current limitation are measured when the output has dropped 100 mV from its value at 13.5 V and Io = 75 mA, while the dropout row's condition says Io = 150 mA.
- **The tab.** The pin table and the text don't say what the tab is connected to; the package figure on page 1 shows the label 2 on both lead 2 and the tab.
- **Table numbering.** The list of tables gives the SOT-223 package mechanical data as Table 8 and the revision history as Table 9, while the headings in the body say Table 7 and Table 8.
- **Thermal board.** The thermal data are quoted for a 2 cm² copper area. Figure 18's axis runs to 2.2 cm² but the curve ends at 2 cm², and Figure 17 shows the two boards as 0.11 cm² and 2 cm² of copper.
- **What was compared.** The general electrical table (page 7) and the stability figure (page 11) were compared with rendered pages. The absolute-maximum and thermal tables were read from the text layer only, so the page stays unverified.
