## How it works

The TPS63020 is a four-switch buck-boost converter. Four N-channel MOSFETs on the die sit two on each side of one external inductor (between the L1 and L2 pins), so it can regulate an output that is above, below or equal to the input. The datasheet aims it at a two- or three-cell alkaline, NiCd or NiMH pack, one Li-ion or Li-polymer cell, or a supercapacitor, any of which can be discharged below 2 V. The TPS63021 is the same part with a fixed 3.3 V output, so this page covers both.

**Buck or boost, depending on the input.** The converter steps down when VIN is above VOUT and steps up when it is below. The datasheet says it always uses one active switch, one rectifying switch, one switch kept on and one kept off, so there is no mode in which all four switch, and the two idle switches cause no switching loss. Close to VIN = VOUT one buck cycle is followed by one boost cycle, and no more than three cycles in a row of the same mode are allowed. A buck-boost overlap control shifts the buck and boost ramps apart or together so that cycles with all four switches active, and cycles with no switching, are kept to a minimum. That keeps the efficiency high at the point where it matters most, with the input close to the output.

**Average current mode.** A fast inner loop regulates the average inductor current, which is rebuilt from the current in the high-side buck switch, and an outer voltage loop from FB sets it. Input and output voltage feed-forward lets the modulator react at once to changes in either. The compensation is internal, so the inductor and output capacitor have to be picked to suit it: the inductor must be at least 1 µH, and Table 1 above lists the capacitor combinations that work.

**Power-save mode and synchronisation.** PS/SYNC low enables power-save mode, which the converter enters when the average inductor current falls below about 100 mA. It then stops, lets the output sag until it reaches a comparator threshold about 2.5% above the nominal output, runs one or more pulses until the output reaches about 3.5% above nominal and the current is below about 100 mA, and repeats. The datasheet calls the result dynamic voltage positioning: at light load the output sits typically about 3% above nominal, which gives headroom for a load step and lets a small output capacitor do the job. The electrical table gives the same regulation as 0.6% to 5% above the PWM value, which for a 3.3 V output is about 3.32 V to 3.46 V (calculated here on the nominal 3.3 V). PS/SYNC high disables power-save mode and forces fixed-frequency PWM at 2.4 MHz typical. A clock on PS/SYNC (2.2 MHz to 2.6 MHz) synchronises the converter through a phase-locked loop that tolerates missing clock pulses and works for clocks below or above the internal one. The input has standard logic thresholds and must not be left open.

**Current limit.** The limit is on the *average* current in the switches, 4 A typical (3.5 A to 4.5 A at VIN = 3.6 V and 25 °C), not on the peak. It is dynamic: it is reduced when VIN falls below 2.3 V, with its lowest value at the minimum supply voltage, where the converter is forced into burst mode to stay alive as long as possible on a weak source. It is also reduced when the die temperature passes the recommended maximum. The datasheet gives no number for either reduction, only the plots of maximum output current below.

**Start-up and short circuit.** After EN goes high the average current limit starts at 400 mA and rises as the output rises, reaching its nominal value at an output of about 1.2 V. If the output doesn't get above 1.2 V the converter assumes a short circuit and keeps the limit low. There is no timer, so the overshoot and the inrush current stay small, and the output ramps in a controlled way even into a large capacitor. During start-up the duty cycle and peak current are also limited.

**Power good.** The datasheet says PG goes to low impedance "as soon as the average inductor current limit is reached". PG therefore watches the current loop, not the output voltage: it is the earliest warning that the output is about to collapse, but it is not a window comparator on VOUT. It is open-drain and can be left open. Pull it up to the logic supply of whatever reads it, within the absolute maximum ratings.

**Protection and grounds.** If FB doesn't track the output (for example a broken feedback connection), the voltage amplifier regulates the output to the overvoltage threshold, 5.5 V minimum and 7 V maximum. Thermal shutdown trips at 140 °C typical and the converter restarts below it, with 20 °C hysteresis. The undervoltage lockout on VINA falls at 1.4 V to 1.6 V with 200 mV hysteresis, and the part restarts when the input recovers to the minimum operating input voltage. In shutdown the converter stops switching, all internal control circuitry is off and the load is disconnected from the input, which means the output voltage can drop below the input voltage. The control ground GND and the power ground PGND must be joined on the board at one point only, ideally close to the GND pin.

## Example application

The datasheet's typical application (Figure 7) takes 2.5 V to 5.5 V in and gives 3.3 V at 1.5 A from a TPS63020, with a 1.5 µH inductor, 2 × 10 µF in and 3 × 22 µF out, a 1 MΩ and 180 kΩ feedback divider, a 100 nF capacitor on VINA, and a 1 MΩ pull-up on PG. ### Output voltage (R1, R2)

R1 = R2 × (VOUT ÷ VFB − 1), with VFB = 500 mV, R1 between VOUT and FB and R2 between FB and ground. The datasheet says R2 "must be kept in the range of 200 kΩ" and uses 180 kΩ in every circuit. The table *Feedback resistors for typical outputs* above checks the datasheet's Table 3 against its own equation (calculated): the 3.3 V, 3.6 V, 4.5 V and 5 V rows land within about 1.5% of their targets, and **the 2.5 V row, 750 kΩ, gives 2.58 V** (the equation asks for 720 kΩ, which isn't a standard value). The divider draws about 3 µA at these values (calculated here).

The datasheet gives no input bias current for FB on the TPS63020, only the TPS63021's 1 MΩ input impedance, so the error that a 1 MΩ-range R1 adds can't be worked out from it. Keep the divider close to the control ground pin, as the layout section says.

For the fixed 3.3 V version, connect FB to VOUT and don't fit the divider.

### Inductor (L1)

The inductor value must be at least 1 µH, to avoid subharmonic oscillation. 1 µH or 1.5 µH is recommended at 1.5 A to 2 A of output current, and 2.2 µH is possible at lower load currents. Table 1 above lists which inductor and output capacitor combinations the datasheet allows. A larger inductor gives less ripple and lower conduction loss but a slower load transient response. Inductor core material matters at 2.4 MHz, and the datasheet warns that small chip inductors lose efficiency mainly to core loss.

For the saturation current the datasheet gives the boost-mode peak at the minimum input voltage, which is the worst case:

- D = (VOUT − VIN) ÷ VOUT (equation 1)
- IPEAK = IOUT ÷ (η × (1 − D)) + VIN × D ÷ (2 × f × L) (equation 2), with η from the efficiency curves or 0.9 as an assumption, and f "typical 2.5 MHz" in the text. The electrical table says 2.4 MHz, which the table on this page uses.

It then recommends an inductor with a saturation current 20% above that. The table *Boost-mode inductor current in the datasheet's two circuits (calculated)* above gives the results for Figure 7 and Figure 28 (calculated). Table 2 lists two 1.5 µH inductors (5.1 A and 5.4 A). **It has no 1 µH part**, although the 2 A circuit uses 1 µH.

### Capacitors (C1, C2, C3)

- **C1 (input).** 10 µF is recommended to improve the line transient behaviour and the EMI, as an X5R or X7R ceramic as close as possible to VIN and PGND. It can be increased without limit. If the supply is more than a few inches away, add bulk capacitance, typically a 47 µF electrolytic or tantalum. The typical circuit uses 2 × 10 µF.
- **C2 (output).** Small ceramic capacitors as close as possible to VOUT and PGND, nominally 3 × 22 µF. There is no minimum ESR and no upper limit on the capacitance, and a larger value gives lower ripple and a smaller drop in a load transient. If large capacitors that can't sit close to the IC are needed, put a smaller ceramic in parallel at the pins. The datasheet's footnote says the effective capacitance can vary by 20% up and 50% down with tolerance and DC bias, which is a large effect for 22 µF at 6.3 V on a 3.3 V output.
- **C3 (VINA).** 0.1 µF between VINA and GND keeps the control supply quiet. It must not be larger than 0.22 µF.

### Worked example: 2.5 V to 3.3 V at 2 A (boost)

This is the 2 A circuit at the bottom of its input range, where the boost current is highest. Everything below is calculated here from the datasheet's equations with η = 0.9, f = 2.4 MHz and L = 1 µH, and nothing was measured.

| Quantity | Value | How |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Duty cycle (boost, ideal) | 0.242 | (3.3 − 2.5) ÷ 3.3 |
| Average input current | 2.93 A | 2 ÷ (0.9 × (1 − 0.242)) |
| Ripple | 0.25 A p-p | 2.5 × 0.242 ÷ (2.4 MHz × 1 µH) |
| IPEAK (equation 2) | 3.06 A | 2.93 + 0.25 ÷ 2 |
| Inductor saturation current to ask for | 3.67 A | IPEAK + 20% |
| Margin to the 3.5 A minimum average limit | 0.57 A (16%) | 3.5 − 2.93 |
| Output power and loss | 6.6 W, 0.73 W | 3.3 × 2; 6.6 ÷ 0.9 − 6.6 |
| Temperature rise, all loss in the device | 31 °C | 0.73 × 41.8 °C/W, so about 116 °C junction at 85 °C ambient |

**The margin is small and the limit is not guaranteed there.** The 3.5 A minimum average limit is specified at VIN = 3.6 V, and the datasheet says the limit is reduced when VIN falls below 2.3 V, so at 2.5 V in the real limit is probably closer to that minimum than to 4 A (our reading). The plot of maximum output current for the TPS63021 at 3.3 V reads about 2.0 A at 2.5 V in (table above, read by eye), so **2 A at 2.5 V in is right at the edge of what the part delivers**. At 3.0 V in the plot reads about 2.4 A of output, a margin of about 20% over the 2 A. That plot is for the TPS63021, the fixed-output version, and we assume the TPS63020 behaves the same at 3.3 V.

**Heat.** At 90% efficiency the loss is under 1 W, and at 85% it is 1.2 W, which on the datasheet's 41.8 °C/W puts the junction at about 134 °C at 85 °C ambient, above the 125 °C recommended limit (table *Heat at 3.3 V out* above). Part of that loss is in the inductor rather than the die, so this overstates the die temperature. Solder the exposed pad to a copper area, as the datasheet says.

## Layout

The datasheet gives these rules and a layout suggestion (Figure 31).

- Use **wide, short traces for the main current path and for the power ground tracks**, because of the high peak currents and the 2.4 MHz switching.
- Place **the input capacitor, output capacitor and inductor as close as possible to the IC**. C1 goes between VIN and PGND and C2 between VOUT and PGND.
- Use **one common ground node for power ground and a separate one for control ground**, and connect the two at one place close to one of the ground pins of the IC.
- Put **the feedback divider as close as possible to the control ground pin**, with short traces for the control ground, separate from the power ground traces, so the power ground current doesn't shift the control ground.
- **Solder the exposed thermal pad**. The datasheet's three ways to improve the thermal performance are better power dissipation in the PCB design, better thermal coupling by soldering the pad, and airflow. The thermal-pad sheet says the pad must be soldered to the board and can go to a copper plane or a heat-sink structure through thermal vias.

The package drawings are on pages 31 to 33 of the PDF (outline DSJ, R-PVSON-N14; thermal pad; land pattern). The body is 3.85 to 4.15 mm by 2.85 to 3.15 mm and 0.80 to 1.00 mm high, with 14 contacts at a 0.50 mm pitch, each 0.30 to 0.50 mm long and 0.18 to 0.30 mm wide. The land-pattern sheet gives 14 pads of 0.24 by 0.60 mm with rounded ends and the two rows 2.8 mm apart, and an exposed pad that is not a plain rectangle: a 2.85 mm long centre block with four 0.20 mm fingers at each end, 4.4 mm long overall and 1.58 mm across. The footprint on the VSON-14 package page uses those numbers, and the sheet's stencil example (0.125 mm, about 81% solder coverage on the centre pad) and its grid of 0.2 mm thermal vias are described there but not drawn into the footprint.

**PGND is the exposed pad.** The pin table gives PGND no pin number and says the exposed pad is connected to PGND, so the power ground of the converter reaches the board only through that pad. The KiCad symbol and footprint number it 15.

Pin 1 is the top-left lead in the datasheet's top view (marked with a circle), pins 1 to 7 run down the left side as VINA, GND, FB, VOUT, VOUT, L2 and L2, and pins 8 to 14 run up the right side as L1, L1, VIN, VIN, EN, PS/SYNC and PG. The pinout figure also labels eight PGND stubs, four at each end of the exposed pad. The thermal-pad drawing is labelled Bottom View, and the outline drawing shows the contacts the same way. Both are turned 90° from the top view, with pin 1 at top left and pins 1 to 7 along the top. The land-pattern drawing is a board view, also turned, with pin 1 at bottom left.

## What the datasheet shows

All plots are typical curves and most state their VIN and VOUT. Efficiency against output current is in Figures 8 to 11, and against input voltage in Figures 12 to 17, all for 10 mA, 500 mA, 1 A and 2 A loads. Reading them by eye:

- **Power-save mode (Figures 8 and 10).** The TPS63020 at VIN = 3.6 V and 2.5 V out sits at about 88% to 91% from 10 mA to 100 mA and reaches about 92% to 93% at 0.5 A to 1 A. At VIN = 1.8 V and 2.5 V out it is about 84% to 85%. The TPS63021 at 3.6 V in is about 94% to 95% over the same range and at 2.4 V in about 88% to 90%. All curves fall off at the highest currents, to about 70% to 80% where they end.
- **Power-save disabled (Figures 9 and 11).** The efficiency at light load is very low, about 2% at 100 µA and about 45% to 55% at 10 mA, and it only reaches about 85% to 93% by 0.5 A, where it meets the power-save curves. Disabling power-save mode costs more the lighter the load.
- **A dip near VIN = VOUT.** In Figure 12 (2.5 V out, power-save mode) the 10 mA curve falls to about 60% for VIN around 2.3 V to 2.6 V, then recovers, which is where the converter is changing between boost and buck.
- **Maximum output current (Figures 1 and 2).** Table above. At 3.3 V out it is about 2 A at 2.5 V in and about 3.2 A at 3.4 V in. At 4.5 V out it is only about 2.0 A at 3 V in and about 2.3 A at 3.6 V in.
- **Regulation and transients.** Load regulation (Figures 18 to 20), load transients (Figures 21 and 22, 500 mA to 1.5 A), line transient (Figure 23, 3.0 V to 3.7 V in at 1.5 A) and start-up from rising enable (Figures 24 to 27, 2.2 Ω load).

The datasheet has no derating curve against ambient temperature, no quiescent current against temperature or voltage plot, and no switching frequency against temperature plot.

## Things to watch

- **The VINA, EN and PS/SYNC node in the figures isn't connected to anything.** In the simplified schematic, Figure 7 and Figure 28 these three pins are joined with C3 to ground and no wire goes to VIN. Rev G (March 2019) removed the VINA-to-VIN connection from all three figures. Built as drawn, that node has no source, so VINA would be unpowered and EN would float. VINA is the control-stage supply, and EN and PS/SYNC must not be left open, so a real circuit needs VINA fed from the input (our reading is from VIN, with C3 as its bypass) and EN driven. Tying PS/SYNC to EN, as the figure appears to, would also hold it high and disable power-save mode (our reading of the pin table).
- **PG is a current-limit flag, not a voltage monitor.** It goes low as soon as the average inductor current limit is reached, so it can be low during a start-up into a heavy load, during a load pulse, or in a weak-input condition, whatever the output voltage is at that moment.
- **EN and PS/SYNC must not be left open.** Both pin descriptions say so. EN high is 1.2 V minimum and low 0.4 V maximum.
- **The 2 A headline needs the input above 2.5 V.** The feature list gives 2 A for VIN above 2.5 V and 3.3 V out. At 4.5 V out the plot reads only about 2.0 A at 3 V in, and below 2.3 V on VIN the current limit is reduced (see the table of maximum output current). The description says "up to 3 A" and section 8.1 says 2 A in boost mode and 4 A in buck mode, none of which is a rating at a stated condition.
- **The average limit is not a peak limit.** The inductor still sees the ripple on top, so choose the saturation current from equation 2 plus 20%, not from 4 A.
- **Start-up needs a higher input than operation.** The minimum input voltage for start-up is 1.8 V typical and up to 1.9 V (0–85 °C) or 2.0 V maximum, against a 1.8 V minimum operating input. The undervoltage lockout (falling) is 1.4 V to 1.6 V. A source that has sagged to about 1.9 V to 2.0 V may keep a running converter on (it stays on until VINA falls to 1.4 V to 1.6 V) but may not start one (our reading).
- **The output sits above nominal at light load.** In power-save mode the regulation is 0.6% to 5% above the PWM value, so a 3.3 V rail can reach about 3.46 V. Check the load against that, or hold PS/SYNC high.
- **Don't set the output near 5.5 V.** The output range ends at 5.5 V, but the output overvoltage protection starts at 5.5 V minimum, and the power-save mode raises the output by up to 5%.
- **The 2.5 V row of Table 3 gives 2.58 V.** Use 720 kΩ with 180 kΩ (or the nearest value) for 2.5 V.
- **The inductor table has no 1 µH part.** Table 2 lists 1.5 µH parts only, and the 2 A circuit uses 1 µH.
- **Capacitor derating is large.** 22 µF 6.3 V ceramics lose a lot of capacitance at 3.3 V, and the datasheet allows the effective output capacitance to be 50% below nominal.
- **Heat.** At 3.3 V and 2 A the table above gives about 116 °C junction at 85 °C ambient with 90% efficiency and about 134 °C with 85%, past the 125 °C recommended limit. The 41.8 °C/W is a standard thermal-metric value. Your board will differ.
- **The temperature range reads two ways.** The description and the recommended operating conditions give −40 to 85 °C free-air (junction −40 to 125 °C). The packaging addendum lists −40 to 125 °C. Our reading is that the addendum gives the junction range, but the datasheet doesn't say.
- **Two grounds.** GND is the control ground, PGND the power ground and the exposed pad, and they must be joined at one point only, near GND.

## Datasheet notes

- **Switching frequency.** The electrical table gives 2200 / 2400 / 2600 kHz, and the text under equation 2 says "typical 2.5 MHz".
- **Output current.** The feature list says 2 A (VIN above 2.5 V, 3.3 V out), the description says "up to 3 A", and section 8.1 says "as high as 2 A in boost mode and as high as 4 A in buck mode".
- **Inductor part number.** Table 2 gives XFL4020-152ME and Table 4 gives XFL4020-152ML for the same 1.5 µH Coilcraft inductor.
- **Capacitor part number.** Table 4 prints the 22 µF capacitor as GRM188R60J226MEAOL, with the letter O where a zero would be expected (our reading).
- **Table 5 and the figure captions disagree in places.** Table 5 lists Figure 19 as load regulation in PWM buck operation, but its caption says power save enabled, while the plot's own label says power save disabled. Table 5 lists Figures 24 to 27 as start-up of the TPS63021, but their captions and labels say TPS63020. Table 5's entries for Figures 14 and 15 both list 2.5 V out and PS/SYNC low, although Figure 15 is the 4.5 V plot and both are power-save-disabled plots, which would mean PS/SYNC high.
- **Start-up voltage rows.** The table prints two rows for the minimum start-up input voltage, 1.5 / 1.8 / 1.9 V at 0–85 °C and 1.5 / 1.8 / 2.0 V with no condition.
- **Line and load regulation.** Both rows say "maximum" and print 0.5% in the TYP column.
- **Tolerance signs.** The table footnotes say the inductance "can vary by 20% and –30%" and the capacitance "by 20% and –50%"; the plus signs have dropped out of the PDF, so we read them as +20%.
- **PGND has no pin number** in the pin table, while the pinout figure shows four PGND stubs at each end of the pad.
- **Revision label.** The header reads SLVS916I, July 2010, revised October 2019. The PDF we used was printed in April 2026 and its packaging addendum is dated 10 Nov 2025, so the technical content is Rev. I with newer ordering information appended.
