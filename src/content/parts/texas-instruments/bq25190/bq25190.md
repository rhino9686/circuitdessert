## How it works

The BQ25190 is a battery management unit built around a linear charger with a power path. The datasheet names three FETs between the input and the battery: an input reverse-blocking FET (Q1), a second FET (Q2) that it calls the LDO converter FET, and the BATFET (Q3) between SYS and the battery. The input side blocks reverse current from SYS to IN and regulates the input current. The buck, buck-boost and LDOs take their power from SYS, so they run from the adapter or the battery without a break when the adapter comes or goes.

**Power path.** SYS is the system rail. With a valid input (above the undervoltage lockout, above BAT by the sleep-exit threshold, below the overvoltage threshold) the device regulates SYS to a target set in SYS_REG: battery tracking (225 mV above the battery, 3.8 V minimum), a fixed 4.4 to 4.9 V, or pass-through where SYS follows IN. The input current limit, ILIM, and the input voltage threshold, VINDPM, protect a weak adapter. If the system load is large, dynamic power path management (DPPM) lowers the charge current first. If the load still exceeds what the input gives, the BATFET turns on in supplement mode and the battery helps. With no input and a battery above its undervoltage lockout, SYS switches to the battery.

**Charging.** Charge current (5 mA to 1 A), battery regulation voltage (3.5 to 4.65 V), the precharge and termination currents, the recharge threshold and the safety timer are all I2C settings. Below about 1.8 V on BAT the charger trickles at 8 mA or 1 mA; precharge runs up to a VLOWV threshold of 3.0 V or 2.8 V; then fast charge at the set current, constant voltage at the regulation voltage, and termination. The default termination current is 20% of the fast-charge current and the default precharge equals the termination current. Several loops can limit the current (constant current, constant voltage, input current limit, DPPM and VINDPM); the one that is dominant takes control. The precharge safety timer is 25% of the fast-charge timer. The TMR2X_EN bit, on at reset, doubles the fast-charge timer whenever a loop other than constant current or constant voltage limits the current, so a heavy system load doesn't trip the timer.

**Battery temperature.** A 10 kΩ NTC on TS, biased by the part, is read against four programmable thresholds (cold, cool, warm, hot), which makes a full JEITA profile possible. Hot and cold suspend charging. Cool reduces the charge current and warm reduces the regulation voltage, each to a programmed value. The cool and warm thresholds are off at reset, so the default is a plain hot and cold window. The TS pin wants a 10 kΩ NTC in parallel with a 10 kΩ resistor; if you don't use TS, tie a 5 kΩ resistor from TS to ground. TS faults are also reported while running from the battery.

**Ship mode.** The lowest-power state, with the BATFET off, drawing 15 nA from the battery. The host enters it by writing the ship-mode bits and the part waits 1 second and then for the input to go away; a long press on MR can do the same. Adapter insertion wakes it (VIN above the undervoltage exit for at least 30 ms), as does holding MR low for tSHIPWAKE with the battery above its undervoltage lockout. LDO1 can be set to stay on through ship mode.

**MR.** An internally pulled-up push-button input. Presses shorter than the programmable tWAKE1 and tWAKE2 durations raise interrupts to the host; a long press (tLPRESS, with a warning interrupt before it) can enter ship mode, cause a hardware reset (SYS shut off and restored), or be ignored.

**Rails and sequencing.** The buck (0.4 to 3.6 V, DVS by I2C or by GPIO VSEL pins), buck-boost (1.7 to 5.2 V) and two LDOs (0.8 to 3.6 V, or bypass switches) can be enabled individually or put into a programmable power-up and power-down sequence, and the GPIOs can drive external rails in that sequence. The reset defaults are 1.8 V on the buck, 4.8 V on the buck-boost, 1.8 V on LDO1 and 3.3 V on LDO2, the same as the datasheet's design requirements.

**ADC.** A 12-bit ADC, off at reset to save power, measures battery voltage, battery current, TS, ADCIN, input voltage, SYS voltage, input current and die temperature, in continuous, one-shot, every-second or every-minute modes, with optional averaging. Three programmable comparators raise interrupts on thresholds. ADCIN accepts an external signal up to 5 V, or can be biased to read a second NTC.

**Protection and the host.** The watchdog is enabled at reset and resets selected registers (or the whole part, by setting) if the host stops talking for 160 s or 40 s. INT is an open-drain output that sends a 128 µs low pulse for each unmasked event. The device is addressed at 0x6C and supports standard, fast and fast-mode-plus I2C with a 1.2 to 5 V pull-up; reads outside the register map return 0xFF.

## Example application

Figure 8-1 of the datasheet shows the part charging a single-cell battery from a 5 V supply and powering four rails. The design requirements (Table 8-1) are a 5 V input, a 4.5 V system voltage, a 4.2 V battery voltage, 1.8 V from the buck, 4.8 V from the buck-boost, 1.8 V from LDO1 and 3.3 V from LDO2. Those are all the reset values, so nothing needs writing for this circuit to start up with these voltages.

### Worked example: charging from 5 V at the reset defaults

1. **Rails.** With no I2C writes, SYS regulates to 4.5 V from the 5 V input (the input must stay above that or SYS drops out with it), the battery to 4.2 V, and the buck, buck-boost and LDOs come up at 1.8 V, 4.8 V, 1.8 V and 3.3 V when enabled by the power sequence or their enable bits.
2. **Current.** The reset charge current code gives 10 mA (calculated from the register formula), so a real charger needs ICHG written; the input current limit resets to 475 mA. For a 500 mA fast charge, the register text gives 40 + (code − 31) × 10 mA above 35 mA, so code 77 (4Dh) is 500 mA (calculated). Termination at the reset setting is 20% of that, 100 mA.
3. **Heat.** A linear charger dissipates the drop from SYS to the battery times the charge current. At 500 mA from 5 V into a battery at 3.2 V (just above the 3.0 V precharge threshold), the drop is 1.8 V, or about 0.9 W (calculated), dissipated between the input FETs and the BATFET. With the JEDEC RθJA of 59.4 °C/W that is about a 53 °C rise. The thermal regulation threshold is 100 °C at reset and can be lowered to 80 or 60 °C.
4. **Load sharing.** If the system load takes more than the input limit allows, the part first cuts the charge current (DPPM), then supplements from the battery. The timer runs at half speed during that.

## Layout

From section 10.1. Put the capacitors from IN to ground and from the SYS balls A5 and F5 to ground close to the IC with wide, short traces; the A5 and F5 capacitors are required. Place the output capacitors on BBOUT, SYS, BAT, LSLDO1 and LSLDO2 close to the IC, too. Join all SYS balls with a wide trace. The second layer should be a ground plane with vias on every ground connection, especially BKGND, BBGND and the capacitor grounds, and the ground plane shouldn't be cut. Route the buck's BKOUT feedback from the output capacitor on its own trace with no current in it, away from the switch nodes; it is a sensitive high-impedance line. Size the high-current traces for the maximum charge current. The datasheet's layout example (Figure 10-1) is a top view of the ball area.

## Things to watch

- **The charger is off the shelf at only 10 mA.** The ICHG reset code gives 10 mA, so host software must set the charge current. The watchdog also resets some registers (including ICHG and the safety timer) if it expires, so keep talking to the part or disable the watchdog.
- **I2C pull-ups are needed.** The pin table asks for 10 kΩ on SDA and SCL and 1 to 20 kΩ on /INT. The figure shows them going to BKOUT, a rail that only exists after the buck is enabled, so the host's I2C bus needs its own supply if you want to talk to the part before the buck is up.
- **Three SYS capacitors.** Table 8-2 and the layout guidelines call for capacitors on SYS balls A5 (4.7 µF) and F5 (10 µF) as well as D5 (10 µF); Figure 8-1 draws one 10 µF. Fit all three.
- **Pass-through and the overvoltage setting are tied together.** SYS can't be set to pass-through with the 18.5 V input overvoltage setting (the reset default), and the overvoltage setting can't be raised while SYS is in pass-through.
- **Ship-mode wake needs a stable input.** Wake by adapter insertion needs VIN above the undervoltage exit for at least 30 ms.
- **The rails have a lot of options.** The sequence, enable and GPIO modes interact (a rail in the sequence can't be enabled on its own); read section 7.3.17 before changing them.
- **Wafer-level package.** A 0.4 mm ball pitch means vias or fine traces from the inner balls. See the package page.
- **The KiCad symbol is ours and not yet opened in KiCad.** It was generated by script and checked against the pin table, but nobody has loaded it in KiCad. The pin types, the pin order and the layout are our choices.

## Datasheet notes

- **Revision.** SLUSF40A, Rev. A (December 2024); the page footers say October 2024, revised December 2024.
- **SYS capacitors.** Figure 8-1 draws one SYS capacitor of 10 µF. Table 8-2 lists three, CSYS_D5, CSYS_A5 and CSYS_F5, and section 8.2.2.2 says the total effective capacitance on all SYS pins must be between 1 and 100 µF.
- **Buck-boost capacitor name.** Section 8.2.2.2 calls the buck-boost output capacitor CBKOUT; Table 8-2 calls it CBBOUT.
- **Input current limit values.** The ILIM text speaks of 100 mA and 500 mA settings, while the electrical characteristics list 90, 475 and 1050 mA.
- **TS resistor.** The pin table says to use a 10 kΩ NTC "in parallel to" a 10 kΩ resistor, and section 7.3.16.1 repeats that with a 3380 B-constant NTC.
- **Termination accuracy.** The electrical table's test condition for termination accuracy is ITERM = 30 mA at 10% of ICHG, while the register default termination is 20% of ICHG.
- **Pin-table check.** Eight rows of Table 5-1 wrap across lines in the text layer; they were compared with the rendered page and agree with Figure 5-1.
