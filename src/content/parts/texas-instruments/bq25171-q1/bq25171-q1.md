## How it works

The BQ25171-Q1 is a linear charger: one pass element between IN and OUT, with the battery on OUT. The system load can sit in parallel with the battery as long as the average load doesn't stop the battery from charging fully within the safety timer; input current is then shared between the two.

**Pin-strapped settings.** Three resistors to ground configure the charger, and the settings are read and latched at power-up (a change is noticed only after the input or CE is toggled). RISET on ISET sets the fast-charge current, ICHG = KISET ÷ RISET. RVSET on VSET sets the charge voltage (Li+) or the NiMH cell count. RCHM_TMR on CHM_TMR sets the chemistry and the safety timer. A short or open pin stops the charger and leaves it in a fault until the input or CE is toggled. For charge currents below 50 mA the datasheet recommends an extra RC on ISET for a steadier signal.

**Lithium charge cycle.** Precharge at 20% of ICHG until OUT passes the per-cell threshold (2.8 V for Li-ion, 2.0 V for LiFePO4), fast charge at ICHG, then constant voltage at the set VREG until the current falls to 10% of ICHG, which ends the cycle. A recharge starts if OUT falls 100 mV (Li-ion) or 200 mV (LiFePO4) per cell below the charge voltage. If OUT is below the short-circuit threshold the charge current is about 16 mA.

**NiMH charge cycle.** The charger works in constant-current mode and ends the cycle when the programmed timer expires or the battery voltage passes the overvoltage threshold. An optional intermittent-charge phase recharges a full battery for 25% of the original timer when the voltage falls below the recharge threshold.

**Safety timer.** The precharge timer is fixed at 30 minutes; the fast-charge timer is set by CHM_TMR or disabled. In thermal regulation the timer runs at half speed, so a 10 hour timer takes 20 hours if the charger is regulating the whole time. Faults that stop charging suspend the timer, and toggling CE restarts it.

**Temperature.** A 10 kΩ NTC (the datasheet recommends a 103AT-2) from TS to ground gates charging between VCOLD and VHOT, typically 0 °C to 45 °C; outside that window the charger goes to standby and signals a recoverable fault. If no sensing is needed, a fixed 10 kΩ resistor from TS to ground allows normal operation. Resistors in series and parallel with the thermistor (the datasheet's Table 7-3) move the window to 0–60 °C, −10–60 °C or −10–50 °C. Separately, if the die reaches 125 °C the part reduces the charge current (thermal regulation), and at 150 °C it shuts down.

**Status outputs.** STAT1 and STAT2 are open drain and can drive an LED; the datasheet says to connect each to a pull-up rail through 10 kΩ. Table 7-4 below lists the states.

**Protection.** Input overvoltage at about 18.4 V, output overvoltage at 104% of the charge voltage, an output current limit near 1 A, thermal regulation at 125 °C and thermal shutdown at 150 °C. IN is rated to 40 V so the part survives load dumps while idle. Both IN and OUT need at least 1 µF.

### Worked example: 5 V in, one LiFePO4 cell, 500 mA

1. **Current.** 300 AΩ ÷ 600 Ω = 500 mA, with precharge 100 mA and termination 50 mA. A 604 Ω resistor gives about 497 mA (calculated). The datasheet's ±10% accuracy puts the real fast-charge current between 450 and 550 mA with a 600 Ω resistor.
2. **Voltage and timer.** 82 kΩ on VSET gives 3.60 V and 100 kΩ on CHM_TMR gives the 5 hour timer, both from the tables.
3. **Heat.** A linear charger dissipates the input-to-battery drop times the current. At 500 mA and 5 V, a cell at 3.6 V costs (5 − 3.6) × 0.5 = 0.7 W, and one just above the 2.0 V fast-charge threshold costs (5 − 2.0) × 0.5 = 1.5 W (calculated). With the JEDEC RθJA of 60.3 °C/W those are 42 °C and 90 °C rises; at 1.5 W the die would reach the 125 °C thermal-regulation point at an ambient of about 35 °C. The datasheet's own EVM figure is 37 °C/W, which gives 56 °C at 1.5 W. If the die gets that hot the charger lowers its current and the safety timer runs at half speed.

## Layout

Place the IN and OUT capacitors as close to the device as possible with short traces. Keep low-current ground connections separate from the high-current battery charge path and join them at a single point, and size the traces for IN and OUT for the maximum charge current. Solder the thermal pad to the board with vias. This site has no KiCad symbol or footprint for this part yet. Pin 1 is IN.

## Things to watch

- **Settings are latched at power-up.** Changing a resistor does nothing until the input or CE is toggled, and a short or open pin latches a fault.
- **Heat sets the real charge rate.** A big drop at 500 mA to 800 mA reaches 125 °C and the current is cut back, which doubles the effective timer.
- **Timer and a loaded battery.** If the system load takes current from the battery's rail, the charge may not finish within the safety timer, and a Li+ timer expiry is a non-recoverable fault.
- **Termination.** The cycle ends when the current falls to 10% of ICHG, so a load on OUT can keep the current above it.
- **Status table and fault text disagree.** Table 7-4 says non-recoverable faults show STAT1 low and STAT2 low; the Fault Mode text lists the same faults with STAT1 low and STAT2 high.
- **TS cannot be left open.** Without a thermistor, use a fixed 10 kΩ from TS to ground.
- **NiMH settings share the VSET table.** The same resistor means a different thing under a Li+ or a NiMH CHM_TMR setting.
- **Input range.** Charging starts at about 3.09 V and needs IN above OUT by the sleep-exit threshold (135 mV typical).
- **The name.** The datasheet is for the -Q1 part; the user's list said BQ25171.
