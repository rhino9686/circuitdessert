## How it works

The TPS745-Q1 is a family of 500 mA low-dropout regulators with a PMOS pass transistor, a precision band-gap and error amplifier, and a power-good output that monitors the voltage at the feedback node. The TPS74512 is the fixed 1.2 V member with an open-drain power-good and a 150 µs delay, and it has active output discharge like every member of the family. Features of the whole family are input undervoltage lockout, a foldback current limit and thermal shutdown.

**Dropout and headroom.** The dropout limit depends on the output voltage band. For 1.2 V to below 1.5 V out it is 285 mV typical and 430 mV maximum at 500 mA, measured with the output at 95% of nominal. The minimum input is therefore the larger of 1.5 V and about 1.63 V at full load in the worst case (1.2 V plus 430 mV, calculated). The rising undervoltage lockout is 1.21 to 1.47 V with 40 mV of hysteresis.

**Enable and discharge.** EN is active high, with a 1.0 V minimum high level and a 0.3 V maximum low level, and can be tied to IN if shutdown is not needed. When the part is disabled, or the input is below the lockout, an internal MOSFET connects the output to ground through 95 Ω typical. The discharge time constant is (RPULLDOWN × RL ÷ (RPULLDOWN + RL)) × COUT, so about 209 µs for 2.2 µF with no load (calculated), and about five of those, near 1 ms, to empty it. PG is pulled low when the part is disabled.

**Power-good.** PG monitors the output through the feedback node. It goes high-impedance when the output rises above 92% of nominal (89 to 96%), after a delay of 165 µs typical, and is pulled low when the output falls below 90% (86 to 93%), within 7 µs typical. The pull-up resistor is required for this open-drain version, with 10 to 100 kΩ recommended. The datasheet says the state of PG is valid only while the input is above its minimum, and below about 0.8 V in there is not enough gate drive to keep the open-drain device on; pulling PG up to the output rather than to an outside rail helps with that.

**Current limit.** The limit is a hybrid brick-wall and foldback scheme. Above the foldback voltage (0.4 × VOUT(NOM), 0.48 V here) the current is limited to 515 to 865 mA (720 mA typical). Below it the limit folds back toward the short-circuit current of 200 to 400 mA (350 mA typical). The output is not regulated in the limit, and if thermal shutdown trips the part restarts when it has cooled, so a persistent fault makes it cycle.

**Start-up.** The output reaches 95% of nominal 200 to 650 µs after EN rises, 500 µs typical. If EN is already high when the input is below the lockout's rising level, the input must be able to sink 1 mA, or a floating input could turn the part on (section 8.1.9).

### Capacitors

The datasheet says the output needs 0.47 µF or more of derated capacitance for stability, and that X5R and X7R ceramics keep their value best over temperature. The recommended range is 1 to 220 µF. An input capacitor is not required for stability, but it helps with a high-impedance supply and with transient response, ripple and PSRR, and the recommended value is 1 µF or larger. The datasheet's design example picks 2.2 µF for both as a compromise of size and cost.

### Heat and the largest load

The datasheet's equations are PD = (IGND + IOUT) × (VIN − VOUT) and TJ = RθJA × PD + TA. Its example is 1.8 V out from 3.3 V at 300 mA on the WSON-6, which gives a maximum ambient of 113.86 °C. For this 1.2 V part at 500 mA and 85 °C ambient, the first design table above shows that the WSON-6 reaches the 150 °C junction limit between 2.5 and 3.3 V in, and the VSON-8 near 3.3 V in. The datasheet's thermal resistances are for its test board and a real one with less copper will be worse.

## Layout

The datasheet's layout guidelines (section 10):

- **Put the input and output capacitors as close to the device as possible.**
- **Use copper planes for the device connections** to help with heat.
- **Put thermal vias around the device,** and only tented vias directly under the thermal pad of the DRV or DRB package, because an untented via can wick solder or paste away from the pad joint.
- The layout examples (Figures 10-1 and 10-2) put COUT and CIN beside the device, the pull-up beside PG, and a signal via from pin 1 to the plane.

**Packages.** The DRV is a 6-pin wettable-flank WSON of 2 × 2 mm, and the DRB is an 8-pin wettable-flank VSON of 3 × 3 mm; the text uses WSON for the first and VSON for the second (the addendum lists the second as SON). The datasheet's drawings are near the end of the PDF; they were not used for this page.

Pin 1 is OUT on both packages. In the datasheet's top view the pins run down the left side from pin 1 and up the right side, ending at IN (pin 6 on the DRV, pin 8 on the DRB), with the thermal pad in the centre. The KiCad symbol is ours, built from the pin table, and its Footprint field is empty: no footprint on this site has been compared with this part's package drawing.

## Things to watch

- **Heat limits the input at full load.** At 500 mA and 85 °C ambient, a 3.3 V input already puts the WSON-6 past 150 °C (calculated). Use a low input or a lower load, or the VSON-8.
- **The dropout is the 1.2 V row, not the 160 mV in the feature list.** The feature list quotes the 3.3 V figure; at 1.2 V out the limit is 430 mV at 500 mA.
- **Noise is given for 0.9 V out only.** The 53 µVRMS figure is not for this 1.2 V part, and the PSRR table is for 1.8 V out.
- **PG needs a pull-up.** The open-drain output has no high level without one, and below about 0.8 V of input it can't be held high at all.
- **PG does not tell you the output is regulated at a low input.** Its state is valid only above the minimum input voltage.
- **A floating input can turn the part on.** If EN is above the rising lockout level, IN must sink 1 mA.
- **The output is actively discharged.** A backup supply or a second source on the same rail will be pulled down by the 95 Ω switch when this part is disabled.
- **Foldback limits restart into a heavy load.** The limit falls to 200–400 mA below 0.48 V, so a load that behaves like a resistor can fail to start up if it needs more than that at low voltage (our reading).
- **NC pins.** The VSON-8 has three NC pins; ground them for better thermal performance as the datasheet says.
- **The family has a lookalike.** TPS745125 is the 1.25 V part, one extra digit.
- **The KiCad symbol is ours and not yet opened in KiCad.** It was generated by script and checked against the pin table, but nobody has loaded it in KiCad. The pin types and the symbol layout are our choices. The exposed pad's number is ours, because the pin table does not number it. ## Datasheet notes

- **Shutdown current in the feature list.** The features give 1.5 µA for shutdown. The table gives 0.1 µA typical with maximums of 1 µA (to 125 °C) and 1.55 µA (to 150 °C).
- **Package names.** The text calls the 8-pin package VSON throughout (changed from WSON in Rev. C), and the packaging addendum lists it as SON (DRB).
- **Push-pull note.** The sentence that the push-pull option is supported only for VOUT of 1.0 V or more appears twice in a row in section 8.1.7.
- **Test conditions.** The electrical table uses VIN = VOUT(NOM) + 0.5 V or 1.5 V, whichever is greater, and CIN = COUT = 1 µF. The PSRR rows use 2.2 µF and a 1.8 V output, and the noise row a 0.9 V output.
- **Pin function of NC.** The pin table says to ground NC for better thermal performance, and the fixed DRB version has three NC pins (2, 3 and 7).
- **Orderable entries.** The addendum lists each part number and a copy ending ".A", both active. The WSON-6 entry for TPS74512 has lead finish SN and MSL 1, the VSON-8 entry NIPDAU and MSL 2.
- **What was compared.** The electrical characteristics and thermal table (page 5) and the front-page circuit were compared with rendered pages. The timing table, absolute maximums and the other text were read from the text layer only, so the page stays unverified.
