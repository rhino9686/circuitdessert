## How it works

The TPS7A8101-Q1 is an adjustable low-dropout linear regulator with a PMOS pass transistor, made in a BiCMOS process. An error amplifier compares the FB/SNS pin with a 0.8 V internal reference and drives the pass device between IN and OUT, so the output is VO = 0.8 V × (R1 + R2) ÷ R2. It takes 2.2 to 6.5 V in and delivers up to 1 A at 0.8 to 6 V out, over an ambient range of −40 to 125 °C.

**What sets it apart is the noise and the ripple rejection.** The reference is filtered by a capacitor on the NR pin. At start-up a quick-start switch connects the bandgap to NR through about 33 kΩ, so the capacitor charges fast. About 100 ms after any enable event the switch opens, and the resistance rises to about 250 kΩ, which makes a low-pass filter for the reference noise. A second capacitor across R1, called C(BYPASS) in the datasheet, reduces the noise from the divider and the error amplifier. With both at 470 nF the datasheet gives 23.5 µVRMS from 100 Hz to 100 kHz. Without the capacitors the figures are lower; see the *Things to watch* section.

**Dropout.** In dropout the pass transistor works as a resistor, so the dropout voltage grows in proportion to the load current. The datasheet limits it to 250 mV at 500 mA, 350 mV at 750 mA and 500 mV at 1 A, which is about 0.5 Ω at most (our arithmetic). Those are maximums, and the table has no typical values. The plots show much lower typical numbers: the 25 °C curve at 1 A sits at about 175 mV for inputs from 5 to 6.5 V, and rises to about 280 mV at 2.5 V in (read by eye, Figure 5). As in any linear regulator, ripple rejection and transient response get worse as the headroom (VI − VO) shrinks towards the dropout.

**No minimum load.** The part is stable with no load. The datasheet says a low-current mode raises the loop gain at very light loads.

**Protection.** The current limit is 1.1 A minimum, 1.4 A typical and 2 A maximum, measured with the output at 85% of its nominal value and VI at 3.3 V or more. The datasheet says the output then sources a fixed current largely independent of the output voltage, and that the part shouldn't stay in the limit for long; it describes no foldback. Thermal shutdown trips at about 160 °C and releases at about 140 °C, so a part that is too hot cycles on and off. The PMOS pass element has a body diode that conducts when OUT is above IN, and that current is not limited.

**Enable and undervoltage lockout.** EN is active high. The part turns on above 1.2 V (for VI from 2.2 to 3.6 V) or 1.35 V (VI from 3.6 to 6.5 V) and shuts down below 0.4 V. The datasheet says EN must not be left floating, and that it can be tied to IN if shutdown is not needed. The input undervoltage lockout rises at 2 V typical (1.86 to 2.1 V) with 75 mV of hysteresis, and ignores input dips shorter than about 50 µs.

**Other versions.** The datasheet's packaging addendum lists a catalog (non-automotive) TPS7A8101 as another qualified version of this part. We have no datasheet for it, so this page covers only the TPS7A8101-Q1.

### Output voltage (R1, R2)

VO = 0.8 V × (R1 + R2) ÷ R2, so R1 = R2 × (VO ÷ 0.8 V − 1). Table 1 of the datasheet uses E96 resistors with R2 fixed at 10 kΩ, and the text says every row is within 1% of its target if the resistors have no error. The check in the third design table above agrees: the rows land between 0.85% below and 0.64% above their targets, and the largest errors are at 2.5 V (2.480 V) and 3.3 V (3.272 V). For 0.8 V, R1 is a short and the FB/SNS pin goes to the output.

**The divider draws 80 µA.** With R2 = 10 kΩ the divider current is 0.8 V ÷ 10 kΩ = 80 µA at every output voltage, more than the 60 µA typical ground-pin current at 1 mA load (our arithmetic). The datasheet says lower values of R1 and R2 reduce the noise injected into the FB pin, so the table's low values are a choice for noise, not for light-load efficiency. If the 80 µA matters, scale both resistors up and accept more noise. The feedback pin current (0.02 µA typical, 1 µA maximum) then shifts the output by that current times R1 in parallel with R2: 7.6 mV at the maximum with the 3.3 V row, about 1% of the reference (calculated).

**Tolerance.** The datasheet's output accuracy of ±3% (±2% over a narrower range) does not include the resistors. The reference itself is listed as 0.79 to 0.81 V. For the 3.3 V row, 1% resistors give 3.183 to 3.363 V over the reference range (calculated). Adding the ±3% accuracy, the ±1% resistors and the 0.85% offset of the table value linearly puts the output between about 3.13 and 3.42 V in the worst case, which is −5.3% and +3.7% against 3.3 V (our arithmetic, a limit and not a probability).

**C(BYPASS).** The capacitor across R1 shunts the divider's noise above 1/(2π × R1 × C), which is 11 Hz for 30.9 kΩ and 470 nF (calculated), well below the 100 Hz to 100 kHz noise band of the datasheet.

### Noise reduction and start-up (CNR, CBYPASS)

The datasheet gives the start-up time as tst = 170 000 × C(NR), with tst in seconds and C(NR) in farads. For the recommended 470 nF that is about 80 ms, which matches the 80 ms in the electrical table. The start-up table above gives other values. The output ramp follows the reference ramp, so the output takes about 80 ms to reach its final value after the enable event (the note under Figure 43). That slows the inrush, and it is also a delay a sequenced system has to wait for.

**The limit is about 0.59 µF.** The quick-start switch opens about 100 ms after the enable event. A capacitor above about 0.59 µF (100 ms ÷ 170 kΩ, calculated) isn't fully charged by then and finishes through the higher resistance, so the start-up time becomes "extremely long" in the datasheet's words and Equation 1 no longer applies. The datasheet also says the noise reduction is almost saturated at 470 nF.

**What the caps buy.** With 100 nF on both NR and the bypass position the noise is 24.09 µVRMS, against 23.54 µVRMS with 470 nF on both (Figure 21, repeated as Figure 35; VI − VO = 0.5 V, 3.3 V out): a 2.3% difference (calculated). The datasheet recommends 470 nF on both for the best noise. Figure 25 and Figure 39 (start-up time against the capacitor) are plotted with C(NR) and C(BYPASS) at the same value, while Equation 1 names only C(NR).

### Input and output capacitors (CIN, COUT)

**Input.** The datasheet says an input capacitor is not needed for stability, and that a low-ESR capacitor of 0.1 to 1 µF close to the regulator is good practice because it counteracts reactive sources and improves transient response and ripple rejection. A larger one may be needed for large, fast load steps or when the regulator is several inches from its source. If the source impedance is not low enough, 0.1 µF may be needed for stability. Table 2 lists 10 µF, the typical characteristics were taken with 1 µF, and the PSRR plots have no input capacitor (C(IN) = 0 F), so none of those plots shows what an input capacitor does.

**Output.** The part is stable with a ceramic capacitor of 4.7 µF or more and an ESR below 1 Ω. X5R and X7R types are recommended for their small drift over temperature. The datasheet evaluated the part with a 10 µF, 10 V, 10% X5R capacitor in an 0805 case. A larger output capacitor reduces the size of overshoot and undershoot and lengthens the transient. A larger C(NR), C(BYPASS) or both improve the line-transient response. The noise barely changes with the output capacitor: 23.54 µVRMS at 10 µF, 23.91 at 22 µF and 22.78 at 100 µF (Figure 24, repeated as Figure 38; VI − VO = 0.5 V).

Our note, which the datasheet doesn't make: a 10 V-rated X5R capacitor loses a large part of its capacitance at a DC bias of 5 or 6 V, so the effective value on a high output can be well under the 4.7 µF stability minimum. Check the maker's DC-bias curve for the output you use.

### Enable (VEN)

The thresholds, the floating-pin rule and the tie-to-IN option are in *How it works*. The EN pin current is 0.02 µA typical and 1 µA maximum when enabled (VI = VEN = 6.5 V). The absolute maximum on EN is VI + 0.3 V or 7 V, whichever is smaller. So VEN must not be driven above the input, which matters when the enable signal comes from a rail that is already up before VI, or stays up after VI falls. A resistor in series with EN is a way to limit the current, but the datasheet says nothing about it (our note).

## Layout

The datasheet's layout rules, with an example board layout (Figure 44):

- **Use separate ground planes for VI and VO,** connected to each other only at the GND pin of the device, to improve the ripple rejection, the noise and the transient response.
- **Connect the ground of the noise-reduction capacitor directly to the GND pin.**
- **High-ESR capacitors may degrade the ripple rejection,** so use low-ESR parts.
- **Put copper under the exposed pad.** On the SON package the main heat path is through the exposed pad to the board. The datasheet says the pad should be attached to an appropriate amount of copper to avoid overheating. The pin table says to connect it to GND, and the package drawing says the pad must be soldered to the board. See *Datasheet notes* for the third statement.

**Package.** The drawing is DRB0008A, a 3 × 3 mm plastic small-outline no-lead package (the drawing is titled VSON, 1 mm maximum height), with the pins on a 0.65 mm pitch and an exposed pad of 1.5 × 1.75 mm (each ±0.1 mm). The example board layout on page 28 has eight pads of 0.31 × 0.6 mm, two rows 2.8 mm apart between pad centres (our reading of the dimension), and an exposed-pad pattern with four narrow fingers 0.23 mm wide. Optional vias of 0.2 mm diameter are shown, and the drawing recommends that vias under paste be filled, plugged or tented. The example stencil uses 0.125 mm thick foil with 84% paste coverage on the exposed pad. This site has no KiCad symbol or footprint for this part yet.

Pin 1 is OUT, pin 2 OUT, pin 3 FB/SNS, pin 4 GND, pin 5 EN, pin 6 NR, pin 7 IN and pin 8 IN, and the thermal pad is on the underside.

## Things to watch

- **Heat sets the load current, not the 1 A.** At 3.3 V out and 5 V in, 1 A is 1.7 W, a 78 °C rise on the datasheet's 45.7 °C/W, and the datasheet gets that figure on a 3 × 3 inch (9 in²) board. With less copper the thermal resistance is much higher (the last design table above), so the practical limit at a larger headroom is a few hundred milliamps. The datasheet says the junction should be kept at 125 °C at most, and at 125 °C ambient that leaves no room for dissipation at all.
- **Design the headroom for the 500 mV maximum dropout at 1 A, not the 170 mV in the feature list.** The table gives maximums only, and the plotted typicals are 175 to 280 mV at 25 °C (by eye) and more when hot.
- **The ripple-rejection numbers need the big capacitors.** The PSRR rows of the electrical table are listed under the table's general test conditions, which have C(NR) = 10 nF and no bypass capacitor. The plots drawn with those capacitor values (Figures 19 and 20, 750 mA) read about 63 dB at 1 kHz and about 39 dB at 1 MHz (by eye), well below the table's 82 dB and 54 dB. The plots with 470 nF on both (Figures 14 to 18) read about 60 to 90 dB at low frequency, depending on load and headroom. The way to reconcile them is that the 80 dB class figures need the 470 nF + 470 nF pair, but the datasheet doesn't say so (our reading). Even then the 1 MHz point of Figure 15 (750 mA, 1 V headroom) reads about 40 dB, not 54 dB.
- **Ripple rejection falls near dropout.** At 0.3 V of headroom the flat part of the curve is about 72 dB against about 88 dB at 0.5 and 1 V (Figure 14, by eye). The datasheet says so in words too.
- **Start-up takes about 80 ms with the recommended 470 nF, and much longer above about 0.59 µF** (see *Noise reduction and start-up*).
- **The output-capacitor minimum is for the effective value.** A 4.7 µF part can fall well below that at DC bias (see *Input and output capacitors*).
- **EN must follow VI.** Its maximum is VI + 0.3 V, and it must not float.
- **Reverse current is not limited.** If the output can be held above the input (a backup supply, a charged capacitor when the input collapses), the body diode of the pass transistor conducts. The datasheet says external limiting may be appropriate.
- **The divider takes 80 µA** from the output with the datasheet's resistors, more than the quiescent current of the part.
- **Not tested where the package can't take it.** The datasheet doesn't test 0.8 V out at 4.5 to 6.5 V in with 750 mA to 1 A, because the dissipation exceeds the package rating. Even at 4.5 V in, 750 mA at 0.8 V out is 2.8 W, a rise of about 127 °C on the 45.7 °C/W (calculated).
- **The pin names and function of IN and OUT.** Each exists twice (pins 7 and 8, pins 1 and 2); connect both of each.
- **KiCad files are not built.** There is no symbol or footprint on this site for the part yet.

## Datasheet notes

**Contradictions and unclear points**, in the order of the datasheet:

- **PSRR in the feature list.** The features give 80 dB at 1 kHz, and the electrical table gives 82 dB at 1 kHz (and 80 dB at 100 Hz). The features' 60 dB at 100 kHz and 54 dB at 1 MHz match the table.
- **Dropout in the feature list.** The features say 170 mV typical at 1 A. The electrical table has no typical dropout, only 250, 350 and 500 mV maximums. The 25 °C curve at 1 A is near 175 mV only for inputs above about 5 V (Figure 5, by eye).
- **Shutdown current.** The device-modes text says 0.02 µA typical. The table says 0.2 µA typical and 2.5 µA maximum (0 to 125 °C), and Figure 12 reads up to about 1 to 2 µA at 125 °C, depending on VI (by eye).
- **Units on the ground-current plots.** Figures 10 and 11 label the ground-pin current axis in mA, with values of about 100 to 250. The table's maximum is 350 µA at 1 A, so the unit is µA (our reading).
- **Start-up arithmetic.** The text says the 33 kΩ resistance with 0.47 µF gives an 80 ms RC delay. 33 kΩ × 470 nF is 15.5 ms. Equation 1's 170 000 (80 ms ÷ 470 nF) implies about 170 kΩ. The table's start-up test condition lists "R1 = 3.3 kΩ" for a 3.3 V output, where Table 1 gives 30.9 kΩ (our reading: a typo).
- **Figure 42's time axis.** It is labelled 50 ms/div, and the ticks run from −0.002 to 0.008, which is 2 ms apart if they are seconds. Figure 43 (same conditions) has ticks 0.05 apart and does match 50 ms/div.
- **Capacitor values differ between sections.** The electrical table's conditions use C(OUT) = 4.7 µF, C(NR) = 10 nF and no C(BYPASS); the typical characteristics use C(IN) = 1 µF; the application curves and Table 2 use 10 µF and 470 nF; and the text recommends 0.1 to 1 µF on the input where Table 2 says 10 µF.
- **The thermal pad.** The pin table says it should be connected to GND, the power-dissipation text says it can be connected to ground or left floating (with enough copper), and note 3 of the package outline says it must be soldered to the board for thermal and mechanical performance.
- **Junction temperature.** The absolute-maximum table allows −55 to 150 °C, while the thermal text says to limit the junction to 125 °C for reliable operation.
- **Figure 45 against the thermal table.** The curve ends near 48 °C/W at 9 in² (read by eye), and the thermal table says 45.7 °C/W for the same JEDEC board.
- **Orderable entries.** The addendum lists TPS7A8101QDRBRQ1 and TPS7A8101QDRBRQ1.A, both active, and doesn't say what the ".A" means.
