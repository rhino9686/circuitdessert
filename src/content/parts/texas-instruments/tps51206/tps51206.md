## How it works

The TPS51206 holds VTT at half of VDDQ, which it reads on VDDQSNS, and it buffers the same half-VDDQ voltage out on VTTREF. There is no REFIN pin and no divider to build; the half-VDDQ comes from inside the part, which is what separates it from the TPS51200 and RT2568.

**Supplies.** VDD (3.3 V or 5 V) powers the control circuits. VLDOIN supplies the output stage and must be at least VTT + 0.4 V, up to 3.5 V; the datasheet's example ties it to VDDQ. In the datasheet's power-up notes VDD, S3 and S5 come first and VLDOIN and VDDQSNS after.

**Power states.** S3 and S5 follow the memory power states (Table 1 below). In S0 both outputs are on. In S3 (suspend to RAM) VTTREF stays on and VTT goes to high impedance. In S4 and S5 (suspend to disk) both outputs are turned off and discharged: VTT through about 7 mA and VTTREF through about 1.3 mA.

**Remote sensing.** VTTSNS goes to the positive node of the VTT output capacitor, as a separate trace from the high-current trace.

**Capacitors.** 10 µF or more on VTT, with no maximum; the VTTREF capacitor must be between 0.22 µF and 1 µF, and the VTTREF pin cannot be left open. The example also uses 10 µF on VLDOIN and 0.1 µF on VDD.

**Protection.** The current limit is 2 A for source and sink. The VDD undervoltage lockout is 2.9 V typical with 0.2 V of hysteresis, and overtemperature protection trips at 150 °C with 10 °C of hysteresis.

## Example application

Figure 24 of the datasheet is the VLDOIN = VDDQ configuration. The design requirements are a 3.3 V or 5 V supply on VDD, VLDOIN equal to VDDQ at 1.5 V and ±2 A of output current. VDDQ feeds both VLDOIN and VDDQSNS with 10 µF, VTT has 10 µF, VTTREF has 0.22 µF and VDD has 0.1 µF.

### Worked example: DDR3 at ±2 A

1. **Voltage.** VDDQ = 1.5 V gives VTT = VTTREF = 0.75 V (calculated). VLDOIN = VDDQ = 1.5 V is above the 1.15 V minimum.
2. **Accuracy.** The VTT tolerance at 2 A is ±40 mV, so VTT stays between 0.71 and 0.79 V at full load, and ±20 mV below 10 mA.
3. **Heat at a steady 2 A.** Sourcing 2 A from 1.5 V to 0.75 V dissipates 1.5 W, and sinking 2 A into 0.75 V dissipates the same (calculated). With RθJA of 70.3 °C/W that is a 105 °C rise (calculated), so the junction would reach the 150 °C overtemperature trip at an ambient of about 45 °C. Our reading is that the 2 A rating is a peak, not a continuous load.

## Layout

The datasheet's guidelines: put the VLDOIN input capacitor close to the pin and the VTT output capacitor close to the VTT and PGND pins, both with short, wide connections. Connect VTTSNS to the positive node of the VTT capacitor as a separate trace routed away from the high-current trace, preferably on another layer; if the voltage must be sensed at the load, put the output capacitor there. Join the GND pin (and the VTTREF capacitor's ground) and the PGND pin (and the VTT capacitor's ground) to the internal ground planes through multiple vias, ideally to two planes. Solder the thermal pad directly and use wide copper and numerous 0.33 mm vias to the ground planes. The datasheet points to the TPS51206-EVM user's guide.

The package is a 2 × 2 mm 10-pin WSON (DSQ). This site has no KiCad symbol or footprint for this part yet. Pin 1 is VDDQSNS.

## Things to watch

- **Power-up order.** The datasheet's notes say VDD, S3 and S5 come first, then VLDOIN and VDDQSNS.
- **VTTREF pin.** It must not be open, and its capacitor must be 0.22–1 µF.
- **VLDOIN headroom.** It must be at least VTT + 0.4 V, and the part dissipates less the lower it is.
- **Heat.** A steady 2 A from a 1.5 V VLDOIN is 1.5 W, which would reach the 150 °C overtemperature trip at about 45 °C ambient with RθJA of 70.3 °C/W; the 2 A is a peak rating.
- **Memory types.** The datasheet lists DDR2, DDR3, DDR3L and DDR4, not original DDR.
- **Category.** This site has no category for termination regulators; the page is filed under "Other".
- **S3 and S5 levels.** High is 1.8 V minimum and low is 0.5 V maximum, so a 1.8 V logic output has no margin.
