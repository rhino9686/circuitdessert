## How it works

The TPS7B6950-Q1 is a high-voltage linear regulator with a fixed 5 V output. It is the 5 V member of a family of three that share one datasheet: 2.5 V (TPS7B6925-Q1), 3.3 V (TPS7B6933-Q1) and 5 V (TPS7B6950-Q1). The block diagram is a band-gap reference and a regulator control block between IN and OUT, with undervoltage lockout, overcurrent detection and thermal shutdown. There is no enable, feedback or adjust pin, so the whole part is three signals: IN, OUT and ground.

**Quiescent current is the headline.** The datasheet gives 15 µA typical and 25 µA maximum at light load over the full temperature range, with the 5 V version tested at 0.2 mA of load and 5.5 to 40 V in. That is low enough to leave on a battery permanently. There is no figure for the current at higher loads in the electrical table, so the extra ground current at 150 mA is not specified.

**Input range and the 5 V version's floor.** The family is rated 4 to 40 V in the recommended-operating table and withstands 45 V for 200 ms. The 5 V version is only specified from 5.5 V in at 1 mA, and its output accuracy is specified for 6.5 V and up at 1 to 150 mA. Below that the output follows the input minus a drop that depends on the load: the datasheet calls this low-voltage tracking and says it can remove the need for a boost converter during cold crank. The input undervoltage detection turns the output on above 3.65 V and off below 3 V (see *Datasheet notes* for how these are printed).

**Dropout.** The 5 V version's dropout is 220 mV typical and 400 mV maximum at 50 mA, and 450 mV typical and 800 mV maximum at 100 mA. There is no value at 150 mA, although the accuracy is specified up to that load. Our reading: for the full 150 mA, plan on a larger drop than the 100 mA maximum and keep the input comfortably above 6.5 V.

**Protection.** The current limit with OUT shorted to ground is 150 mA minimum and 500 mA maximum. The rated output current is also 150 mA, so at the low end of the range there is no guaranteed margin between the load rating and the short-circuit limit (our reading). Thermal shutdown trips at 175 °C typical with 25 °C of hysteresis, and the part cycles on and off if it stays hot. The datasheet says that protection is for overload and is not a replacement for proper heat sinking.

**Soft start.** The output has a soft start that controls the initial current into the pass element and the output capacitor. The datasheet's only start-up capture is a 5 V power-up with a 2.2 µF output capacitor (Figure 26).

### Input and output capacitors

**Input.** The datasheet says a capacitor above 0.1 µF close to IN improves transient performance, and that its voltage rating must exceed the highest input voltage. With 45 V transients that means a 50 V part. The typical circuit shows 10 µF.

**Output.** An effective capacitance from 2.2 to 100 µF with an ESR from 1 mΩ to 2 Ω is required for stability up to 150 mA, and the ESR figure applies at 10 kHz. "Effective" matters for ceramics: a small X5R capacitor loses much of its value at a DC bias of 5 V, so check the maker's DC-bias curve (our note; the datasheet doesn't say this). The datasheet recommends X5R or X7R types and says a bigger capacitor may be needed for fast load steps.

### Heat and the largest load

The datasheet's equations are PD = IO × (VI − VO) + IQ × VI (the second term is negligible), TJ = TA + θJA × PD, and TAmax = TJmax − θJA × PD. The first design table above applies them to the 5 V version at 85 °C ambient. At 14 V in and 150 mA, the part dissipates 1.35 W (calculated). On the SOT-223 that is a rise of 87 °C, so 112 °C at 25 °C ambient; on the SOT-23 it would be a rise of 284 °C, far past the 175 °C shutdown point. The SOT-23 package is only for light loads at a high input, about 34 mA at 14 V and 85 °C ambient. The datasheet's thermal numbers are for a JEDEC 2s2p board with 2 oz copper, which is better than most real boards.

## Layout

The datasheet's layout guidance (section 11):

- **Put the input and output capacitors as close to the device as possible,** on the same side of the board as the regulator. Do not put any capacitor on the opposite side, and avoid long traces, which can hurt performance and cause instability.
- **Keep ESR and ESL low** to get the specified performance.
- **Surround the device with vias** to improve thermal performance.
- If possible, use the same layout as the evaluation board (the TPS7B6950EVM, user's guide SLVUAC0).

The SOT-223 layout example (Figure 27) has the three signal pins along the bottom edge and the tab (pin 4, GND) at the top; the SOT-23 example (Figure 28) has IN and NC on the left, the two GND pins at the bottom and OUT at the top right.

**Packages.** The SOT-23 drawing DBV0005A is the JEDEC MO-178 outline, 1.45 mm maximum height, with a 0.95 mm pin pitch and an example land pattern. The SOT-223 drawing DCY0004A is JEDEC TO-261, 1.8 mm maximum height, with three leads on a 2.3 mm pitch and a tab, and an example land pattern and stencil drawing.

Pin 1 is IN on both packages. On the SOT-23 it is the top-left lead in the datasheet's top view, with pins 1 to 3 down the left side and pins 4 and 5 up the right; on the SOT-223 pins 1 to 3 run left to right along the bottom edge with the tab, pin 4, on top. ## Things to watch

- **The package sets the load current at a high input.** On the SOT-23-5, 14 V in allows about 34 mA at 85 °C ambient (calculated from RθJA = 210.4 °C/W). Use the SOT-223 for anything above a few tens of milliamps at a high input.
- **The 5 V version needs 6.5 V in for its full accuracy specification.** The table lists the ±3% output from 6.5 V and 1 to 150 mA, and the input from 5.5 V at 1 mA. In cold crank the output follows the input.
- **No dropout number at 150 mA.** The table stops at 100 mA (800 mV maximum).
- **There is no enable pin.** The output is on whenever the input is above the undervoltage threshold, so a switchable rail needs an external switch ahead of IN.
- **The load rating and the short-circuit minimum are the same 150 mA.** The current limit with OUT to ground is 150 mA minimum, so a load that draws the full rating can sit at the edge of the limit on a part at the low end of the range (our reading).
- **Output capacitance counts as effective capacitance.** The 2.2 µF minimum is for the effective value at bias and temperature.
- **Input capacitor voltage rating.** The datasheet says the rating must exceed the highest input voltage, which is 45 V for transients.
- **Soft start is internal and not specified.** There is no start-up time in the tables.
- **The catalogue part has its own datasheet.** The addendum lists a non-automotive TPS7B69 as another qualified version of this part. This page covers only the automotive -Q1 family.
- **The KiCad symbol is ours and not yet opened in KiCad.** It was generated by script and checked against the pin table, but nobody has loaded it in KiCad. The pin types and the symbol layout are our choices. The file holds one symbol for each package.

## Datasheet notes

- **Quiescent current unit.** The electrical table's rendered page prints the unit as "A" with the micro sign missing in the PDF's text layer and rendering; the feature list gives 15 µA typical and 25 µA maximum, so the unit is µA (our reading).
- **Undervoltage thresholds.** The row "Ramp VI up until the output turns on" has 3.65 in the MIN column and no typical or maximum, and "Ramp VI down until the output turns OFF" has 3 in the MAX column. The text says the maximum UVLO voltage is 3 V and that no minimum UVLO is specified.
- **Dropout of the 2.5 V version.** It is printed as 1.575 V maximum at both 50 mA and 100 mA, in volts where the other rows use millivolts. For the 3.3 V version the maximums are 799 mV at 50 mA and 800 mV at 100 mA.
- **Test conditions for IQ.** The 2.5 V and 3.3 V versions state no load current; the 5 V version states IO = 0.2 mA.
- **Input range.** The recommended-operating table gives 4 to 40 V for VI and 0 to 5.5 V for VO for every version, while the electrical table gives the 5 V version a 5.5 V minimum input.
- **Orderable entries.** The addendum lists each part number and a copy ending ".A", both active, and doesn't say what the ".A" means.
- **Dates.** The datasheet's own revision is January 2015. The addendum is dated 10 Nov 2025, the tape-and-reel pages April 2020, and the SOT-223 package drawing 05/2026.
- **What was compared.** The electrical characteristics were compared with the rendered page 5. The absolute-maximum, ESD, recommended-operating and thermal tables on page 4 were read from the text layer only, so the page stays unverified.
