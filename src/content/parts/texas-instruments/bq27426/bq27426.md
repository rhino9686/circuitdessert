## How it works

The BQ27426 is a fuel gauge for one Li-ion or Li-polymer cell that lives on the system board, between the battery pack and the rest of the product. It measures the cell voltage on its BAT pin and the current through a 10 mΩ sense resistor on SRP and SRN, and its Impedance Track algorithm turns the two into remaining capacity (mAh), state of charge (%), state of health and voltage (mV), which the host reads over I²C. It powers itself from the battery through an internal 1.8 V regulator, so the only connections the datasheet asks for are the pack's positive and negative terminals, the sense resistor, the host's I²C lines and an optional GPOUT line. The datasheet says three chemistry profiles (4.2 V, 4.35 V and 4.4 V cells) are preprogrammed in ROM to keep user configuration and system microcontroller firmware work to a minimum. It describes the pins, electrical limits, power modes, I²C framing and the application circuit, and points to the BQ27426 Technical Reference Manual for more detail. **The command set, register map and data-memory parameters are not in this datasheet**, so this page doesn't have them either.

### I²C interface

The gauge is an I²C slave with the fixed 7-bit address 1010101 (0x55), so the first byte on the bus is 0xAA for a write and 0xAB for a read. It supports read, incremental read, quick read, one-byte write and incremental write. Writing to a read-only address, and reading above address 0x6B, are answered with a NACK. If SCL or SDA is held low for two seconds the gauge lets go of both lines. The timing is in the design table above (100 kHz and 400 kHz).

- **Wait 66 µs between packets.** At 400 kHz a bus-free time of at least 66 µs must be inserted between every packet addressed to the gauge. Above 100 kHz, use individual 1-byte writes for commands.
- **Don't poll fast.** A read-write standard command needs at least 2 seconds to show its result. Read-only standard commands need no wait, but the host must not issue any standard command more than twice per second, or the gauge may reset when its watchdog timer expires.
- **Clock stretching.** In SLEEP mode the gauge stretches the clock by up to 100 µs on all traffic (it has to wake up). In INITIALIZATION and NORMAL mode it may stretch by up to 4 ms inside a packet. The host's I²C driver has to allow for both.

### BAT and VDD capacitors

The capacitor on BAT bypasses AC ripple to ground on what the datasheet calls a sensitive high-impedance measurement node. The datasheet says it helps most with pulsed loads such as a phone and recommends it for every application. Section 9.1 says at least 1 µF between BAT and VSS, close to the gauge and with short traces; Section 10.1 says 1 µF is required if the line from the pack to BAT could pick up noise. The VDD capacitor must be at least 2.2 µF, close to the gauge with short traces to VDD and VSS, and the regulator must not power anything else.

### The sense resistor

The datasheet recommends a 1% tolerance, 50 ppm drift, 1 W resistor as the best compromise between performance and price, and says any variation of the resistance between SRP and SRN changes the derived current directly. With a precalibrated standard 10 mΩ, 1% part there is no calibration. SRP goes to the battery side and SRN to the system side, each with its own Kelvin connection at the resistor's terminals. The calculated table above shows what 10 mΩ costs: 10 mV and 10 mW at 1 A, and 62.5 mW at the 2.5 A that a ±25 mV input range allows (our reading of the range, see the table's note).

### BIN and the pack thermistor

BIN doubles as the battery-insertion input and the thermistor input. The temperature circuit works with a 10 kΩ NTC at 25 °C, and the default curve-fitting coefficients assume a Semitec 103AT; another curve (the datasheet names JT-2 as an example) needs the coefficients rewritten in RAM. The pull-up resistor may go only to VDD, never to an external rail, and BIN must not be shorted to VDD or VSS.

**What the BIN network does (our arithmetic).** With the pack's 10 kΩ thermistor at 25 °C, BIN is at 1.85 V × 10 kΩ ÷ (1.8 MΩ + 10 kΩ) = about 10 mV, well under VIL (0.6 V) and so "inserted". The highest resistance from BIN to VSS that still reads as low is 0.6 V × 1.8 MΩ ÷ (1.85 V – 0.6 V) = about 860 kΩ, ignoring pin leakage. With no pack, BIN is pulled to VDD and reads high. See Things to watch for the effect of the 1 µA leakage limit on that pull-up.

### Worked example: a 1 A load on a 3.6 V cell

These numbers are calculated from the datasheet's values; nothing was measured.

| Quantity | Value | How |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sense voltage | 10 mV | 1 A × 10 mΩ |
| Loss in R1 | 10 mW | (1 A)² × 10 mΩ |
| Drop in the supply path | 0.28% of 3.6 V | 10 mV ÷ 3.6 V |
| Gauge current, NORMAL mode | 50 µA typical | ICC, TA = 30 °C, VBAT = 3.6 V |
| Gauge current, SLEEP | 9 µA typical | Only when the load is below the 10 mA default threshold |

At 1 A the load is far above the sleep threshold, so the gauge is in NORMAL mode at 50 µA typical, which is 0.005% of the load. In standby at a load below 10 mA it drops to 9 µA. If the product's standby current is above 10 mA, the gauge stays in NORMAL mode unless the Sleep Current threshold is raised (the datasheet names the parameter, Sleep Current, but doesn't say how to change it).

## Layout

The datasheet's layout guidance (Section 10.1 and Figure 10-1):

- **VDD capacitor** of at least 2.2 µF, close to the gauge with short traces to VDD and VSS.
- **BAT capacitor** of at least 1.0 µF between BAT and VSS, required if the line from the pack to BAT can pick up noise; close to the gauge with short traces. (The bullet says "both the VDD pin and VSS", which we read as BAT; see Datasheet notes.)
- **SCL and SDA pull-ups.** Pick the value for the pull-up voltage and the bus capacitance (see the pull-up table above). If the pull-ups are disconnected from the host during low-power operation, use 1 MΩ pull-downs to VSS.
- **GPOUT** goes to a host GPIO even if the host doesn't use the function, so that a gauge in SHUTDOWN can be woken by toggling it.
- **BIN** gets a 10 kΩ pull-down to VSS when no thermistor is connected, and must not be shorted directly to VDD or VSS.
- **Kelvin connections.** SRP to the pack side and SRN to the system side of RSENSE, made right at the resistor's terminals; BAT Kelvin-connected to the pack's positive terminal. The layout example (Figure 10-1) shows a via from the ground pour to power ground.

## What the datasheet shows

The plots don't say how many units, what test conditions or whether the values are typical or limits.

**What isn't here.** There is no accuracy table and no state-of-charge or capacity accuracy, only the three plots above. The command set, register map and data-memory parameters are not given either; the text points to the Technical Reference Manual for the typically expected values. The three chemistry profiles are named only by their charge voltages.

## Things to watch

- **The RAM configuration starts from ROM defaults after every power-up and SHUTDOWN.** The datasheet says all parameters in RAM-based data memory require initialization, either in the lab or by downloading them from the host, and that a POR or a RESET copies the ROM defaults back. So the host firmware has to check Flags() [ITPOR] and reload the configuration through CONFIG UPDATE mode, using the typically expected values the datasheet says the Technical Reference Manual gives.
- **Don't leave GPOUT floating, and don't skip the host connection.** Even if the host never uses SOC_INT or BAT_LOW, wire GPOUT to a GPIO so a gauge that was shut down by mistake can be woken (toggle it). The wake-up needs a rising edge.
- **BIN needs a 10 kΩ to VSS in every build.** A removable pack's thermistor does that job, and an embedded cell or a board with no thermistor needs a resistor. And the 1.8 MΩ pull-up goes to VDD, never to an external rail.
- **The 1 µA leakage limit against the 1.8 MΩ pull-up (our note).** The table lists the input leakage of BIN (with SCL, SDA and GPOUT) as 1 µA maximum. 1 µA through 1.8 MΩ is 1.8 V, nearly all of the 1.85 V VDD, so at the leakage limit the pull-up could not hold BIN above VIH (1.4 V) with no pack present. Typical leakage isn't given, and the datasheet recommends the 1.8 MΩ value itself, so this is a margin to check on a build and not a flaw.
- **Sleep needs a low standby current.** The 9 µA figure applies below the Sleep Current threshold (10 mA by default). A product that idles above 10 mA keeps the gauge in NORMAL mode at 50 µA, and the sleep specification is typical-only and specified by design.
- **Allow for I²C waiting times and clock stretching.** 66 µs between packets, one-byte writes above 100 kHz, no more than two standard commands per second, up to 100 µs of stretching in SLEEP and up to 4 ms otherwise. A host driver with a short clock-stretch timeout can see errors in SLEEP.
- **VDD is not a supply.** Its 1.8 V output is only for the gauge's own use. The pull-up for BIN is the one external load the datasheet allows.
- **The sense input range is ±25 mV.** At 10 mΩ that is a current range of ±2.5 A in each direction (calculated). Check the peak charge and discharge currents against that; a different resistor changes the range and, the datasheet implies, the precalibration.
- **Low-side sensing isn't explained here.** The pin table says "Low-side current sensing can be enabled" and Figure 8-2 draws it, but this datasheet doesn't say which setting enables it. The Technical Reference Manual is the place to look before using it.
- **There is no KiCad footprint yet.** The pad layout above is the datasheet's example and not a file. The KiCad symbol is ours, built from the pin table, and its Footprint field is empty: no footprint on this site has been compared with this part's package drawing.
- **The KiCad symbol is ours and not yet opened in KiCad.** It was generated by script and checked against the pin table, but nobody has loaded it in KiCad. The pin types and the symbol layout are our choices.

## Datasheet notes

**Contradictions and unclear points**, in the order of the datasheet:

- **The BAT capacitor.** Table 6.3 lists CBAT as 0.1 µF nominal (recommending a 5% ceramic X5R close to the device). The pin table, Section 9.1, Section 10.1 and the drawn circuit all say 1 µF (at least 1 µF in Sections 9.1 and 10.1), and the simplified schematic on page 1 labels the capacitor next to BAT 1 µF.
- **Pin-type footnote.** Table 5-1's type column uses PI, AI, DI, DO, DIO and PO, while the footnote defines only "IO = Digital input-output, AI = Analog input, P = Power connection".
- **BIN's type.** The pin table calls BIN a digital input (DI), and the specifications call it a push-pull IO pin (VIOPP in the absolute maximums, VIH(PP) and footnote 3 in Table 6.6). The pin text says nothing about it driving.
- **BIN shorts.** The pin table says BIN must not be shorted to "VCC" or VSS and the layout section says "VDD or VSS". The part has no VCC pin (our reading: VDD).
- **Wake-up from SHUTDOWN.** Section 7.4.1 and Figure 7-5 say GPOUT must be raised from low to high for at least 200 µs. Table 6.8 gives tSHUP as a minimum low time of 10 µs before the wake-up, and Table 6.7 gives a wake-up rising threshold of 1.2 V. These are three different numbers for a related event, and none says how long the line must then stay high other than the 200 µs.
- **Names that aren't pins.** The conditions of every electrical table, tPUCD, and Figure 7-5 use REGIN and VREGIN (and "VCC pin = OFF"); the part has only a BAT pin and no REGIN or VCC pin. Our reading: REGIN is BAT, and the names were carried over from another gauge.
- **"Pin 3 (VSS)".** Section 10.1 says "The actual device ground is pin 3 (VSS)". The package has no pin 3; VSS is ball B2. Our reading: a leftover from another package.
- **The BAT bullet in the layout list** says the capacitor should have short traces "to both the VDD pin and VSS"; it is the BAT pin's capacitor, so our reading is BAT.
- **I²C waiting time.** The text and Table 6.11 give t(BUF) as 66 µs; Figure 7-4 labels the gap "66ms".
- **Sleep current wording.** The features say 9 µA operating current below 10 mA, and the table gives ISLP as the SLEEP current with a measurement every 20 s and the wake comparator disabled. Revision G "clarified SLEEP mode current", and no value has a maximum.
- **IOL sign.** The sink current IOL(OD) is printed as –3 mA (as a maximum); we read it as a sink current of 3 mA.
- **Low-side sensing.** The pin table says low-side current sensing can be enabled "see Typical Applications"; Section 8.2 only shows Figure 8-2 and doesn't name a setting.
- **R1 in the figures.** Both application figures print R1 as "0.01" with no unit; the pin table says 10 mΩ.
- **Duplicate plots.** Figures 8-3 to 8-5 repeat Figures 6-3 to 6-5.
- **Orderable entries.** The addendum lists BQ27426YZFR and BQ27426YZFR.A, BQ27426YZFT and BQ27426YZFT.A, all active, and doesn't say what the ".A" means.
- **Not part of the specification.** Section 8 says its application information is not part of TI's component specification.
