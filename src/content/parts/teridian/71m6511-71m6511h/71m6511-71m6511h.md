## How it works

The 71M6511 is a metering system on a chip. Three analog inputs (IA, VA and IB) and the output of an on-chip temperature sensor go through a multiplexer to a single 22-bit delta-sigma ADC with a FIR filter. The samples land in the data RAM of a separate 32-bit compute engine (CE), which multiplies voltage and current samples, accumulates energy, and computes rms values, line frequency and angles; the 80515 MPU reads the results, drives the LCD, the UARTs and the DIO pins, and handles calibration. A 32.768 kHz crystal is the time base for the whole chip, the RTC and the hardware watchdog included. The CE and the MPU run independently, so a slow firmware loop does not stop the measurement.

The 71M6511 and the 71M6511H are the same silicon with different reference trimming. The H is trimmed in production to under 10 ppm/°C and its temperature behaviour is stored in trim fuses (TRIMBGA, TRIMBGB and TRIMM[2:0]) that the MPU can read; the plain 71M6511 gets fixed coefficients for a typical part and is specified under 50 ppm/°C. TRIMBGB is zero on the 71M6511 and non-zero on the H, which is how firmware can tell them apart.

### Measurement path

- **Inputs are referenced to V3P3A, not to ground.** The data sheet says "VDD referenced": each of IA, VA and IB is measured against V3P3A with a recommended range of ±250 mV peak. Every sensor return (a CT burden, a shunt, a divider's lower leg) goes to V3P3A, as Figures 27 and 28 draw it.
- **Multiplexer.** A normal cycle samples IA, VA and IB; an alternate cycle swaps one slot for the temperature sensor (VA is never replaced) and the CE interpolates the missing sample. The MPU asks for an alternate cycle with MUX_ALT, typically about once a second.
- **Chopping.** The reference is chopper-stabilised; toggling CHOP_ENA between multiplexer cycles averages out DC offset.
- **Sample rate.** MUX_DIV sets 2, 3, 4 or 6 multiplexer states per cycle and FIR_LEN selects 21 or 22 bits. The table follows the datasheet's Table 2; the specifications are written for FIR_LEN = 1 and MUX_DIV = 1.

| MUX_DIV (channels) | FIR_LEN | CK32 states | Sample rate (Hz) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 01 (4) | 0 | 9 | 3640.89 |
| 01 (4) | 1 | 13 | 2520.615 |
| 10 (3) | 0 | 7 | 4681.143 |
| 10 (3) | 1 | 10 | 3276.8 |
| 11 (2) | 0 | 5 | 6553.6 |
| 11 (2) | 1 | 7 | 4681.143 |

The rates are 32,768 Hz divided by the number of states, and they check (32,768 ÷ 9 = 3640.9, ÷ 13 = 2520.6, ÷ 10 = 3276.8). MUX_DIV = 00 is not allowed.

The CE program and I/O RAM are volatile: on power-up the MPU copies images of both (and of the CE coefficients) from flash into RAM before it starts the CE. The first eight I/O RAM bytes (2000 to 2007) hold important system settings and must survive any flash-write sequence. The data sheet's firmware chapters (the I/O RAM map, the SFRs, the CE variables) are not reproduced here.

### Reset, power-up and the V1 comparator

The comparator on V1 watches the supply the meter is protecting. When V1 is below VBIAS (1.5 V), or RESETZ or E_RST is low, all digital activity stops, every I/O RAM bit is cleared, and the oscillator and RTC keep running on the battery if there is one. The 2.5 V regulator keeps powering the digital section as long as V1 stays above VBIAS.

| Event | Time | Where it comes from |
| --- | --- | --- |
| V1 rises above VBIAS, or RESETZ rises | Reset timer starts | Datasheet |
| Reset timer | 4100 crystal cycles, 125 ms | Datasheet, Figure 20; 4100 ÷ 32,768 = 125.1 ms (calculated) |
| Preboot | 32 cycles, about 1 ms | Datasheet; 32 ÷ 32,768 = 0.98 ms (calculated) |
| Hardware watchdog | MPU must write WDT_RST at least every 1.5 s | Datasheet |
| Watchdog overflow | Resets the MPU as if RESETZ were pulled low for half a crystal cycle; WD_OVF is set | Datasheet |

The hardware watchdog cannot be turned off from firmware. For debugging it is disabled by pulling V1 up to V3P3, which also disables the power-fail detection. In normal operation V1 must stay below V3P3A − 0.4 V, which the data sheet writes as "lower than 2.9 V" (see Datasheet notes). If the crystal stops or slows down, WD_OVF is set and the chip resets when the crystal comes back (the specification table gives 25 kHz as the RTC_OK threshold, a maximum).

### Power saving and battery

With V1 below VBIAS, the battery powers the RTC, the crystal oscillator, the MPU RAM and the WD_OVF bit. In normal operation the MPU can shed load; the savings are the datasheet's typical values, with MPU_DIV = 0:

| Measure | Control | Saving (mA) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Disable the CE | CE_EN = 0 | 0.16 |
| Disable the ADC | ADC_DIS = 1 | 1.8 |
| Disable the CKTEST clock output | CKOUTDIS = 1 | 0.6 |
| Disable the emulator clock | ECK_DIS = 1 | 0.1 |
| Flash read pulse 33 ns | FLASH66Z = 1 | 0.04 |
| Disable the LCD boost | LCD_BSTEN = 0 | 0.9 |
| Disable the RTM outputs | RTM_EN = 0 | 0.01 |
| Raise the MPU clock divider | MPU_DIV = X | 0.4 per MHz |

ECK_DIS must be used with care: setting it by accident blocks the ICE port, so flash can no longer be erased or programmed.

### Sensors

The datasheet shows four sensor connections, all returning to V3P3: a resistive divider for the line voltage (VA = Vin × Rout ÷ (Rout + Rin)), a current transformer with a burden resistor (Vout = R × Iin ÷ N), a resistive shunt (Vout = R × Iin) and a Rogowski coil (Vout = dIin/dt). The datasheet also says that the input pins are made for sensors with low source impedance.

Two things follow from the input specifications:

- **Loading on the divider.** The input impedance of an analog pin is 40 kΩ to 90 kΩ. With a 1 kΩ Rout that loads the divider by 2.4% at 40 kΩ and 1.1% at 90 kΩ (calculated), a unit-to-unit spread of about 1.3 points that calibration removes. It also drifts by 1.7 Ω/°C, so keep Rout small.
- **Dynamic range.** 150 nV per LSB and ±250 mV recommended range is about 1.67 million counts peak; the ADC's digital full scale of ±2,097,152 LSB is about ±314 mV (calculated). With the CT example in the design tables, the smallest current in the datasheet's 2000 to 1 accuracy range is 778 counts peak (calculated), well above the 150 nV step, but the ±10 mV input offset is much larger than that signal and relies on chopping and calibration.

### Worked example: a 230 V meter front end (ours)

Using the numbers from the design tables:

1. Peak line voltage at 230 V rms is 230 × 1.414 = 325.3 V; with a 20% overvoltage margin 390.3 V.
2. For 250 mV at that peak, the divider ratio is 0.250 ÷ 390.3 = 640 per million, so with Rout = 1 kΩ, Rin = 1.560 MΩ.
3. Three 523 kΩ resistors in series give 1.569 MΩ and a ratio of 637 per million: VA is 207.2 mV peak at 230 V and 248.6 mV at +20%, inside the ±250 mV range. Each resistor sees 76.7 V rms and the string dissipates 33.7 mW in total (calculated).
4. A 100 A meter with a 2000:1 CT and a 3.3 Ω burden gives 233 mV peak at full load (calculated). A burden of 3.54 Ω would reach 250 mV exactly; 3.3 Ω leaves 7% margin.
5. Supply power: 6.4 mA at 3.3 V is 21.1 mW typical (the feature list says 22 mW) and 9.5 mA at 3.6 V is 34.2 mW maximum.
6. Reset: with the pushbutton held, RR1 and RR2 in the datasheet's Figure 37 draw 3.3 V ÷ 210 Ω = 15.7 mA (17.1 mA at 3.6 V), calculated.

The datasheet does not say what to use for the other V1 divider resistors; the V1 table works out the ratios for a few supplies.

## Layout

- Join V3P3A to V3P3D and GNDA to GNDD on the board, and connect GNDA and GNDD directly to the ground plane. Bypass each supply with at least 0.1 µF next to its pin.
- Keep the capacitance between XIN and XOUT small, and shield the two pins from each other; that also lowers the battery current. For best EMI rejection the datasheet connects the crystal body and the grounds of the two crystal capacitors to GNDD through a ferrite bead, and puts no resistor across the crystal (the oscillator biases itself).
- Mount the reset resistor and capacitor as close as possible to the IC. If the trace from the pushbutton to RESETZ is a problem, leave out RR2.
- Give the V1 divider a header or jumper that can pull V1 to V3P3 for programming and debugging, and make sure it is removed in the finished meter (the watchdog is off with it in).
- The emulator pins have weak pull-ups; the datasheet recommends 1 kΩ pull-ups on the board for E_RST, E_RXTX and E_TCLK.
- The package is a 64-lead LQFP, body 9.8 mm to 10.2 mm square, lead pitch 0.50 mm, seated height 1.40 mm to 1.60 mm; the outline on page 92 is a bottom view with the pin 1 marker at the lower left. The datasheet has no land pattern and no exposed pad.

Pin 1 is the pin at the top of the left side in the datasheet's top view, next to the chamfered corner. This site has no KiCad symbol or footprint for this part yet.

## Things to watch

- **Sensor returns go to V3P3A, not ground.** A divider or burden returned to ground puts the signal outside the ±250 mV window around V3P3A, so the ADC reads garbage or clips.
- **V1 limit depends on the supply.** The data sheet says V1 must stay below 2.9 V to keep the watchdog on, but the comparator spec only guarantees it enabled below V3P3A − 0.4 V, which is 2.6 V at a 3.0 V supply. Size the V1 divider for 2.6 V.
- **Pulling V1 high disables both the watchdog and the power-fail detection.** A debug jumper left in place does that in the field.
- **RX must not exceed 3.6 V.** The datasheet says every pin except the supplies and RX can face a 5 V device. For RX, Figure 34 shows a diode from RX to the 5 V signal (cathode toward the signal, as drawn) with a 100 kΩ pull-up from RX to V3P3; a level shifter also works.
- **Unused pins are not free.** IA, IB and VA must be tied to V3P3A (IA and IB may be tied together); RX and OPT_RX go to V3P3D or GNDD; unused DIO must be outputs; TEST must be grounded.
- **ECK_DIS can lock you out of flash programming.** It stops the emulator clock; setting it by accident means the part cannot be erased or reprogrammed through the ICE port.
- **Flash writes need care.** Interrupts must be off (EAL = 1) and FLSH_PWE set; the first eight I/O RAM bytes must be excluded from the write; a byte can be written twice between erases at most.
- **Obsolete.** The product pages on analog.com list both the 71M6511 and the 71M6511H as obsolete, so this is a part to understand for an existing design rather than to start a new one with. The status comes from those pages as shown in search results; they were not opened in full.
- **The KiCad files are not there yet.** There is no symbol or footprint on this site, and no package page for the LQFP-64.

## Datasheet notes

- **PPMC is printed as −149 but the arithmetic gives +157.** Page 77 says "PPMC = TC1 × 22.46 = −149" for the standard (non-H) settings, while the specifications table (page 88) lists TC1 = +7.0 µV/°C, and 7.0 × 22.46 = +157.2. PPMC2 = TC2 × 1150.1 = −0.341 × 1150.1 = −392 does check. Either the sign or the TC1 value is wrong; verify against the Software User's Guide or the demo code before using it.
- **Crystal coefficient signs.** The example fit has c = −0.00714 ppm/°C², yet the example gives Y_CALC2 = +7 together with Y_CAL = 109 and Y_CALC = 12, which keep the sign of a and b. The datasheet's own correction formula adds the three terms, so one of the signs is inconsistent. The page's design table checks the fit (largest error 0.94 ppm) but not the sign convention of the registers.
- **V1 limit.** Page 83 says V1 must be below 2.9 V "in all cases" to keep the watchdog enabled; the comparator table (page 90) gives the disable threshold as V3P3A − 400 mV (min) to V3P3A − 10 mV (max), which at the 3.0 V minimum supply is 2.6 V.
- **RESETZ capacitor.** The pin description (page 95) says to connect 0.1 µF from RESETZ to GNDD; Figure 37 and its text give 1 nF.
- **VREF capacitor missing from the table.** The pin description asks for 0.1 µF from VREF to GNDA, but the recommended-components table (page 91) has no entry for it.
- **Crystal load capacitors.** 22 pF ±10% in the recommended-components table; "typically 20 pF" in the pin description.
- **VLCD range.** The pin table says 3.3 V to 5.0 V; the operating conditions say 2.9 V to 5.5 V and the boost target is 4.5 V to 5.5 V.
- **Drift of the plain 71M6511.** The feature list says under 50 ppm/°C; the VREF table says −40 to +40 ppm/°C (guaranteed by design).
- **E_RST precautions.** The E_RST pin description sends the reader to the Applications section for its termination, which says nothing about it; the only advice is the 1 kΩ pull-ups under the emulator port. The flash programming text names an "E_RTS" pin that does not exist (E_RST).
- **Pin 10 name.** SEG4/SDATA in the pin table, SEG4/SSDATA in the pinout figure.
- **ADC table conditions.** The ADC table is headed FIR_LEN = 0 but gives the LSB size for FIR_LEN = 1.
- **Figure numbers.** The list of figures (page 4) and the figures in the body do not agree: the list skips Figures 26 and 28, gives the sensor figures as 29 and 30 where the body numbers them 27 and 28 on page 77, and gives the LCD figures as 33 and 34 where the body has 31 and 32. This page uses the body's numbers.
- **Footnote digits in the text layer.** In the text of the PDF, footnote superscripts run into numbers (the 85 °C battery current reads "41" and "121" but is 4 and 12 µA with a footnote). The values on this page come from the rendered pages.
- **Storage temperature.** Printed as −45 °C to 165 °C.
