## How it works

The SPX3819 is a positive LDO with a pass element that drops only 340 mV at 500 mA, a low ground current, and a reference that is brought out to a pin on the fixed-voltage versions so its noise can be filtered. The datasheet also points out that the low output temperature coefficient (57 ppm/°C) makes it usable as a low-power voltage reference.

**Fixed and adjustable.** The fixed versions (1.2, 1.5, 1.8, 2.5, 3.0, 3.3 and 5.0 V) use pin 4 (SOIC and SOT-23) or pin 8 (DFN) as BYP. The adjustable version uses the same pin as ADJ, the feedback input. VOUT = 1.235 V × (1 + R1 ÷ R2), with R1 from VOUT to ADJ and R2 from ADJ to ground. R2 must be above 10 kΩ and gives the best results between 22 and 47 kΩ.

**Noise and start-up.** A 10 nF capacitor on BYP cuts the output noise a lot, and it can be left off where noise doesn't matter. Start-up speed is inversely related to the size of the BYP capacitor, so a larger one gives a slower ramp and omitting it gives the fastest turn-on. The noise figures are 300 µVRMS without a bypass capacitor and 40 µVRMS with 1 µF.

**Enable.** EN high (above 2 V) enables the regulator and below 0.4 V disables it; an open EN pin is shutdown, so EN must be driven or tied to VIN. When disabled the supply current drops to about 0.05 µA.

**Protection.** Reverse-battery protection (VIN and EN are rated −20 to +20 V), current limiting at about 800 mA and thermal shutdown.

**Output capacitor.** One is required for stability. The datasheet says a 2.2 µF aluminum electrolytic works in most circuits, or a 1 µF tantalum, that a regulator supplying 10 mA needs about half the capacitance of one supplying 150 mA, and that bench testing is the best way to choose. On the adjustable version the minimum falls as the output voltage rises.

### Worked example: 3.3 V from 5 V at 300 mA, adjustable version

1. **Divider.** With R2 = 33.2 kΩ and R1 = 54.9 kΩ, VOUT = 1.235 × (1 + 54.9 ÷ 33.2) = 3.277 V (calculated), 0.7% below 3.3 V; 56.2 kΩ gives 3.326 V. The divider carries 3.277 V ÷ 88.1 kΩ = 37 µA.
2. **Headroom.** 5 V less 3.277 V is 1.72 V, against a dropout of 180 mV typical (350 mV maximum) at 150 mA and 340 mV typical (550 mV maximum) at 500 mA.
3. **Heat.** (5 − 3.277) × 0.3 A is 0.52 W, plus up to about 0.1 W of ground-current loss (the ground current at 300 mA is not tabulated; the table gives 25 mA maximum at 500 mA). At 0.52 W the θJA values give a rise of 99 °C in the SOT-23 (191 °C/W), 67 °C in the SOIC (128.4 °C/W) and 31 °C in the DFN (59 °C/W) (calculated). The SOT-23 would reach the 125 °C operating limit at about 26 °C ambient, so a 300 mA load at this drop calls for the DFN or SOIC.

## Things to watch

- **EN must be driven.** An open EN pin is shutdown, so tie it to VIN when you want the regulator always on.
- **Heat in the SOT-23.** At 191 °C/W the 5-pin package limits the usable power to roughly 0.5 W at 25 °C (calculated from 125 °C ÷ the θJA), so a high drop at a high current needs the DFN or SOIC.
- **Noise needs the bypass capacitor, and only on fixed versions.** 300 µVRMS without it; the adjustable version has no BYP pin.
- **Start-up time grows with CBYP.** A 1 µF capacitor for 40 µVRMS also gives a slow ramp.
- **Dropout isn't specified at 2 V and below.** The note says it does not apply to those outputs.
- **Output capacitor type.** The datasheet's values are for aluminum electrolytic (2.2 µF) or tantalum (1 µF); the stability of other types is left to bench testing.
- **Ordering.** The datasheet lists separate part numbers for each fixed voltage in each package (the DFN only in 1.2 V and adjustable, the SOIC only in 5.0 V and adjustable).
- **The KiCad symbol is ours and not yet opened in KiCad.** It was generated by script and checked against the pin table, but nobody has loaded it in KiCad. The pin types and the symbol layout are our choices. The file holds one symbol for each of the three packages, because they number the pins differently. BYP/ADJ is passive because it is the bypass pin on fixed parts and the feedback input on the adjustable part.
