## How it works

The SY8113B is a synchronous buck regulator with both power switches on the same die: an 80 mΩ top switch and a 40 mΩ bottom switch, typical. The note says its proprietary PWM control gives "the highest efficiency and the highest switch frequency simultaneously", that the 500 kHz frequency lets the inductor and capacitors stay small, and that an "instant PWM" architecture gives fast transient response. It doesn't say how the control law works, whether the frequency is fixed or shifts with load or duty cycle, or what the part does at light load. The block diagram shows a PWM control and protect-logic block fed by the internal supply, the 4.5 V input under-voltage lockout, an EN comparator (1.5 V), a feedback comparator (0.6 V), the internal soft-start and thermal protection, with a current-sense amplifier on each switch.

Compensation is built in, which is why the circuit has so few parts. The output voltage is set by two resistors from the output to FB and ground, VOUT = 0.6 V × (1 + R1 ÷ R2) in the note's names. A bootstrap capacitor between BS and LX powers the top switch's gate drive. EN turns the part on when it is pulled high; it must not be left floating. The part ramps its output in about 800 µs (typical) once the input is above the under-voltage threshold.

Current limiting works on both switches. The bottom switch has a valley limit of 3 A minimum and 4.25 A maximum, with no typical value, and the top switch turns off at a peak limit of 6 A maximum. The note doesn't say how the two interact, but with a valley limit the average output current at the limit is the valley limit plus half the inductor ripple (the same relation as for other valley-limited bucks, and our reading of the note). The note's text says only that the part provides "cycle by cycle current limiting and thermal shutdown protection" and that it will "sense the output voltage conditions for the fault protection"; it doesn't describe a hiccup mode or a short-circuit threshold. Its short-circuit scope captures are described under *What the datasheet shows*.

The pin order is unusual for a six-pin part, so check it before drawing a footprint:

| Pin | Name | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | BS | Bootstrap supply for the high-side gate driver. Decouple to LX with 0.1 µF |
| 2 | GND | Ground, and the case position for the θJC measurement |
| 3 | FB | Feedback; connect to the centre of the output divider |
| 4 | EN | Enable. Pull high to turn on; do not float |
| 5 | IN | Input. Decouple to GND with at least 1 µF of ceramic capacitance |
| 6 | LX | Switching node; connect to the inductor |

## Choosing the components

### Output voltage

The output is set by VOUT = 0.6 V × (1 + R1 ÷ R2). The note recommends 10 kΩ to 1 MΩ for both resistors, with large values to cut light-load current, and its example is 3.3 V with R1 = 100 kΩ, where it calculates R2 = 22.1 kΩ. The exact result is 0.6 ÷ (3.3 − 0.6) × 100 kΩ = 22.22 kΩ, and the nearest 1% value, 22.1 kΩ, gives 3.315 V nominal. With the reference's tolerance (0.591–0.609 V) the output lands between 3.265 V and 3.365 V, before the resistors' own tolerance is added. The divider draws 27 µA, which costs 89 µW at 3.3 V out, small beside the 100 µA quiescent current.

### Inductor

Choose the inductance for a ripple current of about 40% of the maximum load, using the highest input voltage: L = VOUT × (1 − VOUT ÷ VIN,MAX) ÷ (fSW × IOUT,MAX × 40%). For 3.3 V, 18 V in, 3 A and 500 kHz that gives 4.49 µH, so the circuit uses 4.7 µH, which gives 38% ripple at 18 V and 34% at 12 V. The note says the part is "quite tolerant" of the ripple amplitude, so the final value can be slightly off the calculation without hurting performance.

The saturation current must be greater than the peak at full load, ISAT,MIN > IOUT,MAX + VOUT × (1 − VOUT ÷ VIN,MAX) ÷ (2 × fSW × L), which is 3.57 A here. That is the note's rule. If you want the inductor to survive the converter running into its current limit, a stricter rule of our own is to size it above the largest valley limit plus the full ripple, 4.25 A + 1.15 A = 5.4 A at 18 V in. The note also asks for a DCR below 50 mΩ for good efficiency, and a core that doesn't lose much at 500 kHz.

### Input capacitor

The note says the RMS current in the input capacitor is IOUT × √(D × (1 − D)), which is 1.34 A at 12 V in and at most 1.5 A at 50% duty. It asks for an X5R or better ceramic placed very close to the IN and GND pins with the smallest possible loop. Its pin table says at least 1 µF; its text recommends 10 µF of low-ESR ceramic. The reference circuit uses 10 µF. Check the capacitor's RMS rating against the current above, and check its capacitance at the DC bias it will see: a 25 V part at 12 V can lose a large part of its nominal value (our note, not the datasheet's).

### Output capacitor

The note asks for an X5R or better ceramic with more than 22 µF, chosen for both steady-state ripple and transient behaviour. The circuit uses 47 µF, the value in the electrical table's conditions. The capacitive part of the output ripple is ΔIL ÷ (8 × COUT × fSW), which is 5.4 mV at 12 V in with the values here, before the capacitor's ESR (ΔIL × ESR) and DC-bias loss are added. The note's ripple scope capture shows more than that; see below.

### Bootstrap and feedforward

The bootstrap capacitor is 100 nF of low-ESR ceramic between BS and LX. The compensation is built in. For applications with large load steps the note recommends a 22 pF ceramic in parallel with R1 to speed up the transient response. With R1 = 100 kΩ the capacitor adds a zero at 1 ÷ (2π × R1 × C4) = 72 kHz (our arithmetic); a different R1 moves it.

### Enable

The note says to pull EN high to turn the part on and not to float it. The electrical table gives the rising threshold as 1.5 V minimum and the falling threshold as 0.4 V maximum, with no typical values, and the absolute maximum on EN is VIN + 0.3 V, so a logic signal that stays below VIN is safe and tying EN straight to VIN is allowed by the ratings. If the chip that drives EN is high impedance in shutdown and IN comes straight from a Li-Ion battery, the note says to add a 1 MΩ pull-down from EN to ground, to stop noise from turning the regulator on.

## Worked example: 12 V to 3.3 V

These numbers are calculated here from the note's formulas. The note doesn't list them, and nothing below was measured. They assume ideal switches and nominal component values, and they use the limits in the note's electrical table.

| Quantity | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Duty cycle at 12 V in | 27.5% (VOUT ÷ VIN) |
| On-time at 12 V and at 18 V in | 550 ns and 367 ns (the part's minimum on-time is 50 / 80 / 120 ns, minimum / typical / maximum) |
| Recommended inductance (40% ripple at 18 V) | 4.49 µH; the circuit uses 4.7 µH |
| Inductor ripple current at 12 V and at 18 V | 1.02 A (34% of full load) and 1.15 A (38%) |
| Peak inductor current, 18 V in, 3 A load | 3.57 A |
| Valley inductor current, 18 V in, 3 A load | 2.43 A, below the 3 A minimum valley limit |
| Output current limit at 12 V in (valley limit + ΔIL ÷ 2) | 3.5 A with the minimum valley limit, 4.8 A with the maximum |
| Output current limit at 18 V in | 3.6 A with the minimum valley limit, 4.8 A with the maximum |
| Saturation current: the note's rule, and the stricter rule | Above 3.57 A, and above 5.4 A to survive the maximum valley limit |
| Input capacitor RMS current at 12 V in | 1.34 A (worst case 1.5 A at 50% duty) |
| Input ripple with one 10 µF ceramic at 12 V in | 120 mV (150 mV worst case), before DC-bias loss |
| Output ripple from the capacitance, 47 µF, at 12 V and at 18 V in | 5.4 mV and 6.1 mV, plus ΔIL × ESR |
| Feedforward zero with R1 = 100 kΩ and C4 = 22 pF | 72 kHz |
| Lowest VIN at which the part starts | Under-voltage lockout is 4.5 V at most, so start-up is only guaranteed from just above 4.5 V; with 0.3 V of hysteresis it should keep running down to about 4.2 V (our subtraction) |

> **The output current limit sits close to the 3 A rating.** With the note's valley limit of 3 A minimum, the output current limit comes to about 3.5 A at 12 V in, so at the worst corner the part has only about 17% headroom above its rating. The note gives no typical limit. Treat 3 A as a ceiling.

> **The input ripple with a single 10 µF ceramic is large.** About 120 mV at 12 V in, before the capacitor loses capacitance under DC bias. The note's minimum is 1 µF, and its recommended value is 10 µF. If the input has a ripple budget, add ceramic capacitance and check the RMS rating, which is about 1.3 A here.

## Other output voltages

The note gives one example of the divider (3.3 V with 100 kΩ and 22.1 kΩ) and plots for 5 V and 1.2 V, but no table of values. The rows below are our arithmetic: the divider uses the same 100 kΩ upper resistor, the inductor follows the note's 40% ripple equation at 18 V in rounded to the nearest standard value (E6), and the saturation current is the note's rule with that inductor. The last two columns are estimates of ours. Two limits come from the on-time and off-time figures. At 500 kHz the minimum on-time of 120 ns (maximum) means the converter can't hold a fixed 500 kHz below about 6% duty, which is 1.08 V out at 18 V in and 0.72 V out at 12 V in. The note doesn't say what the part does below that. At the other end, the 220 ns maximum minimum off-time limits duty to about 89% at 500 kHz, which is why a 5 V output needs roughly 6 V in at full load. The note's 5 V plot starts at 6 V in.

## Layout

The note's layout section is five rules. With a 3 A converter in a six-pin package, the layout decides how well it works and how hot it runs.

- Put as much **ground copper** as the board allows on the GND pin, ideally a ground plane. It sets the thermal and noise performance.
- Put **CIN close to the IN and GND pins** and keep the loop that CIN and the pins form as small as possible.
- Keep the **copper on the LX pin** as small as possible, to avoid noise problems.
- Keep **R1, R2 and the trace to FB** away from the LX net; they must not run next to it.
- Place CIN, L, R1 and R2 close to the IC, for the best efficiency and the least noise.
- If the chip that drives EN is high impedance at shutdown and IN comes directly from a source such as a Li-Ion battery, add a **1 MΩ pull-down** from EN to ground.

The note gives θJA as 100 °C/W, measured in natural convection at 25 °C on a low-conductivity JEDEC 51-3 four-layer test board, and θJC as 11.2 °C/W with pin 2 as the case position. It lists 1 W of power dissipation at 25 °C, which agrees with a 125 °C junction at 100 °C/W (our arithmetic: (125 − 25) ÷ 100 = 1.0 W). Its recommended junction range is −40 to 125 °C and its absolute maximum is 150 °C. On the same arithmetic the allowed dissipation falls to 0.70 W at 55 °C ambient and 0.40 W at 85 °C. It gives the TSOT23-6 outline and a recommended pad layout, but no thermal-via or copper-area guidance beyond the first rule above.

## What the datasheet shows

The note's performance page gives efficiency against load current from about 8 mA to about 3.5 A for three output voltages, each at three input voltages, plus scope captures at 12 V in and 3.3 V out: start-up and shut-down at 3 A, output ripple at 3 A, two load steps and two short-circuit tests. The efficiency plots don't say which inductor and capacitors were used. The block diagram and a short description of the PWM control are the only other technical content.

Reading the curves by eye, in percent, at four load currents. Treat these as rough, about two points either way, since they come from a graph. The last two columns are our arithmetic from the 3 A reading, with the note's 100 °C/W:

The scope captures, read by eye (all at 12 V in and 3.3 V out; the note doesn't state the inductor or output capacitor for them):

- **Start-up and shut-down at 3 A.** The output ramps up monotonically with no overshoot we can see, in about 1 ms by our reading of a 400 µs/div capture, against 800 µs typical in the table. On shut-down, switching continues for a few cycles after EN falls, then the inductor current runs down to zero.
- **Output ripple at 3 A.** About 20 mV peak to peak at 20 mV/div, by eye. That is several times the capacitive-only estimate above, so the capture must include ESR, DC-bias loss or probe effects.
- **Inductor ripple.** The 3 A captures show about 1.5 A peak to peak at 1 A/div. That fits a 3.3 µH inductor (we calculate 1.45 A at 12 V) better than 4.7 µH (1.02 A), so the captures were probably made with a smaller inductor than this recipe's.
- **Load steps.** A 0.3 A to 3 A step at 500 mV/div moves the output by roughly 200 mV down and 300 mV up, and a 1.5 A to 3 A step at 200 mV/div by roughly 130 mV each way, both by eye. Since the output capacitor is not given, don't scale these to a different design.
- **Short circuit.** Both captures (open-to-short and 3 A-to-short) show the output pulled to zero for about 5 ms of a 10 ms cycle, with the inductor current holding at roughly 2 A by our reading, and the output ramping back up when the short is released. The capture looks like a test fixture that applies and releases the short; it doesn't show whether the part latches or hiccups when a short is held.

## Things to watch

- **This is a draft source.** The only copy is Rev 0.1, marked "Confidential, prepared for Internal Use Only", and it comes from a distributor's site. Confirm every value against a current Silergy datasheet before relying on it, and don't treat this page as a released reference design.
- **The note's figure has no values.** The text gives values for the bootstrap capacitor, the feedforward capacitor and one divider example, and its electrical table gives an output capacitor and an inductor for a 1.2 V test. The inductor and input capacitor in this recipe's BOM are our choices, and the schematic caption says so.
- **3 A is a thermal ceiling as well as a current one.** At 12 V in and 3.3 V out, about 91% efficiency at 3 A means about 1.0 W of loss, which is 98 °C of rise at the note's 100 °C/W. That is nearly all of the 1.0 W the note allows at 25 °C, and it exceeds the 125 °C junction limit above 27 °C ambient. The 100 °C/W figure is for a low-conductivity JEDEC board; a board with a good ground plane does better. At 85 °C ambient the note's own numbers allow only 0.4 W of dissipation. At about 93% efficiency (by eye, on the 12 V curve near 1.5 A), 1.5 A out at 3.3 V loses about 0.4 W, so plan on roughly half the current there (our rough estimate).
- **The valley limit is a minimum and a maximum, not a typical value.** The output current limit comes out between 3.5 A and 4.8 A at 12 V in. The short-circuit captures show roughly 2 A in the inductor during the short, which is below the 3 A minimum valley limit; the note doesn't explain this. Design to the table.
- **No light-load behaviour is described.** The note never names a light-load mode (pulse skipping, forced PWM or other). The efficiency curves at 10 mA run between 70% and 93%, which suggests the part doesn't run at full-rate switching at very light load, but that is our inference. If light-load ripple, noise or no-load frequency matters to you, measure it.
- **High-duty operation is not described.** The note gives a 140 / 170 / 220 ns minimum off-time but doesn't say whether the frequency falls as the duty cycle rises. A 5 V output needs roughly 6 V in at full load on a conservative estimate; the note's 5 V plot begins at 6 V in.
- **The input capacitor values disagree.** The pin table says "at least 1 µF", the text recommends 10 µF, and the figure gives none. Use 10 µF or more, check the RMS current and allow for DC-bias loss.
- **The under-voltage threshold and the minimum input are the same number.** The lockout is given as 4.5 V maximum, and the recommended operating range starts at 4.5 V, so start-up at exactly 4.5 V is not guaranteed. The note gives hysteresis (0.3 V typical) but no typical or minimum threshold.
- **The enable thresholds have no typical values.** The note gives 1.5 V minimum rising and 0.4 V maximum falling, with nothing in between, so a divider on EN can't be placed precisely. EN must not float.
- **The efficiency plots run past the rating.** They extend to about 3.5 A, above the 3 A output rating, and don't state the inductor or the capacitors used. The ripple in the scope captures suggests a smaller inductor than this recipe's 4.7 µH.
- **The part page links an LCSC datasheet that we haven't compared with this note.** If the two differ, the datasheet is the better source.
