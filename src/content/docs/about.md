Datasheets tell you what a part can do. Reference designs show you how to use it. But reference designs are scattered across manufacturers' websites as PDFs, each laid out its own way, and they rarely explain why a value was chosen. Circuit Dessert is an attempt to gather them in one open, searchable place and write them up like recipes.

## Why this exists

- **A schematic shows what was built, not why.** A recipe page adds the reasoning behind each part value, a worked example with the numbers, and the layout advice that usually sits in a footnote.
- **Designs are spread across many sites.** Every manufacturer publishes in its own format. Putting them in one place makes it easier to compare approaches and to find a starting point for a part you already have.
- **Specs should be checked.** Part pages are checked against the datasheet, and recipes say which revision of the source their numbers came from.

## What it aims to do

- **Plate designs like recipes.** The parts list is the ingredients, and the reasoning and calculations are the method.
- **Credit every design to its maker** and link back to the original source.
- **Be upfront about confidence.** Say what was checked, which values we calculated ourselves, and mark prose that still needs a human review as a draft.
- **Stay open.** Hobbyists, students and working engineers can all [contribute a recipe or a part](site:contribute/) through GitHub, and anyone can copy the site.

Circuit Dessert is not a substitute for the manufacturer's datasheet. Check the source document before you build a board or place an order.

## Open to use and reuse

The written content and drawings are CC BY 4.0 and the code is MIT, so you can fork the repository and run your own copy, including an internal version for your company. The manufacturers' own documents, figures and trademarks stay with them. The full details are on the [Licenses](site:licenses/) page.
